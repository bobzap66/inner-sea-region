# Reactive Strike and Provoking

This page is a **table-facing tactical guide**, not a replacement for the Pathfinder rules. The remastered name for Attack of Opportunity is **Reactive Strike**.

[View Reactive Strike on Archives of Nethys](https://2e.aonprd.com/Actions.aspx?ID=2256)

## What Can Trigger It?

Reactive Strike can trigger when a creature within the reactor's reach uses a **manipulate** action, uses a **move** action, makes a ranged attack, or leaves a square during a move action.

Not every creature has Reactive Strike. Do not assume that simply being adjacent to an enemy means movement will provoke.

## Movement Matters

Leaving a square within a creature's reach can trigger a reaction. If a move action does not actually leave a square, a reaction triggered by the move action occurs at the end of that action instead.

The **Step** action is specifically designed to avoid reactions triggered by movement.

![[basic-guide-to-attacks-of-opportunity-and-provoking-threaten.jpeg]]

The important tactical question is not simply whether you enter an enemy's reach, but whether the movement or action you use satisfies that creature's reaction trigger.

## Reach

Reach can make the threatened area much larger than the squares immediately adjacent to a creature.

![[basic-guide-to-attacks-of-opportunity-and-provoking-reach.jpeg]]

A creature can also have different reach values for different attacks, so knowing that an enemy has reach does not always tell you exactly which squares are dangerous for every reaction or Strike.

## Critical Hits and Disruption

If Reactive Strike critically hits and the triggering action had the **manipulate** trait, the triggering action is disrupted. Check the current rules for the action being attempted to determine what resources are lost or what part of an activity fails.

## Tactical Use

Provoking a reaction is not automatically a mistake. A durable character can sometimes deliberately draw out an enemy's reaction so that a more vulnerable ally can safely move, cast, manipulate an item, or make a ranged attack afterward. Initiative order matters because reactions normally return at the start of the reacting creature's turn.

Recall Knowledge is often the safest way to learn whether a creature has a dangerous reaction. The other method is to find out experimentally, which is considerably less safe.
