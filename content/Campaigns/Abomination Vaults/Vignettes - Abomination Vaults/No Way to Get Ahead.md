# No Way to Get Ahead


> [!side]
> :   ![[no-way-to-get-ahead-scared-theron-2.png]]
>
>     ![[assets/world-anvil/no-way-to-get-ahead-theron-head-6.webp]]

The agony never dulled. If anything, it sharpened with each heartbeat he no longer had. His eyes—his only living parts—were forced to drink in every cruel detail. His body, stretched taut in its bindings, twitched at the pull of wires and veins of glass. His head, shackled to the slab, could not turn away.

The laboratory stank of alchemy and rot. The seugathi’s tendrils whispered against parchment, spiraling out mad glyphs. The drider shifted in the rafters, its claws screeching stone, hungry eyes glimmering. The augmented goblin fussed with tubing that fed the two green-lit tanks where morlocks floated like malformed children, twitching in half-dreams.

Then the door crashed open.

Light poured in, framing familiar shapes: August with his calm, cutting magic; Captain Longsaddle bristling like a blade himself; the kobold alchemist already clutching fire in glass; Malaroth, staff clenched, eyes storm-dark; a shoony trembling but standing tall; and a hard-eyed human stranger, knife-ready.

His will flared like a dying torch. He tried to bellow their names, tried to warn them, tried to beg—yet what spilled from his mouth was a ragged howl, more beast than man, pain made into sound.

The seugathi froze mid-scribble. The drider scuttled down the wall. The goblin’s grin widened. The tanks split.

Glass rained down as the morlocks clawed into the chamber, howls bubbling through fluid-stained teeth. Spells and fire erupted instantly—August’s incantations crackled in arcs of light, the kobold’s bombs burst like falling stars, the shoony’s bark of courage rose above it all. Captain Longsaddle pressed forward, sword flashing, shield braced.

And Malaroth—Malaroth lifted his staff but stopped. His lips formed words he swallowed again. No skeletons rose, no shadows answered his call. He dared not—He could see the way the Captain’s eyes flicked toward him, full of suspicion, waiting. So Malaroth fought only with the safer arts: bolts of force, a wall of flame, magic that cost him less than truth.

It wasn’t enough.

The morlocks surged. The drider loosed a net of webbing that sent the shoony sprawling. The goblin’s augmented limbs snapped out, tearing a vial from the kobold’s hand before it could burst. And the seugathi—by gods, the seugathi—its mind struck like a hammer, staggering August back through the doorway.

He screamed again, useless, helpless, every shred of his soul reaching for them. But the rescue crumbled. Step by step, they were forced back. First retreat, then rout. The door slammed closed, cutting off the light.

Darkness and silence pressed in again. Only the hiss of broken tanks and the slow scrape of the drider’s limbs remained.

No help was coming.
