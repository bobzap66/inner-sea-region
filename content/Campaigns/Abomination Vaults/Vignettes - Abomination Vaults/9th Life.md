# 9th Life


The dark was more than dark—it pulsed, alive, like the inside of some vast creature’s throat. She tried to breathe but every inhale tasted of iron and stagnant water, every exhale came too quick, too thin. Her body did not feel like her own. Her arms burned as though the bones had been melted and reforged into some new, unguessable shape. She hugged them tight against herself, as if pressure alone could press them back into what they once were.

Memory shattered when she reached for it. Pain. Screaming. A void that swallowed thought and spat her back into this place. Every time she blinked, she saw the afterimage of hands that weren’t hers clawing through the dark.

Something moved in the cell beside her. A shuffle, wet and dragging, like meat pulling itself across stone. Then another sound, from the other side: a scrape that went on and on until her teeth hurt, as if whatever lay there was trying to dig its way free with bare bones. She dared a glance and saw only hints—shapes hunched wrong, limbs that twitched too sharply, as though jointed by a careless god.

She pressed her forehead against the wall, eyes squeezed shut. The stone pulsed warm beneath her skin, almost like a heartbeat. She jerked back, heart hammering, but when she touched it again it was only cold rock.

Whispers seeped in from somewhere beyond—low and wet, in a tongue that slid away from meaning. They coiled in her mind like worms, promising nothing, explaining nothing.

Her arms throbbed again, sharp enough to make her bite down on her own lip. She imagined something inside her still shifting, unfinished. Am I still myself? The question rang, hollow and unanswerable.

The dark swallowed her breath, and every drip of water from the ceiling sounded like a countdown she could not see.

She was not alone. Not with the broken things in the cages. Not with the whispering just beyond hearing. And worst of all—not with herself.
