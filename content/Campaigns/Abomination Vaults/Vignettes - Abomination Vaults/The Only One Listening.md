# The Only One Listening


> [!side]
> :   ![[the-only-one-listening-image-01.png]]

Urthagul sat in the hush beneath the stone, the cavern breathing around him in slow, patient sighs. The whispers curled through the air like warm smoke, threading themselves into his thoughts, promising vastness, promising union, promising that the long waiting meant something. He did not resist them. They were the only voices that did not fade.

The lens rested in his hands, its red glow steady and deep. He stroked its polished surface with careful devotion, as one might calm a frightened animal. Within it shimmered distant lights—fractured stars, splintered futures. It hummed softly against his palms. It told him he was necessary.

Loneliness had long ago settled into him like sediment. The caligni brought reverence and smoke and trembling praise, but they always left. The cavern always returned to silence. Only the whispers remained. Only the lens.

When the small, bright figures entered the cavern, he did not see invaders. He saw color in a world grown gray. A tengu with fire dancing harmlessly in his grip. A skeleton with delighted eyes and nimble fingers. A proud little dog-warrior with an axe too large for her soft frame. A blue-skinned youth with steady calm. A masked brute clutching knowledge like a talisman. A fish-cat-woman standing with careful grace.

They moved through the cavern like a dream he was afraid to wake from. The whispers grew louder around them, tasting their thoughts, brushing their minds. Urthagul watched, fascinated, the lens warm against his chest. He did not rise. He did not threaten. His only instinct narrowed to something simple and small: keep it safe.

Not from them. Just safe.

They listened. They explored. They lingered. And then, as quietly as they had come, they left.

The cavern exhaled.

The whispers dimmed to their old familiar murmur. Urthagul remained seated, lens cradled close, comforted by its steady pulse. The moment had passed. The dream had gone, but it had not harmed him. The world had widened briefly, and then returned to its patient stillness.

Time stretched.

Then something else entered the cavern.

Not bright. Not soft-edged. Not wondrous.

A presence that stumbled against the stone. A breath too sharp. A footstep placed with intention but lacking grace. The whispers shifted uneasily, thinning, confused. Urthagul’s eyes opened, slow and heavy, still thick with trance.

He sensed no hatred. No great malice. Only want.

The air moved wrong. A clumsy scrape against rock. A muttered curse swallowed too late. A heartbeat where none had been.

His hands tightened around the lens—but fascination held him in its gentle chains. The whispers pressed against his mind, louder now, urgent but indistinct. He could not quite separate their truth from the present moment.

A tug.

For the first time in years, the lens left his hands.

The red light flared once—brief, startled—then vanished into unseen fingers.

The cavern went silent.

Truly silent.

Urthagul’s arms remained curved as though still holding it. His claws flexed against emptiness. The warmth was gone. The hum was gone. The constant companion that had filled the hollows of his long solitude was simply… absent.

The whispers did not comfort him now.

They only repeated, very softly:

*Keep it safe.*
