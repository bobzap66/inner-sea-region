# Lumbering Threat


Two days. Two days since he sent old Bertram back to Otari, and still no sign of help. Jollibin Urphell rubbed his pudgy fingers together, staring at the soot-streaked ground outside the cabin. The air still carried the stink of burned wood, but not from a campfire. No, this was something else. Something unnatural.

A week ago, it was just bad luck. A few trees gone up in smoke overnight—not unheard of in the woods, though he’d never seen it happen so fast, so sudden. Then came the animals. A dog, wreathed in flame, tearing through the camp with madness in its eyes. A rat that left a trail of embers, snarling like a demon. The men were rattled after the first attack. After the second, one packed his things and left in the night.

Jollibin spat into the dirt, cursing under his breath. His men were tough, good folk, but even they had their limits. Three injured, one still barely able to stand after that thrice-damned burning dog sunk its teeth into his leg. The They had no camp healer, but one of the men was doing what he could for the wounded. And the worst part? Whoever—or whatever—was out there wasn’t stopping. Three nights in a row, something had tried to burn them out.

First, the storage tent—fire took half their supplies before they managed to douse it. Then, flames licking at the edges of the mess tent, driving the men into a panic. Last night, it was the cabin itself, and if he hadn’t woken when he did, they might all have died. Someone, or something, wanted them gone.

He clenched his jaw, glaring toward the dark line of the trees. Was Bertram even safe? Was Otari sending help, or had the old cook met the same fiery fate as the rest? His gut told him to hold out, to trust in the town. But his gut was also telling him that whatever was coming next... it was going to be worse.

He just hoped his men lived long enough to see that help arrive.
