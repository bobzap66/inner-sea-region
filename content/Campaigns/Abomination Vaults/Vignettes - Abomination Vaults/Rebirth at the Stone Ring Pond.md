### Rebirth at Stone Ring Pond

At dawn, the mists clung low over the standing stones of the pond, the air thick with the scent of peat, sage, and iron. The ancient circle hummed faintly, dew shimmering on carvings so old even Worliwynn the caretaker could not name the hands that made them. Within that ring lay Theron’s head—washed clean, resting on a bed of moss and feathers, surrounded by offerings from beasts and birds alike.

Worliwynn knelt before it, her green curls damp with fog, her two acolytes flanking her like shadows. The ritual of reincarnation was no mere spell—it was an act of persuasion, four hours of pleading with the spirits of life and death to trade places, if only for a moment. The first hour was silence; the world itself seemed to hold its breath. The second saw the runes glow with green-gold light. The third hour made the pond ripple without wind, its mirrored surface warping with unseen power.

By the fourth hour, Worliwynn’s voice had turned hoarse, her chant raw and ancient. Her acolytes swayed from exhaustion, yet still the circle held. Then came the light—seeping from Theron’s eyes, his mouth, and the severed stump of his neck. It shimmered gold and white, not burning but weaving. The neck split, and from it poured the substance of life: muscle knitting, bone forming, sinew stretching downward until a new body took shape beneath the head. Flesh grew in rippling waves, damp and shining with creation’s sweat.

Feathers burst through the skin, black and iridescent, spreading across his shoulders and down his back. The bones beneath grew light and avian, though his hands remained steady and human—built for sword and shield, not talon. When the transformation slowed, two vestigial wings clung to his shoulder blades, twitching faintly as if remembering skies they would never touch. His beak hardened to an obsidian sheen, and his eyes snapped open—gray, sharp, and unmistakably his.

The glow faded. The circle steamed. One acolyte wept; the other only knelt in awe. Worliwynn leaned heavily on her staff, whispering, “By earth and echo... he lives again.”

Theron drew his first breath through his new lungs, the sound half-caw, half-sigh. His gaze fell upon the flaming sword laid beside him. When his fingers curled around its hilt, fire answered, flaring bright as dawn. He stood—steadily, strangely—and the useless wings gave a shiver before settling against his back.

“Feels different,” he rasped, voice dry and familiar. “But alive’ll do.”

As the sun rose over Stone Ring Pond, the mist retreated and the light caught on his feathers, armor, and flame. The circle was quiet again, the ancient stones humming with quiet approval—as though the wild itself had agreed to give him one more chance.
