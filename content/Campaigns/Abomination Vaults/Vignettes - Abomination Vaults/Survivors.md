# Survivors


The cavern echoed with the last hiss of the slain hydra as August and Malaroth pulled themselves back from the brink. Bloodied, drained, and barely able to stand, they staggered toward the shafts that led upward. Neither dared descend into the pool. The faint light of Talia’s implants had already guttered out. Theron’s fire had vanished beneath the water.

The sounds of the battle below — the splashing, the screaming — had ended.

The two survivors met one another’s gaze in the half-dark. August’s hand trembled against his wand. Malaroth’s bone-thin fingers clenched his staff. Neither spoke aloud the words pressing against their lips: they were gone.

Summoning what skeletal remnants remained, Malaroth bought them enough time to limp away. Together, they retreated through the tunnels, up through the long, winding passages of the Vaults, and back toward Otari. Their hearts were heavy, their silence heavier. To go back was to die, and they knew it. They left the others for dead.

---

But death did not claim everyone.

Kiri stirred first. Her lungs burned, her body wracked from near-drowning, but she was dragged from the black water before it finished her. Talia, unconscious and bloodied, was likewise hauled to shore. Both were stripped of weapons and armor, bound, and carried through winding passages by unseen captors.

When Talia finally swam back toward awareness, she found herself locked in a stone cell — a damp, narrow room with a barred door of corroded iron. Her body ached from wounds left untended, her clothes clung wet and heavy. Through the walls she heard Kiri’s muffled coughing somewhere nearby, trapped in her own chamber. She wondered who it was. Probably Theron.

And then came the voice.

Low. Guttural. Its words carried like a knife’s edge through the stone.

“Two more… breathing still. Yesss. Jafaki will be pleased. Fresh subjects… her experiments require strong stock.”

The voice chuckled wetly, the sound of slime sliding on stone. “They will scream well.”

Talia froze, heart pounding in her chest. She had no weapons. No allies. Only the sound of her companion’s shallow breathing in the room beyond, and the promise of horrors yet to come
