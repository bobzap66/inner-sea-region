# Morlibint's Dossier: Closing Remarks

*A final note regarding the events surrounding the Gauntlight and the heroes who brought them to an end.*

The defeat of Belcorra Haruvex marks the conclusion of one of the most significant supernatural crises in Otari's history. For centuries, the Gauntlight stood as a monument to fear, looming over the town and the coastline beyond. Beneath it lay not merely a haunted ruin, but the remnants of a centuries-long campaign of malice, obsession, and undeath orchestrated by a woman unwilling to surrender either her hatred or her ambitions. That threat has now ended. Belcorra Haruvex is destroyed, the dead no longer answer her call, and the Gauntlight itself has collapsed into the depths below. Otari endures.

In the days that followed, the town celebrated. Lanterns lined the harbor, musicians filled the streets, and merchants abandoned their counters to join the festivities. Fishermen who had spent their lives glancing uneasily toward the northern cliffs raised cups in toast, while children played games beneath decorations depicting the fallen lighthouse. At the center of it all stood the heroes responsible for Otari's salvation: August Shepard, Kiri Steelpaws, Talia Nightclaw, Theron, and Comet. Their names were spoken with gratitude in every tavern and marketplace. For perhaps the first time in generations, the people of Otari looked toward the place where the Gauntlight once stood and felt relief rather than dread.

As a scholar, I am generally more interested in records than celebrations. Yet I could not help but reflect upon the irony of the moment. Belcorra spent centuries attempting to ensure that the world remembered her. She sought immortality through fear, domination, and suffering. In the end, it was not the tyrant who was celebrated, but those who stood against her.

The observation led me to a more troubling realization. Otari remembers these events because Otari lived through them. The fishermen remember. The merchants remember. The families who sought shelter during the Gauntlight's attacks remember. Yet beyond this town, how many know what transpired here? How many in Absalom, Magnimar, or elsewhere along the Inner Sea are even aware that a catastrophe centuries in the making was narrowly averted on a remote stretch of coastline?

Belcorra's return was made possible, in part, because knowledge faded. Warnings became stories. Stories became folklore. Folklore became superstition. Each generation remembered a little less than the one before it until the truth was buried beneath rumor and disbelief. History neglected creates opportunities for old evils.

For that reason, I have resolved to compile a complete account of the expedition into the Abomination Vaults. Journals, maps, witness testimony, recovered texts, sketches, and personal recollections shall be gathered into a single volume. The people of Otari deserve to have their story preserved, and the wider world deserves to know what was accomplished here beneath the shadow of the Gauntlight.

During the course of this effort, I encountered one final curiosity. Nothing so dramatic as a contradiction, nor anything so obvious as a missing page. Rather, I found myself occasionally struck by the peculiar impression that certain details resisted easy arrangement. A sketch whose significance escaped me. Notes that seemed to imply context no longer present within them. Several moments in which I found myself reviewing my records with the sensation that some small but important piece of the narrative had slipped from my grasp. I attribute this, of course, to fatigue. Any scholar who spends enough evenings surrounded by old papers will eventually begin imagining patterns where none exist.

Nevertheless, I record the observation here for the sake of completeness. The account is finished. Yet from time to time I find myself with the inexplicable feeling that the story should be slightly longer than it is.

No matter. The truth remains unchanged. Five heroes descended into darkness beneath the earth and returned victorious against a terror that had haunted this coast for centuries. Their deeds deserve to be remembered. And so they shall.
