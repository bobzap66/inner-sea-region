# Morlibint's Dossier: The Children of Belcorra


> [!side]
> :   ![[morlibint-s-dossier-the-children-of-belcorra-image-01.png]]

*Extract from the Morlibint Dossier*

The beings known as the **Children of Belcorra** were not created as soldiers, cultists, or guardians. They were gardeners.

Belcorra required sustenance for the deeper Vaults—fungus, fish, subterranean crops, and the careful management of hostile ecosystems. Rather than oversee this herself, she compelled a community of deep gnomes already dwelling in the caverns to tend these spaces in her name. They were given protection, purpose, and the assurance that their work mattered.

They accepted.

When Belcorra fell, the Children did not disperse. They did not rebel. They did not abandon their duties. They continued to plant, harvest, repair, and plan, convinced her absence was temporary—or a test.

Years passed. Then decades. Then centuries.

Purpose slowly collapsed into procedure.

As numbers dwindled and bodies failed, the Children embraced undeath. This choice appears to have been pragmatic rather than devotional. Undeath preserved labor. It did not restore leadership. The Children endured, but they did not adapt.

**Maintenance replaced intention.**

Their carefully tended farm decayed into something wilder. New creatures arrived. Old systems failed. The Children responded by working harder at tasks that no longer achieved their original goals. Records suggest increasing isolation, fixation, and ritualized behavior. Several individuals entered long periods of torpor, awakening only to resume familiar routines.

Belcorra’s return did not resolve this decay. She issued new directives but provided no renewed structure. Her appointment of an overseer introduced authority without urgency, supervision without purpose. The Children obeyed—because obedience was all they had left.

I do not find evidence of cruelty among them.

**I find exhaustion.**

The Children of Belcorra represent the most unsettling failure of the Farm—not because they rebelled, but because they did not. They demonstrate what happens when duty persists after meaning has eroded, and when loyalty is allowed to substitute for judgment.

They waited for Belcorra to return.

**They did not ask what should happen when she did.**

*— Morlibint*
