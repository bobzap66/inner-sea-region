# Morlibint's Dossier: The Farm Redux

> :   ![[morlibint-s-dossier-the-farm-redux-image-01.png]]

*Extract from the Morlibint Dossier*

The Farm is not a single system, nor was it ever intended to be one. It is a convergence of accommodations—natural caverns reshaped just enough to serve, populations bent rather than broken, and authority asserted unevenly across distance and time. This, more than any singular horror, explains its present condition.

Belcorra Haruvex did not conquer this place in the manner she conquered other levels of the Vaults. She built within it. She exploited what was already viable and imposed structure only where necessary, leaving much of the cavern’s ecology intact so long as it remained productive. The result was not order, but persistence.

The Children of Belcorra represent the clearest example of this persistence curdling into obligation. Enslaved through fear and favor, they continued their work long after its purpose was lost, choosing undeath rather than abandonment. Their failure is not one of loyalty, but of direction. When Belcorra returned, she did not restore meaning to their labor—she merely reassigned it. The system resumed without being corrected.

Elsewhere, belief arose without instruction. The caligni who gathered around Urthagul did so not because they were commanded, but because proximity to endurance bred interpretation. Silence became wisdom. Survival became proof. What they worship is not Urthagul himself, but the idea that something can remain unchanged within a place that erodes everything else.

In contrast, the drow of the upper caverns demonstrate the only consistent refusal observed on this level. They did not adapt themselves to Belcorra’s designs, nor did they attempt to dismantle them. They measured, withdrew, and remained deliberately unimportant. Their resistance is quiet, methodical, and notably effective.

What unites these factions is not allegiance or opposition, but response. Each group encountered the same environment and drew different conclusions. Some endured. Some believed. Some measured and stepped back. None fully understood the system they were participating in, yet all contributed to its continued function.

This is the Farm’s defining trait. It does not require unity, loyalty, or comprehension. It requires only that its inhabitants continue to act—planting, hunting, watching, worshipping, or waiting. Belcorra’s return did not create this dynamic. It merely resumed oversight of a process already in motion.

I find this more troubling than outright domination.

A conquered system collapses when its conqueror falls. A persistent system survives neglect, contradiction, and even ignorance of its own purpose. The Farm endured centuries without direction and was immediately ready to serve again when direction returned.

**Belcorra did not need to rebuild this place.**
**She only needed to remind it who it was for.**

*— Morlibint*
