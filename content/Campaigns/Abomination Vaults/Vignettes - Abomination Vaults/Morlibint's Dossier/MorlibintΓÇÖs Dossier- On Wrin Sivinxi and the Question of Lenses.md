*Filed under: Astrological Observations, Probable Nonsense, Uncomfortable Correlations*

I do not make a habit of recording Wrin Sivinxi’s star readings. Astrology is a discipline with a long and ignoble history of being correct for the wrong reasons, and I prefer my warnings grounded in mechanisms I can diagram.

That said, I was present for this one.

Wrin did not frame her findings as prophecy. That alone warranted my attention. She spoke instead of alignment, geometry, and focus—terms with respectable academic pedigrees. She claimed the heavens were not telling a story, but demonstrating a device.

Four points of light, previously insignificant, had shifted into an exact configuration. Not a constellation. A functional arrangement. I verified the charting myself. The math is sound, irritatingly so.

Wrin called them lenses.

According to her interpretation, each point corresponds to a distinct filter through which reality may be observed when direct attention would be… inadvisable.

The first, tinged red, she associated with directed violence. Not the generation of rage, but its refinement. I would phrase this as a selective amplifier for hostile intent. Distasteful, but plausible.

The second, green and diminished, she tied to post-mortem persistence. Here I objected—until I remembered the number of walking corpses we have cataloged beneath Gauntlight. Continuation without vitality is, regrettably, a recurring theme.

The third, ochre and unstable, governs psychological attrition. Wrin described it poetically as “collapse without spectacle.” I would call it a sustained emotional dampener. We have both observed its effects firsthand.

The fourth point was… problematic.

It is not a star in any meaningful sense. Light behaves incorrectly around it. Measurements resist consistency. Wrin referred to it as a gap that learned how to persist. I dislike that phrasing. I dislike even more that it aligns neatly with known properties of incorporeal entities.

Taken individually, Wrin argued, these lenses are survivable. I agree. History is full of cultists who toyed with fragments and lived long enough to regret it.

Together, however, they do not increase power. They direct attention.

Specifically, the attention of Nhimbaloth.

Here I am compelled to note my skepticism. Great Old Ones do not “look” in any sense that can be usefully analogized. Nevertheless, the underlying principle—that sufficient alignment of related phenomena can force interaction with otherwise indifferent forces—is not without precedent.

Wrin’s most troubling assertion concerns Belcorra Haruvex herself.

She believes Belcorra did not bind the Empty Death, nor attempt to command it. Instead, she constructed a framework in which she could be perceived by it—selectively, and safely. A controlled aperture, one might say.

Belcorra retained one lens close at all times. The others were distributed. This is consistent with what we have observed in the lower levels. I had previously attributed this to paranoia. I now suspect structural necessity.

If the remaining three lenses are brought into alignment simultaneously, Wrin claims the focus inverts.

The implication is clear enough that even I will not insult the reader by spelling it out.

I am not prepared to endorse Wrin’s conclusions wholesale. Astrology remains an interpretive art, and she is prone to poetic excess.

However.

The charts are accurate. The correlations are consistent. And Belcorra Haruvex has demonstrated, repeatedly, an unsettling talent for turning theory into infrastructure.

For now, I record this not as prophecy, but as a working model.

If Wrin is wrong, then we have wasted ink.

If she is right, then at last we understand why Belcorra has proven so difficult to erase—

—and what, precisely, it might take to make her visible to something that does not forgive being noticed.
