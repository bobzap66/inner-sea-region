# Morlibint's Dossier: The Whispering Caverns

![[shared-cover-32ff100f6011.png]]

> [!side]
> :   ![[morlibint-s-dossier-the-whispering-caverns-image-01.jpeg]]

*Extract from the Morlibint Dossier*

There is a cavern within the Farm that resists useful description.

Those who pass through it report whispers. This is consistent. What the whispers *say* is not.

Accounts differ wildly. Some hear fragments of instruction. Others hear reassurance. A few hear nothing at all, yet leave altered—distracted, muted, or unusually calm. I have found no reliable correlation between the content of the whispers and any shared language, belief, or intent.

This suggests a flaw in the question itself.

The prevailing assumption among explorers is that the cavern communicates. I am no longer convinced this is accurate. The evidence better supports a more unsettling conclusion: the cavern reflects.

Prolonged exposure appears to dampen emotional extremes while simultaneously eroding clarity of thought. Subjects do not become agitated. They become *agreeable*. This effect is subtle at first and easily mistaken for insight. Only with time does the cost become apparent—confusion without distress, revelation without understanding.

Notably, certain individuals respond differently. Those with aberrant bloodlines or pronounced magical sensitivity report fascination rather than disorientation. They linger. They listen longer. Some begin speaking aloud, repeating phrases they insist are not their own.

**I do not believe the cavern selects truths.**

**I believe it amplifies whatever is already present.**

This may explain the divergent outcomes observed nearby. One creature dwells within the cavern for decades, gaining a reputation as a mystic without ever claiming authority. Others gather around him, mistaking proximity for wisdom. Still others avoid the place entirely, sensing—correctly, I think—that clarity does not survive prolonged contact.

Belcorra’s role here is telling. She did not attempt to seal or sanctify this cavern. She built around it. She stationed observers. She allowed others to approach it first.

This is not how one treats a tool.

It is how one treats a variable.

I caution any reader of these notes: meaning extracted from the whispers is unreliable. Confidence derived from them is worse. Whatever influence permeates this cavern does not lie—but neither does it instruct.

**It listens.**

**And listening, in this place, is not benign.**

*— Morlibint*
