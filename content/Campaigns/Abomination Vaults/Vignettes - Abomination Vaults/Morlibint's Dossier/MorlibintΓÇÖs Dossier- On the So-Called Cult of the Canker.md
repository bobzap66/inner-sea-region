### A Problem of Persistence

The first underground levels of the Abomination Vaults once housed those who served Belcorra Haruvex directly—scribes, caretakers, researchers, and other workers who labored far from the light of Gauntlight Keep above.

What happened to them after Belcorra’s death is not recorded in any single place. It must be reconstructed from absence, ruin, and marginalia that never expected an audience.

When Belcorra fell, panic seems to have followed. Some servants fled. Some hid. Others did not survive long enough to leave any record at all.

What endured was the work.

### The Library Without a Master

Belcorra’s library continued to function after her death.

This alone is remarkable. Libraries do not persist without direction, supply, or purpose. Yet signs suggest that the scholars below Gauntlight did not abandon their posts. They continued researching, copying, cataloging—behaving as though Belcorra’s absence were temporary, or perhaps irrelevant.

I cannot say when this persistence crossed into devotion. I doubt the scholars themselves noticed the moment.

### On Hunger and Adaptation

Records become fragmentary at this point.

Food stores dwindled. Surface access was avoided. Whether this was fear of discovery or confidence in isolation is unclear.

What followed is described obliquely, if at all. The language shifts. Metaphors of sustenance appear where inventories once were. The tone remains methodical.

Something changed the scholars.

They adapted.

### Belief Without Instruction

At some point, these former servants began to speak of restoration—of gathering what was needed to bring Belcorra back. Not through a single spell or rite, but through accumulation. Preservation. Stockpiling.

They refer to their goal using inconsistent terminology. “Flesh” is mentioned, but rarely described. The emphasis is on corruption, saturation, readiness.

I find no evidence that Belcorra taught them this method.

That troubles me.

### The Shape of a Cult

Later commentators call this group the *Cult of the Canker*. I suspect the name came long after the behavior it describes.

What defines a cult is not doctrine, but fixation. By that measure, these scholars qualify.

They appear to have constructed a physical representation of Belcorra—not a likeness, precisely, but a focus. A place for intention to gather.

Whether this accomplished anything is unknown.

### What Can Be Said with Confidence

The individuals most closely associated with this activity are now dead. Whatever the cult was, it no longer operates in any organized sense.

No document I have found proves that Belcorra communicated with them after her death.

No document disproves it either.

### Suppositions

I suspect the Cult of the Canker was not a cause, but a symptom.

A group of intelligent, isolated individuals left inside a system designed to reward obsession, fear, and continuity. Their transformation may have been inevitable once Belcorra was removed from the equation.

They did not resurrect her.

They may have ensured that resurrection remained possible.

### Personal Note

What disturbs me most is not what these people became, but how little imagination it seems to have required.

They did not receive orders. They did not receive visions. They simply kept working.

Belcorra built a place where devotion could outlive its object.

**That may be her most enduring accomplishment.**

*— Morlibint*
