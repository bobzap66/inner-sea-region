# Business as Usual


A thick haze of pipe smoke hung in the air as Wrab Chertel leaned back in his creaking leather chair, rubbing his temples with thick, calloused fingers. Across from him sat Merek Dunwood, his solicitor, a wiry man with keen eyes and a sharp quill poised over a half-filled ledger. The candlelight flickered as Wrab let out a heavy sigh.

“This business is slipping through my fingers, Merek,” Wrab growled, his voice rough from years of barking orders at his lumber crews. “Every month, it’s something else. The foremen are fighting with the workers over wages, the mills aren’t running on schedule, and the financiers down in Absalom are tightening their purses. I need answers.”

Merek tapped the end of his quill against the parchment. “The labor disputes are the most pressing matter,” he said evenly. “Your loggers claim you’ve cut their bonuses and they’re talking about organizing. If that happens—”

“If that happens, I’ll have no men left to cut trees,” Wrab interrupted, scowling. “I need to know who’s behind all this. Inkleholtz has been sniffing around my contracts for months, and Menhemes—” Wrab’s lip curled. “That old fox acts like he’s above it all, but I wouldn’t put it past him to have a hand in this.”

Merek raised a brow. “You believe they’re meddling in your affairs?”

“I don’t believe in coincidence,” Wrab said. He stood up, pacing the room with heavy steps. “First, the financiers start hesitating, then my workers get restless, then suddenly there’s talk that Inkleholtz’s mills are expanding? Sounds like someone’s playing a long game to force me under.”

Merek scratched a note into the ledger. “Sabotage is a strong accusation, Wrab. If you want me to investigate, I’ll need more than suspicions.”

Wrab stopped and turned, his broad shoulders tense. “Find me proof, Merek. I don’t care how you do it. If they’re scheming against me, I’ll not let them carve up my business like a rotten stump.”

Merek studied him for a long moment before nodding. “I’ll make inquiries. Quietly. But in the meantime, you need to address the labor situation before you lose more men.”

Wrab grunted, running a hand through his graying beard. “Fine. I’ll meet with the foremen. But mark my words, if either of those two are behind this, they’ll regret underestimating me.”

Merek closed his ledger with a crisp snap. “Then let’s see what the truth reveals.”
