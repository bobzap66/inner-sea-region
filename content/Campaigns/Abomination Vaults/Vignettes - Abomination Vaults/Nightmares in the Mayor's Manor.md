## Nightmares in the Mayor’s Manor

  

The tantrum came like summer lightning—bright, sudden, and gone before Oseph Menhemes could even draw breath. One moment, his daughter Dorianna sat pale and trembling on her bed, clutching her old cloth rabbit. The next, she was shrieking—throwing her pillow across the room, shouting that everyone should just leave her alone, that she *hated* them all. Then she froze, startled by the sound of her own voice.

  

“I’m sorry, Father,” she whispered, eyes brimming. “I’m just so tired.”

  

She rubbed absently at her forearm, the same place she always touched lately—gently, as if something beneath the skin itched or burned. Her voice dropped to a quaver. “He’s just trying to help me remember,” she murmured. “That’s all.”

  

Oseph felt his throat tighten. The ruby the adventurers had shown him still glimmered in his mind—how it vanished, how it returned, how Dorianna’s gaze followed it with eerie, hungry recognition. He forced a smile and tucked the blanket around her shoulders.

  

“Rest, my dear,” he said softly. “The New Roseguard will make this right. They always do.”

  

### A Father’s Fears

  

Later, in the amber light of the **Dawnflower Library**, Oseph sat hunched across from **Vandy Banderdash**. The Sarenite priest listened quietly, her golden hair catching the lantern glow.

  

“She used to be so bright,” Oseph said, voice raw. “Always laughing, always full of plans. But now she wakes screaming, says there’s a man in her dreams—‘the motley man,’ she calls him. Gives her toys she hasn’t owned since she was a child.” He scrubbed a hand over his face. “I’d think her fevered if it weren’t for that cursed gem.”

  

Vandy folded her hands. “Dark things stir in Otari again. But your daughter’s strength is her heart, Oseph. Keep her close, and trust the Roseguard. They’ve already faced worse than nightmares.”

  

Oseph looked toward the window, where the fading sun bled red over the rooftops. “Aye,” he said quietly. “But nightmares are patient things, and they always know the way home.”

  

The scent of incense hung in the air, sweet and heavy. Somewhere, bells tolled for dusk. And Oseph Menhemes, mayor of Otari, felt smaller than he had in years.
