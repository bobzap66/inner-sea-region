# Talia Swims with the Fishes


Talia had just a heartbeat to register the frog-like aberration’s leap before its bulk struck her chest. The gibtas bounder’s slick weight drove her backward, boots skidding against the ancient stone. Her heel caught on the edge of an open square hatch — a trapdoor long since rotted away — and then there was nothing beneath her.

She fell.

The world narrowed to a rush of stale, cavernous air and the echo of the fight above, shouts muffled by stone. Instinct sharpened her form; she tucked, twisted, and braced for the impact. Her early training turned what might have been a lethal plummet into a controlled drop. Still, fifty-five feet was no small descent, and the surface of the pool below met her with a tremendous splash.

Above, August Shepard lunged to the edge of a neighboring hatch, wand clutched tight, staring down through the shaft. Below, the glow of Talia’s implants — the silver fire that pulsed just beneath her skin — spilled outward, scattering darkness from the water’s surface like a torch lowered into a crypt.

And in that sudden bloom of light, the cavern revealed its secrets.

Two titans lay coiled in the depths, only their heads resting above the water, eyes closed in uneasy slumber. Hydra necks, half a dozen each, sprawled like knotted vines across the rippling surface. Talia’s plunge shattered their dreams.

Sluggishly, the heads began to stir. One lifted, water cascading off its scales. Then another, and another, until a forest of serpentine necks swayed in the glow. Their eyes opened, reflecting torchlight back in a dozen points of hateful green.

For now, the beasts were sluggish — slow, as if mired in the heaviness of sleep. But their hissing breaths and the rumble of low, waking growls promised that their torpor would not last long.

Talia, sputtering, found herself alone between them in the widening rings of disturbed water.

Above, August’s voice caught in his throat. The rest of the party still fought for their lives against the shrieking gibtas bounders on the floor above, their clash of steel and cries echoing faintly through the hatch. But down here, the danger was hers alone.

The hydras were awake.
