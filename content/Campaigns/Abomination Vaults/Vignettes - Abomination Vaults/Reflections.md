# Reflections


The tavern was not a tavern, not really. The tables stretched too far, their wood warped and endless, while the torches burned with flames that made no heat. The air shimmered like a fever dream, though no one there seemed fevered—only waiting.

Creatures of shapes best glimpsed sideways filled the hall: bodies folded wrong, faces split into too many smiles, eyes blinking in places eyes should not be. They spoke in voices like water through stone, clinking mugs as if rehearsing merriment. All of them leaned forward at every creak of the far door, though it never opened.

He sat among them, smaller than most, though no one stared. A mug rested in his hands, and he lifted it again and again. The ale was cool but never wet, heavy but never filling, a draught of nothing. Each swallow slid through him without quenching, like rain falling through a sieve.

Memory drifted at the edge of sight—like scraps of parchment in wind. He knew there had once been taste, once been music, once been laughter that came from the chest rather than the throat. But here those things lay muted, unreachable.

And yet, he felt the rhythm of the place, the heartbeat of waiting. Every soul—or almost-soul—sat with the same hunger: to be chosen. The meaning of that word was as distant as taste, but it was enough to keep him seated, to keep his hands wrapped around the empty mug.

Somewhere far above—or far below—a ripple stirred, as if the dream itself breathed. The torches flared, shadows grew long, and the door quivered in its frame. All around him the crowd leaned forward, eyes bright with anticipation.

He leaned forward, too. Though he could not remember his name, though his tongue was dust and his belly hollow, the longing in his chest was fierce. Fierce enough that it almost felt like life.
