# The Weight of One Soul


*Private entry, written in the margins of the garrison’s duty log after midnight. The fire’s burned low; the rain hasn’t stopped since the arrest.*

  

Vol Rajani helped save this town five centuries ago. His sword struck beside the Roseguard’s own when they brought down Belcorra Haruvex. They were heroes, all of them — flawed, yes, but bound by something purer than most of us ever touch. I grew up on those stories. Never thought I’d live to see his bloodline reduced to *this*. Carman Rajani — thief, murderer, arsonist. The man nearly burned half of Otari just to nurse his wounded pride. Whatever grace once ran through that family’s veins, he’s long since boiled it out.

  

And yet, he’s the key. Urevian can’t break free of Belcorra’s ghost until a true descendant of her enemy is given over — blood for blood, coin for contract. If the devil takes Carman’s soul, he can return to the Abyss instead of turning on Otari in his rage. It’s a cruel equation, but a clean one. Better to feed one villain to another than watch a whole town burn. I know that. I’ve said it a dozen times tonight. It still tastes like ash in my mouth.

  

The new Roseguard came to me with the plan, and I agreed. Necessary, I told myself. Righteous, even. We couldn’t risk Urevian slipping Belcorra’s leash and turning his wrath loose on us. So I gave the order: bring in Rajani, alive if possible. My men took him without a fight. He’s down in the cell now, cursing the walls, still thinking this is a simple arrest. He doesn’t understand he’s already been sentenced — not by a court, but by fate itself.

  

I tell myself it’s justice. Maybe it is. Carman Rajani’s done more harm than most devils. Still, when I hear the chains clink under my floorboards, I remember that he carries Vol Rajani’s blood — the same blood that once stood where the heroes now stand, choosing the hard road for Otari’s sake. The apple fell far, yes, but even rotten fruit grows from a good tree. I wonder what Vol would think of his descendant’s end — or of me, the man who signed the order.

  

Tomorrow, the Roseguard comes to collect him. I’ll hand them the key, and I’ll stand by my choice. Otari will sleep safely again. But I suspect I won’t. Some ghosts don’t wait for death to start their haunting.

  

*— Captain Longsaddle, Keeper of the Watch, sworn to Otari’s peace*
