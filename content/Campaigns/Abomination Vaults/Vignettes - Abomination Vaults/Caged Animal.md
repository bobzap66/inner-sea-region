# Caged Animal


The dark was absolute—so thick it pressed against her eyes like cloth. She blinked, but nothing changed. The blackness had no depth, no shape, no hint of a wall or door, only the ache of her own breath in her ears.

She wasn’t sure how long she had been there. Time in the dark refused to move. Her stomach gave no clues, her body’s rhythms stolen by fear. She only had the sense—half dream, half certainty—that someone else had once been here with her. She remembered the faint scrape of chains, a muffled groan, the ragged rhythm of another’s breath. But those sounds had gone silent, replaced by nothing. Whoever it was had been taken. Whether it was minutes or days ago, she couldn’t tell.

Her heart thudded hard enough to hurt. Every beat reminded her she was still alive, still waiting, still prey. Her weapon was gone. Her strength was caged with her. All she could do was curl knees to chest, listening for the faintest shift of sound.

Sometimes she thought she heard footsteps—slow, dragging, just beyond the wall of black—but then they would fade like echoes. Sometimes she thought she felt breath on her cheek, but when she struck out with trembling hands, she touched only air.

She didn’t know when they would come for her. Only that they would.

Fear filled the silence, dripping down her spine, wrapping her in its own kind of chain. She pressed her teeth together, tried to steady her breath, but the dark swallowed every effort.

She was alone. Alone, and afraid, and waiting for hands she couldn’t see.
