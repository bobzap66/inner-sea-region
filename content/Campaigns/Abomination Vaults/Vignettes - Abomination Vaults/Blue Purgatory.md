# Blue Purgatory


It’s so quiet here at night, except for the lapping of the waves. I used to find that sound soothing—back when I thought there was still time.

Forty years. Forty years, and no one remembers my name. I’m just the ghost now. The one who didn’t make it. Not a hero. Not even a fisherman anyone tells stories about.

Grandmother, I tried. I really did. Every day, I dragged myself to the docks, hauling nets until my hands bled, until my shoulders screamed. Every coin I earned, I counted it twice. I was so close. Just a few more days, and I could’ve bought the gear, strapped on your sword, and left this tiny camp behind. I was going to make you proud. I was going to be like you—bold and brave, a name sung in taverns, written in history books.

But the sea doesn’t care about dreams, does it? One step wrong, and it takes everything. It pulled me under so fast. Cold. Dark. I couldn’t breathe, couldn’t fight. My fingers clawed for the surface, but the water was stronger than I was. Stronger than I’ll ever be.

Your sword is still carefully hidden away. I kept it clean, polished the blade every week, even though I was too afraid to touch it sometimes. I’d whisper to it, you know. Silly things, like promises. "Soon, Grandmother, soon."

I wonder if you know. If you’re looking down on me, shaking your head in disappointment. Did I waste the gift you gave me? Did I dishonor you?

When the fishermen were here, they ignored me. They’re afraid, like I’m some vengeful spirit here to curse them. I don’t want their fear. I don’t want anything from them. I just… I just want to feel like I didn’t let you down.

Will I ever be able to leave this place? Or is this my penance? To watch the sea, night after night, whispering to the sword I’ll never carry, waiting for an answer that will never come?
