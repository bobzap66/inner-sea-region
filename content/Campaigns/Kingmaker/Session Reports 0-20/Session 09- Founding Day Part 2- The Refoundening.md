### From Linzi’s Journal

*An account of events from 17 Gozran until approximately 27 Gozran, 4710 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

Harrim and Jaethal departed after helping the Tubthumpers survive the Scythe Tree. I am told there were no tearful farewells. Harrim accepted their survival with the mild disappointment of a man whose expectations had once again been contradicted, while Jaethal appeared satisfied that she had demonstrated the proper method for killing an ambulatory tree. The four charter-bearers continued without them.

On the seventeenth of Gozran, they returned to Melianse’s pool carrying the tree feather tokens given to them by Tiressia. The nixie accepted the tokens as suitable restitution for the three ancient trees Corax’s loggers had felled. She released the two enchanted men, allowing them to rejoin their companions, and Corax’s company departed for the new logging ground the Tubthumpers had recommended.

Melianse also fulfilled her promise to teach the company the Inveigle ritual. This is a form of magic used to place others beneath a lasting enchantment. I recognize that such knowledge may prove useful to future rulers, diplomats, and defenders of the realm. I also believe that anyone learning a ritual for bending another person’s will should be watched very carefully until the novelty has worn off.

With Melianse’s trees soon to be restored, the company resumed its interrupted journey toward the tatzlwyrm den. Oleg had asked them to bring back at least one head so that he could have it mounted and displayed in the trading post. After everything they had recently endured, the ensuing battle was almost disappointingly simple. They found the creatures, fought them, killed them, and collected both heads. No one was swallowed, transformed, cursed, dragged into another world, or brought within a breath of death. I am assured that everyone found the experience refreshing.

From there they returned to the Stag Lord’s fortress to retrieve its former master. Several days in the Gozran air had not improved the corpse. The legendary Stag Lord, who had once inspired terror throughout the Greenbelt, was reduced to an aged and increasingly unpleasant burden that had to be wrapped, lifted, transported, and occasionally repositioned when the road became rough.

It is difficult to preserve the dignity of a fallen tyrant while fastening him to a pack animal.

Their road toward Nettles’ Crossing took them near the location marked on the treasure map they had recovered. The map promised an ancient tomb and a magical weapon, which was sufficient to persuade the company that carrying a decomposing bandit lord across the wilderness did not make their journey complicated enough.

The entrance led into an underground barrow older than any settlement now standing in the region. Its cramped passages opened into chambers decorated with worn mosaics, ancient crypts, and great stone faces representing malevolent winds. The air carried the dust of centuries, and everything about the place suggested that its builders had taken considerable measures to discourage visitors.

Those measures remained functional.

As the company entered the central chamber, the four carved faces awakened. They inhaled as if the stone itself had drawn breath, then exhaled tendrils of supernatural darkness. The magic pulled strength from the living and filled the chamber with terror and despair. The Tubthumpers found themselves fighting an enemy built into the walls while attempting to reach the passages beyond it.

The crypts held four skeletal champions sworn to defend the barrow. The first pair announced the intrusion by hammering their swords against their shields. The answering clang from the opposite chamber revealed that two more guardians had heard the summons. All four converged upon the central chamber while the stone faces continued breathing darkness across the battlefield.

Ant’s arrows rattled against ancient armor while Ally’s magic flashed between the pillars. Mairi met the skeletal warriors at close quarters, breaking through shields and brittle bone. Seamus fought wherever the line was closest to collapsing, which is another way of saying that he placed himself in the most dangerous position available and remained there until something forced him to move.

The champions were ancient, but neither age nor death had made them clumsy. They fought in practiced formation, surrounding the living and driving them back beneath the leering stone faces. Only after a bitter struggle did the last skeletal guardian collapse, leaving the Tubthumpers wounded but free to enter the deepest chamber.

There they found the Lonely Warrior.

He had once been a barbarian king whose name had vanished from history. Death had not relieved him of his anger. He waited in the heart of the barrow beside the remnants of his grave goods, guarding a broken blade that had apparently inspired betrayal, murder, and several thousand years of resentment.

The Lonely Warrior rose singing a funeral dirge that pressed against the mind like the weight of the tomb itself. His blows seemed to draw vitality from those they struck, restoring a portion of his own unnatural strength. Even damaged, the weapon in his hand was dangerous, and the ancient warrior wielded it with the certainty of someone who had spent centuries imagining the next intruder’s death.

The fight was close, brutal, and conducted by people already battered by the barrow’s other guardians. The Lonely Warrior refused to yield and struck once more even as the company finally destroyed him. When he fell, the ancient silence returned, broken only by exhausted breathing and the settling of dust.

The weapon he guarded was a broken Aldori dueling sword forged from cold iron. Though its blade had been shattered, traces of powerful enchantment remained within it. Properly repaired, it would carry both a magical edge and the power of flame. Seamus claimed the sword. I admit there is something appropriate about him emerging from a forgotten tomb with a broken Aldori blade in his hands. Heroes and swords both acquire much of their character from how often they have been damaged and put back together.

With the barrow emptied of immediate threats, the company resumed its journey to the Shrike River. At Nettles’ Crossing they carried the Stag Lord’s remains to the water and threw them in.

Davik Nettles rose from the river to receive his murderer.

The dead bridgekeeper seized the Stag Lord’s corpse and dragged it beneath the surface. Whatever vengeance or justice passed between the two dead men occurred below the dark water. Davik did not rise again. Instead, his enchanted ranseur washed gently onto the bank at the Tubthumpers’ feet—the final payment of a debt that had outlived both men who owed it.

The Tubthumpers then turned north. They had been away from Oleg’s for too long, and the work awaiting them was no longer the work of hunters or tomb robbers. The Stag Lord was dead, his followers scattered, and the charter under which they had explored the Greenbelt was nearing fulfillment. Restov expected a report. The people already living in these lands expected protection. Roads, farms, laws, and borders would soon have to replace trails, camps, promises, and guesses.

They returned to Oleg’s a few days before the end of Gozran. Oleg inspected the two tatzlwyrm heads with considerable satisfaction, turning each one this way and that as he decided which would look best mounted in the trading post. Svetlana asked only whether he intended to have it thoroughly cleaned before hanging it anywhere near the tables. He assured her that he did, although not quickly enough to inspire confidence.

I was interested in Seamus.

He looked exhausted, bruised, and entirely too pleased with the broken sword he carried. He placed it down with the care one might give a wounded companion and began explaining what it could become once reforged. I listened, although I suspect I would have listened just as intently if he had been describing a moderately interesting fence post.

When they left Oleg’s, they had been chartered adventurers hunting a bandit. When they returned, there was no bandit lord, no rival power in the northern Greenbelt, and no remaining excuse to postpone the question waiting for all of us.

What would they build in the space he had left behind?

*—Linzi*
