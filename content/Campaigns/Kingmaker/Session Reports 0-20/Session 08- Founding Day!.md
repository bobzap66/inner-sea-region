### From [Linzi](../NPCs/Linzi.md)’s Journal

*An account of events from 11–15 Gozran, 4710 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

Killing the [Stag Lord](../NPCs/Stag%20Lord.md) did not make his fortress any less unpleasant. Once the fighting ended, the Tubthumpers found themselves surrounded by dead bandits, wounded allies, stolen goods, and several difficult decisions that could no longer be solved by hitting the most objectionable person in the room.

The first concerned Akiros Ismort. He had turned against the Stag Lord when doing so mattered most, but he had also served the man for years. The company chose to allow Akiros to take [Auchs](../NPCs/Auchs.md) and depart in peace. Whether that was justice, mercy, or merely exhaustion remains open to debate. Akiros accepted without argument. He had been offered a narrow road away from the man he had become, and for the moment, at least, he took it.

The fortress still held one final danger. Beneath it, among the stolen supplies and damp stone chambers, the party discovered a decrepit old spellcaster. Pyjat and [Tristian](../NPCs/Tristian.md) remained with the company during the confrontation, although neither stayed with the expedition afterward. The old man offered no explanation for himself and the party learned neither his name nor his connection to the Stag Lord. Whatever tragedy had brought him to that cellar died with him. To the Tubthumpers, he was simply one final hostile magician guarding a dead tyrant’s plunder.

Once he had been defeated, they searched the fortress and gathered what could be carried. They left the Stag Lord’s body behind temporarily. A promise had been made to the shade of Davik Nettles, and they intended to collect the corpse when their road next took them through the crossing. First, however, they meant to deal with the tatzlwyrms whose territory lay elsewhere in the [Greenbelt](../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Greenbelt/Greenbelt.md).

They did not reach the tatzlwyrms.

On the road they encountered [Jubilost Narthropple](../NPCs/Jubilost%20Narthropple.md) and his company of gnome surveyors. One of their wagons had become trapped in a river, with its ponies panicking and the current threatening to carry away wagon, animals, supplies, and a considerable portion of Jubilost’s dignity. The Tubthumpers helped rescue the wagon. Jubilost responded with the peculiar sort of gratitude that involves explaining how the rescuers could have performed the rescue more efficiently.

He was, however, an accomplished explorer and mapmaker. The company arranged to purchase copies of several of his maps, which revealed portions of the region they had not yet surveyed. A kingdom had not yet been founded, but already its future rulers were discovering that good maps could conquer nearly as much wilderness as swords—and usually with fewer arrows lodged in [Seamus](../Characters/Seamus.md).

On the thirteenth of Gozran, the company came upon another dispute. Corax and his loggers had begun felling trees beside a secluded pool belonging to a nixie named [Melianse](../NPCs/Melianse.md). Melianse had responded by enchanting two of the men. Corax wanted his workers returned. Melianse wanted the logging stopped, the intruders removed, and the three ancient trees already cut down replaced.

The Tubthumpers managed to prevent the disagreement from becoming a battle. They directed Corax toward another source of lumber and persuaded him to leave Melianse’s glade. Melianse agreed to release the enchanted loggers, but only after receiving a promise that the three lost trees would be replaced. She suggested using tree feather tokens and directed the company toward a dryad named [Tiressia](../NPCs/Tiressia.md), whose grove lay a day’s journey west.

Tiressia was weeping when they found her. A monstrous plant known as a Scythe Tree had established itself to the south and was moving gradually toward her grove. It was not merely an unusually hostile tree. It was an immense, predatory thing whose great limbs swept everything within fifteen feet and struck with enough force to smash bone through armor. Tiressia promised the needed feather tokens if the Tubthumpers destroyed it before it reached her bonded tree.

Naturally, they agreed.

I have collected four different accounts of the battle, and all four disagree about who was struck first. They agree about the sound. Each blow landed like a tree falling in a storm. Its branches swept across the clearing in great murderous arcs, catching anyone who came close while its roots tore through the earth beneath them. There was no safe place from which to fight it. Those who approached were battered by its limbs; those who withdrew watched it stride after them.

The company had no true healer among them. Every wound remained a wound, every broken rib slowed the next dodge, and every person who fell made the [Survivors](../../Abomination%20Vaults/Vignettes%20-%20Abomination%20Vaults/Survivors.md) easier to surround. [Mairi](../Characters/Mairi.md) met the creature’s blows with the wolf spirit’s fury, but even her strength could not stop its branches from throwing her aside. [Ant](../Characters/Ant.md) fired into gaps in the bark whenever he could find a clear shot. Ally’s magic scorched and split the creature’s trunk, yet it continued to advance. Seamus repeatedly placed himself within reach of the thing’s limbs, because Seamus possesses many admirable qualities and almost none of them involve remaining a sensible distance from danger.

One by one, they were driven down. Armor split. Weapons slipped from blood-slick hands. The tree’s enormous shadow passed over them as its branches drew back for another sweep. By every account I have gathered, the Tubthumpers were moments away from being killed.

Then [Harrim](../NPCs/Harrim.md) and [Jaethal](../NPCs/Jaethal.md) arrived.

No one has supplied a satisfactory explanation for their timing. Harrim entered the clearing as though he had merely followed the natural progression of despair to its inevitable destination. Jaethal followed with considerably more enthusiasm. Harrim’s magic kept the wounded alive long enough to rise, while Jaethal attacked the Scythe Tree with the focused displeasure of someone personally offended that a plant had nearly finished the battle without her.

“Everything dies,” Harrim reportedly observed amid the carnage. “Today it appears to be the tree’s turn.”

Jaethal’s own assessment was less philosophical. “You were doing admirably,” she told the exhausted survivors afterward, “if your intention was to fertilize the clearing.”

With Harrim holding death at bay and Jaethal adding her strength to the attack, the Tubthumpers rallied. Ant’s arrows found the damaged places in its trunk. Ally drove magic into the widening cracks. Mairi and Seamus returned to its reach despite having already learned precisely what its branches could do. At last the Scythe Tree shuddered, split, and came crashing down hard enough to shake the glade.

The fifteenth of Gozran ended with the victors scattered around the fallen tree in bloody, exhausted heaps. The tatzlwyrms remained entirely untroubled, Melianse’s trees remained unreplaced, and no kingdom had yet been founded. Nevertheless, an alliance with Tiressia had been won, three feather tokens were within reach, and four future rulers had survived a battle that should have killed them.

I keep finding myself writing that last part about these people.

Seamus returned battered again, of course. I am beginning to suspect that concern for his safety is an occupation from which there are neither holidays nor retirement. Still, when he described that monstrous tree falling and the clearing finally becoming quiet, I felt the same certainty that had troubled me since Lady Jamandi’s feast. These were not merely adventurers stumbling from one disaster into another. They were becoming the sort of people around whom roads, stories, and perhaps even nations would someday gather.

*—Linzi*
