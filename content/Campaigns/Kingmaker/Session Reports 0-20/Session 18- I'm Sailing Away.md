### From [[Linzi]]’s Journal

*An account of events from 19 Rova until 18 Lamashan, 4710 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

The warning delivered by [[Hargulka]]’s leucrottas demanded an answer, but first the Tubthumpers attended to a promise already made. On 19 Rova, they set out in search of the black rattlecap mushrooms requested by [[Elga Verniex]]. These rare fungi grow in a place called the Mud Bowl, which is precisely as pleasant as its name suggests.

They reached the bowl the following day. It was a great depression filled with bubbling, foul-smelling mud, surrounded by mushrooms tall enough to shelter beneath. The fumes turned the stomach, the ground clung greedily to every boot, and one particularly large mound of vegetation proved not to be vegetation at all.

The creature was later identified as a tendriculos: an enormous, ravenous plant that lashed out with roots and vines from among the fungi. The company fought it amid the mud and mushrooms, where every hurried step threatened to become a very slow and embarrassing one. They eventually hacked the monster apart and salvaged fourteen black rattlecaps from the wreckage.

That night brought no proper rest. A forest wyvern descended upon their camp, apparently having mistaken bedrolls, cookpots, and armed adventurers for an uncomplicated supper. The Tubthumpers fought it off, though I am told that sleeping beneath an open sky became rather less appealing afterward.

By 22 Rova, the company had finished constructing two rafts to carry them across the water. On their return journey they stopped at Elga’s hut, delivered her fourteen mushrooms, and asked her to preserve the head of Howls-of-the-North-Wind. Elga agreed and, more importantly, provided them with a lead concerning the location of Hargulka’s stronghold.

They returned to [[Thumpington]] on 26 Rova and resumed the less glamorous work of government. Trade brought much-needed supplies into the realm, new territory was claimed, and work began upon another lumber camp, a bridge, and an inn. Reports of trolls continued to trouble the countryside, but the republic’s people were steadied and its stores replenished.

[[Grigori]] was also brought to trial. The court found him guilty, proving that Thumpington’s laws could answer a poisonous tongue without requiring anyone to cut it out. I consider this an important distinction, even if several citizens expressed disappointment.

More troubling was a report produced through the council’s investigations: the king of the nearby Iruxi village appeared to be under the influence of an ancestral spirit that encouraged increasingly aggressive behavior. Whether that spirit was truly an ancestor remained uncertain, but the matter offered another reason to visit the village while travelling south.

On 9 Lamashan, watchmen serving [[Matteo]] Auditore, Minister of Justice, captured two kobolds spying within the capital. They carried a wand capable of producing a sheet of flame and an enchanted silver weapon, which did little to support their first claim that they were merely lost tourists.

The smaller of the pair was [[Kereek]], a red-scaled kobold dressed in a patchwork orange robe. He possessed a quick tongue, a quicker mind, and sufficient confidence for a creature three times his size. Kereek insisted that they had not been captured at all, but had merely “concluded the information-gathering portion of their mission and opened negotiations.”

His companion, [[Prazil]], was a much larger kobold with green scales, tremendous strength, and an expression of such guileless enthusiasm that maintaining a proper interrogation became difficult. Kereek issued the explanations, corrections, and occasional insults. Prazil nodded along, praised Kereek’s cleverness, and eventually asked whether prisoners were provided lunch.

Their story, once separated from Kereek’s improvements, was a grim one. They belonged to a kobold band called the Treeshouters. Hargulka’s forces had destroyed their home, killing many and enslaving the [[Survivors]]. Kereek and Prazil had been ordered to spy upon Thumpington under threat of death—not merely their own deaths, but those of the kin still held by the trolls.

Prazil’s loyalty to Kereek was immediately apparent. Kereek’s loyalty to Prazil was disguised beneath complaints, commands, and assurances that the larger kobold could not be trusted to remember which end of a map pointed north. He fooled no one except, perhaps, himself.

Once convinced that the Tubthumpers genuinely intended to confront Hargulka, the kobolds agreed to guide them to the troll king’s stronghold. Kereek presented this as a carefully negotiated alliance between equal powers. Prazil declared that they were going to smash trolls and appeared delighted by the prospect.

The company constructed sturdier boats and set sail down the Sellen River, following the current south toward Hargulka’s domain. Kereek and Prazil travelled with them—no longer prisoners, though not yet entirely trusted.

On 18 Lamashan, their boats approached the Iruxi village. Somewhere ahead waited the supposed ancestral spirit, and beyond it the troll king who believed that fear could turn every neighboring people into servants.

Hargulka had sent Kereek and Prazil north as slaves and spies. They returned south as guides to the people coming to destroy him. There is a lesson in that, though I suspect the Troll King will understand it too late.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
