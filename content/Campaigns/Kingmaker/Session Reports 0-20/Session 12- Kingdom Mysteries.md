### From [[Linzi]]’s Journal

*An account of events from 14 Erastus until 15 Erastus, 4710 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

On the fourteenth day of Erastus, the Tubthumpers stood upon the shore of the [[Locations/Inner Sea Region/River Kingdoms/Stolen Lands/Greenbelt/Kamelands/Tuskwater|Tuskwater]] with two newly constructed rafts, a lake of questionable temperament, and an island ahead of them from which sensible travelers were reputed never to return. [[Nok-Nok]] had wandered away before the crossing, presumably in pursuit of some private opportunity for heroism, treasure, or lunch.

Before the party launched, [[Tristian]] approached their camp and offered his assistance. Considering their destination, an additional healer—and one blessed with considerable divine power—was welcomed without much debate. Thus reinforced, they loaded themselves, their equipment, and whatever courage remained after Elga’s warnings onto Ally’s rafts.

The crossing took approximately three and a half hours. [[Seamus]] made what one witness described as a “sad attempt” to help guide the vessels. I have been assured that this description refers only to his skill as a boatman and not to his many other excellent qualities. Whatever difficulties arose, both rafts eventually reached Candlemere Island without sinking, separating, or drifting all the way into the [[River Kingdoms]].

The island’s southern shore was scarcely a shore at all. Dense reeds and green scum blurred the boundary between lake and land, while the remains of an unfinished pier slowly rotted among the vegetation. A thin mist clung to the island regardless of the weather, and the ruined tower at its summit loomed above them through the haze.

Something was waiting beside the pier.

The creature was a shambler: an enormous moving mass of vines, roots, and decaying vegetation with sufficient strength to make the unidentified plant from the previous day seem almost reasonable. It attacked as the party came ashore, catching them while they were still clustered near the rafts and trying to unload their supplies.

The fight was brutal. The shambler seized [[Mairi]] and battered her so badly that she came perilously close to [[Rules/Dying|Dying]]. The others surrounded the creature, cutting and blasting at its tangled body while Tristian struggled to keep everyone alive. They finally brought it down beside the water, leaving an enormous heap of severed vegetation sprawled near their rafts.

Among the ruined cargo scattered near the old pier, they recovered gold and a potion of water breathing. The expedition also acquired an unusual magical kukri named Thirst. The weapon proved aptly named: its enchantment encouraged bloodletting and became particularly vicious after being used to kill a human. It also objected rather strongly to being wielded by anyone of an orderly disposition. I have entered it into the kingdom’s records under “Treasures That Should Be Watched Carefully.”

Having nearly lost Mairi within moments of reaching the island, the party made the astonishingly prudent decision to rest. They camped through the night and continued recovering the following morning before beginning the climb toward the tower.

The path wound uphill through stinging nettles, thorny bushes, and a mist that made the ground difficult to see. At the summit stood the remains of a tower far older than the other ruins scattered across the [[Stolen Lands]]. Only a jagged stone shell remained, choked with weeds and fallen masonry. A dark opening in the floor revealed stairs descending beneath the ruin.

Faint symbols had been carved into the ancient stones. Those able to interpret fragments of the writing recognized blasphemous prayers dedicated to the strange gods of the Old Cults. The entire ruin carried an oppressive sensation of being watched, as though something distant had noticed their arrival and was now waiting to see what they would do.

Before they could descend, an invisible creature attacked.

It revealed itself only in fleeting movements and flashes of unnatural light. The party eventually recognized it as a will-o’-wisp, one of the malicious lights long associated with Candlemere. Worse, the creature was almost entirely immune to magic. This was particularly inconvenient for a company containing Ally and Tristian, both of whom generally prefer their enemies to respect the ordinary rules of spellcasting.

The wisp vanished repeatedly, striking from concealment and feeding upon the fear it created. Most spells passed through it without effect. Somehow—and accounts become less precise at this point—Ally and Seamus managed to destroy it through persistence, desperate improvisation, and what may have been an unreasonable quantity of luck. I am inclined to give them more credit than that. Luck may present an opportunity, but someone must still possess the courage to seize it.

The battle also taught the party an important lesson concerning expedition planning: supplies left behind with a pony are of limited use when one is being attacked by an invisible, magic-proof horror at the top of a haunted island.

With the wisp destroyed, the ruined tower stood open before them. Beneath their feet, stone stairs descended into darkness, toward chambers that had remained sealed beneath Candlemere for thousands of years.

They had reached the threshold. What waited beyond it would be discovered next.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
