### From [[Linzi]]’s Journal

*An account concerning events from approximately 22 through 24 Pharast, 4710 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

After [[Seamus]] caught up with the others beneath [[Locations/Inner Sea Region/River Kingdoms/Stolen Lands/Greenbelt/Kamelands/The Old Sycamore|The Old Sycamore]], I returned to Oleg’s Trading Post. I had accompanied them long enough to confirm two things: first, that they were every bit as brave as I had imagined; and second, that bravery often involves crawling through cramped holes filled with hostile creatures and very little fresh air. My talents seemed better employed preserving their exploits than becoming another exploit that required preservation. I only rarely accompanied them into danger after that day, and the events that follow were related to me upon their return.

Seamus, [[Mairi]], [[Ant]], Ally, and their newly proclaimed hero [[Nok-Nok]] descended once more beneath the Old Sycamore. Mikmek’s rescue had revealed the reason for the war between the mites and the [[Groups/Sootscale Kobolds|Sootscale Kobolds]]: the mites had stolen a sacred statue belonging to Mikmek’s tribe. Recovering it required the party to continue through the remaining warrens, including a deep chasm tangled with the ancient tree’s roots.

The creature waiting there was an enormous whiptail centipede, long enough to coil across much of the chasm floor. The party descended to fight it among the roots and stones below. During the battle, Ally was badly injured, and Mairi attempted to reach her with healing. As Mairi moved to aid her companion, the centipede intercepted her with a vicious reactive strike. The blow landed on Mairi and nearly killed her before she could complete the rescue. I am told the difference between courage and foolishness is whether the person one risks everything to save survives. Ally survived, as did Mairi, so I shall record it as courage.

The party finally killed the centipede, finished clearing the mites from beneath the tree, and recovered the stolen statue. With Mikmek as their guide, they carried it to the Sootscale Caverns in the hope that its return might end the little war that had already killed so many mites and kobolds. There they discovered that the Sootscales had fallen under the influence of a strange purple kobold named [[Tartuk]], who had used fear, magic, and the missing statue to tighten his control over the tribe.

The party returned the sacred relic to [[Chief Sootscale]], giving him the confidence and support he needed to challenge Tartuk’s control. The chief rallied those kobolds who remained loyal to him and led them into battle alongside Seamus, Mairi, Ant, Ally, and Nok-Nok. Tartuk relied upon illusions and vanished from sight during the fighting, leaving his attackers searching the cavern for an enemy who could strike without being seen. It was a difficult and infuriating hunt, but together they finally located him and brought him down. Death stripped away more than Tartuk’s invisibility. His body reverted to its true form, revealing [[Tartuccio]]—the same gnome who had attended Lady Jamandi’s feast and survived the Black Tears’ attack upon her manor.

I cannot claim to have trusted Tartuccio, but discovering that he had disguised himself as a kobold and attempted to build an army beneath the [[Stolen Lands]] exceeded even my expectations of him. There is ambition, there is treachery, and then there is persuading an entire kobold tribe that one is a purple prophet. I would almost have admired the audacity if so many people had not suffered for it.

The victory carried a price for Mairi beyond the wounds she had suffered beneath the Old Sycamore. When she rages, the Wolf spirit within her partially transforms her face, giving her a muzzle and the natural weapons with which the spirit expects her to fight. During the battle with Tartuk, Mairi instead took up manufactured weapons while still in the grip of that rage. In doing so, she violated the spirit’s trust. Its presence left her, taking her rage and the transformation with it.

“The rage was simply gone,” she told me afterward. “I didn’t expect it to feel so lonely.” Mairi is not a woman easily shaken, which made the quiet way she said this considerably more troubling than tears would have been. A wound can be bound and a broken weapon repaired. I did not yet know what might be required to mend a bond with an offended spirit.

With Tartuk dead and the sacred statue restored, the party brokered peace with the Sootscales. The conflict beneath the Old Sycamore was ended, Mikmek was safely returned to his people, and Nok-Nok had acquired enough evidence of his heroism to sustain several years of increasingly elaborate retellings.

On their journey back to Oleg’s, the party encountered two more travelers in need. [[Regongar]] and [[Octavia]] claimed to have escaped enslavement by the Technic League. They accompanied the party back to the trading post, although they remained guarded and had little interaction with their rescuers afterward. By the time I met them, they seemed more interested in putting distance between themselves and their former captors than in recounting the circumstances of their escape. I could hardly blame them.

The journey also carried the party through Nettle’s Crossing, where they encountered a ghost who demanded that they bring him the [[Stag Lord]]’s head or face death themselves. Mairi agreed to the demand and then departed with impressive speed before the apparition could reconsider the terms. After facing an enormous centipede, an invisible gnome, and the loss of her guiding spirit, I believe she had reached the limit of supernatural complications she was prepared to tolerate that day.

They returned to Oleg’s with new allies, a new obligation, and proof that the Stag Lord’s influence had spread far beyond a single bandit camp. Seamus arrived muddy, exhausted, and smiling as though all of this had been a particularly successful outing. I was relieved to see him. I was also beginning to suspect that if these people truly became the heroes of a new realm, keeping an accurate account of them was going to be considerably more difficult than I had anticipated.
