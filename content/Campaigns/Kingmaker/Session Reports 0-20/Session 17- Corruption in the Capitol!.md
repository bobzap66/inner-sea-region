### From [Linzi](../NPCs/Linzi.md)’s Journal

*An account of events from 10 Rova until approximately 18 Rova, 4710 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

[Grigori](../NPCs/Grigori.md) had made himself the loudest man in [Thumpington](../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Thumping%20Waters/Settlements/Thumpington/Thumpington.md), which is not the same thing as being the wisest, though he appeared determined to test the distinction. Wherever a crowd gathered, he was there to explain that every hardship was the council’s fault, every success a deception, and every delay evidence of corruption. I will admit that he was talented. It takes a certain sort of genius to make people furious without ever giving them anything useful to be furious about.

The Tubthumpers first attempted to answer him directly. This was a mistake. Grigori did not argue to discover the truth; he argued to perform before an audience. Every interruption became oppression, every correction became an admission, and every reasonable explanation became another opportunity for him to pull a sentence apart and rearrange its pieces into something uglier.

So the council changed its approach. For seven long days, they answered rumor with fact, accusation with evidence, and anger with patience. They spoke to people in the streets, listened to honest grievances, corrected the most poisonous lies, and reminded everyone that Thumpington belonged to all of us—not merely to whichever man had found the tallest barrel to stand upon.

By degrees, the city cooled. Grigori’s crowds diminished, his speeches lost their power, and the fury he had cultivated began to look rather foolish in daylight. On 18 Rova, when the authorities finally moved to arrest him, he attempted to slip away beneath a change of clothing and a hastily adopted disguise. He was clever, but not quite clever enough. The Tubthumpers recognized him and took him into custody.

What precisely should be done with him remained a harder question. Being an intolerable nuisance is not, by itself, a crime—and if Thumpington ever passes a law making it one, half the council may need to flee the country. If this realm is to be governed by law, then even a man such as Grigori must be charged for what can be proven, not merely punished for making everyone wish to throw him into a horse trough.

With the immediate danger contained, the Tubthumpers turned south. Reports of trolls and other monstrous folk gathering in the [Narlmarches](../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Greenbelt/Narlmarches.md) had become too frequent to dismiss, and the company set out to learn what was stirring beneath the trees.

They found no trolls at first. Instead, they encountered the vicious warg called Howls-of-the-North-Wind and the pack of wolves that hunted beside him. The warg was an enormous and cunning brute, happiest when its pack could surround isolated prey. The fight was fierce, but the Tubthumpers killed Howls and broke the pack. When the remaining wolves fled, the company allowed them to go. There is a difference between a malicious monster and an animal that has decided it would very much prefer to be somewhere else.

Howls’s death should also earn the company a reward from the Lumber Consortium, which proves that occasionally virtue and bookkeeping can be made to travel in the same direction.

Farther south, the Tubthumpers discovered an old hunting lodge under repair. The work was being overseen by Jemal Visser of the Embreth Travellers, a hunters’ fellowship hoping to establish a permanent lodge in the region. Jemal proved fond of grand hunts and even grander stories, and the evening passed pleasantly in the exchange of both. Before the company departed, he promised that they would receive an invitation when the Travellers held their first great hunt.

The forest was less hospitable beyond the lodge. Cries of human distress drew the Tubthumpers toward what appeared to be someone in desperate need of aid. The voices belonged instead to leucrottas—vile, cunning beasts able to imitate the speech of their victims. They sprang their ambush expecting easy prey and found the Tubthumpers waiting at the other end of their jaws.

Once the ambush was broken, a surviving creature offered a warning. A troll king named [Hargulka](../NPCs/Hargulka.md) was gathering monsters in the southern Narlmarches and meant to extend his rule northward. Thumpington, it claimed, would eventually be “assimilated,” and its people would be wise to prepare offerings and obedience.

It is a curious thing. Grigori came armed with words, while Hargulka’s servants came with teeth, yet both carried the same message: be afraid, surrender your judgment, and obey the loudest threat before you.

They will learn that Thumpington is not so easily cowed.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
