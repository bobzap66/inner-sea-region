### From [Linzi](../NPCs/Linzi.md)’s Journal

*An account of events from 22 Lamashan until approximately 14 Neth, 4710 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

The fall of [Hargulka](../NPCs/Hargulka.md) did not immediately send the Tubthumpers home. They spent the remainder of 22 Lamashan tending their wounds and searching the old dwarven fortress. The trolls had gathered the spoils of many raids: coins, jewelry, gemstones, wine, adventuring and repair equipment, a clear aeon stone, a bracelet enchanted for unusual speed, and a wand capable of calling down thunder.

More troubling than the treasure were the trolls’ maps. Their markings showed farms and settlements they had already raided, as well as the reach of the campaign Hargulka had intended to wage against us. One location in the Sellen Hills was marked as a place even the trolls feared. I find that reassuring only if I make a determined effort not to think about it.

On Lamashan 24, the company encountered an enormous deadfall surrounded by caves and the tracks of some great reptilian beast. The creature proved to be a hodag, a horned and ill-tempered monster with entirely too many teeth. The Tubthumpers killed it and took its head as proof, then continued north.

Their journey also brought them to a small woodland cave and into battle with a flame drake. It was not a true dragon, though I doubt that distinction seemed especially important while it was trying to burn them alive. The drake was slain, and the company resumed the road to [Thumpington](../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Thumping%20Waters/Settlements/Thumpington/Thumpington.md) believing the worst of the expedition was behind them.

On Neth 2, they saw the smoke long before they reached the city.

While the kingdom’s leaders were away, an enormous owlbear had torn through Thumpington. Thirty people were dead. The bridge, the herb shop, and the shrine had been destroyed. Euan survived with a badly broken leg, but Gershwin the blacksmith was among those killed. I remember that Gershwin once purchased one of the strange old silver coins the Tubthumpers brought home. It is a small memory, but grief has a way of gathering around small things.

The people had every right to be frightened and angry. Some looked upon the returning ministers not as heroes, but as leaders who had been elsewhere when their city needed them. [Mairi](../Characters/Mairi.md) took the devastation particularly hard. Whatever else might be said of our Prime Minister, no one who saw her face that day could doubt that she regarded every death as a personal wound.

A kingdom turn began on Neth 3 beneath the shadow of the attack. The council worked to calm the city, repair what could be repaired, and reduce the unrest left in the Beast’s wake. Government is sometimes described as speeches, decrees, and seals pressed into wax. More often, it is finding timber for a broken bridge while helping thirty families bury their dead.

On Neth 9, representatives from Tok-Nik-Rat arrived to judge for themselves what we were building in the [Stolen Lands](../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Stolen%20Lands.md). The meeting proved successful, and an envoy was sent to the bog strider village. Even amid mourning, the borders of our little realm continued to widen through words rather than weapons.

The next morning, the Tubthumpers set out to hunt the creature the townsfolk had named the Beast of Thumpington. They reached its lair on the evening of Neth 12. The cavern was a foul garden of enormous mushrooms, crawling spiders, and a shambling mound, all gathered around the owlbear’s den.

The Beast fought with the strength that had shattered buildings and killed scores of our people, but the Tubthumpers brought it down. In the depths of the lair they found an owlbear cub, alive and suddenly alone. Mairi could not bring herself to kill it for the crimes of its parent. She took the cub into her care, because apparently the proper answer to one enormous owlbear is to begin raising the next one ourselves.

By Neth 14, [Seamus](../Characters/Seamus.md) and Kundal had both been freed from the curse of lycanthropy. After trolls, monsters, fire, grief, and curses, Thumpington still stood. Bruised, mourning, and furious—but standing.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
