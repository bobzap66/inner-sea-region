### From [[Linzi]]’s Journal

*An account of events from 15 Erastus until approximately 17 Erastus, 4710 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

There are places where history feels distant: a faded inscription, a broken statue, an old song whose final verse everyone has forgotten. Beneath Candlemere Tower, history apparently waits in the dark until someone comes close enough to strangle.

On the fifteenth of Erastus, the Tubthumpers descended the stairs beneath the ruined tower. Just inside, they discovered a shallow basin buried beneath centuries of leaves and debris. An inscription above it, written in Aklo, proclaimed, “We are those who stand at the threshold.” Beneath the rubbish rested six enormous silver coins minted during the Age of Destiny. Their value as silver was modest. Their value to the right collector was considerably less modest, which is the sort of distinction Jubilost would insist upon making several times.

Farther below, the stone ceiling bore circular scorch marks left by magic unleashed thousands of years ago. Whatever battle had been fought beneath Candlemere, it had involved power far beyond anything the ruins ought to have retained—and yet the oppressive feeling of being watched only grew stronger as the company descended.

The dead had not entirely departed. Three ancient shadows rose against the explorers, remnants of those who had perished in the struggle between the Thresholders and their enemies. The creatures reached for warmth and life with hands made from darkness, but the Tubthumpers destroyed them and continued onward.

Among the crumbling remains of an armory, the company recovered several objects that had survived the passing millennia: a wand later identified as holding the spell *grim tendrils*, a garnet-covered bronze canister containing *dust of appearance*, and a metal rod that burned without ever being consumed. Antson took custody of the burning rod, presumably because a halfling wearing the [[Stag Lord]]’s antlered helm was not yet conspicuous enough.

The next chamber contained a towering statue assembled from great stone spheres, several of them hollow and burning from within. Aklo script around its base named Yog-Sothoth, described both as one who knew the gate and as the gate itself. Past, present, and future, the inscription claimed, were all one within it. This was not comforting.

Once the company entered, the statue ground into motion. Its stone body rolled and shifted without falling apart while the braziers inside it flared hot enough to burn anyone who came near. The Tubthumpers discovered that cold and water could smother those flames, robbing the construct of its worst weapon. After several close calls, they battered the ancient guardian into harmless rubble.

At the lowest chamber they dared explore, they found a vast spiral comet carved into the floor—the unmistakable symbol of [[Pharasma]]. Around it were the blackened impressions of those who had died creating the seal. The magic remained powerful despite its age, placed there to hold something beneath the island.

Unfortunately, the seal had acquired guardians of its own. Two gibbering mouthers rose from the darkness in heaps of eyes, teeth, and shrieking flesh. One engulfed poor [[Connal]] and nearly killed him before the company managed to tear the hound free and destroy both creatures. I understand that Connal has declined to provide a statement, though his subsequent opinions regarding underground architecture have been forceful.

A ring bearing Pharasma’s symbol was recovered from the chamber, but the greater discovery was the seal itself. Whatever lay beneath it had been imprisoned at terrible cost by people powerful enough to scar stone with their deaths. The Tubthumpers made the rare and admirable decision not to open the obviously forbidden door beneath the lair of screaming mouth-things. They intend to return when they are considerably stronger—or, one hopes, considerably less curious.

They crossed Candlemere Lake once more and travelled for several days toward the ruins Jubilost had described. Deep in the [[Locations/Inner Sea Region/River Kingdoms/Stolen Lands/Greenbelt/Narlmarches|Narlmarches]], they found an ancient elven keep nearly swallowed by the forest. Its walls stood twenty feet high in places, thick with moss and twisting vines. The open gate appeared to offer easy passage.

It did not.

As [[Mairi]] crossed beneath the rusted portcullis, the entire mass crashed down upon her, pinning her beneath hundreds of pounds of iron. Ally reacted immediately, conjuring a staircase against the wall so [[Ant]] could climb into the courtyard and reach the old winch. Between magic, machinery, and a great deal of desperate straining, they raised the gate and pulled Mairi free.

Their next discovery was a ruined tower inhabited by enough rats to give the floor fur. Rather than permit the resulting battle to become a contest between teeth and ankles, [[Tristian]] calmed the swarm with divine magic. This allowed everyone to withdraw and reconsider the wisdom of attacking several thousand creatures at once merely because each individual creature was small.

Another tower was occupied by Teorlian, a murderous grimstalker, and the assassin vine he kept as his companion. The vine struck from among the ordinary vegetation while Teorlian attacked from the tower’s walls and arrow slits. The pair had preyed upon travelers for some time, judging by the remains decorating their lair. The Tubthumpers killed them both.

That victory brought no opportunity to rest. Something invisible and impossibly swift flashed through the courtyard and struck Mairi from behind. Pain and fury summoned the wolf spirit within her; her face lengthened into its lupine muzzle as she turned to confront an enemy already somewhere else.

Their attacker was Rigg Gargadilly, a quickling capable of crossing the courtyard almost faster than the eye could follow. He darted from hiding, stabbed, vanished, and reappeared from another direction before anyone could properly answer. Even [[Seamus]]—who is very quick when someone else is in danger—could scarcely bring his blade around before the creature had gone again.

The company eventually wounded Rigg badly enough to drive him from the keep, but they could not catch him. He disappeared into the Narlmarches, leaving the Tubthumpers in possession of a ruined fortress they had not finished exploring, several serious injuries, and the uncomfortable certainty that a murderous fey with a very good memory was still alive somewhere nearby.

They ended the day inside the Forgotten Keep, exhausted and bloodied, with healing and looting still ahead of them. Candlemere had taught them that some doors should remain sealed. The keep, unfortunately, had already let them in.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
