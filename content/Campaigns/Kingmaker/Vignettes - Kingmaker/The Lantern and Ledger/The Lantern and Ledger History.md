# The Lantern and Ledger History

![](../../../../assets/images/shared-cover-110a4fd3e759.png)

*Compiled by Mara Venn, Managing Editor*

> Light for the Present. Record for the Future.  
> —Motto of The Lantern and Ledger

The *Lantern and Ledger* is the national newspaper of the Republic of Thumping Waters. Printed in [Thumpington](../../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Thumping%20Waters/Settlements/Thumpington/Thumpington.md) and carried throughout the Republic, it publishes news, public notices, council decisions, battlefield reports, settlement correspondence, obituaries, advertisements, and the occasional warning that should have been obvious before it required ink.

The newspaper is edited by [Linzi](../../NPCs/Linzi.md), Minister of Communication, and produced by a small staff of correspondents whose assignments range from council chambers to war zones. Its first issue appeared on Sarenith 1, 4714 AR beneath the headline *Speartooth Slain in the Western Hills*. Copies cost one copper piece, although readers are encouraged to pass an edition onward once finished.

### The Press Before the Paper

The history of the *Lantern and Ledger* began several years before its first issue. While serving as Minister of Communication, Linzi became convinced that the growing Republic needed a printing press. Laws, news, and public records still traveled chiefly through handwritten copies, official messengers, tavern retellings, and whatever version of an expedition survived the journey home.

Linzi secretly diverted money from the national treasury to purchase press components and hire [Eobald](../../NPCs/Eobald.md), one of her former teachers in [Pitax](../../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Thumping%20Waters/Settlements/Pitax/Pitax.md), to help assemble and operate the machinery. She intended to reveal the completed press as a surprise and assumed its obvious usefulness would excuse the unauthorized expense. It did not.

The final caravan carrying Eobald and essential components was ambushed. Linzi confessed, and the Tubthumpers followed the caravan's trail through ogres, magical creatures, and the Jaggedbriar Coven. They rescued Eobald and recovered the press components before the hags could use either man or machinery in a sacrificial ritual.

The recovered machinery was not immediately ready to print. Components had been damaged in transit, exposed to weather, and handled by captors who valued them principally as mysterious pieces of metal. With Eobald's help, it took nearly a year to clean, repair, assemble, align, and test the press. Replacement parts had to be made locally, paper and ink secured in reliable quantities, and printers trained to operate machinery previously seen by few people in the Republic.

Once operational, the press spent its first years upon the unglamorous work of government. It printed laws, council proclamations, public notices, tax instructions, construction orders, forms, official records, and standardized copies of documents that previously had to be reproduced by hand. The work gave the Republic a more consistent administration and gave its printers the experience required to produce larger editions without accidentally reversing half the capital's legal code.

The Republic still did not have a regular newspaper. Linzi returned to her journals while the demands of government kept the press occupied and she developed the staff, supply network, and editorial practices needed for a public paper. Only after the machinery and its operators had proven themselves did she move forward with the *Lantern and Ledger*.

### The First Edition

The first issue of the *Lantern and Ledger* appeared on Sarenith 1, 4714 AR. Its leading story reported the death of Speartooth, the legendary smilodon of the western hills. Other articles covered the Republic's newly acquired lake vessel, the recovery of magical objects, recent wilderness dangers, and the appointment of a surrendered marsh giant called Jeffrey to guard a patch of cloudberries.

The inaugural edition also printed a declaration of purpose titled *A Lantern Lit, a Ledger Opened*. It promised public notices, council decisions, news from the settlements, correspondence from readers, and corrections whenever the paper made an error. Space was reserved for the last category while the staff publicly predicted that it would see little use.

The paper has since recorded the closing of the War of the River Kings, the death of [Nok-Nok](../../NPCs/Nok-Nok.md), the liberation of Pitax, the Victory Festival, public weddings and private losses, changes in government, distant expeditions, and threats to the Republic that could safely be described without assisting them.

Alongside such major stories, regular editions report the ordinary business of public life: council schedules, market prices, court notices, road closures, construction, births, deaths, weather damage, festival announcements, shipping, local disputes, and letters whose authors are considerably more interested in a missing goat than in the movements of armies.

### The Staff

#### Linzi, Editor

Linzi founded the newspaper and determines its broad editorial direction. She writes editorials, historical accounts, obituaries, and articles requiring the authority of the [Ministry of Communication](../../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Thumping%20Waters/Ruling%20Council/Ministry%20of%20Communication.md). Her earlier journals remain an important source for events that occurred before the newspaper's founding.

Linzi's position as both editor and Minister of Communication gives the paper exceptional access to government information and creates an equally obvious conflict of interest. The masthead makes that connection public rather than pretending it does not exist.

#### Mara Venn, Managing Editor

Mara Venn oversees daily production, assigns stories, edits copy, and covers government, law, and public works. She also assumes editorial control when Linzi becomes the subject of the news. Engagements, weddings, personal emergencies, and any story in which the editor begins a sentence with “For reasons that are nobody else's business” generally arrive on Mara's desk.

#### Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent

Darya reports from the Republic's borders, distant settlements, military expeditions, and the dangerous country between them. Much of her work is reconstructed from interviews with those present, particularly when accompanying the Tubthumpers would be impractical, suicidal, or both.

#### Sella Copperclaw, Trade and Civic Affairs Correspondent

Sella covers markets, construction, settlement affairs, public celebrations, commerce, and the effects of government decisions upon ordinary households and businesses. Her reporting is responsible for a significant portion of the paper's useful information and nearly all of the complaints received from merchants who believe their advertisement should have been placed nearer the front.

#### Tobin Bramblefoot, Games and Society Correspondent

Tobin reports upon tournaments, games, festivals, performances, society events, and the less formal contests through which citizens determine who must purchase the next round. His department also maintains results, standings, invitations, and corrections submitted by competitors with strong opinions about scoring.

#### [Roald Celinnas](../../NPCs/Roald%20Celinnas.md), Pitax Correspondent and Contributing Archivist

Roald is the proprietor of the Crow's Feather, Pitax's informal library. He contributes historical and magical research, particularly when a subject must be assembled from folklore, contradictory witnesses, and records of doubtful provenance. Roald denies being employed by the paper but continues to return edited drafts, annotations, and invoices for books nobody authorized him to purchase.

### Reporting and Reconstruction

The newspaper does not pretend that a correspondent witnesses every event it records. Expeditions are frequently reconstructed from interviews, field notes, council records, surviving enemies, recovered documents, and Linzi's earlier journals. Articles distinguish direct observation from later testimony where the difference is important.

Names assigned to defeated monsters, cultists, and obscure historical figures are sometimes the result of later research rather than knowledge possessed by the Tubthumpers during the original encounter. Such identifications represent the staff's best conclusions and may be revised when better evidence becomes available.

The staff maintains a corrections policy. Minor spelling errors are corrected in later printings when possible. Substantive errors receive a notice in a subsequent edition. Disputes over whether a quoted remark was intended sarcastically are reviewed according to available testimony, the speaker's established character, and Mara Venn's patience.

### News and the Government

The *Lantern and Ledger* is not wholly independent of the Republic's government. It was founded by a sitting minister, operates with public authority, and regularly prints council announcements. It is best understood as a public newspaper with an official responsibility to preserve the national record.

That responsibility does not require the publication of every fact immediately. Information that would endanger citizens, reveal an active operation, expose an intelligence source, or assist an enemy may be withheld. The Ministry of Communication maintains separate restricted reports for the council. Those documents are not editions of the newspaper and are not available to the public.

Opinion is normally identified through editorials or the *From the Editor's Desk* column. News articles use named bylines whenever the principal reporter is known. When Linzi is personally involved in a story, Mara ordinarily supervises its publication.

### Illustrations and Advertisements

Most illustrated stories use small woodcuts prepared for the press. Major editions may include several images: portraits, diagrams, battlefield reconstructions, views of public ceremonies, or depictions of creatures that readers are strongly advised not to approach merely to test the artist's accuracy.

Paid advertisements appear in a designated section, often near the end of an edition. They provide useful revenue and allow businesses throughout the Republic to reach readers beyond their own settlements. Claims made by advertisers do not constitute investigation, endorsement, magical certification, or a promise that Crazy Akbar will remember the same discount twice.

Advertisements do not count against the editorial staff's preferred limit on news illustrations. This distinction has been explained repeatedly to the printer and at least once to the [Ministry of the Treasury](../../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Thumping%20Waters/Ruling%20Council/Ministry%20of%20the%20Treasury.md).

### Distribution

The *Lantern and Ledger* publishes several regular editions each month. The exact days vary with courier arrivals and the demands placed upon the government press, but readers do not ordinarily wait for a national crisis to receive another paper. Important events may prompt a special edition, while urgent proclamations and warnings are sometimes printed as shorter broadsheets rather than held for the next full issue.

Issues are printed in Thumpington and carried outward by government couriers, merchants, travelers, and settlement representatives. Distant communities may receive several editions together, and local notices are sometimes added before bundles continue onward.

The cover price remains one copper piece. A single copy may pass through many hands before the next edition arrives, particularly in distant settlements. The first issue requested that readers pass the paper onward and refrain from using it to wrap fish until everyone else had received an opportunity to read it. This remains the paper's most frequently ignored circulation policy.

### From the Editor's Desk

> A lantern is useful because someone can carry it into a dark place. A ledger is useful because, afterward, nobody gets to claim the darkness was empty. We intend to do both.  
> —Linzi, Editor and Minister of Communication
