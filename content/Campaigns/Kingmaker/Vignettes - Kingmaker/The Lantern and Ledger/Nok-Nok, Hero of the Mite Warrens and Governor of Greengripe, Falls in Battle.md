## Nok-Nok, Hero of the Mite Warrens and Governor of Greengripe, Falls in Battle

*Lamashan 7, 4714 AR*

**By Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters**

---

### A Hero Before Anyone Asked

There are people who spend their entire lives waiting for someone else to call them a hero. Nok-Nok never had that problem. From the day the Tubthumpers met him beneath the Old Sycamore, he was entirely certain that greatness awaited him. He was a wiry goblin with scarred green skin, a grin large enough to suggest either confidence or imminent trouble, and a pair of kukris he wielded with considerable skill and even greater enthusiasm. He called himself a hero before anyone had given him reason to do so. Then he gave us reason.

Nok-Nok joined the future founders of Thumping Waters as they fought through the mite warrens beneath the Old Sycamore. He helped rescue Mikmek of the Sootscale tribe, recover the kobolds’ stolen sacred statue, and end the conflict that had consumed both tribes. His courage helped make possible the Republic’s first lasting friendship with the people of Sootscale Valley, and for the rest of his life he proudly carried the title **Hero of the Mite Warrens**. He was rarely shy about reminding people of it.

In those early years, Nok-Nok had a remarkable habit of appearing whenever adventure, danger, or the possibility of applause presented itself. He fought beside the Tubthumpers against monsters in the Greenbelt and once arrived at the old Stag Lord’s fortress just in time to help save Mairi from a murderous plant creature. His account of that battle placed somewhat more emphasis on his own contribution than other witnesses considered strictly accurate, but that was Nok-Nok. He possessed the rare ability to arrive precisely when he was needed and the even rarer ability to remember himself as the decisive hero of every encounter.

Beneath the boasting, however, was genuine courage, and beneath the jokes was fierce loyalty. Nok-Nok did not merely want others to believe that he was a hero. He wanted to become the sort of person who deserved to be one. His greatest achievement was not found beneath the Old Sycamore or upon any battlefield. It was Greengripe.

---

### Governor of Greengripe

What began as a ramshackle goblin settlement hidden among the trees became, under Nok-Nok’s leadership, one of the most prosperous and unforgettable towns in the Republic of Thumping Waters. Greengripe grew into a center of alchemy, brewing, smithing, trade, and invention, where goblins, humans, dwarves, and others worked beside one another in workshops that produced equal quantities of useful goods, civic pride, and alarming noises.

Nok-Nok governed loudly, creatively, and with a degree of theatrical chaos that alarmed nearly every visiting official. He called himself the *“Chief of Fun and Fire Safety,”* though his qualifications for the second position were frequently questioned. His only standing order was posted upon the door of Greengripe’s town hall:

> “No explosions before breakfast.”
>
> — Standing order of Governor Nok-Nok

Enforcement was inconsistent.

For all the laughter surrounding his administration, Nok-Nok took the welfare of Greengripe seriously. He knew its citizens by name, visited its workshops, encouraged its children, celebrated its inventors, and defended the town whenever outsiders dismissed it as a collection of unruly goblins playing with fire. He saw what Greengripe could become before anyone else did, and more importantly, he convinced its people to see it too.

---

### Defiant Optimism

Nok-Nok often spoke of what he called **defiant optimism**: the belief that laughter was the best armor against fear. In Greengripe, that belief became a way of life. The town’s songs were loud, its festivals frequent, and its citizens stubbornly joyful. Nok-Nok taught them that being mocked was no reason to become ashamed and that being feared was no reason to become cruel.

He had once worshiped Lamashtu, the Mother of Monsters, but in later years he became living proof that no one is bound forever by the name others place upon them. Nok-Nok knew what much of the world believed goblins were supposed to be, and he spent the rest of his life proving that they could be citizens, builders, soldiers, leaders, friends, and heroes.

---

### Nok-Nok’s Irregulars

From Greengripe’s alchemists, brewers, and demolition experts, Nok-Nok formed the military company that would bear his name: **Nok-Nok’s Irregulars**. They wore bright red sashes and iron helmets shaped like beer mugs, marched to drums made from repurposed kegs, and followed a doctrine officially known as *“dazzle, disorient, detonate.”* The Ministry of Defense assures me this was a legitimate military strategy and not merely three words Nok-Nok enjoyed shouting. The Irregulars were easy to laugh at until one saw them fight.

When Pitax attempted to invade the Republic while its leaders were returning from the Rushlight Tournament, the Irregulars stood with the defenders at Willowfen. Their bombardment shattered mercenary formations and helped destroy an invading force of soldiers, mammoth-riding giants, and wyverns before it could secure a foothold. Nok-Nok had transformed brewers, alchemists, and enthusiastic demolitionists into one of the Republic’s most effective military companies. He was unbearably proud of them, and he had every right to be.

High Marshal Valerie, whose opinions concerning military discipline are well known, offered the following remembrance:

> “I questioned Governor Nok-Nok’s methods often. I never questioned his courage. He understood that soldiers do not fight for formations, regulations, or lines drawn upon a map. They fight for the people standing beside them and the homes waiting behind them. His Irregulars followed him because they knew he would never ask them to face a danger he would not face himself. In the end, he proved them right.”
>
> — High Marshal Valerie

---

### The Battle East of Fort Drelev

Nok-Nok fell east of Fort Drelev during the Republic’s recent battle against the gathered Tuskrider host. The enemy came in overwhelming force, supported by seasoned raiders and mammoth cavalry, and the Republic’s army was driven from the field after a costly engagement. Nok-Nok’s Irregulars suffered devastating losses and were destroyed as an effective fighting force. A handful may have survived. Nok-Nok did not.

As the Irregulars’ position collapsed, Nok-Nok refused to abandon his soldiers. He remained with them, fighting to defend those under his command and buying time for any who could still escape the field. It would have surprised no one had Nok-Nok died attempting something reckless, spectacular, and unnecessarily explosive. Instead, he died protecting his people.

---

### What the Republic Has Lost

There is no humor that can make the loss of Nok-Nok and his soldiers painless. Greengripe has lost its governor. The Republic has lost one of its earliest companions, one of its boldest commanders, and one of the clearest examples of what this country was created to make possible. Many families have lost far more.

The names of the fallen Irregulars must be remembered alongside that of their commander. They were not a joke, a spectacle, or an amusing goblin experiment. They were soldiers of Thumping Waters. They stood against the enemies of the Republic beneath its banner, and they gave their lives in its defense.

---

### The Hero He Became

Nok-Nok would undoubtedly object to any obituary that did not make clear that he died heroically. For once, no exaggeration is required. He began as a strange little goblin beneath an old tree, announcing to a group of weary adventurers that he intended to become a hero. He became the Hero of the Mite Warrens, the governor of Greengripe, the commander of the Irregulars, and a friend to the founders of the Republic. He became an example to everyone who had ever been told they were too strange, too small, too foolish, or born too close to darkness to become something better.

Nok-Nok spent his life demanding that the world recognize him as a hero. In the end, he died not for recognition, glory, or applause, but so that others might live. That is the part he would probably have been proudest of, although he still would have wanted the applause.

---

### For One Night

Years ago, Nok-Nok gave me a small firework after one of Greengripe’s military demonstrations. He told me to light it whenever I needed courage. I kept it upon my desk, unlit, as a reminder of him.

Tonight, Greengripe will be quiet. Tomorrow, I hope someone lights the first firework, then another, then every last one they have. Let the sky burn with color above the town he built. Let the explosions be entirely too loud. Let the Ministry of Defense complain. Let every citizen of Thumping Waters look toward Greengripe and remember the goblin who believed laughter could defeat fear.

For one night, at least, there should be no rule against explosions before breakfast.

**Farewell, Nok-Nok.**

*You were right. You were a hero.*

—Linzi

*Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters*
