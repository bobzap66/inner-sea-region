# Concerning Recent Fey Activity Within the Republic

![[shared-cover-ee82cf8c9a07.png]]

### Supplemental Intelligence Report

#### Concerning Recent Fey Activity Within the Republic

**Prepared by:** Linzi, Minister of Communication

**Distribution:** Prime Minister and Council of Thumping Waters

**Classification:** Council Eyes Only

In the weeks since the incident involving the fey hunter in the western uplands, my office has received a growing number of reports concerning a change in fey behavior throughout the Republic. I have found no evidence that the fey are attacking our people or organizing any campaign against Thumping Waters. The change is quieter than that.

Fey who once warned travelers away from dangerous ground, tolerated minor trespasses, guided the lost toward safety, or offered aid in exchange for some small favor now appear increasingly unwilling to do so. In several cases, they have stood by while citizens were in obvious danger and simply allowed events to take their course.

#### Greenbelt Hunter

**Status: Verified**

A hunter from a farmstead west of Tatzlford crossed an old line of boundary stones while following a wounded stag. Several sprites confronted him, confiscated the stag, broke his arrows, and ordered him to leave immediately. The hunter apologized and explained that the boundary was poorly marked, but the sprites refused to hear him.

> You knew enough to enter. You know enough to leave.
>
> — Unidentified sprite

He returned safely, but local hunters say accidental trespass in that region once resulted in warnings, pranks, or demands for restitution rather than immediate expulsion.

#### The Lost Girl

**Status: Verified**

A seven-year-old girl wandered away from a farm near Tatzlford shortly before sunset and became lost in the woods. Searchers found her body the following afternoon at the bottom of a steep ravine. Tracks suggest she had wandered for several hours before falling.

Multiple members of the search party reported seeing small lights moving through the trees during the night, and the girl’s footprints repeatedly turned toward them. Local families have long told stories of similar lights guiding lost travelers toward roads and farmsteads. Whether the lights were responsible for leading her deeper into the woods cannot be established. What is clear is that, if they were fey, they chose not to guide her home.

#### The Miller’s Field

**Status: Credible**

A miller’s son south of Olegton accidentally damaged a thorn hedge long associated with a nearby dryad. He apologized immediately and offered to replant it, provide labor, or make whatever restitution she considered appropriate. According to both father and son, the dryad refused every offer and informed them that they were no longer welcome beyond the cultivated field.

When the miller reminded her that his family had traded peacefully with the local fey for years, she replied:

> Your rulers have chosen the distance between us.
>
> — Unidentified dryad

No violence followed, but the family has abandoned several acres of woodland pasture they had used without incident for more than a decade.

#### The Broken Cart

**Status: Verified**

A merchant family suffered a broken axle during an early winter storm on a minor road west of Thumpington. They reported seeing several grigs watching from beneath the trees and begged them for directions to shelter, offering food, coin, and even the cart’s cargo in exchange. The fey ignored them.

A Republic patrol found the wagon the following morning. Two adults survived beneath heavy blankets, but their elderly mother died during the night from exposure. The patrol later located a dry, sheltered fey hollow less than half a mile from the road.

#### Old Willow Crossing

**Status: Credible**

A teamster became trapped beneath his overturned wagon near Old Willow Crossing after one of his horses panicked. Another traveler later found him dead. Tracks and disturbed grass suggest several small creatures had gathered nearby while he was trapped, and a collection of tiny footprints circled the wagon repeatedly before departing.

The teamster’s companion, who had gone for help, claims the man had previously spoken of friendly grigs living near the crossing and believed they would help him if he ever got into trouble there. Nothing indicates they caused the accident. Nothing indicates they tried to save him.

#### Assessment

The fey do not appear to be seeking confrontation. They are not burning farms, attacking settlements, or hunting citizens of the Republic. Instead, they seem to be withdrawing the small courtesies and informal protections that have allowed mortals and fey to live alongside one another with relatively little bloodshed.

Accidental trespass is no longer being overlooked. Apologies are being rejected. Travelers who would once have received directions, warnings, shelter, or assistance are being left to whatever fate finds them.

I believe word of the western incident has spread farther than we expected. Whether there is any formal agreement among the fey is impossible to say, and I have found no evidence of centralized orders. It may simply be that the story has passed from glade to glade and that many of them have reached the same conclusion: the rulers of Thumping Waters demonstrated that mercy is optional, and the fey have chosen to treat it the same way.

For years, our relationship with the fey has depended less upon law than upon custom. We leave certain places alone. They warn us when we stray. We apologize when harm is unintended, and they usually allow restitution. A lost traveler might be mocked, frightened, or made to perform some ridiculous task in exchange for directions, but more often than not that traveler still found the road home.

None of these customs were written into treaty, but they saved lives.

I am concerned that we may be watching them disappear.

For the moment, I recommend warning citizens to respect known fey boundaries, remain on established roads where possible, and no longer assume that nearby fey will provide assistance in an emergency. My office should continue collecting reports before we make any broader public declaration.

The fey have not declared themselves our enemies.

They may simply have decided that what happens to us is no longer their concern.

—Linzi

Minister of Communication
