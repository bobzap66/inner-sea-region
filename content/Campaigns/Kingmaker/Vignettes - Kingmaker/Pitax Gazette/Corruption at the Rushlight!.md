# Corruption at the Rushlight!

![[shared-cover-5d0935703bb5.png]]

### The Pitaxian Gazette

*Truth, Taste, and Talent—Naturally.*
**Sarenith, 4714 AR**

*A Civic Report from the Rushlight Festival*

**A RAPID SUCCESSION OF EVENTS AT RUSHLIGHT PROMPTS QUIET QUESTIONS**

The Rushlight Festival is intended as a celebration of skill, artistry, and good faith among the River Kingdoms. It is therefore with some reluctance that the Gazette records a rapid succession of events involving the delegation from Thumping Waters which, within the first two days alone, have given rise to subdued but persistent discussion among officials and observers.

The first of these occurred early in the festival, during what was publicly described as a friendly exhibition on the archery grounds. This informal display paired *Florante Mayank of Gralton*, an official competitor in the Rushlight lists, with *Seamus of Thumping Waters*, who was not entered in the competition proper.

While exhibitions of this sort are not unheard of, several spectators later remarked upon the unusual courtesy of presenting a non-competitor as a representative demonstration of the field. Some expressed concern that such a pairing may have given an incomplete—or misleading—impression of the competitors Mr. Mayank would later face.

*“It is an odd kindness to offer a guide who is not, in truth, a rival,”* observed one attendee.

The matter might have rested there, had the very next day not added further texture to the festival’s narrative.

The official archery contest concluded with a victory for *Anston Crazyfoot of Thumping Waters*, a performance that drew enthusiastic response from his delegation and polite applause from the stands. It was, by all accounts, a strong showing—one that briefly placed Thumping Waters in a favorable light.

Before the second day of Rushlight had concluded, however, tournament staff conducting routine post-event cleanup reported the recovery of several **adamantine-tipped arrows** from the range. Such ammunition is expressly prohibited under Rushlight regulations due to its penetrative advantage and attendant risk.

Officials have confirmed that ownership of the arrows could not be established, and no formal accusation has been made. Nonetheless, the discovery—so soon after the contest’s conclusion—has prompted speculation among observers.

In the same period, it was publicly acknowledged that *Ally Grainger of Thumping Waters* identified a competitor from Daggermark engaged in clear misconduct earlier in the festival—an intervention widely praised for its decisiveness and clarity.

Some have since wondered whether the prompt exposure of one offender may have had the unintended effect of drawing attention away from unresolved irregularities elsewhere.

*“Distraction does not require intent,”* remarked a senior festival steward speaking privately. *“It requires only timing.”*

The final point of comment came not from the lists, but among the dispersing crowds. Before the second day’s festivities had fully concluded, *Velka of the Tubthumpers* was observed engaging extensively with local children near the grounds, distributing coin and small handcrafted toys.

![[corruption-at-the-rushlight-image-01.png]]

Several witnesses noted the conspicuous nature of the display, including the unusually heavy purse from which the coins were drawn, its mouth left untied as currency visibly spilled from it during the exchange. No impropriety is alleged, and no rule was broken.

Yet some attendees remarked upon the sequence of events: an exhibition that blurred competitive lines, a swift victory, the discovery of prohibited equipment, and a public display of generosity—all unfolding within the span of two days.

Taken individually, none of these moments constitute wrongdoing. Considered together, they have prompted quiet reflection among those charged with preserving the integrity of Pitax’s festivals.

The Gazette offers no conclusions.

It merely records the order of events—and leaves readers to consider whether coincidence, like marksmanship, improves with repetition.
