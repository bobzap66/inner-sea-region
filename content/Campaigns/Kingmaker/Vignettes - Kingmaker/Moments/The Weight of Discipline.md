# The Weight of Discipline


Chief Sootscale sat in his chamber, claws tapping a steady rhythm on the stone table. Orderly, measured, disciplined—unlike the thoughts rattling in his skull. Goblins. Always goblins.

He remembered when chaos nearly destroyed his tribe: the lies of [Tartuk](../../NPCs/Tartuk.md)—no, [Tartuccio](../../NPCs/Tartuccio.md)—corrupted their faith, chained their will to a false prophet. He remembered the darkness of Old Sharptooth, and the madness of worshipping a demon who promised nothing but fear. It had taken the Tubthumpers’ aid, the courage of Mikmek, and his own rage to tear down that idol and reclaim his tribe’s dignity.

And now… goblins.

Not wicked, not cruel. Just chaotic. They brewed their alchemical concoctions, true, and supplied the mines with powders and oils—but their young ones delighted in pranks that left patrols humiliated and guards distracted. Their mayor, Nok-Nok—ah, there was no denying his skill. A rogue with kukris flashing like silvered fangs, a fighter who had bled alongside the Tubthumpers against mites and worse. Sootscale respected that. But Nok-Nok could not see the difference between laughter and disorder.

“Discipline is not a chain,” the chief muttered aloud. “It is a ladder.” Kobolds had climbed far—out of demon-worship, out of slavery to lies, into something resembling pride. And every time a goblin prank splintered the rungs of that ladder, Sootscale felt the ground quake beneath his tribe’s claws again.

He did not want war. He did not want blood. He wanted peace, strength, and order. But he would not have his tribe treated like the butt of a joke. That is why he had written to Prime Minister Mairi—formally, properly, with seals pressed into the wax. If the goblins respected no walls, then Thumping Waters must build some.

Chief Sootscale folded his claws, breathing deep. He would not be Tartuk’s fool again, nor Old Sharptooth’s slave. He would not let laughter become a mask for chaos.

If Nok-Nok wanted to be seen as an ally, then the goblin must learn: respect is built on stone, not smoke.
