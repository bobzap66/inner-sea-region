# Looking Into the Void


His face is worn, perpetually set in a melancholy expression, and his voice carries the deep weight of his convictions. He wanders the dirt paths of Olegton, his tattered coat sweeping the ground as he preaches his somber creed.

To Harrim, life is a fleeting candle in the dark—one that’s destined to flicker and die. He stands in the square with his iron symbol of Groteus hanging heavily around his neck, proclaiming the eventual decay of all things. With each word, his voice echoes with the inevitability of time, leaving a disquiet in the air:

"Brothers and sisters, do not cling to the illusion of permanence," he intones to a group of villagers who have gathered out of morbid curiosity. "The end comes for all, and that is not a cause for sorrow, but a release from the torment of existence. Look to the fields; even the crops we harvest wither. In time, so shall we."

Some in the crowd mutter uneasily, while others are drawn in by his somber yet compelling words. Harrim’s message resonates most deeply with those facing hardship, loss, or sickness, as his doctrine offers a form of release from pain—however bleak it may be.

He tells them not to strive against the inevitable. "Do not fear non-existence, my friends," he advises with a heavy sigh, "for it is the destiny of all that lives. Be it tomorrow or many years hence, we will each face the End of Days. Embrace it, and be unburdened by false hopes."

He feels it is his duty to hold up a mirror to life’s frailty and ask the listener to contemplate the void.
