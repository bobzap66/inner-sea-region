# Grigori's Fate


Grigori stood in the center of the courtroom, his hands bound in heavy iron shackles, but his mind raced far more wildly than his legs ever could. The judge’s voice was a distant murmur, announcing the verdict he had expected, but still, the finality of it hit him like a punch to the gut.

Treason. Sedition. The words echoed in his mind, but they felt foreign, distant, like they were being used to describe someone else. He’d been hired for a job—just another job, nothing personal. He hadn’t cared about the politics of it. But execution? That wasn’t part of the bargain.

Whoever hired me knew it would come to this, he thought bitterly. Whoever they are. He still didn’t know. A letter slipped under the door of his inn room, a bag of gold with no name attached, and vague instructions to stir dissent, to rattle the new leaders of Thumping Waters. It was supposed to be easy. Whispers in taverns, songs sung on street corners... nothing dangerous. Nothing like this.

But the leaders had been ready for him. They’d countered his stories, his songs, his carefully crafted rumors with swift denials, clever truths, and the weight of their new republic behind them. His words had turned into smoke, scattered by a wind he hadn’t anticipated. And when the people of Thumping Waters didn’t rise up, when the unrest didn’t catch fire like he’d hoped, the noose had tightened around him instead.

He swallowed hard as the judge pronounced the sentence—execution by hanging. A bard, killed by rope. Ironic, in a way. Killed for words. Killed for music.

He could hear the gasps and murmurs from the crowd, some shocked, others satisfied. His eyes scanned the faces, but he didn’t recognize anyone. No sympathetic glances, no friendly nods. He was alone here, a puppet who had outlived his usefulness.

Whoever set me up is long gone by now, he thought darkly. They’re probably laughing, counting their coin while I stand here waiting for the hangman’s noose. He clenched his jaw, his mind racing through all the times he could have walked away, could have stopped before it got this far. But no, he’d been greedy. Too eager to play the part, too confident in his ability to manipulate the narrative.

I should’ve seen it coming.

The guards moved toward him, rough hands grabbing his arms, dragging him back toward his cell to await the inevitable. He kept his face calm, even though inside, his stomach churned with fear. He wasn’t ready to die. Not like this.
  

What was it all for? A few weeks of rumors, a handful of songs laced with deceit, and now his life was the price. He’d always told himself there was no harm in it, that the power of words was his tool, his weapon, and he could wield it without consequences. But now... now the consequences were closing in, heavy and suffocating.

I don’t even know who I betrayed.

That was the worst part, the part that gnawed at him as he was led away. He didn’t care about Thumping Waters, didn’t care about their leaders, their republic. It was just a game to him. But the game was over, and he had lost. He had been outplayed, outmaneuvered, and now he was the one paying the price for someone else’s move.

I should’ve never taken the job. Should’ve never trusted a faceless patron.

But it was too late for regrets. Too late for anything, really. All that was left now was the cold certainty of the noose, and the bitter taste of a gamble that had gone horribly wrong.
