# Vigil's End


The tomb did not roar or collapse when they emerged.

It simply… let go.

The last skeletal sentinels shuddered, then fell inward on themselves, collapsing into fine gray dust that slid across the stone floor in patient silence. Whatever force had animated them withdrew, as if recognizing that its purpose had finally been fulfilled—or rendered meaningless.

Zorek stood alone.

For untold centuries, he had stood exactly so: straight-backed, unbowed, a living declaration that some oaths do not end simply because the world changes around them. He had been the measure. The final word. The last honest question asked of any who sought the blade. Honor had sustained him where flesh should have failed, and duty had given him strength long after strength should have fled.

Now, as Velka stepped forward bearing Ovirnbaane, its edge weeping slow, viscous blood, Zorek finally moved.

Not to bar the way.

To breathe.

His eyes rested on the sword, and something ancient settled behind them—not surprise, not fear, but the quiet recognition of a burden at last set down. The weight he had carried for thousands of years loosened its grip.

The change came swiftly.

Time reclaimed him all at once. Strength bled away as though the years themselves had been waiting patiently for permission. His shoulders sagged. The proud lines of his face deepened, carved by centuries rushing back to claim what honor had held at bay. The perfect, terrifying vitality that had marked him as Gorum’s chosen champion thinned, revealing the mortal man beneath the legend.

*“So,”* he said, his voice still iron-edged despite the fractures forming within it. *“It is finished.”*

He did not ask after Armag. He already knew.

He knew the tomb had been breached. He knew the blade would not return. He knew the Twice-Born—never truly twice-born at all—had fallen where he stood, crushed beneath the lie that had shaped his life.

Zorek lowered himself to one knee, then the other, waving away any attempt to steady him with a firm, unmistakable gesture. This was not weakness. This was release.

*“I have stood watch,”* he said, *“since before names hardened into nations. Since before Brevoy dreamed itself into being. I stood because honor demanded it. Because Gorum demanded it. Because someone had to.”*

His gaze hardened, and contempt bled into his words like a blade drawn slowly from a sheath.

*“The Sisters of the Bloodshot Eye,”* he spat. *“Hags and liars wrapped in prophecy and rot. They stole a child on his first hunt—a child—and drowned him in a story until he could no longer tell where he ended and the legend began. They did not reincarnate Armag. They manufactured him.”*

His lip curled, ancient disdain laid bare.

*“They shaped him into a weapon and pointed him at Brevoy, a land that rose long after my oath was sworn. Gyronna’s devotees delight in such corruption. They rot what is strong and call it devotion. They twist honor until it screams.”*

A slow breath escaped him—heavy, weary, and satisfied.

*“Their patience has earned them nothing. Their plan lies in ruins. Their god will have to find new hands.”*

Time pressed harder now. His hands trembled. His hair whitened, then thinned. The years no longer waited.

*“There is another blade yet to come,”* Zorek said, and the certainty in his voice silenced the space around him. *“Not one born of rage or hunger. One born of choice.”*

His eyes lifted, distant, as though he were seeing beyond the tomb, beyond the Stolen Lands themselves.

*“That blade does not only kill,”* he continued. *“It can heal. It can mend an ancient wound. It can set right a wrong so old the world has forgotten it was ever made.”*

He looked back at them, expression grave.

*“And it can destroy just as completely.”*

He let the truth settle.

*“In the hands of the wise, it may become a salve. In the hands of the desperate, a scalpel. In the hands of the proud, a weapon that will scar the land forever.”*

His gaze passed over them slowly, deliberately.

*“It will not demand battle. It will offer solutions. And that is why it is more dangerous than any sword that thirsts for blood.”*

At last, his eyes returned to Ovirnbaane.

*“This blade will serve you,”* he said to its bearer. *“It will make you mighty. But it will hunger. Deny it battle long enough, and it will remind you why it was forged.”*

His voice softened, almost reverent.

*“You cannot destroy it. Not in any simple way. To end it, you must carry it to the Boneyard and shatter it upon your own tombstone. A paradox. One even I was never meant to solve.”*

The strength finally left him then—not violently, but with dignity.

*“My vigil is ended,”* Zorek said quietly. *“I am content.”*

He closed his eyes.

Flesh, bone, and ancient faith collapsed into pale dust that drifted across the stone, mingling with the remnants of the tomb’s other guardians. A final breath escaped him—not of pain, but of peace.

For a long while, the Tubthumpers stood in silence, caught between reverence and unease, bearing witness to the passing of something irreplaceable.

An era had ended.

And somewhere beyond the tomb, a blade waited—not for blood, but for a decision that would shape the Stolen Lands forever.
