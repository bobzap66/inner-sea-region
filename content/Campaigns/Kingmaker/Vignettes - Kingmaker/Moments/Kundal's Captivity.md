# Kundal's Captivity


Kundal sat in the cold, dim basement, his back against the rough stone wall. The chains around his wrists were loose enough to let him move, but tight enough to remind him of why he was here.

Why haven’t they come back? His thoughts wandered for the hundredth time today. They promised a cure. They said I’d be free of the beast... but it's been a month.

The memories of his time as a wolf were hazy, as if veiled in mist, but the sharp, visceral feelings of terror and rage still gnawed at him. He couldn’t remember the faces of the people he’d killed—just the blood on his claws, the sound of screams, and the taste of iron in his mouth. It wasn’t him, he knew that. It was the beast. But the guilt was still his. The knowledge that his hands had done the killing, even if they weren’t his in that moment.

They’re afraid of me. And maybe they should be. The thought stung, but he couldn't shake it. He could feel the creature lurking beneath his skin even now, like a caged animal waiting for its moment of weakness to escape. The full moon was coming again soon. What if they forgot about me? What if I turn again and there’s no one here to stop it?

The silence underground was suffocating. Kundal’s breath echoed off the walls, his own heartbeat the only sound he could rely on. Are they testing me? Waiting to see if I’ll lose control without the moon? Or is this punishment for what I did?

He clenched his fists, feeling the rough metal dig into his skin. It wasn’t my fault... but who would believe me? Even I don’t believe it some days. Maybe this place is where I belong. Maybe the beast deserves to be locked away forever, and I’m just a part of it now.

But then, another thought wormed its way into his mind—one that filled him with a quiet desperation. What if there is no cure? What if they were lying? The idea was unbearable, but it wasn’t impossible. They had no reason to trust him, no reason to believe he wasn’t a threat.

The leaders of Thumpington had promised hope, but hope had faded into uncertainty, then into suspicion. If I’m still in here, it’s because they think I’m too dangerous to let out. Maybe they’ll never come back...

He let out a slow breath, trying to calm the tightness in his chest. I’ll survive this, he thought. I have to. I won’t let the beast win. But as the days passed and no one came, that resolve became harder and harder to hold on to.
