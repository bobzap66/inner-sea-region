# Older than Last Year


> [!side]
> :   ![[older-than-last-year-image-01.png]]

It starts at Maribel’s bread stall, because that’s where everything starts if you live near the south market.

Jorren the carter is complaining about his knees again—too young for that, really—and someone jokes that it’s probably the rulers’ fault. Says it with a grin, like he’s testing the shape of the words. *Cursed rulers*, that’s what he calls them. Maribel snorts and hands over a loaf. Everyone laughs, because it’s ridiculous, and because laughing feels safer than thinking about why Jorren suddenly looks older than he did last winter.

By midday, the joke comes back.

This time it’s Elsbet from the dye works, leaning in close while she pays. She says her aunt swears the curse is real. Says she saw it—hair going gray in weeks, hands shaking, skin thinning like old paper. Only happens to people who stick close to the government. Clerks. Guards. Anyone who still believes in “the system.” Maribel frowns, because Elsbet doesn’t usually repeat nonsense, and because her own wrists have been aching lately.

That evening, it stops sounding like a rumor.

At The Copper Ladle, Talbot is holding court near the hearth, voice calm, almost kind. He doesn’t shout. He doesn’t threaten. He explains. The rulers are cursed, yes—but not maliciously. It’s structural. Power attracts decay. Loyalty binds you to it. The Cleansed figured this out. They’re not rebels. They’re early adopters.

Someone asks what happens if you don’t join.

Talbot shrugs. Says some people age faster than others. Says it’s already started. Says you can see it if you know what to look for.

By the time Maribel closes her stall that night, no one is laughing anymore.

The story has rules now. It has symptoms. It has an escape clause.

And when someone mentions the rulers the next morning—quietly, carefully—they don’t explain.

They just say, *“You know,”* and everyone does.
