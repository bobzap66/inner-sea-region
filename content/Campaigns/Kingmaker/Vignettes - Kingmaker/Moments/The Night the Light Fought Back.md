# The Night the Light Fought Back


### From the Journal of [[Linzi]], Minister of Communication of [[Thumping Waters]]

#### The Night the Light Fought Back

You have never heard silence until you have heard [[Candlemere]]’s. It hums in your bones, as though the world is holding its breath to see what you will do next.

The Tubthumpers crossed the lake on two makeshift rafts, their lanterns reduced to pinpricks. I remained on the mainland shore. Someone had to keep notes, watch the horses, and prepare a sufficiently stern expression for anyone who returned without the others.

From where I stood, the lake looked like a mirror and our heroes like shadows moving across the stars.

Then the light came.

Only one, whatever later tavern versions may claim. It drifted above the broken tower in blue-white silence. For a moment it was beautiful. Then it vanished, steel flashed where nothing appeared to be standing, and the shouting began.

A will-o’-wisp is very nearly the perfect enemy for frightening anyone trying to watch a battle from the opposite shore. It disappears. It ignores most magic. It strikes without the common courtesy of remaining visible afterward. Every time it flared into sight, I saw a different piece of the fight: Ant searching the darkness for a target, [[Mairi]] trying to force the thing into reach, Ally throwing spell after spell at a creature famously unimpressed by spells, and [[Seamus]] refusing to accept that this made the effort unreasonable.

Ally and Seamus finally destroyed it. Those present have attributed the victory to courage, teamwork, persistence, and sheer luck in proportions that vary according to the speaker.

The tower did not blaze with cleansing moonlight. The island did not sigh in gratitude. The remaining darkness stayed exactly where it was.

The Tubthumpers went beneath the ruins the following morning and returned with ancient coins, burns, several new questions, and the news that something had been sealed below Candlemere since before anyone sensible began keeping calendars. Connal had nearly been swallowed by a creature made principally of mouths. This was conveyed to me as though it were an ordinary complication of historical research.

The scholars now call what lingers there planar contamination, occult resonance, or evidence of a transdimensional ward. All of those descriptions may be correct.

I call it unfinished.

The Tubthumpers extinguished one light that night. Others still dance above Candlemere, and something beneath the island is still waiting at the threshold.

—Linzi

*Editor’s note: All surviving accounts agree that there was one will-o’-wisp. They disagree on whether leaving much of the useful equipment with the animals was a tactical decision or an educational experience.*
