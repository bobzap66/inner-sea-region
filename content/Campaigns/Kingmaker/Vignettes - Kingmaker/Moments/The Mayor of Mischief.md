# The Mayor of Mischief


Nok-Nok sat on the crooked steps of Greengripe’s “mayor’s hall” (really just the largest hut with a squeaky door), chewing on a stick of dried mushroom jerky and trying to puzzle out kobolds.

“Pranks are gifts,” he muttered to himself. “Gifts of laughter! Gifts of surprise! Gifts of sometimes exploding paint barrels.” He slapped his knee at the memory of young Skribble’s masterpiece—an entire Sootscale patrol covered head-to-toe in luminous green dust. The kobolds had looked magnificent, like a parade of swamp-spirits stomping down the path. And yet Chief Sootscale had not smiled. Not even once! He’d only scowled, sent a letter dripping with official seals, and tattled to Prime Minister Mairi.

Nok-Nok tugged at his belt, feeling the weight of the cold-iron kukri Seamus had given him. A magical blade, no less—its edge always keen, its presence humming like a low song in the metal. Not some bent cleaver or half-melted knife, but a weapon worthy of a commander. Whenever Nok-Nok looked at it, he remembered the moment: Seamus, serious as a priest, pressing it into his hands. “For you,” he’d said, like Nok-Nok was family. And that meant something.

Just like his Irregulars—his proud bombardiers, blasting thunder and fire in the name of Thumping Waters. He’d earned respect.

And now this humorless kobold wanted to lecture him about “order” and “discipline.”

Nok-Nok spat out a fleck of mushroom. “Bah. Order tastes like dirt.” He stood, puffing out his chest. “The Prime Minister knows. Seamus knows. Irregulars know. Without goblins, life is just rocks and rules.”

He grinned wide, teeth flashing, as he imagined Chief Sootscale reading the reply from Mairi. Surely it would be a clever letter, polite and proper on the outside but with a wink hidden inside. The kind of wink only a goblin would catch.

After all, Nok-Nok thought, who could possibly side against laughter?
