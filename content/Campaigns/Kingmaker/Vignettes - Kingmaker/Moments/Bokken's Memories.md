# Bokken's Memories


Bokken stared at his workbench, his hands trembling slightly as he corked a vial of bubbling liquid. The familiar clink of glass brought some comfort, but today, his mind was elsewhere. As he held up the bottle to inspect the potion, his gaze drifted to his right hand. The absence of his pinkie finger, long since healed, still felt like a ghostly reminder.

"Kurmil..." he muttered under his breath, his voice catching.

That name. It hadn't crossed his lips in years, not since the day his younger brother disappeared. The day Kurmil's wild eyes had flashed with something dark and unrecognizable. Bokken could still hear the sharp, manic laughter as the blade came down on his hand, the sudden sting, and the sight of his own pinkie tumbling to the floor.

"He was always... different. Strange, yes, but not... not like that," Bokken whispered to himself. The potion in his hands had stopped bubbling, cooling now, but he barely noticed. "Mother never forgave him, Desna rest her soul. Not after what he did. Not after the way he hit her."

He shuddered, the memory flooding back as if it had only just happened. The look of pain in their mother’s eyes, the sharp intake of breath when Kurmil struck her. That was the moment things changed—the moment Kurmil crossed a line, a line Bokken never imagined his brother capable of stepping over.

"Last I heard, he ran south. Living in a hollow tree, of all places," Bokken chuckled darkly, shaking his head. "Crazy. That fits him, I suppose."

The thought of his brother, out there somewhere, gnawed at him. Was Kurmil still the wild-eyed man who had taken his finger in a fit of madness? Or had the years changed him—made him worse, perhaps? Maybe Kurmil had found peace in his strange exile, or maybe that hollow tree hid something more sinister than Bokken dared to imagine.

"Is he even still alive?" Bokken wondered aloud, his voice barely above a whisper. "And if he is... would I even recognize him anymore?"
