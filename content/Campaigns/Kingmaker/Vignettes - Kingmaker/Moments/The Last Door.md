# The Last Door


> Far from Pitax, a woman watches King Irovetti prepare to die.

The image simply hangs before her among the white thorns: an underground bedchamber, a frightened king trying to look composed, and the beautiful thing beside him wearing the face he currently prefers. Beyond the bedroom door, six figures descend the hidden staircase. They are wounded and exhausted, but they are still coming.

Irovetti checks his sword and glances toward Engelidis.

> “They’ve killed everyone else.”

“Not everyone,” his consort replies.

“You know what I mean.”

The woman among the thorns almost smiles. Irovetti had been so charming when he owned nothing. Hungry, clever, and willing to believe every flattering lie whispered into his ear. She had shown him a throne and let him imagine that reaching it made him exceptional. Now he stood in a borrowed palace, surrounded by the ruins of every promise he had made.

---

Behind the door, someone speaks. The words are too muffled to hear. Metal scrapes against stone, followed by the quiet click of someone testing the latch.

Irovetti raises his weapon.

> “She said they could be broken.”

Engelidis looks toward the empty air, though not quite toward the unseen watcher.

> “Perhaps she was wrong.”

For the first time, irritation crosses the woman’s face. Then it passes. She reaches toward the distant image, stopping just short of touching Irovetti’s cheek.

> “No,” she says softly. “You were simply not enough.”

**The bedroom door begins to open.**

---

The woman turns her attention from Irovetti to the figures entering the room. Her expression is not anger. Not yet. There is curiosity there, and something almost like hope—but whatever gentler feeling might once have accompanied it is conspicuously absent.

> “So much stronger than the others,” she murmurs.

Behind her, a crystal chalice rests among the white thorns. It contains more grains of golden sand than anyone could count.

*There is still room for one more.*
