# The Raven Loosed


The raven circled once above the sleeping camp, its wings slicing through the cold air without a sound. Below, the firelight licked at the edges of the strange, conjured shelter the intruders had made. Six of them — warm, unaware, dreaming.

Mine, Vordakai thought, and the raven’s black eye reflected his own — a single, crimson-glowing gem. Sight bled from flesh to spirit, from bird to master.

The sentry — the tall half-breed in armor — sat by the fire, head turning with the slow patience of prey that did not yet know it was prey. The lich’s mind stirred, whispering to the soul eaters, calling them forward like hounds on a short leash.

They came.

Through the raven’s gaze, he watched them slip through the night, shadows given hunger and form. The first passed through the firelight like mist, the next slid into the yurt without a ripple. The others followed, each seeking the heartbeat they were bound to extinguish.

Steel flashed. Shouts broke the quiet. The half-breed’s elk bellowed in alarm, hooves tearing at the earth as if it could trample shadow. Inside the shelter, magic flared — blue, green, white — the wizard’s fury made light. The halfling’s bowstring thrummed like a heartbeat, the barbarian’s rage split the air in a howl, the rogue’s blade caught the firelight just before it vanished into darkness.

They fought well. Too well.

One by one, the soul eaters dissolved into nothing, their hunger denied. The raven hopped from a branch to a rock, its head cocked. Then, in a voice that was not its own — deep, hollow, and ancient — it spoke:

“Lay down your arms and surrender yourselves to Vordakai. Resist, and your deaths will be without meaning… your souls without rest.”

The campfire hissed in the sudden wind.
