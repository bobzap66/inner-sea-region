### From [[Linzi]]’s Journal

*An account of events from Kuthona 15 through Kuthona 19, 4711 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

The Tubthumpers returned to [[Varnhold]]’s blockhouse on Kuthona 15. The previous night’s battle had left the courtyard muddy, bloodied, and littered with the remains of spriggans and their enormous wolverines, but the timber fortress itself still stood sealed against them.

Having already discovered what happened when they crossed the courtyard at ground level, the company chose a subtler entrance. They climbed to the rooftop watch post and opened the hatch leading into the blockhouse from above. This was an excellent plan until the hatch opened.

The spriggans below heard them almost immediately.

What followed was less an assault than a battle rolling downhill. The Tubthumpers fought from the upper watch station into the second floor, then forced their way downward as more spriggans rallied from adjoining rooms. Doors became barricades, corridors became firing lanes, and every stairway offered the defenders another place from which to strike.

Spriggans are inconvenient enemies in confined buildings. They can swell from gnome-sized creatures into towering brutes in the space of a heartbeat, which makes them formidable but does not make the rooms any larger. The result was a violent confusion of crossbow bolts, splintered furniture, magic, and very large creatures attempting to occupy very little space.

The Tubthumpers continued floor by floor until the surviving defenders broke. Their leader, later identified as Agai, attempted to escape during the final fighting on the ground floor. He did not get far. By the time the battle ended, the Culcheck spriggans who had occupied Varnhold’s blockhouse were dead, and the company finally had the freedom to search the fortress properly.

The rooms told much the same story as the rest of Varnhold. The spriggans had found the village already deserted, then looted whatever caught their interest and destroyed much of what did not. They knew no more about the Vanishing than the Tubthumpers did.

Among the papers scattered through the former quarters of Cephal Lorentus, Varnhold’s master of dispatch, the party recovered five intact pages torn from his spellbook. They contained the spells *Translate*, *Floating Flame*, *Marvelous Mount*, *Peaceful Bubble*, and *Shrink Item*. Two more pages had been lost beyond recovery, which proves once again that barbarians never appreciate a good library until they need someone to identify the things they stole from it.

Agai’s spacious pouch held a small treasury: six hundred gold pieces in assorted coin and jewelry, a payroll coffer containing five hundred silver pieces, and gemstones worth approximately another three hundred and fifty gold. It also contained [[Maegar Varn]]’s enchanted longsword, marked with the crest of House Varn; a magical messenger’s ring; a wand of *Ghostly Carrier*; and an exquisite magical longbow.

The bow was named [[Artifacts and Relics/Skybolt|Skybolt]]. Its construction identified it as the work of Iobarian centaurs, and closer study revealed that it was not merely valuable but sacred—a lost ancestral weapon of the Nomen people. It was the first clue the Tubthumpers had found that offered not only another question, but a means of asking it without beginning a war.

A damaged map in Maegar Varn’s quarters showed enough of the surrounding country to guide the company toward the Nomen encampment. After resting from the assault, they carried Skybolt east into the grasslands.

The Nomen had ample reason to mistrust visitors walking on two legs. Human armies had driven their ancestors from their lands, settlers continued to press into their territory, and someone in Varnhold had quite recently been tanning centaur hides. The patrol that met the Tubthumpers therefore offered them caution rather than welcome.

Skybolt changed the conversation. The company returned the bow freely, and on Kuthona 17 they were escorted into a broad encampment of hide shelters gathered around a great central fire. There they received an audience with Aecora Silverfire, war-priestess of Mother Moon and leader of the Nomen.

Aecora accepted the recovered weapon with solemn gratitude. Returning it did not erase generations of violence between her people and ours, but it demonstrated that the Tubthumpers had come seeking answers rather than territory. That was enough for honesty.

The Nomen, she explained, had long kept watch against an ancient evil named Vordakai. Their traditions described him as a slumbering warlord from the distant past, but war and displacement had stripped away so much of their history that even Aecora could offer little more than warnings. His name was associated with Olah-Kakanket—the Valley of the Dead—and with a narrow trail leading from that forbidden valley into the mountains.

Recently, a Nomen hunter had seen something enormous moving among Olah-Kakanket’s stones. Aecora feared that Varnhold’s people might have entered the valley and awakened whatever her ancestors had sworn to watch. If so, their disappearance was not an attack by the Nomen. It was the consequence of disturbing something both peoples had forgotten how to fear.

The hunter who had seen the shape was Aecora’s daughter, Xamanthe. Headstrong, curious, and unsatisfied by the fragments of history her mother could provide, Xamanthe had disappeared several days earlier. Aecora believed she had broken the tribe’s oldest taboo and entered the Valley of the Dead alone.

“If my daughter still lives, bring her home,” Aecora told them. “If she does not, bring me the truth.”

The Tubthumpers departed with that request and reached Olah-Kakanket around noon on Kuthona 19. The valley was a bleak field of weathered stone, its ancient grave markers worn nearly smooth by ages of wind. Whatever names had once been carved there had outlived their mourners, their language, and almost the memory that anyone had been buried beneath them.

The dead, unfortunately, had not forgotten themselves.

Six skeletal hulks rose to meet the company—towering cyclopean remains large enough to make ordinary skeletons seem almost companionable. Weapons glanced from ancient bone, spells burned through empty ribs, and the creatures struck with the strength of giants who had refused to become less dangerous merely because their flesh was gone.

The Tubthumpers shattered all six. When the last hulk collapsed, broken bones lay scattered across the graves of a civilization so old that none of the victors could name it.

They were not the only witnesses. A raven had watched the battle from a distance with an attention no ordinary scavenger would have shown. When the Tubthumpers noticed it watching them, the bird immediately took flight and vanished into the valley.

A deserted village might be silent by accident. A bird that understands when it has been discovered is something else entirely.

The path to Vordakai lay ahead. By then, the Tubthumpers knew that whatever had taken Varnhold was aware of them—and had begun to watch in return.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
