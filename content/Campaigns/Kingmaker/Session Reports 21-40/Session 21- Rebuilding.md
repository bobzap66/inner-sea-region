### From [Linzi](../NPCs/Linzi.md)’s Journal

*An account of events from 13 Neth, 4710 AR until approximately 8 Abadius, 4711 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

The Tubthumpers left the owlbear’s lair on Neth 13, but they did not take the most direct road home. Instead, they turned toward [Lake Silverstep](../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Nomen%20Heights/Lake%20Silverstep.md) to fulfill a request from Beven Armaki, a travelling chef in search of the lake’s elusive eels. After trolls, drakes, hodags, and owlbears, a few quiet days of fishing must have sounded delightful.

For most of Neth 17 and 18, it was. They rested beside the water, enjoyed one another’s company, and waited for the eels to bite. Then something much larger bit back.

An elasmosaurus erupted from the lake and transformed their peaceful fishing trip into a desperate struggle along the shore. The company took to calling the creature “Nessie,” which is a remarkably affectionate name for something trying to eat you. They managed to drive her away without killing her and, perhaps more impressively, still caught the Silverstep eels they had come for.

The following day, a mated pair of manticores attacked them on the road. The beasts were killed with considerably less trouble than Nessie had caused, though the company’s attempt to harvest their valuable tail spikes ended in failure. There is apparently a delicate art to removing quills from a dead manticore. None of the Tubthumpers knew it.

They reached [Thumpington](../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Thumping%20Waters/Settlements/Thumpington/Thumpington.md) on Neth 22. Two days later, we held a celebration in [Ekundayo](../NPCs/Ekundayo.md)’s honor. I would like to record that the feast was magnificent, the speeches inspiring, and the entertainment unforgettable. Unfortunately, I have sworn to tell the truth. It was a thoroughly mediocre party.

The evening became more interesting when Ntavi, one of Ekundayo’s former companions, arrived. She plainly still carried a torch for him, though she expressed it less through tenderness than through provocation. Ntavi had found Gragglebeard, the hill giant responsible for the deaths of Ekundayo’s family, and she knew precisely which old wound to press.

By the following morning, Ekundayo was gone. Ntavi had successfully goaded him into pursuing Gragglebeard without waiting for the others. The Tubthumpers followed, and on Neth 27 they found him already engaged in battle against the giant and his allies.

Gragglebeard was an enormous hill giant clad in blue armor, powerful enough to make the old stone bridge beneath him seem almost fragile. The Tubthumpers joined Ekundayo in the fight. At its climax, Ekundayo placed an arrow exactly where it was needed. Gragglebeard crashed through the collapsing bridge and disappeared into the gorge below.

Vengeance did not give Ntavi the ending she expected. She asked Ekundayo to leave Thumpington and return to the wandering life they had once shared. He refused. In the midst of all our chaos, he had begun building something here—a purpose, a community, and perhaps even a home. Ntavi left in fury, alone once more.

The turn of the year brought us back to the slower work of repairing the damage left by the Beast of Thumpington. Roads, buildings, and bridges can be rebuilt with timber and stone. Trust requires rather more patient materials.

The council opened new discussions with the [Sootscale Kobolds](../../../Groups/Sootscale%20Kobolds.md) and persuaded them to turn away from the worship of Old Sharptooth. Faith cannot simply be ordered out of someone, so the task ahead is to help them find a spiritual path that does not demand quite so much fear, blood, and evil.

A bridge was also completed on the northern side of the Iruxi settlement then known as the Isle of the Lizard King. The island sits along a busy waterway, and with its people no longer inclined to treat every visitor as a potential meal, trade flourished almost immediately. Merchants arrived, goods changed hands, and the little settlement began to look like a future center of commerce.

Thus the season ended with one bridge collapsing beneath a tyrant and another rising beneath a growing community. I am tempted to call that symbolism. [Seamus](../Characters/Seamus.md) says writers are always doing that after the fact.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
