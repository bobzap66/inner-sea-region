### From [Linzi](../NPCs/Linzi.md)’s Journal

*An account of events on approximately Kuthona 20, 4711 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

The entrance to Vordakai’s tomb waited at the foot of the limestone isle, half-hidden behind creepers where the narrow beach met the cliff. The Tubthumpers had already crossed a valley of graves, survived six fiends sent specifically to murder them, and fought four wyverns in deep water to reach it. Naturally, the dark hole in the ancient stone was the inviting part.

The passage beyond was dry, lightless, and old beyond easy reckoning. Alcoves lined the walls, and a carved arch crossed the corridor beneath runes and cyclopean symbols. Ally determined that the arch had once carried a powerful ward, but its magic had already been discharged.

The dust explained how. Human footprints led from the beach to a broken amphora and then hurried back outside. Six sets of enormous skeletal tracks emerged from deeper in the tomb and followed him, never returning. The newest trail belonged to a centaur who had entered alone and continued onward.

The human tracks were almost certainly those of Willas Gundarson. By taking treasure from the amphora, he had awakened the tomb’s guardians and, quite possibly, Vordakai himself. Willas had escaped the island, but the consequences of his curiosity had followed him all the way back to [Varnhold](../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Thumping%20Waters/Settlements/Varnhold/Varnhold.md).

The broken vessel still held scattered silver and gold, together with another curved band of green stone like the one described in Maestro Pendrod’s notes. It was not a bracelet at all, but a ring made for the finger of a cyclops.

Beyond the shattered remains of an interior wall lay an enormous sepulcher. Its ancient stone biers stood empty, for the six skeletal hulks once buried there were the same creatures the Tubthumpers had destroyed in the Valley of the Dead.

Vordakai had replaced them.

Two leukodaemons guarded the chamber. The skull-faced fiends carried pestilence with them, one attacking from within the sepulcher while the other appeared behind the company and threatened to trap them between. Both bore wounds from a spear and a centaur’s hooves. Xamanthe had reached this room before them, fought fiercely, and lost.

The Tubthumpers killed both daemons and found a concealed door leading deeper into the isle.

Beyond it waited a vast natural cavern, its ceiling heavy with stalactites and its floor occupied by a black, thirty-foot-deep pool. A narrow shelf followed the cavern wall, while stairs descended beneath the murky water into a flooded passage.

The pool was not empty. A tremendous river elasmosaurus surged from the depths, its long neck striking across the ledge while its jaws sought to drag prey beneath the surface. The company fought it in the water and from the narrow stone shelf until the creature finally sank back into the darkness from which it had risen.

A search of the pool bottom uncovered the calcified remains of a much earlier adventurer. The dead traveler still clutched a wand of *Freedom of Movement*, and a ruined belt pouch nearby contained a jade serpent figurine endowed with its own magic. Both had waited beneath the water for a very long time for someone capable of surviving the retrieval.

The submerged stairs and tunnel brought the company into chambers filled with ancient cyclopean pottery. Painted scenes showed one-eyed people worshipping, fighting, competing in athletic contests, and warring against centaurs. Even within a tomb built by a monster, fragments of an entire forgotten civilization remained.

The next chamber was less interested in history than murder.

When the Tubthumpers opened a concealed door, iron portcullises crashed down and sealed the room. Twelve hidden openings in the ceiling released torrents drawn from the Little Sellen River, and the water began rising almost immediately. At the same moment, concealed alcoves opened and released two enormous cyclops zombies.

The company had to fight the undead while escaping a room rapidly becoming a lake. Some held the zombies away from their companions while others struggled against the trap and its sealed exits. Water climbed from their ankles to their knees and higher, churning around combatants too large, too angry, or too dead to care about drowning.

The zombies eventually fell, and the Tubthumpers forced open a way out before the chamber could fill completely. The released water thundered into the adjoining passage and left the company soaked, battered, and alive—which, under the circumstances, counts as an elegant solution.

Above the flooded level, they discovered a shrine devoted to Charon, the Horseman of Death and Boatman of the Styx. Animal-headed figures marched along a black river in the wall carvings, while three unlit lamps rested upon separate altars. Two offering cups bore the residue of blood spilled only days earlier.

Someone triggered the shrine’s defenses. Freezing black fire erupted through the chamber, burning flesh while clawing at memory and will. No one providing testimony has offered a sufficiently consistent account of precisely whose hand caused this, and I have learned that there are questions even a responsible historian should stop asking if she wants anyone to speak to her next time.

Past the shrine, a piscodaemon emerged in a burst of foul mist and demanded to know in whose name the intruders entered the Horseman’s inner sanctum. Whatever answer the Tubthumpers gave did not satisfy it. The resulting argument was brief, violent, and fatal to the daemon.

The chamber beyond was a prison. There, chained among ancient bloodstains, they found Xamanthe Silverfire.

Aecora’s daughter had been paralyzed by Vordakai’s touch and left starving and delirious. The Tubthumpers broke the curse, restored enough of her strength for her to speak, and learned what had happened after the leukodaemons defeated her.

Xamanthe remembered a tentacled fiend bringing just enough foul food to keep her alive. She remembered nightmares, despair, and a nearly skeletal cyclops with a brilliant red gem set into an empty eye socket. He had named himself Vordakai and promised to return when fear and pain had stripped away enough of her will that she would thank him for them.

She admitted that curiosity had led her to defy her mother and enter the forbidden valley alone. She also made it quite clear that, given another chance, she would probably do it again with better preparation. I suspect Aecora will find that confession simultaneously reassuring and infuriating.

The company suspended its exploration long enough to escort Xamanthe completely out of the tomb. Once she was safely beyond Vordakai’s prison, the Tubthumpers turned around and went back inside.

Their return brought them to a central crypt lined with empty burial alcoves. Four soul eaters floated among its pillars—another set of the same fiends that had invaded their camp the previous night, though these served as guardians rather than individual assassins.

The Tubthumpers destroyed all four. Only then did they stop to rest, surrounded by empty graves beneath an island dedicated to a dead empire’s worship of death.

They had found Xamanthe alive and confirmed that Vordakai was no forgotten legend. Somewhere farther above waited the people of Varnhold and the lich who had stolen them.

The Tubthumpers rested because they needed to. They remained because leaving Vordakai alive was no longer an option.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
