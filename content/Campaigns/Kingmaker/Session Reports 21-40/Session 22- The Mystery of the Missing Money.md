### From [Linzi](../NPCs/Linzi.md)’s Journal

*An account of events from approximately 8 Abadius until 31 Abadius, 4711 AR*

The Tubthumpers began the new year by closing several old accounts. Shamblesap was delivered, the head of Howl of the North Wind found its destination, Beven Armaki received his Silverstep eels, and the flame drake’s destruction was properly reported. The company sold what it did not need, improved its equipment, and spent several weeks practicing new skills and preparing for whatever would next attempt to kill them.

Jubilost used the quiet interval to continue his investigation into why gnomes left the First World for this one. His search had led him to a gnome named Xae, who had sought an invitation to something called the Inconsequent Debates. He promised to share what he learned if the Tubthumpers helped him follow the trail. With Jubilost, even the questions have footnotes.

[Valerie](../NPCs/Valerie.md) also spent time among us. She remains determined to be valued for something beyond her beauty, which is entirely reasonable, though the anonymous admirer who sent her an impractical gift appears not to have understood the assignment. She appreciates discipline, manners, and knowledge of warfare. She appreciates [Seamus](../Characters/Seamus.md) rather less, apparently because of his association with [Shelyn](../../../Rules/Deities/Shelyn.md).

Then the treasury accounts exposed me.

I had been taking money from the kingdom. Borrowing it, I told myself, although no one had authorized the loan and I had carefully arranged matters so no one would notice. I used the funds to purchase the components of a printing press and to hire [Eobald](../NPCs/Eobald.md), one of my former teachers in [Pitax](../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Thumping%20Waters/Settlements/Pitax/Pitax.md), to help us assemble and operate it.

I intended it as a surprise. Thumping Waters was growing, and I believed a press could give every settlement the same news, preserve our laws and stories, and place knowledge into the hands of people who had never owned a book. I also believed that everyone would forgive the missing money once they saw what I had built. This was not one of my more mature assumptions.

The final caravan never arrived. Worse, Eobald had been travelling with it. When I confessed what I had done, I could offer the Tubthumpers only the caravan’s route and the location from which I had received Eobald’s last message. They had every right to be furious. Instead, they gathered their equipment and came with me to find him.

On Abadius 31, we found the ambush site. Dead horses lay among the wreckage of a smashed wagon, and the caravan guards had been left where they fell. Six giant vultures, each nearly the size of a horse, were feeding upon the remains. For one dreadful moment I thought one of the bodies was Eobald.

Five of the vultures were killed. The sixth escaped, having demonstrated more practical judgment than most creatures that attack the Tubthumpers. Eobald was not among the dead, and enormous tracks led away from the wreckage.

We followed the ogre trail to the ruins of an abandoned settlement. The ogres occupying it were neither subtle nor clever, but they were numerous, strong, and entirely willing to fight. The Tubthumpers killed them and found the entrance to a much older structure hidden among the ruins.

Inside, lurkers in light concealed themselves within the supernatural illumination. When battle began, they called unicorns to their aid, because apparently even creatures associated with grace and purity can be recruited into an ambush if the magic is unpleasant enough. The Tubthumpers defeated the lurkers and finally found Eobald.

He was alive, barely. His captors had bound him and imprisoned him within a conjured cage of cold iron. Ally and Seamus briefly hoped to take a fragment for use in their spellcasting, but the cage vanished when the magic sustaining it ended. Even the evidence was determined to be inconvenient.

Eobald was safe, but the printing-press components were still missing. He remembered three terrible figures visiting him through the haze of his captivity and speaking of keeping him alive for an offering. Stairs at the far end of the chamber led below. Whatever the hags intended to do with my teacher, they had not yet finished preparing it.

The Tubthumpers descended into a cramped, unfinished basement reeking of smoke, spoiled meat, and stranger ingredients. A cauldron simmered over an impossible little fire. Nearby, bones, herbs, and pieces of my press had been heaped across a worktable, while a circle drawn in blood waited on the floor. The hags had not stolen Eobald and the machinery because they understood either of them. They meant to sacrifice him while calling something from the Abyss, and had kept the press parts only because they looked valuable.

There were three of them—the Jaggedbriar Coven, as my later research suggests—and they did not surrender their prisoner or their prizes. The Tubthumpers fought them around the cauldron and the unfinished ritual circle. When one hag fell, the others abandoned any thought of taking new captives and fought with all the fury of a broken coven. By the end, all three were dead and their ritual would never be completed.

The press components were filthy and in need of repair, but intact. We carried them out with Eobald and what remained of the caravan’s goods. He had lost his spellbook and very nearly his life, yet before we had even reached home he was already speaking about replacement type, corrected tolerances, and all the many errors he expected to find in my designs. I have never been so relieved to be criticized by a teacher.

I began this expedition ashamed of what I had done. I returned no less ashamed, but with Eobald alive, the press recoverable, and a better understanding of what trust costs when one has spent it carelessly. The heroes I deceived still chose to help me repair the damage. That is a kindness I intend to deserve—and, if the press works, one I intend to record properly.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
