### From Linzi’s Journal

*An account of events from 5 Abadius until approximately 15 Abadius, 4713 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

Having killed the spirit naga Ngara and freed one of her marsh-giant servants, the Tubthumpers continued through Hooktongue Slough toward M’botuu. Somewhere inside that boggard knot was Ka-Kekt, the missing son of Tok-Nikrat’s chieftain. Waiting for them as well was Sepoko, priest-king of M’botuu, servant of Gogunta, and recent supplier of sacrifices to the late and unlamented “Legless Lady.”

The sensible approach would have been reconnaissance from a safe distance.

Instead, Ally, Matteo, and Seamus disguised themselves as boggards and walked into the village claiming to be new recruits for Sepoko’s growing army. The guards accepted this explanation with gratifying ease. Mairi, Ant, and Fitzroy remained outside with the unenviable task of waiting to discover whether the plan had worked.

For a little while, it did.

Ally and Matteo investigated the settlement and its shrine to Gogunta while Seamus found a twenty-foot opening leading into the caverns beneath the village. Presented with the choice between finding a cautious route downward and dropping directly into an unknown subterranean chamber, he chose the route most consistent with his reputation.

He landed almost at Sepoko’s feet.

The priest-king asked several entirely reasonable questions. Seamus supplied several entirely unreasonable answers. Suspicion followed, then wardens, then flight. Unfortunately, the caverns beneath M’botuu were utterly dark. Boggards can see in darkness. Seamus cannot, though I am assured he ran through it with tremendous confidence.

He ran directly into the barracks.

Surrounded by M’botuu’s off-duty warriors and unable to see enough to invent a dignified escape, Seamus surrendered. I have been told that this was the wisest decision available to him. I have also been told that he looked very brave while making it, and I have chosen to believe both statements.

Elsewhere in the village, Ally located Ka-Kekt imprisoned inside a hut. She attempted to speak with him and, in the urgency of the moment, dropped her disguise in full view of the nearby sentries. This considerably improved Ka-Kekt’s understanding of the situation and considerably worsened everyone else’s.

The alarm went up. Matteo abandoned his investigation of the shrine, and he and Ally escaped M’botuu before the knot could close around them. They returned to camp without Seamus.

Mairi asked where her nephew was. The most accurate available answer was that he had fallen into a hole.

The following morning, Sepoko led Seamus and Ka-Kekt out of M’botuu under guard. Both prisoners were bound for the Swamp Scar as sacrifices to the Legless Lady. Whether Sepoko knew that Ngara was already dead is uncertain. If he did, the procession was an act of desperation. If he did not, he was marching two captives toward a mistress who would never answer him again.

The remaining Tubthumpers ambushed the procession.

The first warden fell as the party broke Sepoko’s escort and reached the prisoners. Seamus was freed in the middle of the fighting and joined Mairi against the priest-king. Sepoko’s confidence collapsed with his defenses. Wounded and terrified, he abandoned Gogunta and cried out to the Legless Lady for salvation.

Nothing answered.

The surviving warden heard Sepoko’s prayer and understood its meaning. To him, the priest-king’s appeal to another power was unforgivable heresy. He turned his weapon upon Sepoko and joined the attack against his own ruler.

Ant saw an armed boggard move in the confusion and loosed an arrow. There was no time for explanations, and no one had announced that the warden had changed sides. The shot struck before the boggard could complete his rebellion. It was not treachery, only the sort of mistake battles manufacture faster than anyone can correct them.

Mairi and Seamus finished Sepoko together. The priest-king of M’botuu died between aunt and nephew, abandoned by the goddess he had betrayed and the monster he had served.

Ka-Kekt was freed, but the work was not yet finished. The Tubthumpers returned to M’botuu intending to tell its people that Sepoko was dead, that his service to the Legless Lady had ended, and that the sacrifices demanded in her name would end with him.

The priest-king’s lieutenants were not persuaded. With no friendly witness to the battle and little reason to trust armed foreigners carrying news of their ruler’s death, they attacked. The Tubthumpers defeated them, and the remaining boggards fled into the Slough. By the end of the fighting, M’botuu had ceased to exist as an organized power.

Sepoko’s treasury contained coin gathered from years of raids and river tolls, along with black opals, a bracelet decorated with woodland animals, Taldan-stamped gold ingots, healing potions, and a magical harp bearing the likeness of Cayden Cailean. It was a respectable fortune for a ruler who had built his throne in the mud.

The return journey settled several promises made before the expedition began. Sepoko’s head was delivered to Garuum, who accepted proof that his old enemy was dead and surrendered his treasured “magic rock.” It proved to be a runestone capable of granting greater invisibility—considerably more useful than the name had suggested.

Ka-Kekt was restored to his people at Tok-Nikrat. The Bogstriders named the Tubthumpers friends of the bog and promised aid to travelers moving through the Slough. Their chieftain also shared warnings about the surrounding country: restless dead, giant slugs, deadly azure lilies, the ancient bridge and its fertile cloudberry fields, the haunted shore of Lake Hooktongue, a many-headed serpent in the southwest, and the enormous creature for which the lake itself was named.

The Bogstriders offered something greater as well. If Thumping Waters extended its protection over their home, Tok-Nikrat would join the republic willingly. It was an extraordinary offer from a people who had every reason to distrust outsiders and a welcome reminder that not every border must be drawn with blood.

Finally, the enormous dragonfly wings recovered earlier in the expedition were delivered to the Erastilian craftsman who had requested them. In payment, he presented the party with Charlton’s Cape, an enchanted garment suited to deception and sudden departures. After hearing how the infiltration of M’botuu had proceeded, I believe both properties may prove useful.

Thus ended M’botuu: not through a siege, but through three disguises, one ill-considered descent, a rescue, an ambush, a misunderstood defection, and the stubborn refusal to leave anyone behind.

Seamus insists that he did not fall into the hole and that the descent was intentional.

I have recorded his objection.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
