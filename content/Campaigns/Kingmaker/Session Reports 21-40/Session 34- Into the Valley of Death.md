### From [Linzi](../NPCs/Linzi.md)’s Journal

*An account of events from the night of Kuthona 19 through approximately Kuthona 20, 4711 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

The raven that watched the Tubthumpers destroy the skeletal guardians of Olah-Kakanket might have escaped without confirming anyone’s suspicions, had it behaved like an ordinary raven. Instead, it watched with altogether too much interest and fled the moment [Fitzroy](../Characters/Fitzroy.md) moved against it.

By then, there was little comfort left in coincidence. [Varnhold](../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Thumping%20Waters/Settlements/Varnhold/Varnhold.md) had vanished. Aecora Silverfire had named the ancient evil sleeping beyond the Valley of the Dead. Now something wearing a bird’s shape had watched the Tubthumpers fight and carried word of them deeper into the mountains.

The company made camp that night beneath the shelter of their Explorer’s Yurt. Fitzroy kept watch beside the fire while Wapati rested nearby and the others slept within. Walls of enchanted canvas are very useful against wind, rain, and snow. They are rather less useful against creatures that regard walls as a polite suggestion.

Six soul eaters entered the camp.

Each had been sent after a particular member of the company. They slipped through the walls of the yurt and fell upon their chosen victims almost at once, turning the cramped shelter into a confusion of claws, weapons, spells, and half-awake heroes attempting to find enough room to stand.

Fitzroy fought from outside while the others battled within and around the yurt. Antson’s arrows and Ally’s magic struck at shapes that seemed made more from malice than flesh. [Seamus](../Characters/Seamus.md) fought beside them with a swashbuckler’s daring, [Mairi](../Characters/Mairi.md) met the fiends with the fury of the wolf spirit, and [Matteo](../Characters/Matteo.md)’s bladed gauntlets gave the creatures something solid to regret.

The Tubthumpers destroyed all six.

Later study identified the attackers as conjured fiends called soul eaters. Such creatures are not wandering predators. They must be summoned, given specific prey, and sent hunting with magic capable of locating those targets across great distances. Whoever commanded them knew every member of the company well enough to assign each one a personal assassin.

This revelation, together with what they had learned in Varnhold and from the Nomen, led the company toward a particularly unpleasant conclusion: Vordakai was likely a lich, an immensely powerful spellcaster who had contrived to survive his own death. More importantly, he knew that the rulers of [Thumpington](../../../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Thumping%20Waters/Settlements/Thumpington/Thumpington.md) were coming for him.

The following morning, the Tubthumpers continued along the mountain trail until the land opened above a dark tarn fed by a plunging waterfall. In the center of the lake rose Vordakai’s Isle, a sheer limestone monolith nearly one hundred feet tall. Black smoke curled from vents near its summit, though no settlement, forge, or friendly chimney could be seen.

The path ended at the cliffs. The company secured ropes and descended more than a hundred feet to the water below, lowering weapons and packs where they could and trusting the knots with rather more than their dignity. At the shoreline, they entered the cold, dark water and began swimming toward the island.

They were roughly halfway across when four wyverns attacked.

The creatures struck while the company was spread across the lake, some still nearer the cliffs and others already approaching the island. Fighting a flying dragon is difficult. Fighting one while swimming is considerably worse, because the dragon has wings, claws, and a venomous tail while the hero has very pressing opinions about breathing.

The wyverns swooped, circled, and attacked from every side, forcing the Tubthumpers to fight wherever the water had caught them. Those able to use bows or magic fired across the lake while the others met the beasts whenever they came within reach. Wapati, deprived of any sensible footing, had as much cause as anyone to object to the arrangement.

Despite the terrible ground—or absence of it—the company killed all four wyverns.

Knowing that dragons seldom terrorize a mountain lake without accumulating something worth stealing, the Tubthumpers searched the creatures’ lair before continuing. Among the remains of less fortunate travelers they recovered old Ustalavic silverware, loose coin, several green spinels, a magical maul bearing an Issian coat of arms, and a scroll carrying the spell *Sending*.

By the end of Kuthona 20, the company had crossed the lake and reached the stony beach at the base of Vordakai’s Isle. Somewhere within that column of ancient limestone waited the fate of Varnhold, the missing Nomen hunter Xamanthe, and an enemy who had already learned all six of their names.

The Tubthumpers had crossed the Valley of the Dead and survived the welcome prepared for them. The only road left led inside.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
