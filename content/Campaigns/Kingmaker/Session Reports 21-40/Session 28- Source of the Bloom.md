### From [[Linzi]]’s Journal

*An account of events from Arodus 31 through Rova 2, 4711 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

There are few plans more likely to make a sensible person nervous than deliberately releasing a dangerous prisoner. The Tubthumpers, however, had already tried keeping hold of the goblin chief they believed to be Muglund, and he nearly escaped them yet again. After knocking him unconscious for the second time, they decided that a prisoner so determined to flee might prove more useful as a trail than as a source of answers.

They allowed him to escape and followed at a distance. Their quarry led them away from [[Greengripe]] and into a cavern so beautiful that it immediately became suspicious. Vivid flowers crowded the stone, luminous mushrooms cast strange colors across the walls, and vegetation unlike anything native to the [[Stolen Lands]] flourished without sun or season. It had all the abundance of the First World, but none of its joy. Beneath the perfume of the blossoms lay the damp stink of decay.

The people dwelling there soon dispelled any hope that the place was merely an unusually aggressive garden. They wore the symbols of [[Lamashtu]] and had filled the caverns with prayers to the Mother of Monsters. Their shrine showed three-eyed jackals hunting through a verdant wilderness, joining Abyssal worship to a twisted reverence for the First World. The connection the Tubthumpers had suspected was now carved plainly into the walls.

The cultists fought to protect their sanctuary. The company pressed through fungus-choked passages, beds of swelling bloom pods, and chambers where nearly every beautiful thing seemed prepared to burst, poison, bite, or pray for their deaths. The flowers proved no more innocent than the worshippers tending them.

Their fugitive finally abandoned his disguise. “Muglund” was Virthad, a doppelganger priest of Lamashtu who had murdered Greengripe’s true spiritual leader and taken his place. His purpose had been to make the goblins appear responsible for the Bloom, leaving the cult’s real work hidden while [[Thumpington]] wasted its strength punishing the wrong people.

Virthad was not the architect of the plot. That honor belonged to Darivan, the human priest who led the Cult of the Bloom. He confronted the Tubthumpers within the deeper caverns and very nearly killed [[Mairi]] during the battle. Whatever else may be said of Darivan, he was no pulpit-bound schemer who expected others to die for his convictions. He fought for them himself, and died beside Virthad and the remaining cultists who defended the Cradle.

Darivan had made his quarters inside an explorer’s yurt, a wonderfully practical piece of magic that the Tubthumpers recovered after his death. More importantly, they found his books, journals, and notes. The cult leader had been drawn to these caverns by visions of a place where the boundary between the mortal world and the First World had once been torn thin.

Such wounds are called breach scars. Most heal in time. This one had become infected with Abyssal corruption, and the taint prevented the scar from closing. Darivan regarded that ancient damage not as a danger to be mended, but as an opportunity. He used its mingled energies to devise the monstrous bloom ritual and spread his curse through the Stolen Lands.

His writings also revealed the weakness in his design. The power sustaining the curse had been gathered into a monstrous chimera called Akuzhail. Darivan boasted that the creature obeyed him when addressed in Abyssal, but admitted that the destruction of this “chimeric host” would ruin everything he had created. Kill Akuzhail, and the Bloom would lose the source from which it drew its strength.

There was one more witness beneath the earth. Beyond a wall Darivan had raised to conceal him, the Tubthumpers found the remains and lingering spirit of Jin Durwhimmer, a one-armed gnome druid of the Green Faith. Jin had discovered the corrupted breach years earlier and died when his attempt to heal it failed. Shame and unfinished purpose had bound his ghost to the caverns ever since.

Jin begged the company to destroy the Bloom and cleanse the scar. If they succeeded, he promised that before passing beyond he would entrust them with his knowledge of three powerful rites: primal call, commune with nature, and awaken animal. His account confirmed what Darivan’s notes had already suggested. The curse, the cult, and the wound between worlds had all been fastened to the same living anchor.

By Rova 2, the Tubthumpers had killed the minds behind the Cult of the Bloom and learned how their plague could be ended. Unfortunately, knowing which monster must die is not quite the same thing as killing it. Somewhere beyond the riot of flowers and luminous fungus waited Akuzhail—and within Akuzhail waited the Bloom of Lamashtu.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
