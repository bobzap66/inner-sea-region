### From [[Linzi]]’s Journal

*An account of events surrounding the Talon Peak expedition, from 17 Abadius until approximately 31 Abadius, 4714 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

The road home from M’botuu offered one final reminder that [[Locations/Inner Sea Region/River Kingdoms/Stolen Lands/Hooktongue Slough|Hooktongue Slough]] does not surrender its dead merely because the living have finished with them. Three bog mummies clawed their way from the mire and lurched toward the Tubthumpers, streaming black water and carrying the swamp’s accumulated perfume with them.

The creatures brought with them a drowning curse capable of filling a living person’s lungs with water. Fortunately, they were more alarming than difficult. The party cut them down, and the bodies returned to the mud with considerably less ceremony than they had used in leaving it.

After weeks of boggards, nagas, restless dead, and disagreements with the local wildlife, the Tubthumpers returned to the work for which no sword is sufficiently sharp: governing.

The following months were busy ones. Roads pushed farther through the republic, shops and inns opened their doors, and new houses rose where maps had once shown only wilderness. [[Tok-Nikrat]] was drawn more securely into the realm, its people connected to their neighbors without being asked to surrender what made their home their own. Trade reached toward distant Kyonin and Artume, and the republic’s settlements grew wealthier and more confident.

Not every difficulty could be solved with stone and lumber. [[Mairi]] and [[Seamus]] worked to ease quarrels between the goblins and their new neighbors. That peace required patience, listening, and the occasional firm reminder that citizenship carries obligations in both directions. By all accounts, Mairi supplied the firmness and Seamus supplied enough words for both of them.

I spent much of that time pursuing a project of my own. A proper printing press remained just beyond reach, but each craftsman consulted, each mechanism examined, and each coin set aside brought it nearer. Someday these accounts will not need to be copied one page at a time. I find the thought almost indecently exciting.

There were darker matters as well. Whispers reached the council of a secret cult devoted to Gyronna taking root somewhere within the republic: rumors of sacrifices, ill luck, and neighbors turning suspicious eyes upon one another. The Minister of Magic’s first attempt to contain the threat failed, and the resulting unease increased the republic’s unrest. The cult’s location and true strength remained unknown.

On a rainy morning on 17 Abadius, the Tubthumpers set aside ledgers and petitions and departed in search of an intact roc egg. The creature’s nest lay atop Talon Peak, amid the ruins of an old watchtower. Four stretches of the journey were easy enough, after which the land became steep, broken, and finally cruel enough to make every mile feel like three.

They reached the peak on the twenty-first. Wind tore across the summit, and the ruined tower clung to the mountain above drops no sensible person would approach willingly. They tried to approach the nest stealthily, but hiding from something accustomed to hunting from the clouds proved difficult. Three enormous eggs waited in the tower—and above it circled their mother.

A roc is not merely a large bird. It is a storm with feathers, talons large enough to close around a person, and the disconcerting habit of treating armed adventurers as portable meals.

It struck before the Tubthumpers could secure the nest. [[Matteo]] and [[Ant]] were snatched up and carried into the sky. I am told that the sight of Ant dangling from those talons was especially alarming, though his size may have made him somewhat easier for the roc to manage.

Ally and Mairi took to the air after it while Seamus fought from the broken tower below. The battle scattered itself across open sky, crumbling stone, and the narrow summit. Spells flashed through the clouds. Steel struck feather and flesh. The roc climbed, wheeled, and tore at anyone reckless enough to come within reach.

Mairi, flying with the wolf spirit plain upon her face, finally closed her jaws upon the creature and struck with such ferocity that the roc was knocked helplessly prone in midair. Before it could arrest its fall, gravity claimed it.

The roc fell.

Ant fell with it.

Mairi dove after him.

The roc was still alive as it fell, but the 250-foot impact killed it. Mairi reached the rocks below to find Ant broken and motionless, but not yet beyond help, still tangled in the dead creature’s claws. She stopped his bleeding and dragged him back from the edge. There are victories that look heroic from a distance. Up close, most of them look like someone kneeling in blood and refusing to let a friend die.

With the roc dead from its fall, the Tubthumpers claimed one of the three intact eggs. Each weighed approximately two hundred pounds, but a Shrink Item spell reduced their prize to a size that could be transported without requiring a wagon built for giants.

The remains of the roc’s previous victims yielded thirty loose gold pieces, a valuable gold ring, enchanted full plate, an enchanted scimitar, a Staff of Elemental Power, and a spacious pouch. Inside the pouch were twelve polished gremlin skulls. I cannot explain why anyone possessed twelve polished gremlin skulls, but I admire the decision to store them neatly.

The egg reached the republic intact and became the centerpiece of an omelet competition of wholly unnecessary magnificence. Cooks arrived with peppers, herbs, cheeses, sausages, and theories. The resulting celebration drew visitors, merchants, and an astonishing quantity of coin. The influx of resources helped establish a new luxury tavern without drawing upon the republic’s ordinary reserves.

It may be the first time in our history that knocking something out of the sky directly improved the hospitality industry.

Further administration followed. A mansion rose in [[Thumpington]], roads continued to spread, and new agreements bound the republic to friends beyond its borders. Then, with the government temporarily less likely to collapse in their absence, the Tubthumpers returned to Hooktongue Slough.

They first examined a region known as the Sinking Bog. The place proved to be a treacherous expanse of quicksand and slime, entirely unsuitable for construction. The surveyors’ recommendation can be summarized as follows: do not build there.

On 31 Abadius, the Tubthumpers reached Tok-Nikrat and began searching for the giant slugs reported by the bog striders. They had prepared for one or two.

They found a nest.

The enormous creatures surged through the vegetation, armored in slime and spitting streams of acid. The slough offered uncertain footing and few clean lines of attack. Seamus was battered badly, and Matteo was caught beneath the crushing weight of one of the beasts. For a time, the battle seemed less like fighting animals than resisting the swamp itself as it tried to swallow them.

The Tubthumpers rallied, discovering amid the chaos that the creatures were remarkably vulnerable to ordinary salt. Unfortunately, no one had brought any—a rather impressive oversight for people who had so recently organized the largest omelet competition in the republic’s history. The knowledge would be useful another day; for the moment, bladed gauntlets had to tear through rubbery flesh, arrows and spells had to find their marks, and Mairi had to meet the creatures at the distance she prefers. One by one, the slugs collapsed into their own corrosive trails until the nest was silent.

The party emerged wounded but successful and harvested twelve vials of giant-slug acid. When those vials were delivered to the alchemist who had posted the bounty, the Tubthumpers received four moderate elixirs of life and three greater alchemist’s fires—all of which must have seemed considerably more valuable after discovering how many giant slugs could occupy one patch of swamp.

Thus ended an account that began with drowned corpses, passed through months of roads and diplomacy, climbed to a battle in the clouds, celebrated victory with an omelet, and concluded waist-deep in acid-spitting slugs.

Some historians will insist that the growth of nations is a stately and orderly process.

Those historians have never met the Tubthumpers.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
