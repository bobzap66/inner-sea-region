### From [[Linzi]]’s Journal

*An account of events from Arodus 13 through Arodus 21, 4711 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

The hunt began with a wyvern, which is the sort of sentence that makes for a thrilling story only when one already knows the people in it survived. The creature descended upon the Tubthumpers in a fury of green scales, teeth, and venom. It was no ordinary wyvern, either. Strange flowers and thorny growths clung to its hide, and it seemed possessed of a cruelty beyond simple hunger. After a hard fight, the Tubthumpers killed it and claimed their first great victory of the hunt.

Its lair offered an explanation for some of that cruelty, though not a comforting one. The remains scattered there had been mutilated rather than eaten, as if the creature had killed merely for the pleasure of it. Beneath a carpet of vivid flowers lay the carcass of a bear whose body had burst open from within. The blossoms growing from it had neither soil nor sunlight, yet flourished all the same.

The next roars led the Tubthumpers to Edina and Jolia Aldori, who had found rather more hunt than they had bargained for. A many-headed hydra had the sisters fighting for their lives. The Tubthumpers joined the battle, overcame the beast’s remarkable ability to heal, and killed it before either Aldori sister became a permanent addition to the scenery.

Annamede Belavarah, meanwhile, had selected a high stone ledge from which to enjoy the wilderness at a safe and elegant distance. She had brought food, wine, music, and no apparent intention of allowing the hunt to interfere with her relaxation. Elsewhere, three Embeth Travelers had prepared an ambush for a krooth. Once the Tubthumpers confirmed that the hunters were neither missing nor in need of rescue, they courteously left them to their quarry.

The wolves proved more talkative than the hunters. Glazulin, a great white winter wolf, confronted the party at the head of his warg pack. He regarded Annamede’s camp as an intrusion and intended to remove it. [[Fitzroy]] persuaded him to wait while the Tubthumpers tried to convince her to leave peacefully, arguing that wolves and hunters might share the region without filling it with one another’s blood.

In return, Glazulin described something dreadful. Animals had begun exploding into masses of flowers, after which monsters crawled from their remains. He had witnessed an elk burst apart and release the hydra. The wyvern and other recent arrivals, he believed, had been born in much the same way. The Tubthumpers carried his warning to Annamede, who received it with considerably more skepticism than gratitude.

Across the river, [[Seamus]] discovered that the entire countryside was steeped in life-giving magic from the First World. The influence was strongest on that bank and so nourishing that animals scarcely needed to eat. This explained how so many great predators could occupy such a small territory without exhausting its prey, but it did not explain who had brought the magic there—or why.

The party avoided a territorial herd of enormous elk and followed the unnatural growth to a narrow ravine. There they found foul, rotting fruits unlike anything native to the [[Stolen Lands]]. Most of the plants were already [[Rules/Dying|Dying]], but one giant flytrap remained both alive and exceedingly hungry. It swallowed [[Mairi]] and [[Connal]] before Seamus put flame to it, and the Tubthumpers cut their companions free from the dead plant.

Beneath the ruined vegetation they found signs of a gathering. Careful examination showed that a supernatural divine ritual had been performed there sometime during the preceding month. Whatever had happened was no accident of the First World. Someone had done this deliberately.

By noon on Arodus 14, the Tubthumpers had won the hunt. They returned to the lodge that evening, where the surviving hunters compared trophies over roast krooth. [[Jamel Visser]] announced that the restored waystation would henceforth be known as Embeth Lodge. It should have been a triumphant ending.

Then Timol screamed.

Those present described him doubling over as something moved beneath his skin. His body ruptured in a snarl of vines and wildflowers, and from that terrible bloom crawled a basilisk. The assembled hunters fought together and killed the creature. Any celebration ended there, among overturned tables, terrified faces, and the remains of a man who had been alive moments before. Fortunately, they discovered that blood taken from a freshly slain basilisk could reverse its petrification if applied within the hour.

The investigation that followed supplied a name for the horror: the Bloom. Its victims were not transforming into monsters. A curse was opening a portal inside each victim, using the body’s destruction to drag some extraplanar horror into our world. These creatures killed from malice rather than hunger. Evidence pointed toward a cult operating somewhere in the wilderness, one that wished to tear down everything being built in the Stolen Lands and return the region to the dominion of monsters.

On Arodus 19, the Bloom struck [[Sootscale Valley]]. A kobold burst apart in the middle of the settlement, releasing a griffon into the streets. The Tubthumpers reached the valley the following day. Mairi’s ferocity left even the griffon shaken, and the party brought it down before it could claim still more lives.

They had scarcely caught their breath when [[Jubilost Narthropple]] arrived on the morning of Arodus 21. He had uncovered a lead concerning a missing friend and wanted the Tubthumpers beside him when he followed it. After the things they had witnessed during the preceding week, the arrival of a familiar gnome with an ordinary mystery must almost have seemed reassuring.

Almost.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication

They returned to warn Annamede, but she met them with obstinance and skepticism. Whether or not their warning took root, they couldn’t say, but there was no time to waste. They crossed the river, continuing their search for these grotesque, flower-covered remains. Nightfall would bring answers—or at least the screams of those who found them first.

Sean, attuned to the arcane, unraveled a mystery about the land itself. The region was steeped in First World magic, its life-giving energies ensuring that the creatures here felt no hunger, sustained as if by an invisible force. The very balance of nature had been upended, with predators forsaking their instincts, sustained without the need to hunt.

Continuing their trek, they stumbled upon a herd of giant elk, their aggression evident in the way they presented their massive horns. Recognizing a fight they did not need, the party wisely kept their distance. Along the way, they discovered decayed First World fruits, their properties too degraded to be of use.

A far greater challenge awaited them—a monstrous, carnivorous plant that wasted no time in attacking. In the chaos of battle, both Mairi and Connel were swallowed whole. Sean, with his flaming sword, incinerated the creature, freeing his comrades from its grasp. Once the battle ended, they uncovered something even more disturbing—a site where, within the past month, a supernatural divine ritual had been performed. The significance of the ritual remained a mystery, but its presence confirmed their suspicions: something was deeply, unnaturally wrong here.

Victorious from their trials, the party returned to the lodge on the evening of the 14th. The night brought some respite, with a feast of roast kooth. Yet Annamede, ever dismissive, scoffed at the notion of divine rituals shaping the wilds around them. As the evening closed, Jamel bestowed a name upon their haven—Embeth Lodge.

Timon, Jamel's servant, was taken suddenly. As his body exploded, a new horror emerged—freshly bloomed basilisk. If one were petrified by its gaze, the only cure was swift—fresh basilisk blood, applied within the hour of the creature’s death. But this was only the beginning.

The true nature of the horror plaguing the land came to light. People were not merely dying—they were exploding, their bodies giving way to monstrous beings that killed not for hunger, but for malice. A cult was at the heart of it, driven by a singular purpose: to return the [[Stolen Lands]] to the dominion of monsters. The curse they wielded was one of conjuration, tearing open portals within their victims, birthing horrors from another plane.

The 19th brought a dire event—to the north in [[Sootscale Valley]], a kobold was torn apart from within. From his body emerged a griffon, rampaging through the settlement. The party rode hard, reaching the valley on the 20th, prepared for battle. The griffon met its match in Mairi, who rattled the beast with a show of force, leaving it shaken before the party put it down.

The morning of the 21st brought an unexpected visitor—Jubilost. The gnome had been searching for them, bearing news of a lead on his missing friend. His urgency was clear. The party had survived another ordeal, but their journey was far from over.
