### From [[Linzi]]’s Journal

*An account of events from approximately 1 Pharast until 21 Desnus, 4711 AR*

Winter gives heroes a very different sort of enemy. Snow closes roads, rivers sulk beneath ice, and even the most dedicated monster slayer eventually discovers that a roof requires maintenance. Some of the Tubthumpers spent part of the season with their families. [[Seamus]], Ally, [[Mairi]], and [[Ant]] remained closely involved in the republic’s work, which meant fewer swords drawn but considerably more arguments about lumber, roads, and where one is permitted to build a stockyard.

The months were not wasted. [[Thumpington]] gained a general store and later a marketplace. [[Tatzlford]] built a stockyard and a lumberyard. [[Olegton]] added a marketplace, a mill, and a stone wall. The iruxi settlement was formally named [[Longtail Island]] and received a bridge, a smithy, and a mill of its own. The old [[Stag Lord]]’s fort was finally claimed in the republic’s name. It is pleasant to see a place once used to terrorize the countryside reduced to an item on a construction ledger.

Growth brings complications along with walls and workshops. Squatters settled upon land claimed by the republic, creating a problem that could not responsibly be solved by simply shouting, “We own that now.” Reports of a hidden cult also continued, though its members remained considerably better at hiding than our investigators were at finding them.

The most important diplomatic work concerned the [[Groups/Nomen Centaurs|Nomen Centaurs]]. Their people have observed a truce with the humans of the region since before either side kept written records. They had little interest in friendship for friendship’s sake and still less interest in watching settlers claim their lands, but our representatives respected their boundaries and spoke well. The centaurs agreed to peaceful relations and trade: not an embrace, perhaps, but a firm understanding that neither side need reach for a weapon.

By spring, the Tubthumpers were ready to exchange council chambers for open country. They left on 8 Desnus and reached the territory of the boggard we had heard about on the afternoon of the 9th. Seamus used a translation spell to speak with him, which was fortunate, as very few diplomatic manuals include the proper conjugation of a boggard croak.

His name was Garuum. He had once belonged to the boggard settlement of M’Botuu, far to the west in the deep swamp. According to Garuum, he attempted to seize leadership of his people, was condemned to death, became drunk, broke his manacles, and escaped. I cannot decide whether this is a tale of remarkable determination or an argument against leaving prisoners near alcohol.

Garuum had no wish to leave his chosen patch of swamp, but he welcomed the Tubthumpers within it. He warned them to be wary of M’Botuu and described several ruins nearby: a destroyed temple to the north and, farther west, a statue of an elk-horned man. His territory, he said, was rich in the remains of people who had once expected their buildings to last forever.

The party reached the statue on 11 Desnus and recognized the elk-horned figure as [[Erastil]]. Two enormous spiders had made their home nearby. The creatures attacked, the Tubthumpers killed them, and enough usable silk was harvested to satisfy two parts of a local weaver’s request. Three more such harvests would still be needed, a fact which did little to improve anyone’s enthusiasm for searching the undergrowth.

A sinkhole investigated on the 13th proved to contain nothing of interest. This is worth recording because explorers deserve occasional recognition for climbing into ominous holes and discovering only an ordinary hole.

On 16 Desnus, the Tubthumpers reached the cairn whose location Jubilost had passed along nearly a year before. Within it they recovered an old ring fashioned from green wood, a relic associated with the [[Groups/Tiger Lords|Tiger Lords]]. Time had outlived whoever was buried there, but it had not entirely erased the mark of that ancient Kellid tribe.

The journey home was mostly quiet until the 18th and 19th, when the party discovered a field of old spiked snares. Ant found the first in the traditional adventurer’s fashion—by being caught in it. The others located and disarmed six traps in all. They appeared to have been abandoned for some time, leaving no sign of the hunter, bandit, or other unpleasant person who had originally set them.

The Tubthumpers returned on 21 Desnus to find the republic larger than they had left it and still demanding attention. Roads had stretched, settlements had grown, and new neighbors had become something other than mysteries on the edge of a map. That is the strange rhythm of ruling the [[Stolen Lands]]: explore long enough and the unknown becomes part of your country; remain home long enough and your country creates entirely new unknowns for you.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
