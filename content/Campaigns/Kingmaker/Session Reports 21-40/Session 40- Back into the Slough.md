### From [[Linzi]]’s Journal

*An account of events in early Calistril, 4714 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

The Tubthumpers returned to [[Locations/Inner Sea Region/River Kingdoms/Stolen Lands/Hooktongue Slough|Hooktongue Slough]] with [[Amiri]] beside them. This was not another survey or bounty. Somewhere in or beyond the marshes were the last [[Survivors]] of the [[Groups/Six Bears|Six Bears]], the people among whom Amiri had grown up and from whom she had fled.

Amiri once summarized the difficulty plainly: “I like to stab and slash and kill things. And that’s not really a feminine pursuit. So I never really got along with them real well.”

The truth beneath that sentence was uglier. The Six Bears had imposed strict expectations upon their women, and Amiri had refused every one of them. Certain members of the tribe eventually sent her to confront a frost giant, expecting the giant to dispose of their problem. Amiri survived. She returned carrying the giant’s enormous sword and learned why she had been sent.

What followed drove her into exile and had been following her ever since.

The Slough presented a more immediate problem first. A twelve-headed hydra erupted from a stagnant pond and attacked with enough jaws to make counting them seem an irresponsible use of time.

Every head struck independently. [[Fitzroy]] caught the opening assault upon his shield, which came perilously close to shattering under the barrage. Amiri and [[Seamus]] met the creature in the water while the others attacked from whatever footing the swamp permitted.

At first, the Tubthumpers concentrated upon the heads. This proved discouraging. Wounds closed, severed flesh regenerated, and the hydra responded to every success by remaining distressingly full of teeth.

Seamus finally cut through one neck with a flaming stroke so precise that the blade severed and cauterized it in the same motion. The head did not return. Once the party understood that the creature could be denied its regeneration, they wore down its immense body. Ally’s final spell brought the hydra crashing into the pond.

Beneath the water lay one of its previous victims, an elven adventurer whose equipment had largely rotted beyond use. One weapon had survived: an enchanted longbow bearing a powerful Impactful rune, capable of striking with magical force and hurling a victim backward.

The journey also gave the party cause to examine Amiri’s oversized sword more carefully. Its unnatural ability to repair itself was not merely an unusual enchantment. The blade was a prison, and a living soul remained trapped within it. A freedom ritual might release that soul, but such magic lay far beyond anything the Tubthumpers could then perform. Amiri, for all her attachment to the weapon, was willing to see its prisoner freed.

Farther into the Slough, the party encountered a desperate band of Pitaxian clerics, merchants, and performers. They were hungry, limping, and frightened, and their account of flight from [[Pitax]] appeared sincere. The Tubthumpers escorted them through the marshes and directed them toward safety in Thumping Waters.

Their arrival did more than save lives. A republic that accepts people fleeing danger reminds its own citizens what their nation is supposed to be. Some of the unrest troubling the realm eased as word of the rescue spread.

Then came a smaller act of cruelty with consequences no less real.

[[Ant]] used divination to search for [[Nilak]], Amiri’s closest childhood friend. The spell succeeded. He saw that Nilak was alive.

The [[Artifacts and Relics/Oculus of Abaddon|Oculus of Abaddon]] had begun asserting its own will through him. It compelled Ant to look Amiri in the eyes and say that Nilak was dead and that the scrying had failed.

There was no advantage in the lie. It did not protect Ant, mislead an enemy, or advance any plan. Its only purpose was to wound Amiri. That pointlessness may be the most frightening thing about it.

Despite the false report, the Tubthumpers continued following the rumors until they found the Six Bears’ camp. Battle had already begun.

Only seven members of the tribe remained. Nilak, now their chieftain, had gathered the survivors behind a failing wall of ice while mohrgs and armored graveknights hammered their way toward them. The camp bore the evidence of earlier attacks: abandoned tents, dead horses, and far too much empty space for so few living people.

The Tubthumpers charged into the undead horde. These were not shambling corpses. They flanked, isolated targets, and supported one another with the discipline of soldiers. One graveknight carried an aura that clawed at divine magic itself, repeatedly interfering with Fitzroy’s attempts to heal the wounded.

The battle became a desperate one. Several of the Tubthumpers were brought down. Their animal companions suffered terrible wounds, though all survived. A number of the Six Bears’ ordinary horses were less fortunate.

Still, the wall held long enough. The Tubthumpers destroyed the last mohrg and broke the final graveknight, leaving all seven members of the Six Bears alive.

That should have been the beginning of a reconciliation.

Instead, Fitzroy accused the survivors of trespassing within the republic and hunting one of its citizens. Nilak denied that they had come for Amiri. The Six Bears had been fleeing south from a powerful ghost that followed them wherever they went, sending undead every few weeks and demanding the return of the blade Amiri had stolen.

Fitzroy continued to question their right to cross the border in pursuit of a “murderer.” Nilak’s exhaustion hardened into fury. She accused him of cowardice, rejected his claims of honor, and told him, “You dishonor the symbol of [[Erastil]] you wear on your breast.”

The bridge burned quickly after that. Nilak refused the party’s offer of aid and declared that the Six Bears would leave. Amiri had found her friend alive only to watch any hope of reunion collapse beneath accusations that should never have followed a rescue.

The Tubthumpers nevertheless followed the undead trail. It led to a shallow, stagnant pond and a desolate island of sodden cypress, tangled vines, and ankle-deep mud. There the spirit hunting the Six Bears waited with the last of its graveknight and mohrg servants.

The ghost was named Fionn. He cared little for Amiri herself, but he recognized her sword immediately and addressed it as his brother.

The soul imprisoned in the blade was Kean. Both brothers had come from the First World, and both had been exiled from it. Fionn believed that consuming Kean’s soul would reverse his curse and permit him to return. Whatever grief or betrayal had once joined them had curdled into an obsession strong enough to slaughter nearly an entire people.

The Tubthumpers refused to surrender the sword.

Fionn answered with supernatural cold and waves of void energy while his undead minions attacked at his command. The struggle on the island was every bit as desperate as the defense of the Six Bears’ camp, fought through mud, freezing air, and the grasping remains of soldiers dead for centuries.

The party destroyed Fionn’s servants and finally broke the ghost’s manifestation. It was a victory, but not an ending. Fionn would return. Now that he had seen the blade, he could continue hunting it—and Amiri—until Kean was released.

The first step would be to contact the prisoner. Nilak knew of a ritual called Call Spirit, but the Tubthumpers would need to find a sufficiently accomplished druid or priest to teach or perform it. Calling Kean might reveal another path. Otherwise, freeing him would require a freedom ritual beyond their present reach.

So the Tubthumpers left Spirit’s Isle carrying a sword with one brother trapped inside it and the promise that another would rise again to claim him.

Amiri had entered the Slough hoping to find what remained of her old family. She found Nilak alive, learned that the rest were nearly gone, and watched seven exhausted survivors walk away from an offered home.

Some wounds are made by hydras. Some by ghosts. Some by old traditions, careless accusations, or a single lie spoken by a friend who no longer entirely commands his own voice.

The last kind may be the hardest to cauterize.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
