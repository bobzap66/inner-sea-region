### From Linzi’s Journal

*An account of the year of peace following Vordakai’s fall and of the expedition from 2 Abadius until approximately 4 Abadius, 4713 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

For one full year after Vordakai’s defeat, the Tubthumpers did not go looking for another tomb, monster, cult, or ancient curse.

This has been described as a year of peace. As Minister of Communication, I must object. Peace suggests rest. What followed was twelve months of petitions, road surveys, trade negotiations, construction reports, budget arguments, public ceremonies, and meetings whose greatest achievement was scheduling the next meeting.

The rulers of Thumping Waters gave themselves wholly to the work of governing. Varnhold was formally welcomed into the republic and began the long recovery from its vanishing. New roads bound Thumpington more closely to Olegton, Embeth Halls, Sootscale Valley, and Tatzlford. Walls rose around several settlements, farmland expanded, and new quarries helped supply the stone demanded by a growing nation.

Jubilost and Antson expanded the republic’s commercial agreements, while formal trade was established with Daggermark. No one involved in those negotiations has provided me with an account fit for innocent readers, which I consider proof that the agreement was successful.

Greengripe also supplied the republic with a new military force: Nok-Nok’s Irregulars, better known as the Greengripe Bombardiers. They were enthusiastic, unusually well supplied with explosives, and only inconsistently aware of the difference between those two qualities.

Their recruitment contributed to some unrest among goblins who feared that the republic valued them chiefly for their willingness to stand near lit fuses. Mairi confronted the complaints directly, listened to the grievances, and settled the matter without anyone being thrown through a wall. This was generally considered a diplomatic triumph.

By the end of the year, the kingdom had grown stronger, safer, and considerably better connected. The work was worthwhile. It was also enough to remind the Tubthumpers why they had once preferred being attacked by trolls.

They began adventuring again shortly after the new year.

The list of unfinished business had grown while they governed. The bog striders of Tok-Nikrat reported that Ka-Kekt, the leader’s son, had disappeared near the boggard settlement of M’botuu. Garuum, an exile from that same boggard knot, wanted revenge upon its priest-king, Sepoko, and offered a magic stone in payment for his death.

Other requests were less personal but no less hazardous. A craftsman needed the wings of enormous dragonflies. Someone else required a dozen vials of acid from giant slugs. A roc egg was wanted intact, which is the sort of qualification people add when they will not personally be climbing into a roc’s nest.

The various reports from Hooktongue Slough suggested that these errands might connect. Sepoko was attempting to unite the boggards into an army against the growing number of outsiders entering the marsh. His followers still worshipped Gogunta, the many-headed demon goddess of amphibians, but captives from rival boggard communities were being taken elsewhere for sacrifice. Their destination was a tangled place known as the Swamp Scar.

On 2 Abadius, the Tubthumpers passed through Tatzlford and collected another western rumor. Baron Hannis Drelev had reportedly taken a beautiful young lover, and his wife was not only aware of the affair but furious about it. At the time, this seemed like the sort of scandal that belonged safely on the other side of the Slough.

The first danger they found was considerably larger and faster than gossip.

A marshy glade ahead glittered with hundreds of dragonfly wings. Most belonged to harmless insects, but four enormous specimens with sixteen-foot wingspans descended upon the party. Their mandibles could seize a person, transfer the victim into their hooked legs, and carry them into the air.

The Tubthumpers fought among shallow pools, fallen logs, and cypress roots while the dragonflies darted through their formation. Arrows and spells followed creatures that seldom remained in the same place long enough to be struck, while those fighting at close quarters tried to avoid being snatched from the ground.

All four enormous dragonflies were killed. Their wings were harvested with considerably more care than the battle had allowed, providing the materials needed for the promised commission.

Farther into the Slough, the company found tracks left by marsh giants. Such giants are generally solitary, intelligent, and more interested in finding food than starting wars, making it suspicious that two had traveled together toward the same place as a trail of boggards.

The tracks led to the Swamp Scar, an immense ridge of fallen trees, tangled roots, and decaying vegetation piled by years of shifting currents. Burrows and narrow passages honeycombed the mound. Humanoid bones lay among the debris, including the remains of boggards sent there as offerings.

The creature accepting those offerings was a spirit naga named Ngara.

Ngara preferred servants to corpses. She had spent years charming travelers and sending them back into the marsh to bring her new victims. Sepoko himself had fallen under her influence and had begun supplying the naga with captives taken from rival boggard communities.

When the Tubthumpers entered her hidden garden, Ngara welcomed them warmly and invited them to remain. They declined. She expressed her disappointment by attacking their minds.

Ngara seized control of Mairi and turned her against the others. It was the second time in recent memory that an enemy had forced Mairi to threaten the people she would ordinarily die to protect, and the experience was no easier for repetition.

Ally broke the naga’s hold before Mairi could do lasting harm. She then discovered that Ngara’s two marsh-giant guardians were not willing servants either. Both had been inveigled so thoroughly that they fought to protect the creature enslaving them.

The battle filled the Scar’s enclosed garden. The giants waded through the strange vegetation with clubs raised while Ngara slipped among roots and stones, weaving compulsions and illusions. The Tubthumpers had to defend themselves without forgetting that three of the people attacking them were victims.

Ally broke the enchantment on one of the giants. The other remained under Ngara’s control and was killed before it could be freed.

Ngara died soon afterward. With the naga’s influence gone, the surviving giant departed in peace, shaken by the discovery of how thoroughly its will had been stolen.

The boggard bones scattered through the Scar confirmed what the rumors had suggested. Sepoko had been sending prisoners to Ngara, perhaps without fully understanding the power directing him. Ka-Kekt might be among the captives still held at M’botuu, if he had not already been sent west as another meal.

The Tubthumpers therefore continued deeper into Hooktongue Slough. Their first expedition in a year had already brought giant insects, an enslaving serpent, two dominated giants, and a scandal involving Baron Drelev.

M’botuu waited ahead.

So much for easing back into adventuring.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
