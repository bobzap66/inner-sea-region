### From Linzi’s Journal

*An account of events beginning approximately Kuthona 21, 4711 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

Resting in Vordakai’s tomb did not make it a safer place. It merely gave the Tubthumpers enough strength to continue climbing toward whatever waited above them.

A crooked stair brought them into a cavern dominated by a lake of bubbling black tar. Sulfurous steam stung their eyes and throats, while narrow shelves of stone offered the only safe footing around the pool. Waiting upon the far ledge was Cephal Lorentus, once Maegar Varn’s wizard and master of dispatch.

Vordakai had murdered him and then made continued service part of the punishment. Cephal’s flesh was blackened, his robes hung in tatters, and his broken neck left his head lolling at an impossible angle. Enough of the wizard remained to cast spells. Nothing remained that could be reasoned with.

Cephal took to the air and fought from above the tar, hurling magic across the cavern while trying to drive his former countrymen’s rescuers into the scalding pool. The Tubthumpers endured the heat, the fumes, and the dead wizard’s sorcery until they brought him down. Whatever portion of Cephal Lorentus had remained imprisoned inside the thing was finally released.

Beyond the cavern, they found a hidden route climbing to an opening high above the beach. It offered another entrance to the cairn, though one inconveniently placed for anyone unable to fly or survive a sixty-foot fall.

Another concealed chamber bore carvings of a hand marked with a central eye and surrounded by still more staring eyes. The meaning was unclear, but by then no one doubted that the sensation of being watched within the tomb had been entirely justified.

Deeper within the crypts lay the body of Willas Gundarson. His curiosity had awakened Vordakai and doomed Varnhold, but he had recovered from the lich’s compulsion in time to understand what he had done. Armed with little more than a hunting knife, he had made his last stand among the opened graves.

Guilt and terror had not allowed Willas to rest. His shade rose against the Tubthumpers with all memory of his former life burned away, leaving only despair and hatred for the living. They destroyed the wraith and granted Willas the peace he had been unable to find for himself.

The company rested again in the dead-end crypt. Beyond it, they found the reason Willas had broken free of Vordakai’s call.

A great stone table filled the next hall, surrounded by thirty-three murdered citizens of Varnhold arranged in a parody of a feast. The tops of their skulls had been removed and their brains taken. Maegar Varn sat at the head of the table. Caspar Morgarion, priest of Erastil and Maegar’s longtime companion, was among the dead beside him.

Vordakai had not devoured them from hunger. The lich consumed the memories of his victims, learning about the world that had arisen during his long sleep by tearing knowledge from the people he murdered.

Four cyclops zombies guarded the feast. The Tubthumpers met the hulking dead between the pillars and tables and destroyed all four. No triumph followed. There are victories that leave no room for cheering.

A balcony above the feast hall led into chambers older than the tomb itself. One contained a sulfurous pool whose surface erupted as an elemental tsunami surged from below. The creature struck with the force of a collapsing wave, battering the company through water and stone until they finally broke the magic sustaining it.

Beyond the guardian lay an octagonal chamber built from slabs of pale crystal. Its domed ceiling formed an inverted eye staring down at a circle carved into the floor. Cyclopean symbols covered the walls, and cold light seemed to seep from the stone itself.

Ally determined that the chamber was a wound in the world: a place where the boundary between Golarion and Abaddon had worn dangerously thin. It was here that Vordakai had first discovered the Oculus of Abaddon, and here that its influence had helped turn an already cruel ruler into something far worse.

They could not destroy the chamber. They could only continue through it and confront the creature who had made it his sanctuary.

Vordakai awaited them upon a throne made from bones. A red pool lay before him, surrounded by the mutilated remains of his most recent victims. The Oculus burned within one of the lich’s empty sockets as he studied the intruders who had survived every guardian placed between him and the outside world.

He offered them a place beside him. Submit, serve him, and help reduce the civilizations of the new age to ruin—or die with the people they had come to save.

The Tubthumpers refused.

Vordakai answered by turning their own strength against them. His magic seized Mairi’s mind and drove her to attack her companions. Connal, unable to understand why the woman he trusted had suddenly changed sides, followed her into the violence. Another spell overwhelmed Matteo and sent him fleeing from the tomb.

For a terrible span of moments, the company fought Vordakai, Mairi, and their own fear at once. They refused to abandon Mairi or answer the lich’s control with killing blows. Their voices and magic finally broke his hold, bringing Mairi and Connal back to themselves while the others kept the lich from finishing what he had begun.

Once united again, the Tubthumpers overwhelmed him. Vordakai’s spells failed, his ancient body broke apart, and the ruler who had terrorized the Nomen for generations collapsed among the remains of his stolen kingdom.

The Oculus of Abaddon remained.

The crimson gem promised power to anyone willing to look into it for too long. Antson Crazyfoot had spent years fighting beside heroes armed with great magic, divine favor, and destinies that announced themselves with considerably more ceremony than his own. Whatever the Oculus showed him, it convinced him that its power could be made useful.

Before anyone could stop him, Ant drew a blade and cut out his own eye.

He forced the Oculus into the bleeding socket. The artifact fused itself into his flesh as though it had been waiting for him. When Ant looked up again, one eye belonged to the halfling his friends knew. The other burned with the light of Abaddon.

Ant insisted that he remained himself. His companions had little choice but to believe him for the moment, though none mistook the Oculus for a harmless prize.

Behind Vordakai’s sanctum, they found the Dungeon of Souls. Forty-two glass jars stood within niches in the walls, each containing a plume of pale light and the stolen soul of a Varnhold citizen. Opening the jars released the prisoners and restored their bodies as they had been when Vordakai captured them.

Among those restored was Maestro Ervil Pendrod, the scholar whose disappearance had first drawn attention to Varnhold’s fate. The survivors emerged confused and frightened, then learned how many of their neighbors—including Maegar Varn—would never return with them.

Grief did not diminish their gratitude. The rescued citizens pledged their loyalty to Thumping Waters, trusting the nation’s rulers to rebuild what Vordakai had broken and protect the lives he had returned.

The Tubthumpers also brought Xamanthe Silverfire home to the Nomen. Aecora received her daughter with tears, relief, and what I am reliably informed was a look promising a truly historic argument once guests were no longer present. In gratitude for Xamanthe’s rescue and Vordakai’s destruction, the centaurs presented the company with Skybolt, an ancestral enchanted bow of their people.

Even the journey home refused to be entirely uneventful. The company located several giant spider lairs, destroyed their occupants, and harvested enough silk to complete a long-delayed commission for clawed bracers. Other finished investigations brought additional rewards, including payment for the recovered cyclopean tablets and equipment suited to further occult research.

Vordakai’s defeat did not restore Varnhold overnight. Homes stood empty, fields had gone untended, and every returned citizen carried the memory of waking in a glass prison after friends and family had vanished forever. Rebuilding would require roads, supplies, patient government, and a great deal of time.

Those labors waited beyond the journey home. For the moment, the Tubthumpers returned with forty-two rescued citizens, the friendship of the Nomen, an ancient bow, and a halfling wearing a piece of Abaddon where his eye had once been.

Vordakai had meant to make the people of Varnhold into a library for a dead empire. Instead, their rescue gave them another chance to choose their own future.

That seems the better use of memory.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
