# Matteo

![](../../../assets/images/matteo-portrait.png)

### From the Journal of Linzi

Matteo Auditore scares me. There, I said it. Not in the “he’s going to stab me” kind of way—though, honestly, I wouldn’t put it past him if he thought it necessary—but in the “he already knows exactly how and why he’d do it” sort of way. He’s terrifyingly practical, the kind of man who can look at a battlefield—or a person—and instantly decide which pieces are worth saving. It’s not cruelty. It’s calculation. Cold, efficient, and utterly without apology.

He doesn’t yell, doesn’t posture, doesn’t even seem to feel angry. When Matteo kills, it’s with the same calm he’d use to write a letter. He makes plans inside of plans, and sometimes I catch him looking at us all like he’s weighing odds rather than faces. There’s no comfort in his competence—it’s too sharp, too deliberate. You trust Matteo not because you want to, but because you know if he says he’ll get something done, it’s already been done. Probably twice.

Still… I think there’s something human buried under all that steel and shadow. He’s not cruel for fun, and he never hurts without reason. Maybe that’s what’s so frightening—he doesn’t kill out of passion or rage, but out of necessity. And if Matteo Auditore ever decides that you’re necessary? Then, gods help you, he’ll protect you with the same cold precision he uses to end his enemies. I respect him. I even like him, sometimes. But no matter how long I live, I don’t think I’ll ever be comfortable in a room alone with him.
