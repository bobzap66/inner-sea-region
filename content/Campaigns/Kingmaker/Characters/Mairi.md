# Mairi

![[mairi-portrait.png]]

### From the Journal of Linzi

Mairi Blackwood was the first Prime Minister of Thumping Waters—the warrior who carved a republic out of the wilderness with her blade and her will. She didn’t begin as a stateswoman; her diplomacy was more “bastard sword in one hand, handshake in the other.” But she learned quickly. I like to think I helped with that—teaching her how to smile through an insult, how to let silence sting louder than shouting, and how to turn stubbornness into strategy. Mairi never became polished, but she became something far better: authentic. When she spoke, she spoke truth, and somehow that was enough to make people believe in her—and in themselves.

She ruled the way she fought: fierce, loyal, and utterly fearless. Through trolls, cults, and crises, she carried Thumping Waters on her shoulders without ever letting the weight show. Yet, even in victory, there was a kind of loneliness in her eyes, as if she feared setting the sword down meant she’d lose her purpose. That’s why I think her husband, Euan—the quiet herbalist with gentle hands and deaf ears—was so important to her. He grounded her, softened her edges, reminded her there was still peace worth fighting for. He stayed behind in Thumpington when she left, still tending his herbs and his silence, waiting for the day she might come home.

When Mairi departed—taking Amiri’s cursed sword south to the Seven Arches to free the spirit bound within—she left behind more than an empty chair at the council table. She left a nation shaped in her image: proud, bold, and a little rough around the edges. She’s no longer our Prime Minister, but she’ll always be the heart of Thumping Waters. I like to imagine her out there even now, Connel the great Irish wolfhound at her side, laughing in the face of danger, her sword gleaming in the dawn. And someday, when she returns, I hope she’ll find we’ve kept the fire burning just as she taught us to.
