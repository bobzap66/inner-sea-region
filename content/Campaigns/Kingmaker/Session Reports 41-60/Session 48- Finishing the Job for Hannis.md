> ### RESTRICTED INTELLIGENCE REPORT
>
> *For council circulation only — Not for publication*
>
> *Compiled by the Ministry of Communication*
>
> #### Lake Transport Capability
>
> The cutter purchased at Fort Drelev can be reduced to portable size through the use of a wand and restored at the water’s edge. This gives the Tubthumpers an unusual ability to deploy a lake-capable vessel without sailing it from an established harbor.
>
> Public reporting describes the cutter’s usefulness but deliberately withholds the means by which it can be transported. The wand and vessel should be secured separately when practical, and knowledge of their operation should remain limited.
>
> #### Recovered Equipment
>
> The ahuizotl’s lair yielded the Guise of the Smirking Devil, a magical mask that strengthens the wearer’s ability to intimidate and can release a burst of void energy. A shocking magical longbow and two enchanted viper arrows were also recovered.
>
> The chimera lair yielded additional magical objects, including a wand of lightning bolt. Public accounts acknowledge recovered equipment without identifying its capabilities or current bearer.
>
> #### Reconnaissance Methods
>
> Antson Crazyfoot obtained useful information about the chimeras’ movements by questioning the local vegetation. This capability allowed the company to enter the lair while the pride was absent and prepare the battlefield before the creatures returned.
>
> The public account attributes the advantage to careful reconnaissance. Antson’s ability to gather information from plants should not be disclosed unnecessarily, particularly in territory where an enemy might otherwise overlook surrounding vegetation as a security concern.
>
> #### Condition of the Company
>
> Speartooth seized and mauled Velka, badly damaging her armor before Antson delivered the killing shot. The company relied upon magical concealment, summoned allies, and concentrated attacks during the engagement.
>
> The aurumvorax battle caused more serious losses. Cinder was mauled nearly to death, Velka’s already-damaged armor failed completely, and Seamus was grappled and repeatedly raked. Chain lightning and regenerative magic were essential to the victory.
>
> These details have been removed from public reporting. Replacement or repair of Velka’s armor should be prioritized, and the company’s enemies should not be permitted an accurate assessment of its present vulnerabilities.
>
> #### Recovered Hoard
>
> The aurumvorax burrows contained a large quantity of silver, a golden lion figurine, and a particularly keen magical spear. The public edition reports only that silver and enchanted objects were recovered.
>
> A complete inventory should be maintained by the Ministry of Treasury. Public disclosure of exact quantities or properties would encourage theft and provide hostile observers with unnecessary insight into the republic’s resources.
>
> #### Jeffrey and the Cloudberries
>
> The surviving marsh giant has accepted the name Jeffrey, agreed to cease eating humanoids, and been left as provisional guardian of the cloudberry patch. The arrangement is public, but reports should avoid publishing directions to the site or estimates of its commercial value.
>
> Jeffrey’s reliability remains untested. Periodic inspection is recommended before farmers, merchants, or gatherers are encouraged to work near him.
>

## SPEARTOOTH SLAIN IN THE WESTERN HILLS

*Antson Crazyfoot brings down the legendary smilodon after a savage battle at its lair.*

*By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent • Additional reporting by Linzi*

---

The hills west of Lake Hooktongue are quieter today. Speartooth, the great smilodon blamed for missing travelers, slaughtered livestock, and enough frightened stories to earn a name of its own, has been killed by the Tubthumpers.

The hunt formed part of a wider expedition through the Hooktongue country during the closing days of Desnus. By its conclusion, the company had destroyed several persistent threats, opened new routes across the lake, and appointed a marsh giant to protect a patch of cloudberries.

The last result requires slightly more explanation than the others.

*Speartooth clears the hunters’ traps in an artist’s reconstruction of the celebrated hunt.*

**FIELD REPORT**

**Speartooth:** Killed; bounty proof recovered

**Ahuizotl:** Killed; lair cleared

**Chimera Pride:** Destroyed

**Marsh Giants:** Three killed; one surrendered

**Aurumvoraxes:** Six killed; burrowed hill reclaimed

**Lake Transport:** Cutter acquired for future expeditions

#### Inside This Edition

**A Cutter for the Lake**

A newly acquired vessel promises safer and more reliable passage across Lake Hooktongue.

**Jeffrey, Provisionally**

A surrendered marsh giant receives a name, a warning, and an unexpected civic responsibility.

#### Recovered from the Wilds

**Guise of the Smirking Devil**

An enchanted silver grin recovered from an ahuizotl’s collection of trophies.

**The Hill Reclaimed**

Aurumvorax burrows yield silver, enchanted objects, and several compelling reasons to distrust small golden animals.

### A LANTERN LIT, A LEDGER OPENED

*By Mara Venn, Managing Editor*

![](../../../assets/images/screenshots/Session%2048%20-%20Finishing%20the%20Job%20for%20Hannis/session-48-finishing-the-job-for-hannis-45.webp)

*Linzi examines the first edition as press workers begin the inaugural printing of The Lantern and Ledger.*

With this edition, the first regular newspaper printed in Thumping Waters begins publication.

The Lantern and Ledger was founded by Linzi, Minister of Communication, following the completion of Thumpington’s new printing press. Until now, accounts of the republic’s expeditions have survived principally in Linzi’s journals, official records, and the frequently contradictory stories of those who returned alive. The new press will allow those accounts—and the affairs of the growing nation around them—to reach the public directly.

Linzi will serve as editor. Mara Venn will manage the paper’s daily operation and coverage of government, law, and public works. Tobin Bramblefoot will report upon games, festivals, and society; Sella Copperclaw upon trade and civic affairs; and traveling correspondent Darya Holt upon the borders, distant settlements, war, and the hazards between them.

Future editions will include public notices, council decisions, reports from the settlements, correspondence from readers, and corrections when this paper gets something wrong. We anticipate that last department will be used rarely, though the space has been reserved.

Copies of The Lantern and Ledger are offered for one copper piece. Readers are encouraged to pass each edition onward once finished, provided they do not use it to wrap fish until everyone else has had an opportunity to read it.

### TWENTY-TWO INCHES OF PROOF

*By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent*

High Marshal Valerie’s standing bounty placed two thousand gold pieces upon Speartooth’s immense head. The creature had preyed upon travelers and livestock throughout the western hills, leaving behind tracks, carcasses, and a pair of upper fangs measuring nearly twenty-two inches apiece.

The hunters prepared a line of traps at the smilodon’s lair. Speartooth struck the first, recognized the danger, and leapt cleanly over the rest. A plan may work exactly once and still be considered useful.

The beast landed among the hunters and immediately demonstrated why it had acquired both a name and a price. A savage close-quarters battle followed before the Tubthumpers brought it down.

> It hit the first trap. Then it looked at the others and decided it had learned enough.
>
> — A witness to the hunt

Antson Crazyfoot delivered the killing shot. The company removed Speartooth’s enormous fangs as proof for the High Marshal and carried them home to collect the bounty.

The hills are quieter for it.

---

### A CUTTER FOR THE LAKE

*By Sella Copperclaw, Civic Reporter*

The expedition began on Desnus 18 with an Umbral Journey to Fort Drelev, where the company purchased a cutter for travel upon Lake Hooktongue.

The vessel will support exploration, patrols, and transport along the lake’s difficult shores. Officials have declined to publish details of its storage or deployment, beyond confirming that it can be moved where needed without requiring an overland shipyard.

The Ministry of Communication nevertheless recommends that nobody misplace it. Replacing a missing ship would involve paperwork, negotiations, and an unacceptable amount of explaining.

### THE SMIRKING DEVIL

*By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent*

The company destroyed an ahuizotl that had relied upon deception to survive far longer than it deserved. Its lair contained a collection of trophies whose decorative value depended heavily upon one’s tolerance for skulls.

Among them were a boggard skull set with silver and moonstones, a dwarven skull retaining seven gold teeth, and a human skull wearing an ice-blue mask fashioned into a wicked silver grin.

The mask, now called the Guise of the Smirking Devil, proved magical. It and several other useful objects were recovered for safekeeping. Their precise properties will not be printed for the convenience of future enemies.

---

### LIGHTNING IN THE LAIR

*By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent*

Further exploration led the company into territory claimed by a pride of chimeras. Careful reconnaissance allowed the Tubthumpers to confront the creatures at their lair rather than in open country.

When the chimeras returned, Ally Grainger’s lightning tore through the pride before it could organize a defense. One creature attempted to escape, but did not travel far.

Several magical objects were recovered from the lair and returned to Thumping Waters.

---

### JEFFREY, PROVISIONALLY

*By Sella Copperclaw, Civic Reporter*

![](../../../assets/images/screenshots/Session%2048%20-%20Finishing%20the%20Job%20for%20Hannis/session-48-finishing-the-job-for-hannis-46.webp)

*“Jeffrey,” provisionally employed in the defense of the republic’s cloudberries.*

In a fertile region rich with cloudberries, the Tubthumpers encountered four marsh giants. Three were killed in the fighting. The fourth surrendered.

During the ensuing discussion, someone called the survivor “Jeffrey.” The name was intended more as a joke than a formal act of state. The Tubthumpers nevertheless accepted it immediately, and Jeffrey has answered to it ever since.

Rather than execute or exile him, the company ordered Jeffrey to stop eating humanoids and appointed him provisional guardian of the cloudberry patch. Jeffrey accepted both conditions.

The berries remain intact. Their long-term productivity is uncertain. A marsh giant has been given clear instructions, considerable responsibility, and very little supervision. Further reports will follow as circumstances demand.

### THE HILL RECLAIMED

*By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent*

The expedition concluded at the burrow-riddled hill where aurumvoraxes had previously defeated the Tubthumpers and nearly killed several rescued nobles.

Six of the small golden predators attacked. Their size should not be mistaken for fragility. An aurumvorax possesses eight powerful legs, a remarkably dense body, and the disposition of a bear trap that has developed personal ambitions.

The renewed battle was exceptionally severe, but the Tubthumpers killed all six creatures and finally secured the hill.

A substantial quantity of silver and several enchanted objects were recovered from the burrows. An inventory has been submitted to the government, where it can be read by authorized officials and anyone capable of enduring the relevant forms.

---

### FROM THE EDITOR’S DESK

*By Linzi, Editor and Minister of Communication*

You are holding the first edition of The Lantern and Ledger.

For years, I have kept the story of Thumping Waters in journals: the roads traveled, the monsters defeated, the mistakes survived, and the people who built a country where maps once offered only uncertainty. A journal remembers. A newspaper must do something more. It must carry that memory into kitchens, workshops, council chambers, guardhouses, and every settlement whose people deserve to know what is being done in their name.

This paper will publish news, notices, public records, correspondence, corrections, arguments, celebrations, and—when absolutely unavoidable—the results of children’s boat races. It will praise courage without pretending courage makes anyone infallible. It will question power even when the people holding it are my friends.

The lantern is for seeing clearly. The ledger is for keeping the account.

We intend to do both.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
