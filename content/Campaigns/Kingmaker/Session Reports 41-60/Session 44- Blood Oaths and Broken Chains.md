### From [[Linzi]]’s Journal

*An account of events in late Calistril, 4714 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

Removing a tyrant is difficult. Deciding what should replace him is harder. The Tubthumpers began the days following [[Hannis Drelev]]’s death by attempting both governance and diplomacy, then sensibly returned to a problem that could be solved with swords.

They met with Imeckus Stroon, Lady Pavetta’s brother and the cleverer mind behind much of [[Fort Drelev]]’s government. Despite [[Velka]] having driven his sister from the kingdom, Imeckus was willing to negotiate. His loyalty extended chiefly to Pavetta’s safety, status, and standard of living; the late baron inspired considerably less devotion.

An arrangement began to take shape. Lord [[Terrion Numesti]] would serve as the fort’s principal adviser and, in practice, direct its recovery. Fort Drelev would move toward an alliance with—and perhaps eventual incorporation into—Thumping Waters. In exchange, Pavetta would be kept safe and allowed some measure of the comfort she considered indispensable.

Imeckus also provided the information the party most needed. [[Armag the Twice-Born]] and a company of elite [[Groups/Tiger Lords|Tiger Lords]] had established a camp in a box canyon north of the fort. There they were excavating an ancient burial place, and among their hostages was Terrion’s daughter Tamary.

The Tubthumpers rode north to recover her. Smoke rising from the canyon revealed the Tiger Lord camp, and the party initially approached openly in the hope that the hostage might be released through negotiation.

“We can’t kill animals. Only humans,” [[Fitzroy]] observed while the party considered its principles.

“Only humans,” Antson agreed.

Velka clarified the matter: “Only sentient creatures would die by my blade. I want to hear them say no.”

The Tiger Lords said no with axes.

The warriors guarding the canyon were fanatics defending a sacred undertaking. They shouted a warning and charged without allowing diplomacy to begin. When still more barbarians emerged from the tents, the Tubthumpers withdrew, regrouped beyond the canyon, and prepared for the battle they had been denied any means of avoiding.

They returned under layers of protective magic. Ally opened the assault with an arrow salvo that tore through nearly half the enemy force. The Tiger Lords fought with terrifying intensity, growing more dangerous as they were wounded and concentrating their fury upon individual targets. One warrior killed [[Seamus]]’s warhorse with a single blow.

Fitzroy and Velka held the front line. Seamus moved through the press of warriors, Antson chose his targets from range, and Ally followed her opening attack with chain lightning and blazing bolts. The canyon became a killing ground. By the end of the battle, all twelve Tiger Lord fanatics lay dead.

Inside the camp, the party found five noble hostages confined in a cage. One was [[Tamary Numesti]]. She was overjoyed to learn that Kisandra had escaped and sent the Tubthumpers to find her. Tamary and the other captives confirmed that Hannis Drelev had surrendered them to the Tiger Lords as tribute for his peace.

They also described another captive company. The Tiger Lords had forced those adventurers into the nearby tomb one at a time. Each day fewer returned, until none remained.

Four bloodstained suits of enchanted full plate hung from a fence as trophies. Ally recognized them as the armor of the Iron Wraiths, another chartered company present at Lady [[Jamandi Aldori]]’s feast years before. Their signature possessions hung beside the armor: a vitalizing composite shortbow, a horn of blasting, a shocking warhammer, and a returning trident.

The equipment had been left outside. Its owners had not returned to claim it.

The Tubthumpers moved Tamary and the other liberated hostages into their magical yurt outside the canyon, where they could shelter while the company investigated the tomb.

Its antechamber was guarded by two shrouded priestesses of Gyronna, goddess of hags, hatred, and spite. Seamus had just suggested, “We could try and get the drop on her,” when the guardians rendered the question academic by attacking.

The priestesses filled the chamber with hostile divine magic. Repulsion divided the battlefield. Divine wrath sickened the company. One summoned a shadow demon, while another protected herself within a globe that swallowed lesser spells before they could reach her. Confusion briefly turned Fitzroy against his own companions, and he struck Velka while his mind was not his own.

Velka was eventually knocked unconscious and came perilously close to death. The others held together, battered through the priestesses’ protections, and destroyed the summoned creature. As one guardian lay [[Rules/Dying|Dying]], Fitzroy promised, “I curse you from the grave.”

“You sooner than I,” she answered.

She was mistaken.

Both priestesses died, and the entrance to Armag’s Tomb passed into the Tubthumpers’ hands. Behind them waited five rescued hostages and the abandoned armor of four dead heroes. Ahead waited the trials that had killed them—and somewhere beyond those, Armag the Twice-Born.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
