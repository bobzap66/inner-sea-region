> ### Republic Chronicle — Restricted
>
> *Compiled by Linzi, Minister of Communication. Not for public dissemination.*
>
> #### Western Border Engagement
>
> **Enemy force:** Two Ozthooms capable of producing multiple shadow doubles.
>
> **Outcome:** Both original creatures destroyed. All doubles dissolved immediately upon the originals’ deaths. No permanent casualties reported.
>
> The Ozthooms approached without speech or audible signal. Each produced false copies that could move and attack independently, creating the appearance of a substantially larger force. Fitzroy determined that the doubles depended upon the survival of their originals. Antson Crazyfoot identified cold iron as an effective weakness and destroyed one original with a cold-iron arrow.
>
> **Assessment:** When encountering similar creatures, identify and concentrate attacks upon the original bodies. Cold-iron weapons should be issued to future survey parties operating near the Thousand Voices.
>
> #### Letters and the Knotted Darkwood
>
> Seamus received a letter bearing personal knowledge consistent with his father, Mikhail, followed by a taunting communication attributed to Reyoknar. The first letter requested that Seamus return to the knotted darkwood tree beside the burned remains of his childhood home.
>
> A box tied to the tree with Mikhail’s distinctive firewood knot contained a whetstone, a black key, a strip of leather marked by Volantris, a raven feather dusted with pale powder, and a fresh letter. Magical examination indicated that the letter had been written under coercion by a frightened man surrounded by cold stone and old smoke.
>
> The ancient tree reported that the box had been placed two days earlier by a presence larger than the tree itself. It described the presence as feeling like “not life.”
>
> The marked leather ordered surviving Volantris operatives to destroy a compromised station’s records and withdraw along a smuggling route known as the Sable Road. The directions pointed toward a disused limestone quarry.
>
> #### Burning Volantris Safehouse
>
> The party reached the station while Volantris agents were burning the building and destroying records. A courier attempted to escape with documents. Seamus entered the structure and rescued the chained bookkeeper Marek Tane. Fitzroy removed an iron-bound chest. Ally Grainger used Dancing Fountain to extinguish a burning records cart.
>
> One courier escaped while wounded. Seamus overtook and nonlethally subdued a second fleeing operative. Any surviving prisoner should be held separately from Marek and questioned before Volantris becomes aware of precisely which records were recovered.
>
> **Recovered material:** Mikhail’s unsent proof-of-life letters, Volantris safehouse lists, centuries of coded correspondence, the Charleton dossier, and an enormous scale belonging to an exceptionally old umbral dragon.
>
> #### Marek Tane’s Testimony
>
> Marek confirmed that Volantris kept Mikhail alive as leverage over Seamus. False leads were released when Seamus’s search slowed. Contradictory evidence appeared whenever he approached the truth. Selected targets were placed along his path so that his pursuit of Reyoknar repeatedly advanced Volantris objectives.
>
> > A dead hostage can frighten someone once, but a living one can write a letter every year.
> >
> > — Marek Tane
>
> Marek described Seamus as having been, without his knowledge, “the best member of Volantris” because of the number of operations his manipulated pursuit had served.
>
> **Assessment:** Every person, organization, and crime connected to a prior Reyoknar lead must be reviewed. Some enemies Seamus fought may have been genuine Volantris assets; others may have been disposable targets selected for elimination. No earlier lead should be treated as reliable merely because it produced a real enemy.
>
> The Charleton dossier and safehouse lists may identify additional prisoners or people being prepared as leverage. Marek’s information also raises concern for Benjin’s surviving family, including his daughter. Their location and safety should be established discreetly.
>
> #### Stranger’s Clearing
>
> Mikhail was found restrained upon a raised platform inside a shadow-laced execution harness consisting of restraints, tubes, poison, and mechanical components. The device was designed to kill him after Seamus arrived, forcing Seamus to choose between pursuing Reyoknar and remaining to save his father.
>
> Volantris agents defended the platform. An obsidian mirror provided a remote link through which a Reyoknar projection observed and addressed the party. The dragon responsible was never physically present in the clearing.
>
> Ally used Knock to force part of the mechanism. Elowyn first attempted to purge the poison, then redirected divine power into her mace and shattered a critical tube before the apparatus completed its work. Fitzroy and Matteo removed the remaining restraints while the others held back the Volantris agents.
>
> **Outcome:** Mikhail rescued alive. Execution device disabled. Local Volantris force defeated. Obsidian mirror fractured. Remote projection terminated. No route to the projection’s origin remained.
>
> #### Identity Behind Reyoknar
>
> When the execution failed, the Reyoknar projection stretched, fractured, and unfolded into the image of a colossal umbral dragon. The scale recovered from the safehouse matched the nature and apparent age of the projected creature.
>
> Mikhail identified the dragon as Ilthuliak. Reyoknar was never a separate human commander. The name and appearance were a persona used by Ilthuliak to manage agents, manipulate Seamus, and conceal the intelligence directing Volantris operations.
>
> Ilthuliak is also connected to the destruction of the tribe of Mairi’s mother and to the black scale previously recovered beside the remains of an ancient silver dragon. The precise chronology of those events remains uncertain, but the repeated evidence indicates that the dragon has operated across generations.
>
> **Threat assessment:** Ilthuliak is an ancient umbral dragon with a long-established intelligence network, access to remote projection or communication through obsidian mirrors, and a demonstrated preference for manipulating captives and false identities rather than exposing herself directly. She should be treated as a strategic threat rather than a conventional monster.
>
> **Recommended actions:**
>
> 1. Decode the Charleton dossier and prioritize any living captives or threatened relatives.
>
> 2. Compare the recovered safehouse lists against known Volantris activity, prior Reyoknar leads, and unexplained disappearances.
>
> 3. Research obsidian mirrors capable of carrying projections across great distances. Do not activate or transport recovered fragments without magical containment.
>
> 4. Review the black scale and the ancient silver dragon remains with Ilthuliak’s identity in mind.
>
> 5. Provide Mikhail secure quarters, medical care, and protection from observation or extraction attempts. Questioning should proceed only as his condition permits.
>
> 6. Restrict public reporting to Mikhail’s rescue and the fact that Reyoknar was a false identity. Do not publish Ilthuliak’s name, Volantris routes, surviving operatives, safehouse locations, dossier contents, or the mirror’s capabilities.
>
> *Editor’s private note: Seamus spent years believing that finding Reyoknar would explain what had been taken from him. Instead, he found that even the name of his enemy had been chosen to make him useful. Mikhail is alive. For now, that must be the part we hold onto.*

## VICTORY, VOWS, AND A FATHER RETURNED

*The Republic celebrates peace, [[Seamus]] and Linzi announce their engagement, and a journey through fire brings Mikhail home after years in captivity.*

*By Mara Venn, Managing Editor • Additional reporting by Darya Holt and Sella Copperclaw*

---

The Victory Festival was intended to mark an ending.

[[Pitax]] had been defeated. The armies were home. For the first time in months, the streets of Thumpington filled with music instead of casualty reports, and the only companies marching through the capital carried instruments.

Before the festival ended, Seamus had asked Linzi to marry him. She said yes.

Before the lamps had gone cold, however, Seamus received a letter from the father he had believed lost for years. What followed carried the Tubthumpers from a burned childhood home to a criminal safehouse in flames and, at last, to the place where Mikhail was being held.

Mikhail survived. He has returned with his son.

The festival marked an ending after all—only not the one anyone expected.

### SHADOWS OVER THE WESTERN BORDER

*By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent • From the testimony of those present*

The western border of the Republic does not end at a gate or milestone. Roads become tracks, tracks become deer paths, and the trees grow older until the Thousand Voices begins without announcing itself.

The forest was once called the Branthlend, although few now use that name. Travelers know it instead for the murmurs that follow beneath its boughs. Some voices mock. Others grieve. None reveal their speakers.

The Tubthumpers had spent more than two weeks surveying this unsettled frontier when two winged creatures descended from the darkening sky. They resembled dragons stripped down to bone, claw, and shadow. Fire burned within their hollow chests and spilled through their teeth, but they approached without challenge, cry, or warning.

The creatures produced shadow doubles as they attacked, rapidly turning two enemies into a crowded aerial assault. [[Fitzroy]] Fulgrim III recognized that the imitations depended upon the originals, while Antson Crazyfoot identified the creatures’ vulnerability to cold iron.

Once the deception was understood, the ministers concentrated their attacks upon the true creatures. Seamus destroyed one. Antson’s cold-iron arrow brought down the other. Their shadow doubles vanished with them.

The creatures have been identified as Ozthooms, predatory fey associated with places where shadow and the First World press close against the mortal world. Their appearance provides further evidence that the western boundary deserves continued study and considerable caution.

> The forest offered no thanks. Its whispers continued long after the fighting ended.
>
> — Darya Holt

### THE REPUBLIC CELEBRATES

*By Sella Copperclaw, Trade and Civic Affairs Correspondent*

The Victory Festival of 6 Gozran brought citizens, veterans, performers, and visitors from across the Republic into Thumpington. It honored those who fought the war, those who supplied it, those who endured it, and those who did not return from it.

Banners crossed the streets. Food stalls crowded the squares. The Red Crescent Troupe supplied music, spectacle, and precisely the sort of cheerful disorder that causes ministers of the treasury to develop headaches and newspaper reporters to sharpen their pencils.

Asmeranda Ilata joined the visiting performers and dignitaries. Riverbend Park opened to the public, offering the capital a place built for rest rather than defense. The newly completed Greengripe Amphitheatre received its formal dedication during the festivities.

#### A Memorial Among the Living

The amphitheatre is named in honor of Nok-Nok, Governor of Greengripe, and the members of Nok-Nok’s Irregulars who fell during the war with Pitax.

The Irregulars were not parade-ground soldiers. They were goblins, bombardiers, scouts, [[Survivors]], and citizens who stood between the enemy and their homes. Their names will now be preserved in a place made for music, laughter, and public gathering.

> May every cheer raised within the Greengripe Amphitheatre remind us that the freedom to celebrate was purchased by those willing to stand in its defense.
>
> — Memorial dedication

#### The Return of Leroy

The loudest welcome of the festival belonged to an old friend.

Leroy—king, rooster, musician, and a creature for whom ordinary titles have never been entirely sufficient—returned to the stage. Age now shows upon him, though witnesses report that he retains the bearing of a monarch and the instincts of a performer who knows precisely how long to hold an audience in silence.

His music carried the joy that has always followed him, tempered by the understanding that no performer, not even Leroy, can argue with time forever.

> May the lamps keep on burning long after I’m gone.
>
> — Leroy

They will.

#### A Personal Entry

There are occasions when an editor must acknowledge that she has become part of the news.

During the festival, Seamus proposed to Linzi. Those close enough to hear report that she accepted without requesting revisions, an editorial process, or a second draft.

The Lantern and Ledger has confirmed that its editor remains capable of performing her duties, although the managing editor has exercised unusual authority over this particular article.

> He asked. I said yes. Some details should belong to the people who lived them before they belong to history.
>
> — Linzi, Editor

### NEW DOORS OPEN IN [[Willowfen]]

*By Sella Copperclaw, Trade and Civic Affairs Correspondent*

The festival was not the Republic’s only sign of recovery. [[Willowfen]] has completed a marketplace and welcomed several new establishments: the Empty Purse tavern, Meemaw’s Medicinal Tonics, and the memorably named [[Melianse]]’s Revenge lumberyard.

The Empty Purse’s proprietor insists that the name describes the condition in which satisfied customers leave competing taverns. The Ledger has not independently verified this explanation.

Together with Riverbend Park and the Greengripe Amphitheatre in Thumpington, the projects represent a welcome change in public priorities. During the war, nearly every new structure was expected to stop an army, house an army, supply an army, or explain why an army had not yet been stopped.

The Republic is building places for trade, medicine, drink, music, and idleness again. This is what peace looks like before anyone has time to paint it.

### THE PAST REACHES FOR [[Seamus]]

*By Mara Venn, Managing Editor • Details withheld where publication could endanger an active investigation*

The Victory Festival had barely ended when Seamus received two letters. One appeared to have been written by his father, Mikhail, whose uncertain fate had driven him for years. The second invoked Reyoknar, the enemy Seamus believed responsible for the destruction of his childhood home and the disappearance of those he loved.

The timing was plainly deliberate. The sender waited until Seamus had chosen a future and then placed his oldest wound directly before him.

Before he left Thumpington, Linzi asked him for a promise.

> Say it to me again when you get back.
>
> — Linzi

The Tubthumpers accompanied Seamus to the remains of his childhood home, where an old knot, a hidden box, and a letter written under coercion showed that Mikhail had survived. The clues also pointed toward a criminal safehouse whose occupants were already destroying their records.

### EVIDENCE FROM THE FIRE

*By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent*

The ministers reached the safehouse to find it burning. Agents moved through the structure destroying documents. A cart of records burned outside, a courier fled with additional papers, a prisoner remained chained on an upper floor, and an iron-bound chest sat amid the heat and smoke.

The fire presented three demands at once: save the captive, preserve the evidence, and prevent the agents from escaping.

Seamus entered the building, opened the captive’s restraints, and carried him clear as the structure approached collapse. The rescued man has been identified as Marek Tane, a bookkeeper compelled to work for the organization responsible for Mikhail’s captivity.

Fitzroy recovered the iron-bound chest. Ally Grainger used a dancing fountain of magical water to extinguish the burning cart and preserve records the agents had intended to erase. Not every enemy was captured, but enough evidence survived to lead the ministers onward.

The contents of the recovered records remain part of an active investigation and will not be reproduced here. The Lantern and Ledger can report that they confirmed years of deliberate deception and provided the location where Mikhail was being held.

### THERE IS NO REYOKNAR

*By Mara Venn, Managing Editor*

The trail ended at an abandoned logging camp known as Stranger’s Clearing.

Mikhail was alive but restrained, and armed agents stood between him and freedom. The device holding him had been constructed to turn his rescue into another instrument of cruelty. The Tubthumpers refused the choice prepared for them. While some held back the agents, others dismantled the restraints and brought Mikhail free.

The precise means by which the captors observed and addressed Seamus are being withheld. So are the records and identities recovered during the operation. Those details concern an active threat whose reach extends beyond the Republic.

What can be entered into the public record is this: Reyoknar was not the man Seamus had spent years pursuing. The name was a mask used to direct his grief, anger, and hope toward ends chosen by someone else.

Once Mikhail was safe, he gave his son the truth that had been denied to him.

> There is no Reyoknar. He’s gone. He never existed.
>
> — Mikhail

The architects of the deception remain beyond the Republic’s reach. Their agents, prisoners, and secrets have not all been found.

But Mikhail is alive.

He returned to Thumpington with his son.

### FROM THE EDITOR’S DESK

*By Linzi, Editor and Minister of Communication*

A newspaper prefers endings. They fit neatly beneath headlines.

The war ends. The festival begins. The hero proposes. The missing father comes home. The villain is named, confronted, and defeated before the final column.

Life has shown remarkably little respect for our preferred arrangement.

The war left graves that still require names. Leroy sang as beautifully as ever, and everyone listening understood why that beauty hurt. Seamus asked me to share his future only moments before his past demanded him back. Mikhail is home, but years taken from a family cannot be restored by crossing a border.

Nor was the enemy at the end of Seamus’s road the enemy he had imagined. A name he carried for years has dissolved, leaving a greater danger behind it.

That danger will have its day in these pages when printing what we know no longer threatens the people still searching for it.

For today, I will record what certainty permits.

The lamps burned. Leroy played. Seamus asked. I said yes.

He left. He returned.

And he did not return alone.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
