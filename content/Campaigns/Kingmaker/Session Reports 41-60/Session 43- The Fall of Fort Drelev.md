### From [[Linzi]]’s Journal

*An account of events beginning on 26 Calistril, 4714 AR, reconstructed from the testimony of those present*

The Tubthumpers have carried the war to [[Fort Drelev]]—not yet with armies, but with a ring, a flower, a few well-chosen lies, and one remarkably ill-chosen fireball.

Before setting out, the council attended to the republic itself. New construction continued across Thumping Waters, roads and defenses were strengthened, and the nation’s armies were prepared for the conflict now gathering along its western frontier. The work was less dramatic than fighting trolls at a river crossing, but nations are defended as much with stone, grain, and careful accounts as they are with swords.

The Tubthumpers then traveled west through [[Tok-Nikrat]] toward Fort Drelev. Near sunset, they discovered a group of chuuls occupying a cavern beside the water. The creatures had hunted travelers there for some time and decorated their lair with trophies—and rather tasteless images—of their victims.

The chuuls were dangerous, amphibious things with powerful claws, but Ally ended much of the argument with a chain of lightning that tore through their ranks. After killing the creatures, the company recovered several valuables from the remains of their victims, including boots enchanted to lengthen the wearer’s stride, a silver-capped quarterstaff carved with birds of prey, and an iron medallion carrying magic against fear.

They reached Fort Drelev at dusk and found its gates guarded, its streets subdued, and its people living beneath a curfew enforced by mercenaries. The settlement was founded before [[Thumpington]], yet neglect and oppression had hollowed it out. Shops stood closed. The stables had been stripped of horses for the [[Groups/Tiger Lords|Tiger Lords]]. The great temple of [[Erastil]] remained unfinished—an enormous monument to all that Fort Drelev might have become and had not.

At the Bellwether Soup Kettle, guards ate without paying while the family running it struggled to survive. The Iron Steeple Inn stood nearly abandoned, its windows broken, its shutters hanging crooked, and anything valuable long since carried away. Even a slammed door was enough to make people flinch.

The Tubthumpers entered the town by subtler means than usual. Ally bent one guard’s opinion in their favor while [[Seamus]] occupied the others with a performance. Once inside, they searched for the sign [[Kisandra Numesti]] had instructed them to present. There was no flower seller to be found, but Antson eventually located a suitable long-stemmed flower, and Seamus placed it through Kisandra’s jade ring.

At the Velvet Corner, they found [[Satinder Morne]] keeping a careful smile on her face while preventing Drelev’s guards from destroying her establishment. When Seamus displayed the ring and flower, she led the company into a concealed back room where honest conversation remained possible.

Satinder warned them that killing Baron [[Hannis Drelev]] would not, by itself, free the town. Another tyrant could take his place unless the people first learned that Drelev’s soldiers could be resisted. The Tubthumpers would need to undermine his power quietly, aid the townsfolk, remove isolated patrols and the hill giants enforcing the curfew, and avoid turning Fort Drelev itself into a battlefield. Their task was not merely to conquer the town, but to give it back to its people.

That plan survived until nightfall.

One of Drelev’s hill giants emerged after curfew, and the confrontation moved into the abandoned Iron Steeple Inn. Ally, apparently deciding that subtlety had completed its assigned portion of the evening, cast a fireball inside the building.

The giant was killed. The inn caught fire.

The Tubthumpers immediately turned from giant-slaying to firefighting and managed to save the structure before the blaze consumed it. The Iron Steeple still stands, though it now smells of smoke, scorched timber, and the enduring lesson that one should never allow Ally to define the phrase “avoiding damage to the town” without asking several follow-up questions.

Despite the spectacle, the victory mattered. One of Drelev’s brutal enforcers was dead, the people had seen that his servants could be defeated, and the Tubthumpers had begun the difficult work of loosening the baron’s grip without replacing his cruelty with their own.

Satinder also revealed the existence of a secret escape tunnel north of the town. It runs beneath the walls and into the caverns below Drelev Keep. When the Tubthumpers are ready to strike at the heart of the baron’s rule, they will not need to approach through the front gate.

The fire beneath Drelev has been lit. In one unfortunate case, quite literally.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
