> #### Operation: Pitaxian Infiltration
>
> **Duration inside the city:** Approximately ten days.
>
> **Method of entry:** Magical transport to the southern rubble district under cover of darkness. Party members used ordinary disguises and Greater Masquerade Scarves.
>
> **Operational objective:** Establish sufficient civic support to isolate Irovetti, prepare the city for an uprising, and secure assistance for an assault upon the House of a Hundred Doors.
>
> #### Confirmed Contacts and Commitments
>
> **Eobald:** Supplied background on Pitax’s theaters, Banded Houses, temples, and artistic community. He recommended the Red Crescent Theatre and Annamede Belavara as useful points of contact and warned against attracting attention prematurely.
>
> **Atlee Quinge, Red Crescent Theatre:** Offered the theater as a safe house after Seamus secured his confidence through a successful performance. Requested recovery of missing singer Asmeranda Ilata, believed confined within the palace.
>
> **Gasperre Liacenza, Falling Star:** Agreed to provide merchant support and spread word when the uprising begins.
>
> **Joffrey Vascari, Riversong Trading House:** Agreed to obstruct Irovetti’s supply lines. Ally secured his cooperation through a commercially framed appeal.
>
> **Eliste Strocalle, House Strocalle:** Agreed to misdirect shipments and provide refuge once the other three Banded Houses were committed. House Strocalle should be considered the least reliable and most exposed of the four.
>
> **High Priest of Calistria:** Offered two safe houses and associated liberation support. Requested that someone eventually poison Irovetti’s wine. No commitment was made to the proposed assassination method.
>
> **Temple of Desna:** Offered sanctuary and support through its existing work with citizens displaced by the wardens.
>
> **Saufie Cintost, lighthouse keeper:** Agreed to misdirect Pitaxian military vessels onto rocks when activated. Preserve her identity until the fleet action is complete.
>
> **Cayid, gargoyle sculptor:** Maintains concealed compartments in statues throughout Pitax. These may serve as dead drops for messages and small supplies. Obtain a complete location key if possible; assume any single cache may eventually be compromised.
>
> **Roald Celinnas, tavern-library proprietor:** Reported that members of Irovetti’s inner circle, possibly including his consort, may be shapeshifting monsters. His arcane guidance should improve recognition of disguised creatures inside the palace.
>
> **Ingris, cheesemaker:** Offered her back room as a safe house and is sending inferior stock to the palace. Her son was beaten to death by wardens. Do not expose her involvement for the sake of a joke, however tempting the cheese story may be.
>
> **Annamede Belavara:** Continued anti-Irovetti propaganda and monitored public sentiment. She now judges the city prepared to rise when given a signal. Her public prominence offers some protection, but Irovetti’s tolerance should not be mistaken for safety.
>
> #### Public Sentiment
>
> Anti-Irovetti graffiti and broadsheets are spreading through markets and taverns. Satirical performances draw growing audiences, and criticism formerly confined to private rooms is increasingly public. This indicates readiness but also raises the likelihood of premature arrests if the final signal is delayed.
>
> Recommendation: coordinate the uprising only after the palace command structure is disrupted. A public rising while General Avinash Jurrg and the wardens remain organized would produce unnecessary civilian casualties.
>
> #### House of a Hundred Doors: Entry Report
>
> **Entry point:** Northern servants’ and provisions passage.
>
> **Defenses encountered:** Concealed tripwire controlling two iron portcullises; multiple arrow slits, presently unmanned; troll guardian immediately beyond the interior double doors.
>
> **Result:** Portcullises forced open by Fitzroy and Elowyn. Troll killed, with Seamus delivering the final blow. Party is now inside the palace and has reached fifteenth level of capability.
>
> **Assessment:** The unmanned arrow slits suggest reduced staffing, poor discipline, internal sympathy, or confidence that the trap and troll were sufficient. Do not assume deeper defenses are similarly neglected.
>
> #### Priority Objectives Inside the Palace
>
> Locate and recover Asmeranda Ilata. Identify and neutralize General Avinash Jurrg before he can coordinate palace resistance. Locate Evindra’s stolen shawl and the cursed fey blade. Confirm the identity and nature of Irovetti’s consort and any other suspected shapeshifters. Capture or kill Irovetti before he can escape or issue orders against the city.
>

## [[Pitax]] AWAKENS

*Merchants, artists, priests, and old families turn against Irovetti as the Tubthumpers carry the war through the gates of his own palace.*

*By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent • Additional reporting by Linzi and Mara Venn*

---

The road from Littletown leads east.

Only days after finding that settlement burned and emptied, the Tubthumpers entered [[Pitax]] without banners, heralds, or an army at their backs. They went disguised as ordinary travelers and spent ten days listening to the people King Castruccio Irovetti claims to rule.

They found no city of villains. They found merchants protecting livelihoods, artists trying to preserve their craft, priests sheltering the forgotten, and families learning to endure the ambitions of a king who has mistaken fear for loyalty.

By the end of those ten days, safe doors stood ready to open, messages moved through the city, royal suppliers had discovered new limits to their enthusiasm, and public mockery had begun replacing private whispers.

Then the Tubthumpers entered the House of a Hundred Doors.

The first entrance was trapped. The first room contained a troll. Neither prevented them from crossing Irovetti’s threshold.

### TEN DAYS, A HUNDRED WHISPERS

*By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent*

Before entering Pitax, the Tubthumpers consulted [[Eobald]], the theatrical director who escaped the city years ago. They found him rehearsing a drama based upon the Rushlight Tournament, complete with Pitaxian villains. His advice was less theatrical than his present occupation might suggest: learn which institutions still commanded trust, do not mistake royal patronage for affection, and remain unnoticed until the moment came to act.

The party acquired stronger disguises and entered the city at night through its southern rubble district. For the next ten days, they moved quietly among those whom Irovetti’s proclamations rarely describe except as an audience, a workforce, or a source of taxes.

The city’s artists spoke of royal money that arrives tied to royal approval. Merchants described trade bent around the convenience of the court. Priests tended to citizens displaced or discarded by Pitax’s prosperity. Even members of the old families appeared more interested in surviving Irovetti than defending him.

No single conversation made a rebellion. Taken together, they revealed that the rebellion had already begun in private.

![[session-56-pitax-awakens-10.webp]]

*A performance at the Red Crescent Theatre becomes part audition, part introduction, and part declaration of purpose.*

[[Seamus]] made particularly effective use of Pitax’s oldest political instrument: a room full of people listening to a song. His performance before members of the Red Crescent Theatre earned more than applause. It demonstrated that the visitors from Thumping Waters understood the difference between conquering a city and asking its people what future they wanted.

Elsewhere, the Tubthumpers found merchants willing to resist royal interference, houses prepared to shelter those in danger, artisans able to move information unseen, and religious communities already practiced at helping people whom the wardens preferred to overlook.

Readers will forgive this newspaper for withholding particular doors, routes, and names while the king’s wardens still carry keys and cudgels. It is enough to report that Irovetti possesses far fewer friends than he believes—and that many of those he has counted as obedient subjects have begun counting one another instead.

### ANNAMEDE’S VERDICT

*By Tobin Bramblefoot, Games and Society Correspondent*

Annamede Belavara, remembered in Thumping Waters for defeating Seamus in the Rushlight boasting competition, has continued applying her talents to the reputation of King Irovetti.

During the Tubthumpers’ ten days in Pitax, Miss Belavara served as an unusually sharp measure of the city’s changing mood. Graffiti critical of the king spread across walls. Satirical performances drew larger crowds. Hostile broadsheets passed from hand to hand in taverns and markets. Jokes once told behind closed doors acquired audiences.

By the end of the effort, Miss Belavara judged that the city was ready to move when the signal came. She also offered assessments of several Tubthumpers. Ally was brilliant but occasionally surprised by her own success. [[Ant]] proved that the gods sometimes create heroes as a joke. Seamus could charm anyone except reality.

> I have nothing to say about [[Fitzroy]].
>
> — Annamede Belavara

The Lantern and Ledger can identify no more complete destruction of a public figure achieved with fewer words.

### A SINGER BEHIND PALACE WALLS

*By Mara Venn, Managing Editor*

One absence shadowed the growing cooperation inside Pitax. Asmeranda Ilata, the celebrated singer of the Red Crescent Theatre, has not returned after being summoned to perform for King Irovetti.

Her fellow performers believe she remains somewhere within the House of a Hundred Doors. The palace has offered no persuasive public explanation for her continued absence.

The distinction between royal invitation and royal captivity has become dangerously thin in Pitax. When a monarch may summon an artist and simply decline to let her leave, patronage has become imprisonment with better furnishings.

The Tubthumpers have promised to look for Miss Ilata as they advance through the palace.

### THE CHEESE RESISTANCE

*By Sella Copperclaw, Trade and Civic Affairs Correspondent*

Revolutions are commonly illustrated with swords, torches, and banners. They may also be conducted through invoices, delayed shipments, misplaced crates, and dairy products of declining quality.

One Pitaxian cheesemaker, having endured both royal wardens and a grief no parent should bear, has reportedly begun reserving her least successful work for palace delivery.

> It really is their fault.
>
> — A Pitaxian cheesemaker, upon being asked to send the palace her worst stock

The Lantern and Ledger does not encourage food waste. We acknowledge, however, that some cheeses appear to have found the rulers they deserve.

### NATURALLY, IT WAS TRAPPED

*By Darya Holt, Traveling Correspondent*

After ten days of preparation, the Tubthumpers approached a servants’ entrance on the northern side of the House of a Hundred Doors. The passage was intended for provisions and palace labor rather than visiting dignitaries, which made it considerably more useful.

A concealed tripwire dropped iron portcullises across the passage, striking the leaders of the advance and trapping part of the company between the gates. Fitzroy and Elowyn forced the barriers upward while the others prepared for an attack from the arrow slits lining the walls.

No arrows came. Whether through neglect, overconfidence, or the quiet erosion of palace discipline, the firing positions were unmanned.

Beyond the double doors waited a troll large enough to fill the first chamber. It greeted the intruders with a roar that sent fear through nearly the entire party.

The fight was short and punishing. Ant loosed arrows at close range and suffered for the proximity. Fitzroy blessed the company before meeting the creature in close combat. Elowyn struck with sufficient force to make the troll regret underestimating masked clergy. Seamus tumbled past its guard, cut it with a fiery finishing stroke, and ultimately delivered the killing blow.

One troll is not a palace conquered. It is, however, one fewer guardian between the people of Pitax and the king who has hidden himself behind a hundred doors.

### FROM THE EDITOR’S DESK

*By Linzi, Editor and Minister of Communication*

We have spent much of this war learning what King Irovetti is willing to do to people beyond his sight. Littletown showed us the answer in ashes. The seven tribes showed us how readily he treats another people’s lives as military property.

Pitax teaches a different lesson.

A kingdom is not its king. It is not even its palace, however many doors the palace may possess. It is the singer who refuses to flatter, the priest who opens a hidden room, the merchant who loses track of a shipment, the sculptor who leaves space where a message may rest, and the cheesemaker who finally decides the royal table has eaten well enough.

Irovetti has walls, wardens, monsters, and titles. The people of Pitax have begun to recognize one another.

There comes a moment when fear does not vanish but ceases to be lonely. No bell announces it. No royal proclamation records it. A citizen simply speaks and discovers that the person beside her has been waiting to say the same thing.

Pitax is very near that moment.

The Tubthumpers have crossed the threshold of the House of a Hundred Doors. Whatever waits inside, Irovetti should understand this much: they did not enter his city alone.

—Linzi, Minister of Communication
