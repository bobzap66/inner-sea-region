# Eobald

![[eobald-portrait.png]]

*Compiled by the College of Letters and Public Record, [[Free University of Thumping Waters]], with archival assistance from the [[Ministry of Communication]] and interviews with Eobald and [[Linzi]]*

> He had lost his spellbook and very nearly his life, yet before we reached home he was already discussing replacement type, corrected tolerances, and all the errors he expected to find in my designs. I have never been so relieved to be criticized by a teacher.
>
> — Linzi

Eobald is a human wizard, educator, performer, and printing expert originally from [[Pitax]]. Once one of Linzi's teachers, he escaped King Castruccio Irovetti's increasingly restrictive city as part of the same secret caravan carrying the final components for [[Thumpington]]'s printing press. The journey nearly ended with his murder by the Jaggedbriar Coven. Instead, Eobald was rescued by the Tubthumpers and helped establish the machinery that would later print the *Lantern and Ledger*.

In the years since, Eobald has worked as a theatrical director, adviser on Pitaxian affairs, teacher of rhetoric and performance, and founding lecturer at the Free University of Thumping Waters. His career joins several institutions that appear separate until someone must explain an idea to the public: the classroom, the stage, the archive, and the press.

  

### At a Glance

**Ancestry:** Human

**Homeland:** Pitax

**Current Residence:** Thumpington

**Profession:** Wizard, educator, theatrical director, and printing expert

**University Appointment:** Founding Lecturer in Rhetoric, Performance, and Printing

**College:** College of Letters and Public Record

**Known Associate:** Linzi, former student and Minister of Communication

**Notable Possession:** A brass wand shaped like a writing quill

  

### Appearance

Eobald is a well-dressed man with warm brown skin, dark hair, a close beard, and the animated expression of a lecturer who has just reached the important part of an explanation. In Pitax and during formal appearances, he favors rich red, blue, and gold garments whose theatrical cut reflects the city's artistic culture without requiring an actual stage.

He is often depicted carrying several books at once, with scroll cases, writing tools, and small magical instruments secured about his person. His most recognizable implement is a brass wand shaped like a writing quill. Eobald used the wand to create illusory objects during lectures, allowing students to examine magical diagrams, machines, historical scenes, and other examples that would be difficult or expensive to place physically inside a classroom.

The wand was recovered after the attack upon his caravan. Linzi recognized it immediately among treasure taken by the ogres who served the Jaggedbriar Coven.

  

### Teacher of Pitax

Before coming to Thumping Waters, Eobald taught in Pitax. His instruction combined formal scholarship with performance, illusion, practical demonstration, and a storyteller's understanding of attention. He possessed broad learning in arcane and occult matters, the natural world, history, society, and craft. He was equally comfortable explaining a magical principle, constructing an illustrative illusion, evaluating a machine, or showing a student why a technically correct speech had nevertheless lost its audience.

Among his students was Linzi, who remembered him not merely as an entertaining instructor but as a teacher capable of turning communication into a craft. Her later work as chronicler, minister, editor, and public speaker carries his influence, particularly her conviction that knowledge has little civic value if it cannot be made understandable to the people expected to use it.

Eobald's lectures frequently relied upon magic. He could create illusory objects and disguises, reshape the appearance of terrain, conceal himself, send messages through dreams, preserve writing upon secret pages, and employ minor magical servants for ordinary work. This did not make his teaching effortless. Students report that he regarded magic as a means of illustration rather than a substitute for preparation.

  

### Leaving Pitax

Under King Irovetti, Pitax's artistic and educational institutions existed beneath increasing political pressure. Patronage rewarded praise, public culture served royal vanity, and talented people learned that disagreement was safest when hidden inside a performance.

By the time Linzi began seeking the machinery and expertise required for a national printing press, she had heard that Eobald wanted to leave Pitax. She contacted him privately and hired him to accompany the press shipment. The caravan was routed through Pitax so he could be smuggled out with the final components.

Eobald communicated with Linzi through *dream message* spells while the caravan combined river ferries and overland roads on its journey toward Thumpington. The messages allowed him to report progress without relying upon correspondence likely to attract attention. They stopped after the caravan entered the [[Locations/Inner Sea Region/River Kingdoms/Stolen Lands/Greenbelt/Narlmarches|Narlmarches]].

At the time, Linzi had not told the rulers of Thumping Waters that she had diverted national funds to purchase the press. Eobald therefore became both the expert upon whom her plan depended and a missing person she could not ask the government to find without revealing what she had done.

  

### The Jaggedbriar Captivity

Ogres serving the Jaggedbriar Coven ambushed the caravan in the Narlmarches. They killed its guards, smashed a wagon, carried away the press components, and delivered Eobald to the hags' enclave. He survived the initial attack badly injured.

The coven imprisoned him inside a cage formed from long iron fingernails and bound his hands behind him with cords of woven hair. Lurkers in light guarded him within an ancient dwarven chamber, keeping him alive while preventing him from resting or escaping. The hags intended to sacrifice him during a ritual meant to summon an Abyssal servant of Mestama.

Eobald's spellbook was destroyed during his captivity. Without it, with most of his prepared magic already spent and his body weakened nearly to death, he had little hope of overcoming the cage and its supernatural guards. He nevertheless identified the prison as hag magic and gathered what information he could from the conversations of his captors.

Linzi confessed the missing funds after the Treasury discovered the discrepancy. The Tubthumpers followed the caravan's route, found the ambush site, tracked the ogres, and entered the Jaggedbriar Enclave. They defeated the lurkers in light and freed Eobald. He warned them about the coven below and the ritual for which he had apparently been preserved.

Eobald did not volunteer to descend with them. He had one remaining hit point, no spellbook, few prepared spells, and recent practical evidence concerning the consequences of confronting three hags unprepared. His desire to return to civilization has occasionally been described as cowardice by people who learned the story from a tavern. The university regards this interpretation as unsupported by both the medical record and common sense.

The Tubthumpers destroyed the Jaggedbriar Coven and recovered the press components from the unfinished ritual chamber. The machinery required cleaning and repair but remained intact.

  

### The Thumpington Press

Rescue did not produce a functioning printing press immediately. The components had crossed much of Avistan, survived a violent ambush, been handled by ogres, stored by hags, and arranged upon a worktable whose previous use did not involve publishing.

Eobald helped clean, repair, assemble, align, and test the machinery. Replacement parts had to be manufactured locally. Paper and ink required dependable supply. Printers had to be trained in machinery few citizens of the young Republic had ever seen. The work took nearly a year before the press could operate reliably.

Its first products were not newspapers. The Government Printing Office produced laws, proclamations, public notices, tax instructions, forms, construction orders, and standardized records. These materials gave the Republic a consistent administrative language and gave the printers the experience required to produce larger editions.

The *Lantern and Ledger* published its first issue on 1 Sarenith, 4714 AR. Eobald was not its editor, but his instruction and technical work made the paper possible. The distinction matters to him. He helped build the instrument; Linzi and the newspaper staff decided what to do with it.

  

### Return to the Stage

Eobald continued his work in performance after settling in Thumping Waters. By the closing years of Irovetti's reign, he was directing theater and drawing upon his knowledge of Pitax to create productions whose comedy carried political memory.

When the Tubthumpers prepared to enter Pitax during the War of the River Kings, they found Eobald rehearsing a drama based upon the Rushlight Tournament, complete with Pitaxian villains. His advice was more restrained than the production. He urged them to identify the institutions that still possessed public trust, avoid mistaking royal patronage for genuine loyalty, and remain unnoticed until they were ready to act.

Eobald recommended the Red Crescent Theatre and Annamede Belavara as valuable contacts. He explained the importance of Pitax's theaters, temples, trade houses, and artistic community and helped the Tubthumpers understand that resistance to Irovetti already existed. Their task was not to invent it, but to recognize it and earn its confidence.

That counsel influenced the campaign that politically isolated Irovetti before the assault upon the House of a Hundred Doors. Eobald did not return as a conqueror. He helped the people who had once smuggled him out decide how to approach the people who remained.

  

### The Free University

Eobald helped establish the College of Letters and Public Record when the Free University of Thumping Waters was chartered in 4716 AR. He serves as a founding lecturer in rhetoric, performance, and printing.

The college's principal building was named Eobald Hall in recognition of his work as a teacher and of his role in establishing the Republic's first working press. Eobald has publicly described the honor as excessive and privately submitted several pages of corrections to the dedicatory plaque. The hall contains lecture rooms suited to performance and illusion as well as printing rooms where students can carry a text from its first draft to its finished copies.

His courses treat public communication as a system rather than a talent possessed by naturally persuasive people. Students study how information is composed, illustrated, copied, performed, transmitted, altered, financed, restricted, and remembered. A proclamation, stage play, lecture, newspaper article, and historical monograph may use different forms, but each asks an audience to trust both a message and the institution carrying it.

Eobald also teaches the physical practice of printing. Students learn type, layout, correction, press operation, paper, ink, maintenance, and the economics of producing enough copies to matter. He is particularly skeptical of proposals that begin with the phrase *the press will pay for itself* and do not include arithmetic.

His use of illusion remains part of his teaching. The brass-quill wand allows diagrams to move, mechanisms to separate into visible components, and scenes to be reconstructed before a class. The university requires students to label illustrative reconstructions clearly, a rule adopted after several first-year students cited one of Eobald's demonstrations as eyewitness evidence.

  

### Scholarship and Magical Practice

Eobald is a trained wizard with particular strength in illusion, communication, and the manipulation of written knowledge. His surviving record identifies fluency in Common, Dwarven, Halfling, Hallit, and Sylvan. His scholarship spans arcana, occultism, nature, society, craft, performance, and academic history.

His magical repertoire has included dream communication, invisibility, illusory disguises and objects, mirrored images, magical servants, concealed pages, warped terrain, and the ordinary cantrips useful to a working lecturer. He is capable of offensive magic but is not principally known as a battle wizard.

The destruction of his spellbook at Jaggedbriar forced him to rebuild a personal collection accumulated through years of teaching. Eobald seldom discusses the financial and intellectual cost separately. A spellbook is simultaneously a magical instrument, professional archive, working notebook, and record of the scholar who assembled it.

His experience contributed to the university's policy of maintaining secure copies of essential research rather than allowing a discipline to depend upon one person's vulnerable collection. He supports access to knowledge while remaining attentive to dangerous material, privacy, and the ease with which a copied error can become more authoritative merely by becoming common.

  

### Personality and Teaching

Eobald is animated, sociable, intellectually broad, and comfortable before an audience. His theatrical instincts make his lectures memorable, but his work with machinery has given him little patience for inspiration that refuses measurement. He appreciates wit, demonstration, and dramatic timing; he also expects corrected proofs, maintained equipment, and sources that survive inspection after the applause ends.

He is capable of caution without timidity. His escape from Pitax required secrecy. His rescue from Jaggedbriar required other people to take risks he could not take himself. His later advice concerning the liberation of Pitax emphasized patience, trusted institutions, and careful preparation rather than a theatrical entrance.

Students sometimes arrive expecting Linzi's former teacher to resemble Linzi. They discover two people with a shared belief in stories and sharply different relationships with revision deadlines. Their disagreements are affectionate, technically detailed, and frequently continued in writing after everyone else has left the room.

  

### Legacy in Thumping Waters

Eobald's most visible legacy is the existence of print as an ordinary part of Republican life. Laws can be distributed consistently. Citizens can read national news. Scholars can publish research in multiple copies. Settlements can receive the same instructional material without waiting for a scribe to reproduce it by hand.

Linzi conceived the press, obtained its components, and accepted responsibility for the unlawful means by which she funded it. The Tubthumpers rescued the caravan's surviving expert and recovered the machinery. Craftspeople repaired and reproduced its parts. Printers learned to operate it. Eobald's place in that history is neither sole inventor nor incidental assistant. He was the specialist who turned a collection of damaged components into a working institution and taught others enough that the institution no longer depended upon him.

His work at the Free University extends the same principle. A teacher succeeds not when students remain dependent upon his explanations, but when they can preserve, question, reproduce, and improve what he taught them.

> A machine that only one person can operate is not an institution. It is a hostage.
>
> — Eobald, lecture to the College of Letters and Public Record
