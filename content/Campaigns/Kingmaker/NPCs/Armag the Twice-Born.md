# Armag the Twice-Born

![[armag-the-twice-born-portrait.png]]

*As recorded by [[Linzi]], Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters*

Armag the Twice-Born is one of those names that sounds impressive until you ask a very reasonable follow-up question and no one can give you the same answer twice. Born once normally, and then… what? Born again in anger? Born again after punching something large and furry? The [[Groups/Tiger Lords|Tiger Lords]] are notably evasive on this point, which I suspect is because clarity is considered a personal failing among barbarians.

Regardless, Armag leaned into the title with remarkable enthusiasm.

#### Rise of a Legend

What we now know—thanks to terrified peasants, exhausted nobles, ancient guardians, and the sort of revelations that only come *after* everything has gone terribly wrong—is that Armag was never “twice-born” at all. He was made.

Years ago, he challenged the reigning Tiger Lord chieftain and killed him publicly, securing leadership in the traditional Nomen fashion: loudly and with witnesses. Efficient, brutal, and culturally unimpeachable. From there, he drove the Tiger Lords like a weapon with no safety—raiding into Drelev’s territory, taking hostages, and turning borderlands into proving grounds for his ambition.

Contemporary reports agree on his defining traits: cruel, ruthless, charismatic, and frighteningly effective at convincing others that his rage was destiny.

#### The Lie Behind the Title

What those reports did not know—what even Armag himself did not fully understand—was that his legend had been carefully cultivated since childhood.

The Sisters of the Bloodshot Eye, devotees of the hag goddess Gyronna, abducted Armag as a boy during his first hunt. They raised him on lies layered with ritual, prophecy, and carefully curated brutality, teaching him that he was the reincarnation of the ancient warlord Armag, returned to finish an age-old conquest. They shaped his identity until there was no space left for doubt.

By the time he took power, Armag was not merely convinced—he was *weaponized*.

Their long game was precise: aim a living legend at [[Brevoy]], a nation that did not exist when the first Armag lived, and let history be rewritten in blood. Armag believed himself chosen. The Sisters knew better. To them, he was a tool.

#### The Tomb and the Sword

This manipulation explains Armag’s obsession with the ancient tomb attributed to the original Armag. He believed it was proof—confirmation that his life had meaning beyond violence. That within lay Ovirnbaane, the soul-sword that would complete his rebirth and cement his legend.

He found the sword.

He took it up.

And in doing so, he slaughtered his own followers in a divine frenzy, his eyes and blade weeping blood as Ovirnbaane devoured what little remained of the man beneath the myth. When the Tubthumpers confronted him, Armag the Twice-Born was already surrounded by the corpses of his companions—killed not by enemies, but by the destiny he had been taught to crave.

He died there, in the tomb he believed was his birthright.

#### Aftermath

The Sisters’ plan, decades in the making, collapsed with him. Their Twice-Born was revealed as a lie, their weapon broken, their prophecy reduced to ash. Whatever satisfaction Gyronna took in the attempt, it was not enough to save her servants from failure.

So that is the truth, as best as it can be known:

Armag the Twice-Born—never reborn, never chosen, but carefully constructed. A conqueror forged from stolen childhood, cult manipulation, and a story told too often to be questioned.

A legend manufactured, then destroyed by the very blade meant to prove it real.

If there is a lesson here—and I am increasingly convinced the [[Stolen Lands]] insist on lessons—it is this: destiny is rarely born. It is far more often *taught*, and those who teach it are rarely the ones who bleed for it.
