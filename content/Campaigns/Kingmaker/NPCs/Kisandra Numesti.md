# Kisandra Numesti

![[kisandra-numesti-portrait.png]]

### Kisandra Numesti

*“I grew up in a city of swords and honor. I thought I knew what courage meant—until I had to run for my life through a swamp to find it again.”*

#### Overview

When **Kisandra Numesti** stumbled out of the [[Locations/Inner Sea Region/River Kingdoms/Stolen Lands/Hooktongue Slough]] and into the bog strider settlement of **[[Tok-Nikrat]]**, she was exhausted, starving, and so coated in mud that she barely looked human. But even through the delirium, she managed to speak a warning—one that would change the fate of [[Tatzlford]]. Her flight from **[[Fort Drelev]]** carried across the wilderness the news of an approaching army: Drelev’s soldiers, Tiger Lords, and trolls marching to sack the border town in a bid for favor with **King Irovetti of [[Pitax]]**. By chance or providence, she reached the Tubthumpers just in time to save hundreds of lives.

#### Appearance

Kisandra is a young woman in her early twenties, with dark brown hair usually tied into a practical braid, sharp features softened by fatigue, and eyes the color of wet earth. She’s slight of frame but carries herself with purpose, her movements revealing a blend of Restovian grace and frontier grit. Her once-fine traveling leathers are now patched with scavenged cloth and stitched tears—each one a story of survival. Even covered in grime and scars, she still holds the posture of someone raised among dueling nobles: proud, straight-backed, and quietly defiant.

#### Background

Kisandra was born in **[[Restov]]**, the youngest daughter of **Lord Terrion Numesti**, a respected Brevic knight and swordsman of Rostlandic heritage. When **Baron Hannis Drelev** received his charter to found a new colony along the western frontier, Terrion followed him as both friend and commander—a decision that would cost him everything. Fort Drelev was meant to be a bastion of Brevic honor in the wilds, but Drelev’s ambitions and arrogance soon soured its promise. Within a few short years, he traded loyalty for survival, surrendering to the **Tiger Lords** and the rule of **King Irovetti of Pitax**.

When the Tiger Lords demanded noble hostages to guarantee Drelev’s obedience, Terrion refused to send his eldest daughter, **Tamary**. The baron’s response was swift and cruel: Terrion was imprisoned, Tamary was seized regardless, and Kisandra was placed under watch as a potential bargaining chip. She managed to escape with the help of a sympathetic servant, slipping into the marshes under cover of darkness with nothing but a cloak, a knife, and her father’s signet ring. Her plan was simple—reach Tatzlford and warn them of Drelev’s impending treachery. The Hooktongue Slough, however, had other plans.

For days she wandered lost among the mists and muck, evading boggards and worse. Starving, feverish, and out of hope, she collapsed near Tok-Nikrat, the enclave of the bog striders. By fortune or fate, the Tubthumpers were using the settlement as a staging ground for their campaign to clear the Slough of threats. When they found her, she was barely conscious—but she managed to croak out her warning before fainting: “Drelev’s coming… with trolls.”

#### Personality

Kisandra bears the heart of her Rostlandic heritage—proud, principled, and sharp-tongued when provoked—but years under Drelev’s rule have tempered her idealism with realism. She is clever, observant, and brave in the way that desperate people are brave: because there’s simply no other choice left. Though she still carries herself with the manners of a noblewoman, her sense of honor now lies with those who act, not those who command. She has little patience for titles and none for cowardice. Her sense of humor, dry and unexpected, surfaces when least expected—usually right after she’s done something reckless.

Kisandra is deeply loyal to her family, particularly her father, whose quiet defiance against Drelev’s corruption shaped her understanding of right and wrong. She is haunted by guilt for escaping when he could not, and for her sister’s fate among the Tiger Lords. That guilt has hardened into resolve: she will not stop until both are free. Beneath the fear and exhaustion lies a steel that has nothing to do with swords—a courage born from outrage, love, and loss.

#### Role in the Battle of Tatzlford

Kisandra’s warning changed everything. Had she arrived even a day later, Tatzlford would have been a smoking ruin. Traveling with the Tubthumpers from Tok-Nikrat, she guided them through the safe paths of the marsh and shared what she knew of Drelev’s forces: a mercenary host under **Ameon Trask**, supported by Tiger Lord warriors and trolls. Her knowledge gave the defenders their first true strategy—and their best chance at survival. During the battle itself, Kisandra fought not with steel, but with grit. She ferried messages and water between barricades, tended the wounded, and refused to take shelter even when ordered to. By dawn, when the last of Drelev’s army broke and fled, she stood beside the Tubthumpers, shaking with exhaustion but alive to see what she had saved.

#### The Plea

When the fires of the battle finally dimmed, Kisandra approached the Tubthumpers to make a plea. “My father still rots in Drelev’s dungeon,” she said, “and my sister is a prisoner among the Tiger Lords. I can’t save them alone.” She described Drelev’s alliance with Pitax and the growing cruelty of his rule—the fear, the executions, the way his soldiers patrolled Fort Drelev like conquerors rather than protectors. Her voice carried no drama, only the steady weight of someone who has seen too much to bother lying.

To aid the heroes, she offered a single **jade ring**—a token of trust from **Satinder Morne**, the proprietor of Fort Drelev’s gaming hall and brothel. “She’s no friend to the baron,” Kisandra said quietly. “Show her this, and she’ll help you.” She did not explain the full truth—that Satinder was her secret lover, one of the few people in Fort Drelev who still dared to show kindness under tyranny. To Kisandra, that ring was more than a token of alliance—it was a promise of love and defiance, and a hope that both might survive what was coming.

#### Current Role

In the aftermath of the battle, Kisandra remained in Tatzlford, helping Mayor **[[Loy Rezbin]]** and **[[Latricia Rezbin|Latricia]]** coordinate repairs, organize refugees, and distribute supplies. Her Restovian education and noble upbringing made her a natural at leadership, though she insists she’s “just keeping busy.” Beneath that humility lies a quiet restlessness—her mind forever westward, toward the fort that still imprisons her family. To the people of Tatzlford, she is a hero. To herself, she’s simply unfinished business with a name and a sword.

Those who know her well say Kisandra Numesti is the living heart of the rebellion against Drelev—not through might or magic, but through the kind of courage that refuses to die quietly. When the Tubthumpers march to free her homeland, she will be waiting at the edge of the swamp, her father’s ring in hand and vengeance in her eyes.

## General Details

##### From the Chronicle of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

*Notes on Kisandra Numesti*

They say she stumbled out of the swamp like a ghost—mud-caked, half-starved, and clutching her father’s signet as if it were the last piece of the world worth holding on to. I didn’t see it myself, but everyone in Tatzlford talks about it: how this young noblewoman from Restov came crawling out of Hooktongue Slough, eyes blazing and voice raw, to warn a town she’d never even seen before that death was on its way.

That’s the thing about Kisandra Numesti—she doesn’t act like someone who’s trying to be brave. She just is. She talks about her family’s fall from grace the way some people talk about the weather, like pain’s just something you live through and keep walking. She’s all steel and tired eyes, the sort of woman who’s been hurt too badly to waste time being delicate about it. But underneath all that grit, there’s kindness too. She’ll snap at soldiers twice her size, then spend the next hour patching up their wounds with hands that still shake from fear she refuses to show.

People in Tatzlford call her a hero. She calls herself “someone who ran in the right direction for once.” I think that’s unfair. I’ve written about a lot of heroes—some who command armies, some who command songs—but Kisandra? She’s the kind who changes the ending of a story just by surviving long enough to tell it.

*—Linzi*
