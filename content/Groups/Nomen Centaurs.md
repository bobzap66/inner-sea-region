# Nomen Centaurs


### The Nomen Centaurs

The Nomen centaur tribe are the proud descendants of the Rashalka—the “Thundering Hooves”—who once ruled the northern Casmar Steppes in vast herds that thundered across the horizon. Though their numbers have dwindled to a fraction of what they once were, their pride and strength endure. The Nomen remain the undisputed masters of Dunsward, guardians of the sacred plains and keepers of the oldest songs of the land.

#### Culture and Beliefs

The Nomen are a matriarchal people led by their war-priestess, **Aecora Silverfire**, chosen champion of Desna’s ancient aspect known as *Mother Moon*. Aecora rules with both wisdom and steel, uniting faith and discipline in equal measure. Her word is law among the tribe, and her authority extends beyond mortal rank—she is seen as the living voice of Mother Moon, whose light guides her people across the endless grasslands.

Mother Moon is both protector and judge. She embodies freedom, travel, and the promise of safe passage under the night sky, yet her anger is said to fall upon oath-breakers and those who forget the old ways. The Nomen measure their lives by the rhythms of the heavens: births and betrothals on the full moon, funerals on the waning crescent, and great councils during eclipses when prophecy rides on the shifting light. Music, dance, and the drumming of hooves are their prayers, each rhythm echoing the pulse of the earth beneath them.

Generations ago, the Nomen fought the Taldan Phalanx and Horse, and though they held the plains, the price was ruinous. The slaughter of their warriors decimated the male population, and the tribe rebuilt as a predominantly female society. Women became soldiers and riders of renown, while men turned to the bow and the hunt. To this day, Nomen culture honors balance: the sword to defend, the bow to provide, the moon to guide them through darkness.

#### Society and Daily Life

The Nomen live as nomads, moving their camps with the herds and the seasons. Each encampment forms a great circle of hide tents marked by painted banners of lineage and honor. At the center burns the sacred fire—a living symbol of their unity. Around it, the tribe gathers to dance, sing, and recount the deeds of their ancestors. The ground shakes beneath their hooves as drums echo across the steppe, and the sight of their moonlit war dance is said to stir awe even in the bravest hearts.

Every role within the tribe is purposeful. The warrior-maidens train relentlessly and patrol Dunsward’s borders. The hunters bring back game and news of the plains. The priestesses tend the flame, oversee births, and pass down the stories of the Rashalka. The young are taught to ride before they can walk, and to sing before they can speak. All know that their lives belong to the tribe, and through the tribe, to Mother Moon herself.

Today, the Nomen number around two hundred souls. They are not numerous—but each is worth ten of any other warrior. Aecora’s leadership holds them together: stern, proud, and guided by vision. Under her, the Nomen have endured famine, exile, and loss. The silver in her mane and the quiet fire in her eyes are said to shine brighter with every victory and every sorrow borne in silence.

#### Alliance with Thumping Waters

The Nomen’s relationship with the Republic of Thumping Waters began with distrust. To Aecora Silverfire, humans were a reminder of conquest and betrayal, their settlements creeping ever closer to sacred ground. Yet fate wove a different path. When her daughter, **Xamanthe Silverfire**, vanished while exploring the ruins of Vordakai’s ancient stronghold, it was the heroes of Thumping Waters—the Tubthumpers—who braved the cursed halls and brought her home alive.

The rescue of Xamanthe changed everything. The tribe’s suspicion gave way to cautious respect, and Aecora’s gratitude ran deeper than words. When the Tubthumpers returned the legendary longbow **Skybolt**—an ancient weapon of the Rashalka, lost to the ages—Aecora took it as a sign from Mother Moon herself that a new era of trust had begun. In a ceremony beneath the full moon, she presented Skybolt to the heroes in return, acknowledging them as true allies of the Nomen people.

Since that night, the plains between Dunsward and Thumping Waters have remained at peace. The Nomen still keep to their ways, but envoys travel between their camps and the republic’s border forts, bearing messages, gifts, and the first tentative bonds of friendship. Though centuries of mistrust cannot be erased in a single generation, Aecora’s leadership—and the Tubthumpers’ deeds—have given both peoples hope that the steppe and the city might share the same sky.

#### Faith and Myth

The Nomen tell that their ancestors were born from the tears of Mother Moon as they fell upon the earth, mingling with the blood of the plains. Each centaur’s soul carries a spark of her light, and when a Nomen dies, her spirit gallops skyward to join the stars. Aecora Silverfire teaches that the constellations are the campfires of the ancestors, watching from above. On nights of omen, she claims to see their shadows racing along the horizon, running beside the living herd.

The Nomen still dance beneath the moon, still whisper their prayers into the wind, and still guard the plains that birthed them. Their power is not measured in cities or armies, but in endurance—the quiet, stubborn rhythm of hooves against the endless earth. And under Aecora’s watchful gaze, that rhythm continues, steady and eternal.

## General Details

##### From the Journal of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

*The Night of Skybolt*

I wasn’t there that night—but I’ve told the story so many times that even the moon must believe I was. It’s become one of those tales that outgrows the people in it. Ask Mairi, and she’ll say it was just diplomacy. Ask Seamus, and he’ll swear thunder rolled at every step. Ask Ant, and he’ll get a faraway look, mutter something about arrows and destiny, and then go clean his bow for the next two hours. But me? I’ll tell you what really happened—or at least what should have.

The Nomen rode in from every horizon, a living storm of muscle and moonlight. Two hundred centaurs, their armor gleaming like the scales of night itself, galloped across the plain and encircled the Tubthumpers’ camp. The earth shook, and even the stars seemed to lean closer to watch. Then came Aecora Silverfire, radiant as dawn on a blade’s edge, and beside her—her daughter, Xamanthe, alive. The girl who had vanished into the jaws of death itself, returned from Vordakai’s cursed tomb through courage, friendship, and the sheer bloody-minded refusal of my friends to let evil have the last word.

A hush fell over the plain. The centaurs lowered their lances. The wind stopped. Even the campfires dared not crackle too loudly. Aecora spoke, and her voice carried for miles. “You returned my daughter when all hope was ash. You restored our honor when we thought it buried. You gave us back our heart.” Then she lifted **Skybolt**—that ancient longbow, heirloom of the Rashalka, once stolen by spriggans and reclaimed by heroes—and it caught the moonlight like it was drinking from the heavens.

She placed the bow in Antson’s hands, and for a heartbeat, even the wind held its breath. “Let this bow fly not for blood,” she said, “but for peace—between your people and mine, between the living and the lost.” The crowd of centaurs bowed their heads, and the heroes of Thumping Waters stood among them, human and halfling and elf alike, lit by fire and starlight.

And then—because this story deserves a proper ending—the Nomen began to sing. Their voices rose like thunder made of music, and the plains answered. Some say the moon dipped lower to listen. Some say the stars themselves flickered in time. And some say, when Aecora lifted her hand and declared peace between the Nomen and the people of Varnhold, a shooting star crossed the sky and burned away every shadow Vordakai had ever cast.

Now, I’m not saying all that happened. But if you had been there—if you had seen Skybolt shining in Ant’s hands—you might believe it, too.

*—Linzi*

Editor’s note: The facts are mostly accurate. The embellishments, artistically necessary.
