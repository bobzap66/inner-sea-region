# Tiger Lords


The Tiger Lords like to introduce themselves the way some people introduce expensive wines: lots of emphasis on lineage, boldness, and the promise of violence. They’re a roaming Kellid tribe from the far north, tracing their roots to the ancient warbands that followed the original Armag back when history was still deciding what it wanted to be. If you ask a Tiger Lord, they’ll tell you their ancestors conquered half the world. If you ask anyone else, the estimates get more modest. Somewhere between “unstoppable tide of battle” and “deeply committed joggers with axes.”

  

What sets them apart from your everyday raiders is their symbolism. They revere dire tigers—honestly, the regular-sized ones aren’t terrifying enough—and claim an enormous smilodon named Speartooth as their totem spirit. This gives their culture a certain aesthetic: stripes, roaring, and the general vibe of people who think subtlety is a trap laid by the weak. Their warriors paint themselves in bold patterns, stalk into battle with bone-clattering bravado, and celebrate victory with the kind of enthusiasm normally reserved for feast days and regrettable weddings.

  

The modern Tiger Lords travel widely, far beyond the borders of their homelands. They’ve served as mercenaries in places like [Pitax](../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/River%20Kingdoms/Stolen%20Lands/Thumping%20Waters/Settlements/Pitax/Pitax.md) and along the edges of [Brevoy](../Locations/Inner%20Sea%20Region/Brevoy/Brevoy.md), earning more coin and equipment than most Kellid nomads ever see. Imagine a caravan, but everyone is flexing and no one is selling spices. Their wanderings have left them more worldly than their reputation suggests, though still firmly committed to the idea that strength should be demonstrated loudly and often.

  

Their leadership is… straightforward. They are ruled by a chieftain chosen through combat. No ballots, no debates, no committees. You want to lead? Win the fight. The system is ruthlessly efficient, assuming you don’t mind a structure where succession planning involves a great deal of bleeding.

  

Under [Armag the Twice-Born](../Campaigns/Kingmaker/NPCs/Armag%20the%20Twice-Born.md), the Tiger Lords have become more aggressive—“more” being relative to a baseline that already included raiding, pillaging, and bellowing challenges at people minding their own business. Armag drove them west, south, then straight into conflict with Baron Drelev. Some say they were pushed to it by “the Sisters,” a group of Gyronna-worshipers with rather pointed opinions about vengeance. The Tiger Lords listen to them, which is rarely a sign of anyone’s improving judgment.

  

For all their ferocity, the Tiger Lords are not mindless brutes. They’re a people bound by tradition, pride, and a very dramatic sense of destiny. They believe the past isn’t dead—it’s just waiting for someone strong enough to pick it back up. Whether that destiny leads them to glory or disaster usually depends on who’s holding the biggest axe that week.

  

So far, the Tubthumpers have clashed with the Tiger Lords mostly as Armag’s extended shadow, but we’ve yet to see how they’ll respond when the shadow gets dragged into the light. I’m keeping several pages blank for future entries—preferably ones that don’t require me to describe how many stripes someone painted on their face during the battle.

## General Details

##### From the Journal of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

*On Tigers, Totems, and Unexpected Roots*
  

It isn’t every day you discover that one of your kingdom’s founders spent her childhood among a tribe of Kellid berserkers who worship a prehistoric murder-cat the size of a wagon. But that’s Mairi for you—quietly carrying around revelations like extra daggers, only mentioning them when absolutely necessary or when a particularly loud Tiger Lord happens to shout something familiar across a battlefield.

  

Her mother was adopted into the Tiger Lords long before Mairi was born, and Mairi grew up in their camps—hide tents, painted faces, roaring bonfires, the whole dramatic package. She learned their stories, their songs, and, I imagine, the fine art of not getting trampled during enthusiastic tribal games. It must have been a strange childhood, but then again, “strange childhoods” seem to be our party’s unofficial membership requirement.

  

She left almost twenty years ago, choosing her own road instead of the one laid out by tradition and very large axes. Yet the past never quite lets go. Sometimes, when a Tiger Lord bares their teeth in that very specific, very Kellid way, I’ll catch a flicker in Mairi’s expression—a ghost of memory, or maybe a moment of wondering who she might’ve become if she’d stayed.

  

Whether her old ties turn into a bridge, a bargaining chip, or something more dangerous remains to be seen. But it’s there in her story now, coiled and waiting—like a tiger in tall grass, silent until it decides to matter.

  

*—Linzi*

Editor’s note: Speartooth, the Tiger Lords’ mythical smilodon totem, remains very real in the minds of its followers and thankfully nowhere near the republic’s borders.
