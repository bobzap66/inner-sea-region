*As recorded and revised by Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters*

> The blade does not ask whether you are strong enough to wield it. It asks whether you are willing to become it.

I have not held Ovinrbaane.

I want that recorded immediately, in case anyone finds these pages without knowing who I was or how careful I tried to be. I have seen the sword. I have stood close enough to feel the air around it tighten like a breath held too long. That was more than enough.

Its name is commonly translated as *Enemy of All Enemies*. That sounds like the sort of boast warriors carve onto weapons when they have exhausted all sensible names. Ovinrbaane is not boasting. It is stating a purpose.

Ovinrbaane is an unspeakably ancient two-handed greatsword. It does not sleep as other relics sleep. When it rests, it listens. When it is idle, it grows impatient. Even within its scabbard that impatience can be felt as a low, animal pressure, like a hunting hound straining against its leash.

This is a blade that expects blood not as a pleasure, but as a rhythm. When that rhythm is interrupted, Ovinrbaane begins correcting its bearer.

#### The Sword That Offers Solutions

Ovinrbaane seldom needs to issue commands. Commands can be recognized, questioned, and refused. Instead, it offers solutions.

It whispers certainty. *You are right. You are chosen. Mercy has already failed. This enemy will never stop. You have always been the person capable of doing what must be done.*

The danger is not that the sword makes a good person cruel overnight. The danger is that it makes cruelty feel justified, then necessary, and finally inevitable.

Those who bear Ovinrbaane for long periods begin to change. Their tempers shorten. Their posture stiffens. Their faces harden. Old scars appear to echo older portraits. Witnesses have claimed that a bearer’s features slowly come to resemble the ancient carvings of Armag himself.

Whether the sword restores Armag’s spirit, reproduces his memories, or merely forces another person into the shape of its preferred master remains uncertain. The practical distinction may be smaller than scholars would like.

This is how Armag returns—not by climbing from a grave amid fire and prophecy, but by being remembered too thoroughly. Ovinrbaane does not resurrect the man. It rebuilds him, decision by decision, using whoever is strong, wounded, or desperate enough to keep listening.

#### History in Thumping Waters

##### The Tomb of Armag

The Tubthumpers recovered Ovinrbaane from Armag’s Tomb after defeating the warlord who called himself Armag the Twice-Born. Zorek, the tomb’s ancient guardian, revealed that the Twice-Born had not been a reincarnated hero. He had been cultivated as a weapon by the Sisters of the Bloodshot Eye and consumed by the legend he was meant to embody.

Zorek also revealed the only known means of destroying the sword: its bearer must carry Ovinrbaane into the Boneyard and strike it three times against their own gravestone. Having delivered this exceptionally inconvenient information, he crumbled into dust and left the living to decide what to do with it.

##### Velka of the Sootscale

Velka claimed Ovinrbaane and accepted responsibility for carrying it from the tomb. From that point forward, the greatsword almost entirely replaced the trident and shield with which she had previously fought.

When Velka was killed during the final battle against the Kingdom of the Cleansed, Ovinrbaane was waiting when Tristian restored her to life. Its welcome was neither relief nor gratitude. The sword mocked her for dying without making proper use of it and called the incident wasted potential.

Velka continued to carry the blade until Arodus 4714 AR, when the destruction of the cult beneath Sootscale Valley convinced her to leave the adventuring road and remain with her people. At that time, Ovinrbaane passed into the keeping of Fitzroy Fulgrim III.

##### Fitzroy Fulgrim III

Fitzroy did not receive an ordinary weapon. He inherited every expectation, appetite, and unfinished transformation contained within it.

I cannot say where Fitzroy ends and Ovinrbaane begins. That uncertainty is important. The sword’s influence does not erase the bearer and replace him with a convenient villain. It works through convictions already present: duty, certainty, anger, pride, fear, and the sincere belief that someone must be strong enough to act.

Fitzroy eventually became troubled by the Tubthumpers’ treatment of defeated enemies and by the person he believed he was becoming among them. In Neth 4715 AR, he resigned as Minister of Defense and returned to Brevoy with his household.

Ovinrbaane went with him.

#### Current Status

**Current bearer:** Fitzroy Fulgrim III

**Last confirmed location:** Traveling toward Brevoy after departing Thumping Waters

**Disposition:** Active, intelligent, blood-hungry, and capable of gradually reshaping its bearer into another Armag

Fitzroy left as a friend who feared what continued compromise was doing to his conscience. He also left carrying a sword that turns certainty into appetite and conviction into identity.

Both truths must be preserved.

I will not call Fitzroy an enemy because of what Ovinrbaane may someday make of him. Nor will I pretend that distance has made the danger smaller. The sword is now beyond the Republic’s observation, carried by a man already asking whether he still recognizes himself.

If Fitzroy reads this, I hope he understands why I wrote it.

If Ovinrbaane reads it through him, I hope it understands that we are still watching.

## Mechanics

#### Mechanics and Inner Workings

##### Ovinrbaane, Enemy of All Enemies

**Item Level** 20; **Rarity** Unique; **Traits** Artifact, Cursed, Evocation, Intelligent, Magical; **Alignment** Chaotic Neutral; **Usage** held in two hands; **Bulk** 2; **Base Weapon** Greatsword

Ovinrbaane is a cursed greatsword infused with an unquenchable hunger for battle. It does not merely encourage violence. It reshapes identity, memory, and purpose, patiently rebuilding the warlord Armag through whoever proves strong enough to wield it.

##### Intelligent Item Statistics

**Perception** +27; **Senses** precise vision 60 feet, imprecise hearing; **Communication** empathy; **Will** +33

**Skills** Deception +28, Intimidation +32

**Ability Modifiers** Intelligence +0, Wisdom +3, Charisma +4

##### Weapon Statistics

**Damage** 2d12 slashing; **Group** sword

**Fundamental and Property Runes** +2 potency, striking, wounding

##### Curse — Blood Without End

Ovinrbaane constantly urges its bearer toward violence and chafes whenever it is not used to slay worthy foes. If 24 hours pass without Ovinrbaane being used in an encounter of at least moderate difficulty, its bearer becomes irritable and short-tempered.

At the start of the bearer’s first turn in the next moderate or more difficult combat, the bearer is stupefied 2 until the end of that turn.

Long-term psychological and behavioral effects should include a gradual shift toward Ovinrbaane’s alignment, increasing certainty that the bearer is Armag reborn, and physical changes that make the bearer resemble the original Armag.

##### Blessing of Slaughter

**Activate** one action, command; **Frequency** three times per day

Ovinrbaane casts *bless*. The sword can activate this power only while its bearer is fighting a foe it deems worthy—a creature of the bearer’s level or higher.

##### Break the Chains

**Activate** reaction; **Frequency** three times per day; **Trigger** Ovinrbaane’s bearer becomes immobilized or restrained

Ovinrbaane casts *unfettered movement*, heightened to 8th level, upon its bearer. The sword can activate this power only while its bearer is fighting a foe it deems worthy.

##### Unmake the Spell

**Activate** reaction; **Frequency** three times per day; **Trigger** an undesirable spell effect with a duration affects Ovinrbaane’s bearer

Ovinrbaane casts *dispel magic*, heightened to 8th level, with a counteract modifier of +31. The sword can activate this power only while its bearer is fighting a foe it deems worthy.

#### Destruction

Destroying Ovinrbaane requires confronting mortality without conquest, victory, or legacy to soften it.

Its bearer must carry the sword into the Boneyard and strike three blows against their own gravestone, causing Ovinrbaane to shatter. Finding one’s own gravestone before death is a paradoxical challenge and may require the bearer to die, be judged, pass into the afterlife, and then be restored through divine intervention or an extraordinary effort by their allies.

Alternatively, if Ovinrbaane remains unused for one hundred years, its powers become dormant. While dormant, it has Hardness 30 and 120 Hit Points and can be destroyed normally. At the end of any round in which it remains intact, the blade awakens if it succeeds at a DC 5 flat check.

For most mortals, sealing Ovinrbaane away may be more achievable than destroying it. The blade, naturally, has opinions about this proposal.
