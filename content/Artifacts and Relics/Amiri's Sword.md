# Amiri's Sword

*As recorded by [[Linzi]], Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters*

I don’t know the sword’s true name.

[[Amiri]] never gave it one, and I don’t think the blade would answer if she did. Names have power, and this sword already carries too much of it.

What I know is this: it was taken from a frost giant long ago, pulled from a corpse that should have stayed dead. That alone should have been warning enough. Giants do not bind their dead lightly, and the First World never does anything by accident. Whatever soul lives in that blade did not wander there on its own.

For years, the sword was silent. Heavy, brutal, reliable—an extension of Amiri’s strength and anger, nothing more. It cut because she swung it. It endured because she did. If there was a presence within it, it slept, or waited, or simply watched.

Then one winter afternoon, it broke.

Not in battle. Not under some great foe’s blow. Just a practice swing, a moment of carelessness, steel meeting wood—and the blade snapped cleanly in half. I’ve never seen Amiri look the way she did in that moment. Not frightened. Not angry. Lost.

And then the sword chose to wake up.

The pieces pulled themselves together. Light traced the break like a memory refusing to fade. The steel knit itself whole again, as if the idea of being broken had offended it. When the glow faded, the blade was flawless—and unmistakably changed.

Alive is the wrong word. Aware is closer.

From that moment on, the sword reacted. Not always, not clearly, but enough to be noticed. It drank in cold and answered it. It resonated near certain places, certain spirits. And eventually, in the [[Locations/Inner Sea Region/River Kingdoms/Stolen Lands/Hooktongue Slough|Hooktongue Slough]], something spoke to it.

A ghost—ancient, bound by the same First World curse—called the sword brother.

That was when we understood the truth we’d been circling: the blade does not merely contain a soul. It imprisons one. A spirit bound into steel, held there not as punishment, but as a knot—one piece of a larger, cruel design meant to trap more than one being at a time.

The ghost wanted what was in the sword. Not to free it, but to consume it, to unmake his own torment by devouring another’s. When we refused, the fight that followed felt less like a battle and more like two wounds trying to tear each other open.

When it was over, we were left with knowledge instead of answers.

The soul in the blade can be freed—but not easily, and not safely. The ritual required lies beyond us, buried in traditions older than our maps and kinder than our enemies. And until that day comes, the sword remains what it is: a prison that fights back against being one.

That is why [[Mairi]] took it south.

Not because Amiri could not bear it—but because she could. And because someone had to choose responsibility over strength. The sword was never corrupting Amiri, not in the way cursed things usually do. It didn’t whisper. It didn’t push. It endured with her. That, somehow, made it worse.

I think the blade trusts Amiri.

I think that is why it hurts her to be without it.

Wherever it rests now—wrapped in care, carried toward the Seven Arches, guarded by a woman who has already given everything once—I suspect the sword is waiting. Not impatiently. Not angrily. Just… aware. The way it was before it broke. The way it will be until someone finally keeps the promise the steel was never meant to hold.

Amiri will find her way back to it someday. Of that, I have no doubt.

And when she does, I hope the blade is finally ready to let go of what it has been carrying all this time.

## Mechanics

### Amiri’s Sword

*Also known among the Tubthumpers as “The Bound Blade.”*

**Item Level** 14
**Rarity** Unique
**Traits** Magical, Necromancy, Evocation, Cursed
**Usage** held in two hands
**Bulk** 2
**Base Weapon** Bastard Sword (Large)

This massive blade was taken from a fallen frost giant and bears the weight of more than steel. Bound within it is a trapped spirit, held fast by a curse of the First World—a prison that resists breaking, even as it calls to something far worse.

#### Statistics

**Damage** 2d12 slashing
**Hands** 2
**Group** Sword
**Size** Large

#### Fundamental Runes

- +2 potency
- Striking
- Wounding

#### Description

Amiri’s sword houses a bound soul, imprisoned rather than slain. The blade does not communicate directly, but it reacts to danger, spiritual proximity, and attempts at destruction. Once awakened, it repaired itself after being broken, revealing the presence of the trapped spirit within.

The sword counts as magical for the purposes of overcoming resistances and immunities.

#### Awakening — Refusal to Break

**Frequency** once per day

The first time each day the sword would be destroyed or rendered unusable, it instead repairs itself at the end of the round, restoring itself to full functionality. This effect does not prevent temporary penalties or conditions, only permanent destruction.

#### Drawback — The Hunter Beyond the Veil

The soul bound within Amiri’s sword has a brother, also cursed by the First World. This brother-spirit is aware of the blade’s existence and location and is actively seeking to reclaim the trapped soul—by force if necessary.

While the soul remains bound within the blade, the following conditions apply:

- The brother-spirit periodically manifests through undead, haunts, possessed creatures, or other spiritual agents, as determined by the GM. These manifestations are relentless and show no concern for collateral damage.
- The spirit will pursue the sword across great distances and over long periods, abandoning other goals to reclaim the soul within.
- Any creature carrying or protecting the blade becomes a valid target. The spirit will kill without hesitation to reach it.
- These manifestations grow more frequent and dangerous over time if no effort is made to free the bound soul.

This drawback cannot be suppressed, bargained with, or mitigated by conventional magical means. Only freeing the bound soul through the appropriate ritual can end the pursuit.

#### Resolution

If the soul within the blade is freed, the brother-spirit’s pursuit ceases immediately. The sword’s traits and abilities should be re-evaluated, as the curse binding it is broken and the blade’s nature fundamentally changes.
