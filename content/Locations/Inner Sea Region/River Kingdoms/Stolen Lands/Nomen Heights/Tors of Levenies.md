# Tors of Levenies


### The Tors of Levenies

Rising from the northern [Kamelands](../Greenbelt/Kamelands/Kamelands.md) like the shattered bones of the world, the Tors of Levenies dominate the horizon—a jagged wall of stone thrust skyward by forgotten cataclysm. From afar they gleam pale and silent in the sun; up close, they are a realm of cliffs, mist, and echoes, where the wind moans like an ancient voice remembering its grief. Few who venture into the Tors return unchanged.

#### Geography

The western faces of the Tors are sheer cliffs that plunge hundreds of feet into the valleys below, while the eastern slopes rise more gently but are broken by hidden chasms, cold tarns, and sudden drop-offs. Even experienced climbers find themselves misled by false ridges and echoing ravines. Beneath the peaks lies a vast labyrinth of caverns, some natural and others shaped by the hands of giants. Explorers speak of wyverns nesting in the crags, blind serpents writhing through the depths, and veins of strange ore that glow faintly like embers in the dark. In the deepest tunnels, carvings of one-eyed titans hint at civilizations far older than memory.

#### Talon Peak and the Roc’s Nest

The tallest and most famous of the Tors is **Talon Peak**, a needle of black stone rising higher than any mountain in the region. Upon its windswept summit nests the legendary **Roc of Talon Peak**, a creature so vast that its wings darken the Kamelands when it takes flight. Scholars debate whether the bird is a natural marvel or the last survivor of a bygone age, but the people of Thumping Waters know this much—the Tubthumpers climbed the peak and lived to tell of it. Their journey to retrieve one of the Roc’s eggs has become the stuff of song, proof that courage can reach even the heavens themselves.

#### The Valley of the Dead

Southeast of Talon Peak lies the **Valley of the Dead**, a bleak gorge steeped in fog and silence. The valley floor is carpeted with bones—some human, some centaur, and some belonging to creatures long extinct. The cliffs that hem it in are carved with the faint outlines of gigantic faces, their eyes hollow and weeping streaks of mineral-stained water. The air tastes of iron and old curses. No animal will enter, and travelers report hearing whispers that echo their own heartbeats. The [Nomen Centaurs](../../../../../Groups/Nomen%20Centaurs.md) say the valley is cursed ground, the place where an ancient war ended and the dead were swallowed whole by the earth.

#### Vordakai’s Tomb

Directly west of the Valley of the Dead rises one of the most haunting sights in all the [Stolen Lands](../Stolen%20Lands.md): a solitary island at the center of a dark lake, crowned by a **six-hundred-foot column of stone**. This monolithic spire, called **Vordakai’s Spire**, is no natural formation. Its entire height has been hollowed and carved into a single cyclopean tomb—the final resting place of the lich-lord **Vordakai**, last remnant of the ancient cyclops empire that once ruled these lands.

Within those towering walls, the Tubthumpers uncovered a labyrinth of petrified bodies, undead sentinels, and chambers fit for giants. Here they learned the grim fate of Varnhold, whose souls the lich had stolen to rebuild his kingdom. Deep within the column’s heart, the heroes of Thumping Waters confronted Vordakai himself and ended his centuries-long slumber in a battle that shook the mountain. Their victory freed the people of Varnhold and sealed the lich’s power beneath stone once more.

The spire now stands silent above the still lake. The republic has barred passage to the island, and even the bravest scholars visit only under guard. Locals swear that on clear nights the lake reflects the column perfectly, forming a single unbroken tower stretching into the stars—an omen that the eye of Vordakai still watches, waiting for the world to forget.

#### Legends and Superstition

The Tors of Levenies are a land where myth and memory mingle. The centaurs say the mountains are the broken ribs of a slain god; the dwarves claim they were raised to bind an ancient giant’s heart. Wanderers speak of lights moving through the mists, of hoofbeats echoing where no beasts walk, and of a single vast eye that opens in the cliffs when the moon is full. Whether these are remnants of the lich’s fading magic or something older still, none can say.

For the people of Thumping Waters, the Tors remain both monument and warning—a place that proves the republic’s courage, but also reminds them how close the past always lies beneath their feet. From Talon Peak’s summit, one can see the whole of the Stolen Lands spread below, shining like a promise. Yet every gust of wind from the east carries the same quiet message: not everything buried stays dead.
