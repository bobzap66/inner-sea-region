# Dunsward


The frontier region known as Dunsward forms the easternmost reach of the Stolen Lands, where the rolling plains give way to the endless steppes of Casmaron and the haunted ruins of ancient Iobaria. This is a land of wide horizons and older memories—a place where the wind itself seems to carry the weight of centuries. To the west lie the settled territories of Thumping Waters; to the north, the distant city of Restov watches uneasily over the border.

#### The Land

Dunsward is a country of low, grassy plains, scattered with ancient stone outcroppings and lonely barrows that predate recorded history. The soil is rich but rarely tilled, for few dare to settle where the centaurs ride. Herds of wild horses and aurochs roam the grasslands, and storms sweep across the steppe with terrifying beauty. To travelers from the Greenbelt, the sky feels impossibly vast here, the stars sharp and cold against the dark.

Ruins of Iobarian makeways and crumbling fortresses still dot the land, their stones carved with strange runes that scholars of Thumping Waters struggle to decipher. Some believe Dunsward was once a vital trade route linking the north and east before time and tragedy erased its roads from the maps. Now, only centaurs and the most daring explorers dare to cross its breadth.

#### The Nomen Centaurs

The proud Nomen tribe rules Dunsward, as they have since before the rise of Brevoy. They are fierce, traditional, and deeply spiritual, revering their ancestors and the spirits of the land with equal devotion. Their herds and hunting parties roam the plains freely, bound by the ancient laws of the steppe. To them, Dunsward is not merely territory—it is sacred ground, the burial place of heroes and the body of their goddess, who they say once ran these hills in mortal form.

For generations, an uneasy truce has existed between the Nomen and the humans of the west. Neither side crosses the border without cause. The Nomen do not raid settled lands, and in return, the humans do not build upon the plains. But that line grows thinner with each passing year. Scouts of Thumping Waters have begun exploring the eastern marches, seeking trade and diplomacy—but to the centaurs, even peaceful intrusion feels like a threat. A single misstep could turn parley into bloodshed.

#### Politics and Tension

Since the rise of Thumping Waters, the balance of power in the Stolen Lands has shifted. What was once a chaotic wilderness now bears the mark of human order and ambition, and Dunsward stands at the edge of that expansion. The Nomen watch the growing republic warily, uncertain whether its promises of freedom and unity will extend to them—or consume them.

Rumors from travelers speak of changing winds among the centaur tribes. Some Nomen elders advocate diplomacy, curious about the Tubthumpers’ deeds and the defeat of the bandit lords. Others speak of prophecy, warning that the coming of a new kingdom heralds the awakening of old powers buried beneath the plains. In the high barrows, fires burn at night—signal beacons or rituals, none can say for sure. Dunsward holds its breath, waiting to see whether the future will bring alliance or war.

#### Present Day

Today, Dunsward remains a no-man’s-land between two worlds: the ordered republic of Thumping Waters and the boundless steppe of Casmaron. Few humans live here, and those who do are hardy traders or wanderers who understand the old ways. The centaurs still rule the open plains, swift as the wind and proud as their ancestors, and the ruins of Iobaria sleep beneath the grass, whispering to those who dream of forgotten empires.

Whatever shape the future takes, Dunsward will remain a threshold—between civilization and wildness, between past and promise, and between the living and the long-dead gods of the plains.
