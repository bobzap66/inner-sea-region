# Thumpington

![](../../../../../../../assets/images/shared-cover-2c2d28b87bde.png)

*Compiled by the College of Letters and Public Record, [Free University of Thumping Waters](Free%20University%20of%20Thumping%20Waters.md), with archival assistance from the [Ministry of Communication](../../Ruling%20Council/Ministry%20of%20Communication.md) and selections from the journals of [Linzi](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/NPCs/Linzi.md)*

> Thumpington was not built because this was an easy place to live. It was built because enough people decided difficulty was a poor reason to leave.
>
> — Linzi

Thumpington is the capital of the Republic of Thumping Waters and the largest settlement upon the Shrike River. It began as a rough camp of prospectors, laborers, merchants, and hopeful settlers drawn to the Crack Rock gold strike. In six turbulent years it has become the seat of a national government, a center of trade and learning, and the civic heart of a republic stretching across much of the [Stolen Lands](../../../Stolen%20Lands.md).

The city's confidence is recent and hard-earned. Thumpington has survived wilderness isolation, political unrest, the attack of the enormous owlbear remembered as the Beast of Thumpington, repeated supernatural crises, and the War of the River Kings. Its people have rebuilt homes, bridges, roads, and public trust often enough that construction has become nearly as important to the capital's identity as the structures themselves.

### The City upon the Shrike

Thumpington stands primarily upon the northern bank of the Shrike River. Its waterfront developed around Shrike Landing, where prospectors and supply boats first came ashore, while newer streets climb toward the civic heights occupied by Castle Thumpington, Charter Hall, the ministry offices, and residences built as the government expanded.

Endurance Bridge carries travelers, carts, and livestock across the river. The broad stone span replaced an earlier wooden bridge destroyed by the Beast of Thumpington. Its keystone bears the word *Endure*, the same word carved upon the memorial to those killed in the attack. Locals sometimes call it the Enduring Span, though official maps retain the formal name. The bridge is both a piece of essential infrastructure and the city's preferred answer to anyone who mistakes survival for good fortune.

Stone walls protect the settlement. Within them, paved streets, sewers, public wells, and magical streetlamps distinguish the capital from the mining camp it once was. The lamps cast a warm amber light after sunset and have become one of the most recognizable sights in the Republic. They are admired by visitors, appreciated by night workers, and blamed for attracting insects by citizens who believe every public improvement should also repeal summer.

The city's growth has not followed a perfect plan. Workshops stand beside houses older than the roads serving them. Taverns occupy buildings that began as storehouses. Government offices spread into neighboring properties when ministries outgrew their original rooms. Thumpington's architecture therefore records its history visibly: timber frontier buildings, rebuilt stone quarters, ambitious civic halls, religious foundations, and new institutions competing for space within the same walls.

  

### From Gold Camp to Capital

The Crack Rock gold strike drew the first large wave of permanent settlers. Prospectors arrived seeking wealth, followed by traders, laborers, craftspeople, farmers, brewers, guards, and families whose livelihoods did not depend upon personally finding gold. What began as a camp soon became the most practical location for the government authorized by the Republic's founding charter.

On 1 Desnus, 4710 AR, the Republic of Thumping Waters was formally founded with Thumpington as its capital. Its founders possessed an exploration charter, several hard-won alliances, and considerably less administrative experience than the work required. Early government shared space with ordinary town life. Council business, supply contracts, petitions, military planning, and arguments about roads took place wherever a table and enough chairs could be found.

Castle Thumpington and Charter Hall eventually established a permanent civic center. Mansions and official residences followed, as did the Blue Star Exchange, the Hall of Common Works, an embassy, Bridgeward Barracks, the expanding Goldbank Market, and the offices that would become the Republic's ministries. Thumpington ceased to be merely the founders' headquarters and became a capital whose institutions continued operating while those founders were absent, injured, or beneath ruins whose builders had left discouraging inscriptions.

  

### The Beast of Thumpington

In late 4711 AR, while the Tubthumpers were away from the capital, an enormous maddened owlbear attacked Thumpington. The creature tore through streets and buildings, destroyed the wooden bridge, flattened the shrine of [Sarenrae](../../../../../../../Rules/Deities/Sarenrae.md), and killed more than thirty people before disappearing into the western woods. Gershwin the smith was among the dead. Fires and structural collapse left much of the settlement damaged, while the absence of its famous protectors caused fear to harden into anger.

The Tubthumpers returned to smoke visible long before they reached the city. They first assisted with relief, spoke to [Survivors](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Abomination%20Vaults/Vignettes%20-%20Abomination%20Vaults/Survivors.md), and helped stabilize public order. On Neth 10 they departed to hunt the Beast, tracking it through the [Narlmarches](../../../Greenbelt/Narlmarches.md) to a lair filled with enormous fungi, spiders, and other predators. They killed the owlbear on Neth 12.

Within the den they found a starving owlbear cub. Mairi refused to kill it for the destruction caused by its mother. She fed it, brought it home, and named it [Lorcan](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/Characters/Animal%20Companions/Lorcan.md), meaning *little fierce one*. Lorcan later grew into a familiar guardian around the capital's training grounds. Citizens are advised that his history of good behavior does not convert him into a suitable animal for unsupervised petting.

Rebuilding continued well after the Beast's death. Endurance Bridge was raised in stone. Roads and homes were repaired. A sewer system and broader paved avenues improved the reconstructed quarters. The [Temple of the First Light](Temple%20of%20the%20First%20Light.md) expanded its relief work, and [Tristian](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/NPCs/Tristian.md)'s Dawn, founded after the attack with Tristian's financial support, became both a home and a school for children who had lost families or security.

Every Fireday, candles are lit at the memorial bearing the names of those killed. Their [Reflections](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Abomination%20Vaults/Vignettes%20-%20Abomination%20Vaults/Reflections.md) upon the Shrike remain one of the city's quietest public observances.

  

### Government and Public Institutions

Thumpington is the seat of the Ruling Council and the Republic's eight ministries. Castle Thumpington contains council chambers and executive offices, while Charter Hall houses courts, civic records, petition rooms, and much of the daily administration of the capital. Ministry buildings, archives, storehouses, and official residences occupy the surrounding quarter. The capital also receives foreign representatives through its embassy and serves as the center from which national budgets, laws, military orders, public notices, and a remarkable quantity of correspondence travel across the Republic.

The government is one of the city's largest employers, though the boundary between public administration and ordinary civic life is rarely clean. Printers produce both official forms and newspapers. Scholars consult for ministries while disputing their conclusions. Merchants supply armies and then petition the Treasury concerning payment. Citizens encounter national government not as an abstract power but as clerks, guards, judges, couriers, inspectors, healers, and queues in buildings that were not designed for queues.

Second Dawn Hospital was completed during the War of the River Kings. It provides organized treatment beyond the work of temples, individual healers, and military surgeons. The name reflects both the Sarenite tradition of healing and the hope that recovery grants each patient another beginning. Construction required only half the lumber originally reserved for the project. The unused timber remained public property despite several persuasive private proposals involving porches.

  

### Learning, Archives, and the Press

The Lantern Archive, Thumpington's combined public library and museum, preserves books, maps, relics, public records, and objects gathered during the Republic's early expeditions. Its collections include ordinary civic materials alongside works recovered from abandoned settlements, ancient ruins, enemy strongholds, and private holdings whose previous owners displayed greater enthusiasm for acquisition than cataloguing. The institution's name refers both to the city's famous streetlamps and to the conviction that knowledge should illuminate public life.

The Compass Tower and the city's magical shops made Thumpington a center of supernatural study before it possessed a formal university. The tower's name reflects its original purpose: not merely to accumulate magical power, but to help the Republic find a safe direction through unfamiliar phenomena. The [Ministry of Magic](../../Ruling%20Council/Ministry%20of%20Magic.md) added government records concerning curses, Blooms, artifacts, planar disturbances, and unusual creatures. These collections proved essential when the Republic confronted dangers whose principal weakness could not be discovered by striking them repeatedly, although striking remained an important part of several investigations.

The Government Printing Office grew from press components secretly purchased by Linzi and recovered from the Jaggedbriar Coven in 4711 AR. With [Eobald](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/NPCs/Eobald.md)'s assistance, the press became operational after nearly a year of repair and assembly. It first produced laws, forms, proclamations, and public notices. On 1 Sarenith, 4714 AR, it printed the first issue of the *Lantern and Ledger*, now the national newspaper of Thumping Waters.

The paper changed the capital's public life. Council decisions, settlement news, casualty reports, advertisements, corrections, obituaries, and arguments could circulate in consistent form rather than depending entirely upon handwritten copies and tavern retellings. The government press remains a public institution, while the newspaper maintains its own masthead and editorial practices under Linzi and Managing Editor Mara Venn.

  

#### The Free University

The Free University of Thumping Waters was chartered in 4716 AR as a public institution independent of any single ministry, temple, patron, ancestry, or magical tradition. Its principal campus occupies a growing collection of halls within Thumpington and shares access to the city's archives, printing network, workshops, and public institutions. Its principal reading room and research collection, the Open Treasury, is housed within the Lantern Archive and is open to townspeople as well as enrolled scholars.

Eobald Hall houses the College of Letters and Public Record and is named for the human lecturer, printer, and theatrical director whose work helped establish both the government press and the university's tradition of public scholarship. The Surveyors' Court serves the College of Roads and Rivers. Four Lamps Hall brings arcane, divine, occult, and primal scholars together within the College of the Four Traditions. The Irregulars' Workshop provides the practical teaching hall of [Nok-Nok](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/NPCs/Nok-Nok.md) College, where unconventional experience is treated as a qualification rather than an embarrassment.

Nok-Nok College and the Nok-Nok Irregulars Fellowship honor the late governor of [Greengripe](../Greengripe/Greengripe.md) by extending advanced education to students whose ability would not be recognized by conventional academies.

The arrival of students and teachers has already changed neighborhoods around the university halls. Rooms once rented to visiting merchants now house scholars. Taverns advertise debates with the same confidence previously reserved for musicians. Booksellers have discovered that required reading is more profitable when instructors disagree about which edition is required.

  

### Faith, Healing, and the Dawnflower

The faith of Sarenrae occupies a prominent place in Thumpington without serving as the Republic's official religion. Tristian, priest of the Dawnflower and current Minister of Magic, helped make compassion, healing, and redemption visible parts of the capital's recovery. The [Temple of the First Light](Temple%20of%20the%20First%20Light.md) provides worship, counsel, teaching, food, and care to people who require assistance regardless of whether they arrive with an appropriate theological explanation.

The temple's braziers burn with enduring flame, and its windows cast gold across the street at dawn. Tristian's Dawn was established after the Beast's attack with funds provided by Tristian and grew under the temple's protection into a school and home. Its name honors both its benefactor and the Sarenite promise that hardship need not determine the shape of a child's future. Locals often shorten the name to *the Dawn*. Tristian remains a familiar visitor, carrying books, bread, and the conviction that care offered quietly is no less sacred than a public miracle.

Behind the temple rises the unfinished Cathedral of the Dawn. Designed to stand taller than Castle Thumpington, the cathedral is intended to mark the Republic's tenth anniversary in 4720 AR. Its construction is supported by contributions from artisans, wealthy patrons, congregants, and ordinary citizens. Admirers describe the future spire as faith rising above temporal power. Treasury officials describe it as a multiyear capital project and have asked that both descriptions include cost estimates.

Other faiths maintain shrines, priests, congregations, and visiting representatives throughout the city. Thumpington's religious life reflects the same mixture found across the Republic: inherited traditions, frontier necessity, immigrants' devotions, personal conversions, and citizens who become notably reverent when a supernatural danger begins asking questions.

  

### Trade and Industry

Goldbank Market receives grain from [Olegton](../Olegton/Olegton.md), iron and metalwork from [Sootscale Valley](../Sootscale%20Valley/Sootscale%20Valley.md), timber from [Embeth Hall](../Embeth%20Hall/Embeth%20Hall.md), goods carried through [Varnhold](../Varnhold/Varnhold.md) and the western marches, and foreign wares arriving through [Brevoy](../../../../../Brevoy/Brevoy.md) and the river trade. Locals call it the Goldbank, a name inherited from the market's beginnings beside the prospectors' weighing tables. Gold first established the settlement, but government, crafts, brewing, milling, construction, printing, education, hospitality, and commerce now support far more of its people.

Shrike Landing remains crowded with cargo, passengers, messengers, and boatmen familiar with the river and its connected waters. Warehouses and workshops stand close to its piers, while specialized shops occupy the busier streets climbing toward the civic center. Smithies, mills, breweries, artisan ateliers, magical suppliers, and general traders ensure that a visitor can purchase nearly anything commonly available in the Republic and receive confident directions toward someone who claims to sell the rest.

The Hall of Common Works mediates among established trades and newer professions created by the capital's growth. The Blue Star Exchange, the city's principal bank and counting house, works alongside the Treasury offices to support public contracts and long-distance commerce. Its name derives from the blue star upon the Republic's currency and seals, though detractors maintain it was chosen because bankers prefer celestial objects safely beyond taxation. The city has learned that national ambition produces reliable demand for stone, paper, ink, glass, lumber, food, trained labor, and explanations for why delivery will occur next week rather than this one.

  

### Culture and Daily Life

Thumpington retains the openness and noise of a frontier town despite its government buildings and growing grandeur. Laborers, soldiers, civil servants, scholars, priests, merchants, adventurers, refugees, and foreign visitors share its markets and public houses. News travels rapidly, opinion travels faster, and the return of the Tubthumpers can still empty a street before any official announcement explains whether the crowd is gathering to celebrate or take shelter.

*The Dawn's Rest* provides expensive rooms and restrained hospitality to diplomats, prosperous merchants, and visitors who prefer their adventures described after dinner. *The Thumper's Drum* serves laborers, soldiers, students, travelers, and anyone willing to accept that a crowded room is part of the establishment's character. Numerous smaller inns, cookshops, breweries, and taverns compete for customers across the city.

*The Roc's Omelette*, founded by the ambitious cook Jamery Gerbasken, is among the capital's most fashionable dining establishments. Its name commemorates the enormous roc egg recovered from Talon Peak by the Tubthumpers for Gerbasken's celebrated omelette competition. Cooks came bearing cheeses, peppers, herbs, sausages, and opinions strong enough to require official mediation; the resulting festival proved so successful that Gerbasken established the tavern without drawing upon the Republic's construction reserves. The original omelette is long gone, but the establishment retains a reputation for elaborate meals, civic celebrations, and private parties—including Linzi's final unmarried evening before her wedding to Seamus. Gerbasken assures diners that no current dish contains genuine roc egg, citing expense, ecology, and the structural limitations of the roof.

Riverlight Hall hosts music, debates, public meetings, seasonal celebrations, and performances that often continue into the surrounding streets. Its tall river-facing windows inspired the name, especially during evening events when their light can be seen from Endurance Bridge. The Lantern Archive offers quieter forms of entertainment. The Compass Tower, university halls, and workshops attract citizens interested in discovery, while Goldbank Market attracts citizens interested in discoveries that can be purchased and carried home.

Thumpington's culture balances memorial and celebration. Bells and candles commemorate the dead. Festivals reclaim streets once used by soldiers and relief crews. Official ceremonies rarely remain solemn for their entire duration, partly because the Republic values humor and partly because several of its most honored citizens would regard excessive dignity as a hostile act.

  

### Riverbend Park and Greengripe Amphitheatre

Riverbend Park opened during the Victory Festival of 6 Gozran, 4715 AR. Built after the War of the River Kings, it gave the capital a public place intended for rest rather than defense, supply, or emergency shelter. Paths, lawns, river views, and open gathering spaces now draw families, veterans, students, performers, and workers escaping offices during favorable weather.

The Greengripe Amphitheatre stands within the park and honors Nok-Nok, Governor of Greengripe, and the members of Nok-Nok's Irregulars who died in the war. The Irregulars were goblins, bombardiers, scouts, survivors, and citizens who stood between [Pitax](../Pitax/Pitax.md)'s forces and their homes. Their memorial was deliberately made as a place for music, laughter, argument, and public gathering rather than a silent monument separated from daily life.

> May every cheer raised within the Greengripe Amphitheatre remind us that the freedom to celebrate was purchased by those willing to stand in its defense.
>
> — Memorial dedication

University ceremonies, visiting performers, civic addresses, and festivals now use the amphitheatre. Its association with Nok-Nok College has encouraged an increasing number of fireworks. Municipal authorities continue to maintain that commemoration does not suspend the capital's rules concerning explosions before breakfast.

  

### Principal Landmarks and Services

- **Castle Thumpington:** Seat of the Ruling Council and executive government.
- **Charter Hall and the Ministry Quarter:** Civic administration, records, courts, and public offices.
- **Endurance Bridge:** Stone bridge rebuilt after the Beast's attack and marked with the word *Endure*.
- **Temple of Sarenrae:** Center of worship, healing, relief, and education.
- **Cathedral of the Dawn:** Monumental temple under construction for the Republic's tenth anniversary.
- **The Free University:** Public university founded in 4716 AR.
- **The Lantern Archive:** Combined public library and museum containing archives, relics, scholarly materials, and the university's Open Treasury.
- **Government Printing Office:** Printer of laws, public notices, and the *Lantern and Ledger*.
- **Riverbend Park:** Postwar public park upon the river.
- **Greengripe Amphitheatre:** Performance space and memorial to Nok-Nok and the fallen Irregulars.
- **Second Dawn Hospital:** Public medical institution completed during the War of the River Kings.
- **Tristian's Dawn:** Sarenite home and school founded with Tristian's financial support after the Beast's attack.
- **The Compass Tower:** Center for magical practice and consultation predating the university.
- **Goldbank Market and Shrike Landing:** Principal centers of trade, shipping, and public commerce.
- **Riverlight Hall:** Venue for performances, debates, meetings, and seasonal celebrations.
- **The Hall of Common Works:** Guildhall serving the capital's crafts, trades, and emerging professions.
- **Bridgeward Barracks:** Headquarters and quarters for the guards responsible for the bridge, river approaches, and adjoining streets.
- **The Blue Star Exchange:** Principal bank and counting house of the capital.
- **The Dawn's Rest, The Thumper's Drum, and The Roc's Omelette:** Three of the capital's best-known establishments, ranging from diplomatic lodging to crowded public house and fashionable dining.

  

### Thumpington in 4716 AR

Thumpington entered 4716 AR victorious, grieving, and busy. The War of the River Kings no longer threatens its streets, but veteran care, reconstruction, diplomatic work, and support for damaged settlements continue. Pitax's future relationship with the Republic remains under negotiation. The western war has ended without making the western frontier safe.

The capital is also preparing for dangers that armies and stone walls may not answer. Reports of the Wild Hunt, increased unease among the fey, and discoveries beneath Candlemere have drawn attention from the Ministry of Magic, the [Ministry of Intelligence](../../Ruling%20Council/Ministry%20of%20Intelligence.md), temples, archives, and the new university. Thumpington's scholars study warnings recovered from ancient sites while its builders continue work upon hospitals, halls, roads, parks, and the cathedral.

That combination defines the modern city. Thumpington is proud without believing itself finished, prosperous without forgetting ruin, and increasingly learned without losing the practical suspicion that every theory should eventually survive contact with weather, budgets, and actual monsters.

At sunset, the Shrike catches the last light beneath Endurance Bridge. The Cathedral of the Dawn's unfinished spire turns gold above the rooftops. Streetlamps, hearths, temple windows, university halls, tavern doors, and the printing office answer one by one. The city does not shine because darkness never reached it. It shines because its people keep lighting it again.

  

### From [Linzi's Journal](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/Vignettes%20-%20Kingmaker/Moments/Linzi%27s%20Journal.md): The Beast and the Broken Bridge

*The worst thing about heroes is that the monsters never seem to wait for them to come home.*

When the Beast attacked, Thumpington burned without the Tubthumpers there to defend it. The bridge fell. The shrine was broken. People we knew became names we had to carve into stone. By the time our heroes returned, the town was ash, accusation, and the terrible silence that follows a bell nobody has the heart to ring.

They could not undo what happened. They helped us rebuild, and then they went into the woods to make certain it did not happen again. When they returned, the Beast was dead. More importantly, they returned carrying its hungry cub instead of another trophy.

The bells rang that night for the first time since the fire. I remember the sound crossing the broken river and people stepping out of their homes to hear it together. That is how I remember Thumpington: not as the place where the bridge broke, but as the place where we learned to build one that would endure.

—Linzi

*Editor's note: Linzi maintains that she did not cry during the bell-ringing. Her earliest draft has suffered water damage in a remarkably localized area.*

  

### From Linzi's Journal: The Hatchling

Lorcan was small when Mairi found him, although *small* is a relative term when discussing owlbears. He was starving, frightened, and surrounded by the aftermath of a fight he could not understand. Mairi knelt in bloodstained armor and offered him food.

People said she was bringing a monster into the city that another monster had nearly destroyed. Mairi believed that strength and kindness were not opposites. Lorcan grew beneath her care, followed her through the training fields, attempted to eat several objects whose owners considered them important, and eventually became one of Thumpington's guardians.

The Beast destroyed our city. Its child protects it. That is not irony. It is what happens when someone decides the next story does not have to end like the last one.

—Linzi

## General Details

##### From the Journal of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

*The Hatchling*

Everyone in Thumping Waters knows about the Beast of Thumpington—how it burned, how it fell, and how the Tubthumpers slew it. What most people forget is that the story didn’t end in that cave. After the fight, when the echo of steel and flame faded, Mairi found something small amid the ruin: a single owlbear cub, half-starved, half-terrified, blinking at the heroes who had just slain its mother. There was a moment, or so I’m told, when no one moved. Then Mairi knelt, blood still fresh on her armor, and offered the creature food instead of fear.

Most thought she was mad. An owlbear? Tame? It was like trying to adopt an avalanche. But Mairi didn’t see a monster—she saw a chance to end the cycle of violence that had nearly destroyed our home. And so she named him **Lorcan**, meaning “little fierce one” in the old tongue, and brought him back to Thumpington. The first few months were… lively. Lorcan tried to eat the laundry, the stables, and once, briefly, Ant’s quiver. But Mairi was patient, and the cub was clever. Where others saw claws, she saw potential. Where others heard roars, she heard loyalty waiting to be earned.

It’s been two years now. Lorcan is no longer small—he stands nearly to a horse’s shoulder, his feathers thick and his eyes bright with that same wild intelligence. He patrols the training fields outside Thumpington, following Mairi like a living shadow. Children wave from the walls when they see him. Some still flinch, remembering the Beast that came before, but even the fearful admit: this one is different. This one protects.

I’ve seen them together—woman and owlbear, warrior and ward. There’s trust there, and something softer too. Mairi doesn’t talk about what the cub means to her, but I think Lorcan is proof of what she’s always believed: that strength and kindness aren’t opposites. That sometimes the only way to break a curse is to love what once frightened you. The Beast destroyed Thumpington. Lorcan guards it. That’s not irony—it’s grace.

*—Linzi*

Editor’s note: Citizens are reminded not to pet Lorcan without permission, no matter how fluffy he looks. He is still, technically, an apex predator.
