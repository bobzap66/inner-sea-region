# Free University of Thumping Waters

![[shared-cover-2c2d28b87bde.png]]

*An institutional profile maintained by the Office of Public Record, with additions supplied by the university's founding faculty*

> Knowledge found in danger, purchased through sacrifice, or preserved by chance does not belong only to the person fortunate enough to carry it home.
>
> — From the Charter of the Free University

The Free University of Thumping Waters is a public institution of higher learning headquartered in [[Thumpington]]. Chartered in 4716 AR during the Republic's reconstruction after the War of the River Kings, it was founded to preserve knowledge gathered during the nation's turbulent first years, train people capable of serving its growing communities, and make advanced learning available beyond the wealthy families, temples, and private academies that have traditionally controlled it.

The university is young enough that several of its halls are older than the institution occupying them, its faculty contains more experienced practitioners than conventional academics, and the precise difference between a laboratory and a securely reinforced cellar remains the subject of continuing inspection. Its ambitions are nevertheless national. The founders intend it to become a place where the Republic studies its past, prepares for dangers still poorly understood, and teaches knowledge useful to people who cannot abandon their homes for years of distant study.

  

### At a Glance

**Founded:** 4716 AR

**Principal Campus:** Thumpington

**Type:** Chartered public university

**Common Name:** The Free University

**Motto:** What We Learn, We Owe to All

**Admission:** Open by examination, demonstrated ability, or community nomination

**Principal Library:** The Open Treasury, within the Lantern Archive

**Publication Mark:** FUTW

  

### Founding

Thumping Waters acquired knowledge long before it possessed an institution capable of caring for it. The Tubthumpers returned from expeditions with maps, tablets, magical formulae, witness accounts, foreign records, natural specimens, and books recovered from abandoned settlements, ruined fortresses, Vordakai's tomb-city, the House of a Hundred Doors, and other places where scholarship was rarely the most immediate danger.

Responsibility for those materials fell across several institutions. The [[Ministry of Communication]] preserved the national record and operated the government press. The [[Ministry of Magic]] gathered reports concerning curses, Blooms, artifacts, and planar disturbances. The public library and museum whose collections are now housed in the Lantern Archive accumulated books, relics, and recovered collections. [[Jubilost Narthropple]], [[Roald Celinnas]], priests, explorers, craftspeople, and independent scholars maintained bodies of knowledge that depended heavily upon particular people remaining alive, available, and willing to answer questions.

The war with [[Pitax]] exposed the weakness of that arrangement. Records were scattered, trained specialists were drawn into military service, and irreplaceable knowledge remained concentrated in cities and private collections vulnerable to fire, occupation, or political control. The war also demonstrated the strength of institutions that could outlast a ruler. Pitax's theaters, temples, Academy of the Arts, and even the crowded library of the Crow's Feather preserved communities of learning that Irovetti could patronize, intimidate, and exploit without fully possessing.

After the war, educators, ministers, archivists, veterans, and settlement representatives proposed a national university. The Ruling Council granted its charter in 4716 AR. The word *Free* was included to describe both its public purpose and its promised freedom from control by any single ministry, temple, patron, ancestry, or school of magic. It has never meant that buildings, books, meals, ink, glassware, or alchemical fireproofing cost nothing, a distinction the [[Ministry of the Treasury]] requested be recorded prominently.

  

### The University Charter

The charter establishes the university as a public corporation rather than a department of the national government. The Republic provides land, initial funding, legal recognition, and continuing support approved through the national budget. In return, the university must maintain public accounts, preserve its collections, serve communities beyond the capital, and admit students without preference based upon ancestry, sex, wealth, legitimacy of birth, religion, or inherited title.

The university may criticize government policy, publish conclusions inconvenient to its patrons, and refuse political alteration of scholarly findings. It remains subject to ordinary law. Academic freedom does not authorize theft, coercive experimentation, the concealment of an active danger, the opening of sealed extraplanar gates, or describing an avoidable explosion as peer review.

Religious teachers and institutions may participate without surrendering their doctrines. No faith may claim authority over the entire university, and no student is required to profess a religion as a condition of admission. The same principle applies to arcane, divine, occult, and primal traditions: each may be taught according to its own methods, but none may declare all other traditions illegitimate by university decree.

  

### The Founding Colleges

The Free University is organized into colleges that share the central library, press, lecture halls, residences, and administrative offices. Students may belong principally to one college while studying under teachers from several.

  

#### The College of Letters and Public Record

The College of Letters teaches history, languages, rhetoric, literature, diplomacy, journalism, archival practice, and the interpretation of law and public records. It works closely with the Ministry of Communication while remaining institutionally separate from it. Its students assist in preserving oral histories from communities whose traditions do not ordinarily enter written archives and in translating public works into the languages used throughout the Republic.

[[Eobald]], one of [[Linzi]]'s former teachers in Pitax, helped establish the college's instruction in rhetoric, performance, printing, and public communication. His earlier work assembling Thumpington's first press made him particularly insistent that writers understand how texts are reproduced, altered, distributed, and occasionally set backward in type.

The college is principally housed in Eobald Hall, named for the founding lecturer whose work joined the classroom, stage, archive, and press. Its lecture rooms are designed for both conventional instruction and public performances, while its printing rooms allow students to follow a text from composition through typesetting and distribution.

  

#### The College of Roads and Rivers

The College of Roads and Rivers teaches surveying, engineering, architecture, agriculture, navigation, cartography, logistics, and the management of public works. Its name reflects the two systems that bind the Republic's distant settlements together. Instruction combines lectures with supervised work upon roads, bridges, waterways, farms, fortifications, and civic buildings.

Its principal Thumpington building is the Surveyors' Court, arranged around an open yard where maps, models, instruments, paving methods, drainage systems, and proposed structures can be examined at useful scale. Students are reminded that a beautifully drawn bridge which cannot survive a river is an illustration rather than an engineering achievement.

The college maintains traveling instructors and seasonal courses outside Thumpington. Its charter specifically warns that a national university which requires every useful student to leave a frontier community permanently may weaken the places it was founded to serve.

  

#### The College of the Four Traditions

The College of the Four Traditions studies arcane, divine, occult, and primal magic without treating any one tradition as the sole proper foundation of supernatural knowledge. Its subjects include magical theory, ritual practice, planar phenomena, curses, wards, healing, enchanted objects, and the identification of dangerous effects.

Four Lamps Hall is the college's principal teaching building. Four lights burn above its main entrance, each maintained according to a different magical tradition. No official meaning is assigned to which lamp catches first during bad weather, though this has not prevented wagering among students.

The college works closely with the Ministry of Magic but is not its training bureau. Research involving intelligent artifacts, involuntary subjects, active curses, planar breaches, powerful necromancy, or materials recovered from restricted sites requires formal review and suitable containment. Current interests include the First World, [[Wild Hunt]] traditions, ancient cyclops magic, and the Pharasmian and Thresholder remains beneath Candlemere.

  

#### [[Nok-Nok]] College of Practical Arts and Civic Service

Nok-Nok College teaches field medicine, alchemy, emergency response, wilderness survival, defensive construction, settlement administration, scouting, and other disciplines whose value becomes clearest when a plan has failed and people still require help. It is named for Nok-Nok, Hero of the Mite Warrens, governor of [[Greengripe]], commander of Nok-Nok's Irregulars, and one of the Republic's earliest friends.

The Irregulars' Workshop serves as the college's principal practical hall. Its classrooms open directly into work yards, kitchens, treatment rooms, reinforced laboratories, and storage bays intended for exercises that cannot be taught responsibly from a lectern. The workshop's standing rule is that an unconventional method must still leave someone able to explain why it worked.

Nok-Nok spent much of his life insisting that he was a hero. He died east of [[Fort Drelev]] after refusing to abandon the Irregulars as their position collapsed, remaining with his soldiers and buying time for others to escape. The college honors not only his courage but the people he led: goblins, bombardiers, scouts, [[Survivors]], and citizens who did not resemble a parade-ground army but stood between the enemy and their homes.

Its unofficial motto is *A Hero Stays*. The phrase is not taught as an instruction to seek a glorious death. Faculty instead use it to describe the obligations of leadership: preparation before danger, presence during it, and responsibility for those who must live afterward.

  

### Admission and the Nok-Nok Irregulars Fellowship

Applicants may enter through written examination, demonstrated magical or practical ability, previous instruction recognized by the faculty, or nomination from a settlement, temple, guild, military formation, or community authority. Preparatory courses provide literacy, numeracy, academic languages, and other foundations to capable students denied earlier schooling.

The Nok-Nok Irregulars Fellowship supports students whose talent would not be recognized by conventional admissions. Fellows may come from poor households, remote settlements, displaced communities, unconventional apprenticeships, military service, or peoples excluded from established academies. Selection considers courage, ingenuity, loyalty, service, and potential rather than polish.

The fellowship provides tuition, lodging, meals, books, and preparatory instruction. Its recipients are known collectively as the Irregulars. No fellow is required to enter government or military service, but each is expected to return some benefit to the public through teaching, healing, construction, research, civic work, or service to a community of the fellow's choice.

  

### Governance

A Board of Fellows holds the university's charter in trust, approves its budget, appoints its chief officers, and protects the institution's independence. Its founding membership includes educators, scholars, craftspeople, representatives of the Republic's communities, and persons experienced in public administration. No serving minister may command the university by virtue of office alone.

The Rector directs ordinary university affairs. Each college selects a dean under procedures approved by the Board. Faculty govern instruction, examinations, research standards, and admission within the limits of the charter. Student and settlement representatives may petition the Board and participate in designated committees, particularly those concerned with access, housing, and teaching beyond Thumpington.

The university maintains relationships with the national ministries without belonging to any of them. Communication assists with printing and archives. Magic consults upon dangerous research. Interior coordinates land, construction, and instruction in distant settlements. Justice advises upon the charter and protection of research subjects. Treasury audits public funds. Defense and Intelligence may request expert work, but restricted sponsorship must be disclosed to the Board even when its operational details cannot be published.

  

### Libraries, Collections, and the University Press

The Lantern Archive is Thumpington's combined public library and museum. Within it, the Open Treasury serves as the university's principal reading room and research collection. The arrangement keeps advanced holdings beside a library available to ordinary citizens rather than enclosing scholarship within a separate academic collection. The Open Treasury's name expresses the university's belief that preserved knowledge is a form of public wealth.

Materials recovered from dangerous sites may be copied, translated, or studied through the university while the originals remain in secure government or religious custody. Restricted collections are catalogued even when access to their contents is limited, preventing secrecy from becoming disappearance.

Roald Celinnas of the Crow's Feather has advised the university on acquisitions, preservation, and the identification of rare works. Negotiations concerning a formal relationship with the Crow's Feather remain incomplete and do not transfer ownership of Roald's collection. Roald has corrected this point often enough that it has been entered into the institutional record.

The University Press publishes lectures, translations, maps, field reports, catalogues, monographs, and inexpensive practical manuals. It uses the Republic's established printing network by agreement with the Ministry of Communication while maintaining separate editorial review. The press may also publish corrected editions of government research when the evidence has changed.

  

### Standards of Scholarship

University publications are expected to identify their author or compiler, institutional role, principal sources, and the difference between observed fact, supported interpretation, disputed testimony, and conjecture. Anonymous publication is permitted when naming a researcher would create a specific danger, but the responsible editor must preserve the author's identity in a sealed record.

Researchers are encouraged to use the following terms consistently:

- **Confirmed:** Supported by direct examination, reliable records, or multiple independent sources.
- **Probable:** Strongly supported but not directly demonstrated.
- **Contested:** Supported by conflicting evidence or credible disagreement.
- **Unverified:** Reported but not independently confirmed.
- **Conjectural:** A proposed explanation that extends beyond the available evidence.

A university affiliation does not make a conclusion official policy. It indicates that the work was produced or reviewed under the university's standards. Government assessments, religious teachings, personal testimony, and university scholarship may reach different conclusions from the same evidence and should be identified accordingly.

The customary attribution for a university article is:

*Compiled by [Name], [Position or Field], Free University of Thumping Waters, with [contributors or institutional assistance, if applicable]*

  

### Relations with Pitax

The Free University has invited cooperation with Pitax's Academy of the Arts, the Red Crescent Theatre, and the Crow's Feather. Atlee Quinge, Speaker for Arts and Education, and Headmistress Atalia Gitaren have discussed exchanges of teachers, performers, and students. Such cooperation does not place Pitaxian institutions under Thumpington's control.

Some founders favor an eventual federated university in which established institutions retain their own charters while sharing teachers, libraries, recognition of study, and access to public funding. Pitax's political relationship with Thumping Waters remains under negotiation, and no final university arrangement has been adopted.

  

### Traditions and Public Life

Students of Nok-Nok College call themselves Irregulars, a name increasingly applied with varying degrees of accuracy to students throughout the university. The college's emblem is a battered shield before a rising firework. Its highest student honor, the Last to Leave Medal, recognizes someone who protected, supported, or accepted responsibility for others rather than the student with the highest examination marks.

University ceremonies include music, public lectures, demonstrations, debates, and fireworks held in cooperation with the Greengripe Amphitheatre. The timing of the fireworks remains subject to Thumpington law. Greengripe's suspension of its rule against explosions before breakfast upon the night of Nok-Nok's memorial has not been recognized as a permanent academic exemption.

The university remains too young to possess ancient customs, although this has not prevented students from inventing several and providing contradictory accounts of their antiquity. Its most serious tradition is simpler. At the opening of each year, the names of teachers, students, patrons, soldiers, explorers, and unknown preservers whose sacrifices placed knowledge in the university's care are entered into the public record.

> A university should not teach unusual people to become ordinary. It should teach all of us how much we lose when unusual people are never given the chance to begin.
>
> — Linzi, Minister of Communication
