# Longtail Island


### Longtail Island

Once a swampy patch of mud and reeds defended by crude palisades, **Longtail Island** has transformed into one of the most striking success stories in the Republic of Thumping Waters. Nestled in the wide curve of the Shrike River, the island settlement serves as both a symbol of reconciliation and a living example of what happens when strength, cunning, and civic pride pull in the same direction. Under the steady leadership of **Mayor Vesket**—formerly [[King Vesket]] of the Longtail lizardfolk—Longtail Island has shed its tribal past to become a thriving, industrious river town of surprising sophistication.

#### Overview

The island lies between the northern and southern channels of the Shrike River, connected to the mainland by a pair of sturdy **bridges** built under the supervision of Thumping Waters’ Ministry of Works. Once little more than marsh and silt, the island has been drained, paved, and strengthened with stone embankments and a new **sewer system**—a civic marvel the lizardfolk speak of with quiet pride. **Wooden walls** still ring the settlement, though they are reinforced now with watch platforms and a central **watchtower** that keeps a steady eye on the water traffic flowing past day and night.

The muddy huts that once housed the Longtail tribe have been replaced by tidy **houses** and workshops built of timber and clay. **Paved streets** wind through the island’s interior, leading to the heart of the town, where the **town hall** and **keep** stand side by side. The former serves as the center of civic administration; the latter, a stout but elegant stone structure built atop Vesket’s old throne-hall, now houses the garrison and mayoral offices. Between them lies a small park lined with torches and carved totems depicting the Longtail ancestors—an homage to the tribe’s past and its enduring spirit.

#### Leadership

**Mayor Vesket** rules with the same commanding presence that once made him feared as a chieftain, tempered now by the pragmatism of a statesman. Though still towering and sharp-toothed, he has become a surprisingly diplomatic figure, preferring negotiation to intimidation and progress to pride. His motto, carved above the doors of the town hall, reads: “The strong protect; the wise build.” Under his rule, Longtail Island has become a model of cooperation between lizardfolk and humans—an achievement few would have believed possible a decade ago.

Vesket maintains an elite **garrison** and **barracks** staffed by both human and lizardfolk soldiers, trained to patrol the bridges and waterways. His advisers include scholars from [[Thumpington]], river pilots, and elder shamans of the old tribe. The result is a leadership council that blends martial efficiency with ancient intuition—a government as much at home in a council chamber as it is knee-deep in the river mud.

#### Town and Economy

Longtail Island thrives on trade, craftsmanship, and river commerce. Its **lumberyard** and **mill** process timber from upriver forests, while the **smithy** and **specialized artisan’s shop** produce fine tools, weapons, and watercraft fittings for export throughout the republic. A well-stocked **general store** supplies travelers, and the **granary** keeps the island’s stores full even during lean seasons. The **herbalist** trades in potent river herbs and tinctures said to come from “the water’s memory.” Fishermen and artisans share the riverside market with merchants from [[Olegton]] and Fort [[Locations/Inner Sea Region/River Kingdoms/Stolen Lands/Greenbelt/Kamelands/Tuskwater|Tuskwater]], while barges loaded with goods queue along the docks waiting for passage under the island’s twin bridges.

Visitors find comfortable lodging at the island’s **inn**, the *Floodlight Rest*, famous for its roaring hearth and quiet view of the western channel. Though the island’s working population remains largely lizardfolk, many human families have settled here in recent years, drawn by fair wages and strong community. A small **embassy** now hosts visiting diplomats from the capital and the nearby centaur lands of [[Locations/Inner Sea Region/River Kingdoms/Stolen Lands/Nomen Heights/Dunsward|Dunsward]], marking Longtail Island as both frontier and bridge between peoples.

#### Faith and Culture

A modest **shrine** at the island’s center honors both Hanspur, god of rivers, and [[Gozreh]], lord of wind and water—a fusion of old Longtail beliefs and new republican faiths. The shrine’s priesthood includes both lizardfolk and human clerics, who maintain a steady peace between old spirits and new laws. Festivals on the island are lively affairs, full of drums, torches, and music that echoes across the river for miles. Visitors from other towns often remark that the Longtails’ celebrations are as disciplined as they are joyful—a reflection of Vesket’s influence.

At the island’s northern edge lies a quiet **cemetery**, where the Longtail dead are interred beside the fallen from the Bloom. The site’s centerpiece is a carved stone pillar depicting both human and lizardfolk hands lifting the same torch—a powerful reminder that this island, once divided by fear, now thrives through unity.

#### Modern Role

Today, Longtail Island stands as both gateway and guardian of the Shrike River. Its garrisoned bridges control one of the republic’s key trade routes, and its craftsmen provide essential materials for shipbuilding and fortification. The Ministry of Commerce regards it as a vital link in the river network connecting Olegton, Fort Tuskwater, and the southern marches. More importantly, it serves as living proof that the republic’s ideals—cooperation, courage, and renewal—can reshape even the most unlikely of beginnings.

At sunset, the torches along the bridges blaze to life, and the water below mirrors their glow. From the keep’s balcony, Mayor Vesket often watches the light stretch across the river and says, quietly, “Once, the world feared the Longtails. Now it crosses our bridge.”

## General Details

##### From the Journal of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

*The King Who Learned Paperwork*

If you had told me years ago that **King Vesket**—the enormous, scale-covered, spear-waving lizardfolk who once tried to feed us to a ghost—would end up as one of the republic’s most competent mayors, I’d have laughed myself right into the river. And yet here we are. Life’s funny like that. Terrifying, but funny.

Back then, Vesket ruled the Longtail tribe from a muddy throne on an island fortress surrounded by wooden stakes and bad decisions. He was under the thrall of a **will-o’-wisp**, a cruel and clever thing pretending to be the spirit of his ancestors. It whispered lies into his ear—about destiny, vengeance, and glory—and Vesket believed them all. The Tubthumpers freed him from that glowing parasite’s influence, though it took a great deal of shouting, bravery, and one ill-advised lightning spell. When the smoke cleared, the wisp was gone, Vesket was blinking in confusion, and the rest of us were reconsidering our life choices.

What happened next still surprises me. Instead of clinging to his crown or retreating into shame, Vesket chose to rebuild. He cast aside his title, called himself **Mayor Vesket**, and told his people that strength should serve, not rule. I remember him saying, “The river flows forward, not back.” I wrote that one down immediately. It’s still one of my favorite quotes from a seven-foot-tall reptile with better penmanship than Seamus.

When I visited **Longtail Island** again years later, the place had changed completely. The muddy huts were gone, replaced by paved streets, workshops, and a proper keep. The air smelled of sawdust and bread instead of swamp gas. Vesket himself greeted me at the new **town hall**, wearing a cloak instead of a crown and holding a clipboard. He gave me a tour of the new **granary**, the **barracks**, and even the **sewer system** (which he is unreasonably proud of). “The old king fought for power,” he told me. “The new mayor fights for plumbing.”

It’s strange, isn’t it? We used to fear the Longtails. Now we trade with them, celebrate with them, and cross their bridges without a second thought. Watching the torchlight ripple across the river that night, I realized something simple and true: redemption isn’t a miracle—it’s hard work, well lit. And no one proves that better than Mayor Vesket of Longtail Island, the king who conquered his own ghosts and built something better in their place.

*—Linzi*
