# Tailforge Atelier

![[tailforge-atelier-portrait.png]]

*A Lantern and Ledger guide to the renowned riverside forge of Longtail Island, compiled from the settlement gazetteer, guild records, and the travel recollections of Velka of the Sootscale*

> Metal is the river that forgot how to move. Heat it, strike it, and remind it.
>
> — Ssekesh

Tailforge Atelier is a specialized riverside forge and metalworking house on Longtail Island. Its master smith, Ssekesh, produces fine tools, weapons, ceremonial work, bridge hardware, and watercraft fittings for customers throughout the Republic of Thumping Waters. The atelier is best known for its current-powered bellows, open riverward forge, and the disciplined patience with which its craftspeople combine Longtail tradition and modern engineering.

Longtail Island's municipal smithy performs most routine repair and high-volume work. Tailforge Atelier accepts projects requiring unusual materials, close tolerances, original design, or a client prepared to discuss one hinge for an entire afternoon.

  

### At a Glance

**Type:** Specialist forge, artisan workshop, and apprenticeship house

**Location:** Riverside workshop district, Longtail Island

**Master Smith:** Ssekesh

**Primary Work:** Fine tools, weapons, watercraft fittings, bridge hardware, and ceremonial metalwork

**Known For:** Tide-driven bellows, river quenching, mixed-material work, and Velka's reforged trident

**Materials:** Iron, steel, bronze, brass, copper, imported coral, shell, timber, and worked stone

**Associated Person:** Velka of the Sootscale

**Workshop Maxim:** *The strong protect; the wise build*

  

### A Forge between Two Channels

Longtail Island occupies a wide curve of the Shrike River between northern and southern channels. Twin bridges connect its paved streets and workshops to the mainland, while barges queue beneath the watchtower and along reinforced stone embankments. Tailforge Atelier stands close to the working river, where freight, timber, fishermen, and craftspeople pass throughout the day.

The forge opens toward the water through a broad set of shutters and folding doors. Older Longtail artisans call the moving air *sea-breath*, although the Shrike lies far from any ocean. The name describes a river wind heavy with moisture, smoke, and the promise that every object leaving the island may travel farther than its maker.

From the road, the atelier is recognizable by a long slate roof, a copper tail-shaped weather vane, and the steady rhythm of bellows rising beneath the less regular sound of hammers. At sunset, sparks from the open forge reflect across the channel among the torches of the bridges.

  

### History

Longtail Island was founded on 12 Neth 4710 AR and grew from the Longtail tribe's marshy refuge into a fortified river town. Under Mayor Vesket, its mud paths and crude palisades became paved streets, workshops, reinforced walls, bridges, drainage, and public buildings. The island's transformation required metalwork at every stage.

Ssekesh first worked in the settlement's general smithy, repairing tools, spearheads, fishing gear, gate fittings, and the innumerable brackets demanded by rapid construction. His skill with difficult repairs and watercraft hardware gradually drew commissions that could not be completed efficiently beside ordinary municipal work.

The town granted him a narrow riverside plot for a specialist workshop. Tailforge Atelier began with one hearth and a hand-operated bellows. Later expansions added a quenching floor, fitting dock, material gallery, apprentice room, and the water-powered mechanism now associated with the forge.

Its development reflects Mayor Vesket's own transformation from King Vesket into a civic leader. Longtail strength once served a ruler's fear. At Tailforge, strength is measured by what it protects, repairs, and helps others build.

  

### The Atelier

  

#### The Riverward Forge

The main forge occupies a stone floor beneath a high roof vent. Broad riverward doors provide light and ventilation, but bronze screens catch stray sparks when the wind rises. The largest hearth accommodates long fittings, weapon heads, and work requiring several smiths. Smaller hearths allow apprentices and specialists to maintain different temperatures without interrupting one another.

Tools are stored openly by function and size. Each hammer bears a mark identifying its maker and intended balance. Ssekesh teaches that choosing a familiar hammer for the wrong task is laziness disguised as loyalty.

  

#### The Tide Bellows

The forge's famous *tide-driven bellows* are powered by a narrow waterwheel set within a protected side channel. A geared shaft lifts weighted bellows in a slow, adjustable rhythm. Gates allow the smiths to regulate the flow or disconnect the mechanism during floods, debris jams, maintenance, or work requiring hand control.

The Shrike has no true ocean tide at Longtail Island. Local craftspeople use *tide* for the channel's repeated rise, fall, and reversal of pressure around passing barges, seasonal water, and the island's divided current. The name survived every technical correction because *variable-flow assisted bellows* offended the workshop's remaining sense of poetry.

When the current is strong and the mechanism properly tuned, the bellows produce a deep, breathing cadence beneath the hammering. Visitors often interpret the sound as religious. Apprentices interpret it as a reminder to grease the gears.

  

#### The Quenching Floor

A covered stone channel supplies quenching water drawn from the Shrike and passed through settling basins. Fresh water is tested before delicate work; floodwater carrying heavy silt is not used merely because it arrived dramatically.

Separate troughs contain water, oil, brine, or specialized mixtures appropriate to different metals and treatments. Velka blesses a small vessel of river water before Ssekesh's first strike when she is present. Finished pieces associated with her rites receive a final cooling pour from that vessel rather than being plunged into it.

  

#### The Fitting Dock

A short reinforced dock allows smiths to measure, install, and test hardware directly upon boats and barges. Tailforge produces gudgeons, pintles, rings, cleats, braces, chain, locks, rudder fittings, pole shoes, and protective plates suited to the Shrike's working craft.

The dock also receives bridge components too awkward to carry through the workshop. Ssekesh's staff cooperate with river pilots and the Ministry of Works when designing pieces exposed to current, vibration, collision, and seasonal flooding.

  

#### The Material Gallery

The atelier works primarily in iron, steel, bronze, brass, and copper, but its most distinctive commissions combine metal with shell, imported coral, clay, timber, or carved stone. Coral reaches the island through southern trade rather than the Shrike itself. Damaged or culturally significant pieces may be incorporated without pretending that fragility has become strength.

Samples are displayed with notes about source, behavior under heat, appropriate uses, and failures. Ssekesh regards a cracked decorative fitting as more instructive than an intact object whose maker refuses to explain it.

  

### Ssekesh

Ssekesh is a lizardfolk smith of deep slate-colored scales, quiet pride, and exceptional patience. He speaks sparingly while working and expects questions to be considered before they are repeated. His reserve has sometimes been mistaken for coldness by visitors accustomed to human shopkeepers. Apprentices know that he expresses approval by entrusting someone with a more difficult problem.

He combines traditional Longtail understandings of river, weather, bone, shell, and practical survival with techniques exchanged through the Republic's expanding craft network. Ssekesh does not divide these bodies of knowledge into primitive and modern. A method either serves the work, can be tested, and can be taught—or it cannot.

As master of a prominent workshop, Ssekesh advises Longtail Island upon bridge fittings, garrison equipment, river hazards, and contracts for specialized metalwork. He has no desire for elected office. Mayor Vesket values his counsel partly because Ssekesh is willing to describe an impressive proposal as badly designed.

  

### Velka's Trident

Velka of the Sootscale brought Ssekesh an all-metal trident that other smiths had declined to repair. Its construction made ordinary haft replacement impossible, while damage near the head threatened both balance and strength. Ssekesh examined it and told her, *Metal is the river that forgot how to move. Let's remind it.*

They reforged the weapon together. Ssekesh managed heat, alignment, reinforcement, and tempering while Velka blessed the river water and spoke prayers of transformation. The work required them to preserve enough of the original metal for the weapon to remain recognizably hers without preserving every weakness that had nearly broken it.

The resulting trident carries subtle lines where new metal joins old. Ssekesh refused to polish them away. Velka considers the marks a record of survival rather than imperfection.

Islanders say the forge burned blue during the work and that the river became completely calm. Ssekesh denies both claims. Velka smiles and says the river remembers who listens.

  

### Apprenticeship and Work

Tailforge accepts a small number of apprentices from both lizardfolk and human households. Instruction includes fire management, tool care, drawing, measurement, metal preparation, fitting, first aid, water safety, and the safe operation of the bellows mechanism. Every apprentice spends time at the municipal smithy learning ordinary repair before specializing.

Ssekesh requires apprentices to understand the setting in which an object will work. A bridge fitting must be studied at the bridge. A rudder fitting must be installed upon a boat. A ceremonial spear must be discussed with the people whose ceremony gives it meaning.

The atelier exchanges techniques and occasional apprentices with the Joined Hands in Tatzlford and other craft organizations across the Republic. Velka's travels have made these relationships less formal and more reliable: drawings, material samples, failed parts, and strongly worded advice move along the same river routes as trade goods.

  

### Work of the Tailforge

The atelier's river fittings support the barges and fishing craft that make Longtail Island a gateway of the Shrike. Its bridge hardware helps maintain the twin crossings guarded by the island's garrison. Tools and weapons leave through the riverside market for Olegton, Fort Tuskwater, and the southern marches.

Tailforge also accepts ceremonial and diplomatic commissions. The nearby embassy has ordered presentation pieces intended for visitors from Thumpington and the Dunsward. Ssekesh insists that diplomatic metalwork must remain usable. A beautifully decorated knife that cannot cut is, in his opinion, an expensive apology waiting to happen.

The forge has contributed repairs and commemorative fittings to the keep, ancestral park, central shrine, cemetery, and carved pillar honoring the Longtail dead and those lost to the Bloom. Such work is priced modestly or donated when the workshop can afford it.

  

### Faith and Custom

  

#### The First Quench

The first completed work of each month is cooled with water carried from the central shrine shared by Hanspur and Gozreh. The custom honors river, wind, and the Longtail understanding that no craft exists apart from the place supplying its materials and power.

  

#### Hammer and Current

At the completion of an apprenticeship, the new journeyworker strikes a practice bar in time with the tide bellows, then stops while the mechanism continues. The lesson is that a craftsperson joins an existing rhythm and leaves it for others; neither arrival nor departure makes the river begin or end.

  

#### The Useful Gift

Ceremonial commissions produce a small accompanying object from the same material: a hook, needle, nail set, buckle, or other practical item. The formal piece goes to its recipient. The useful gift enters the common tool chest or is given to a household that needs it.

  

#### Blue-Fire Stories

When the forge flame burns blue for an ordinary chemical reason, apprentices are expected to identify that reason before attributing the event to Velka. Once the explanation is recorded and any hazard corrected, they are free to tell the story however they please at the Floodlight Rest.

  

### Place in Longtail Island

Tailforge Atelier captures the character of modern Longtail Island: ancestral knowledge beside republican engineering, martial strength redirected into public protection, and river commerce supported by disciplined craft. Its tide bellows depend upon the same divided current that makes the island strategically valuable. Its commissions travel beneath the bridges its hardware helps preserve.

The forge also demonstrates the limits of simple stories about reconciliation. Longtail Island did not transform because its people abandoned their past. It transformed because they decided which parts of that past should guide the future and which should no longer rule it. At Tailforge, old metal is neither worshiped nor discarded automatically. It is tested, heated, joined, and made capable of different work.

  
> The old king fought for power. The new mayor fights for plumbing. I make the hinges for both doors, but only one of them opens toward tomorrow.
>
> — Ssekesh
