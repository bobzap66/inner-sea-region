# Tatzlford


### Tatzlford

Nestled amid the whispering pines of the [Narlmarches](../../../Greenbelt/Narlmarches.md), **Tatzlford** is a riverside town built on faith, hard work, and the kind of optimism that can only survive on the frontier. It sits astride a wide **ford on the Skunk River**, where the water runs shallow and silver over smooth stones—a place once haunted by monsters, now alive with the sound of hammers, laughter, and song. Founded by **Mayor [Loy Rezbin](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/NPCs/Loy%20Rezbin.md)** and his wife **Latricia**, priestess of [Erastil](../../../../../../../Rules/Deities/Erastil.md), Tatzlford has grown from a brave experiment into one of the republic’s most peaceful and prosperous river towns.

#### Overview

Tatzlford’s wooden **walls** encircle a cozy collection of timber houses, gardens, and workshops. The air smells of pine resin and malt, and the quiet rush of the Skunk River can be heard from nearly every street. A sturdy **bridge** spans the ford itself—rebuilt after the town’s early trials—and remains the main artery for trade and travel between the Narlmarches and the western hills. Within the walls, **paved lanes** lead to a shaded **marketplace** surrounded by tidy shops, a cheerful **inn** called *The Otter’s Rest*, and the squat, welcoming **town hall** that doubles as both meeting house and civic court.

The town’s centerpiece is its **monument to the founding of Tatzlford**, a beautifully carved obelisk of riverstone set in the middle of a green **park**. The monument depicts Loy and Latricia standing side by side, holding tools and a sheaf of wheat, with the Skunk River curling around their feet. A plaque at its base reads simply: *“Faith builds what fear cannot.”*

#### Leadership

**Mayor Loy Rezbin** remains the town’s leader, equal parts diplomat, merchant, and idealist. His easy charm and tireless energy make him a favorite among the citizens. Beside him, **[Latricia Rezbin](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/NPCs/Latricia%20Rezbin.md)** tends the town’s spiritual needs from her modest shrine to Erastil, guiding both farmers and families with patience and humor. Together, they embody the Erastilian virtues of community, self-reliance, and neighborly good sense. Where other towns are ruled by bureaucrats or generals, Tatzlford is ruled by consensus, kindness, and the occasional well-aimed sermon.

Loy’s office in the **guildhall** is open to all, and the mayor himself is known to settle disputes personally—often with a handshake, a mug of beer, and a promise to help mend whatever was broken. Latricia’s influence extends through the surrounding farms and lumber camps, where her teachings on balance and stewardship have helped maintain peace between settlers and the forest’s more ancient denizens.

#### Trade and Industry

The people of Tatzlford earn their living from the land and river both. The nearby forests feed a thriving **lumberyard** and **mill**, whose output supplies much of the region’s construction timber. The **brewery** produces the town’s famous honey-brown ale, *River’s Blessing*, which is exported as far as [Olegton](../Olegton/Olegton.md). Fishermen ply the Skunk River for trout and perch, while the **stockyard** and **trade shop** bustle with caravans carrying furs, lumber, and grain. Even the **dump** has become an unlikely symbol of efficiency—carefully managed by local volunteers to keep the town spotless and the wildlife safe.

The **guildhall** houses the town’s growing community of craftsmen: millwrights, brewers, coopers, and stonemasons. Though small, the guild system has helped maintain Tatzlford’s reputation for honest trade and fine workmanship. The **jail**, located just behind the town hall, is rarely used; serious crime here is almost unheard of, and most disputes are settled long before tempers reach that point.

#### Culture and Community

Tatzlford is a place that feels perpetually on the edge of festival. Its people work hard, but they sing while they do it, and every season brings a reason to celebrate. The **festival hall** hosts weekly dances, harvest markets, and sermons, and the town’s green **park** is often filled with families and travelers resting in the shade. Latricia’s faith infuses the town’s spirit: every home has a small symbol of Erastil by the hearth, and even the brewery begins each batch with a blessing for “good harvests and honest work.”

Visitors often remark that Tatzlford feels like something out of a storybook—too cheerful, too clean, too kind. The locals just smile and pour another drink. They know what they’ve built, and they’ve fought hard for it. The scars of the old frontier may be buried beneath the paving stones, but they’re not forgotten; they simply remind everyone how far faith and cooperation can carry them.

#### Modern Role

Today, Tatzlford serves as the main river port for western Thumping Waters, linking the Narlmarches to the Shrike and beyond. Barges carry goods downstream to Fort [Tuskwater](../../../Greenbelt/Kamelands/Tuskwater/Tuskwater.md) and Olegton, while merchants from the south bring luxuries and news in return. The town’s steady economy and tranquil atmosphere make it a popular stop for travelers, pilgrims, and retirees looking for peace by the water. The republic’s Ministry of Commerce even cites Tatzlford as “a model of sustainable frontier development”—a fancy way of saying they’re doing everything right.

At sunset, when the golden light hits the bridge and the Skunk River shines like glass, the townsfolk gather in the park to share food, drink, and laughter. Loy always says the same thing to new arrivals: “This is what we built when the world told us it couldn’t be done.” And every night, as the river hums and the lamps flicker, it’s clear that Tatzlford still believes him.

## General Details

##### From the Journal of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

*Amiri and the Art of Town-Building (Poorly, but Passionately)*

When **Loy and Latricia Rezbin** came to Thumping Waters asking for help settling a riverside town in the western Narlmarches, they were very specific: “We need someone brave, dependable, and experienced in the wilderness.” The council nodded solemnly, considered their options, and sent **Amiri**. To this day, I’m still not sure if that was an act of faith or a dare to the gods.

Amiri led the expedition west with a wagon full of supplies, two dozen settlers, and enough confidence to frighten a bear. Unfortunately, the Narlmarches are less impressed by confidence than by common sense—something the expedition occasionally lacked. Between flooded fords, venomous insects the size of helmets, and trees that fell in suspiciously uncooperative directions, progress was slow. When the bridge timbers washed away for the second time, Amiri tried to drag the river back into its bed by shouting at it. For the record, it didn’t work, but the river definitely knew she meant business.

There were bandits. There were bogs. There was, according to one very nervous carpenter, “a frog the size of a house that ate our anvil.” By the time they reached the chosen site on the Skunk River, half the settlers were ready to turn around and the other half were too stubborn to admit they agreed. But Amiri planted her greatsword in the mud, glared at the horizon, and said, “We’re staying. The forest will learn who’s boss.” The forest, for its part, seemed skeptical.

Weeks turned to months. The lumber warped. The first bridge collapsed. The second bridge caught fire. The third bridge, blessed by Latricia herself, finally held. Amiri fought off a pair of tatzlwyrms that wandered too close to the camp (hence the town’s name), declared them “good omen meat,” and had them roasted for the first Founders’ Feast. By the time Thumping Waters’ surveyors arrived, they found a muddy but thriving community—half-finished, half-flooded, and entirely too proud to care. The official charter signing had to wait another season while Amiri and the settlers rebuilt everything that had washed away, but no one doubted their resolve.

When the town was finally ready to join the republic, Amiri stood beside Loy and Latricia at the river’s edge, covered in mud, sawdust, and victory. She handed the mayor a mug of the first batch from the new **brewery** and said, “It’s a good start. Next time, we build uphill.” And just like that, Tatzlford was born—out of faith, grit, and one woman’s very loud refusal to lose an argument with a forest.

*—Linzi*

Editor’s note: The Ministry of Communication recognizes that Amiri’s approach to wilderness settlement violates several modern safety guidelines, including but not limited to shouting at rivers, punching wildlife, and using tatzlwyrms as food sources.
