# Guildhall of the Joined Hands

![[guildhall-of-the-joined-hands-portrait.png]]

*Compiled by the College of Letters and Public Record, Free University of Thumping Waters, from Tatzlford's municipal gazetteer, guild records, and interviews with the members of the Joined Hands*

> A settlement is not built when the wall goes up. It is built when someone promises to mend the wall after the first winter.
>
> — Inscription in the contract hall

The Guildhall of the Joined Hands is the principal guildhall, apprenticeship center, tool library, mutual-aid house, and secondary civic meeting place of Tatzlford. Its members include carpenters, smiths, leatherworkers, millwrights, brewers, masons, wheelwrights, coopers, sawyers, teamsters, and other craftspeople whose work transformed a frontier crossing into a defended river town.

It stands near the shaded marketplace, within easy walking distance of the town hall, the Otter's Rest, and the bridge across the Skunk River. The smell of pine resin, forge smoke, worked leather, and the nearby brewery mingles in its yard. On guild nights, members commonly end their arguments over mugs of Tatzlford's honey-brown River's Blessing ale.

The hall is best known beyond Tatzlford through Velka of the Sootscale, who visits its workshops whenever her travels bring her west. She describes every forge and workbench as a kind of chapel: places where patience, skill, and creation become forms of prayer. The guild's members vary considerably in their response to this theology, but several display her driftwood charms above their tools.

  

### At a Glance

**Type:** Guildhall, workshop, and mutual-aid house

**Location:** Tatzlford

**Membership:** Craftspeople, tradespeople, apprentices, and associated laborers

**Governing Body:** The Joined Bench

**Principal Members:** Garrin Whetforge, Lissa Tarmel, and Fenno Pike

**Known For:** Apprenticeships, public works, shared tools, emergency repair, and contract mediation

**Associated Shrine:** The Bell at Tatzl Ford

**Motto:** *No Hand Builds Alone*

  

### Founding

Tatzlford was founded on 3 Arodus 4710 AR by Loy and Latricia Rezbin and the settlers who followed them into the Narlmarches. Amiri led the original expedition through flooded fords, hostile wildlife, and a succession of bridge designs that proved less permanent than their builders intended. By the time the town received its charter, cooperation was not an abstract civic virtue. It was how the settlers kept roofs overhead and the road open.

Tatzlford's early economy depended upon timber, livestock, river traffic, and the constant repair demanded by frontier weather. The settlement built a lumberyard and stockyard during its first major period of growth, followed by additional housing, a jail, wooden walls, and a bridge. Each project required craftspeople to share labor, scarce tools, transport, and imported materials.

The Joined Hands began as an informal agreement among workers who met beneath a lumber shed to divide public contracts and prevent the same urgent repair from being promised to three different officials. The arrangement expanded after several winters demonstrated that broken roofs, frozen wheels, injured workers, and spoiled hides did not respect the boundaries between trades.

Members pooled funds to purchase a central property near the road between the town hall and lumberyard. The first guildhall was little more than a meeting room attached to a shared workshop. Later additions enclosed a forge yard, timber shed, leather loft, records office, apprenticeship rooms, and emergency stores.

The name *Joined Hands* was selected to emphasize cooperation without requiring every craft to surrender its own customs. The guild's seal depicts three open hands supporting a bridge beam.

  

### The Hall

The guildhall is a broad timber-and-stone building designed around a central work court. Its walls display little decoration that cannot also hold a tool, illuminate a bench, identify an exit, or keep rain outside. The workmanship is nevertheless exceptional. Joints are exposed where students can study them, metal brackets carry the marks of their smiths, and every major repair is dated rather than concealed.

  

#### The Contract Hall

The front chamber contains the Joined Bench, public notice boards, work ledgers, and long tables used for negotiation. Contracts involving guild members may be registered here, creating a record of price, materials, responsibilities, expected completion, and what will happen if weather, war, monsters, or official indecision make the original terms impossible.

Registration is not compulsory for private work, but clients who refuse a written description of an expensive project are regarded with suspicion. The hall's clerks maintain standard measures and sample contracts for citizens unable to hire a legal specialist.

  

#### The Mayor's Room

A small office opening from the contract hall is reserved for Mayor Loy Rezbin. Its door is ordinarily open when he is present, and citizens bring him disputes, petitions, business proposals, and problems too minor for the civic court but too persistent to ignore. Loy is known for resolving many of them with a handshake, a mug of beer, and an agreement about who will help mend what was broken.

The office does not make the guildhall Tatzlford's seat of government; the nearby town hall remains the meeting house and civic court. It reflects the practical overlap between a mayor concerned with growth and the tradespeople who must turn public promises into bridges, roofs, barrels, harness, and drains. Latricia Rezbin also uses the contract hall when her work among the farms, families, and lumber camps requires a larger table than her modest shrine to Erastil can provide.

  

#### The Common Workshop

The common workshop contains benches, saws, drills, clamps, measuring tools, anvils, hoists, and other equipment too expensive or infrequently used for every craftsperson to own separately. Members reserve equipment through a slate board and contribute either coin or maintenance labor.

Apprentices learn that returning a dull, broken, or dirty tool without reporting its condition is a more serious offense than breaking one during honest work. The former endangers the next user; the latter merely proves that objects remain objects.

  

#### The Forge Court

An open-roofed stone court contains the guild's largest forge, quenching troughs, coal stores, casting floor, and heavy lifting frame. Garrin Whetforge supervises work requiring unusual heat or several smiths. Rainwater is directed away from the forge and into a covered cistern rather than permitted to carry ash into the river.

  

#### The Timber Shed

The timber shed adjoins the road to Tatzlford's lumberyard. Wood is stacked by species, dimensions, age, and intended use. Lissa Tarmel maintains sample joints and failed pieces for instruction. She insists that students learn as much from a beam that split after two winters as from one that survived twenty.

  

#### The Leather Loft

Fenno Pike's leatherworking rooms occupy a ventilated upper wing away from the kitchens and public meeting spaces. The loft contains cutting tables, forms, stitching frames, drying racks, and locked cabinets for dyes, oils, glues, and chemicals. Apprentices throughout the guild come here to learn harness repair, since almost every Tatzlford trade eventually depends upon an animal, wagon, belt, glove, boot, or strap.

  

#### The Relief Stores

A reinforced cellar holds rope, nails, canvas, timber braces, lamp oil, blankets, replacement handles, buckets, simple tools, and preserved food for fires, floods, sieges, and structural collapse. The stores are rotated through ordinary guild work so that emergency supplies do not become useless while waiting for an emergency.

  

### The Joined Bench

The guild is governed by the Joined Bench, an elected group representing different trades and forms of work. No trade may hold more than one seat at a time, and at least one seat is reserved for a wage laborer or journeyworker who does not own a workshop.

The bench registers apprenticeships, maintains common property, mediates disputes, negotiates public contracts, and administers the mutual-aid fund. Its authority is contractual rather than governmental. The guild may suspend membership or refuse access to shared resources, but crimes and civil judgments remain matters for Tatzlford and the Republic.

Mayor Loy Rezbin may address the bench and request work on behalf of the town, but he does not appoint its members or vote in guild elections. This separation is deliberate: the mayor keeps an office in the hall so that government can hear working people, not so that government can own their organization.

Guild meetings are open to members and apprentices. Clients may attend portions concerning their contracts. Debate is frequently loud, especially when the subject involves timber grading, imported hardware, or whether a particular explosion should be classified as a workplace accident or an avoidable act of optimism.

  

### Membership

Membership is open to craftspeople and associated workers who practice within Tatzlford or contribute regularly to its public works. Applicants demonstrate competence, agree to guild measures, contribute to the mutual-aid fund, and accept responsibility for correcting dangerous work.

The Joined Hands does not require members to operate independent businesses. Journeyworkers, seasonal laborers, caravan specialists, and household craftspeople may join. This prevents the guild from becoming solely an association of shop owners deciding the conditions under which everyone else must work.

Members place a handprint or traced hand in the guild ledger. Retired members remain recorded without being required to pay dues. The pages reveal hands marked by burns, missing fingers, claws, scales, unusual proportions, and ink applied with someone else's assistance.

  

### Apprenticeships

The guild registers apprenticeship agreements and reviews them annually. Each agreement states what the apprentice will be taught, the expected duration, working hours, food and lodging arrangements, compensation, ownership of completed practice work, and a process for ending an unsafe or exploitative placement.

Apprentices spend part of each season in the common workshop learning skills outside their principal trade. A carpenter may study tool repair, a smith may learn harness maintenance, and a leatherworker may learn enough structural bracing to recognize when a damaged building should be abandoned rather than entered.

Instruction also includes arithmetic, measurements, contract reading, first aid, fire response, and the safe storage of materials. Students unable to read receive instruction without losing their apprenticeships. The guild considers literacy a tool and treats withholding it as equivalent to locking away a shared saw.

  

### Mutual Aid

A portion of dues and contract fees supports members injured or displaced by work, fire, flood, war, or illness. Assistance may include food, medical care, replacement tools, temporary lodging, funeral costs, or continued support for an apprentice whose master can no longer teach.

The fund also supports surviving households after a member's death. Aid is not conditioned upon the deceased having operated a profitable workshop. The guild was built by people who understood that necessary work and wealthy work are not always the same thing.

Disputes over assistance are reviewed by members who do not have a direct financial interest in the decision. Fenno Pike advocated this rule after observing that anxious people should not be required to argue publicly for their own worth before receiving groceries.

  

### Public Works

The Joined Hands has contributed to Tatzlford's walls, bridge, stockyard, lumberyard, housing, jail, drainage, and sewer construction. The guild does not claim sole credit for these projects. Teamsters, laborers, engineers, soldiers, officials, quarry workers, and imported specialists all participated.

Its most important contribution is continuity. Public structures require inspection and maintenance after the dedication speeches end. Guild teams survey the bridge after floods, inspect wooden walls for rot, repair communal pumps, and maintain lists of buildings suitable for emergency shelter.

Members also service the lumberyard and mill that supply construction timber, repair casks and machinery for the brewery that produces River's Blessing, and keep the stockyard's gates, fences, scales, and harness in working order. The guild cooperates with local volunteers who maintain Tatzlford's unusually orderly dump, salvaging safe hardware and usable material without allowing refuse to threaten the river or forest wildlife.

The guild also advises the settlement when a proposed economy in materials will create greater repair costs later. Such advice is not always welcomed, particularly when budgets have already been announced.

  

### The Defense of Tatzlford

When Ameon Trask led Drelev's army against Tatzlford, the guildhall became a repair depot and supply station behind the main defensive positions. Members reinforced doors, repaired bowstrings and harness, distributed axes and hammers, prepared fire buckets, and built stretchers while the Narlmarch Hunters fortified the town hall and the Tatzlford Guard met the invaders in the field.

Guild members fought when the battle reached the streets, but their larger contribution was keeping the defenders supplied and the settlement functioning. Damaged wagons were stripped for braces. Stockyard gates became barricades. Apprentices carried water, messages, and bandages through routes memorized during fire drills.

After the attackers broke, the hall served as a registration point for the injured, missing, displaced, and dead. The guild's mutual-aid fund was nearly exhausted during the months that followed. Contributions from other settlements replenished it, establishing several relationships that continue through shared apprenticeships and emergency agreements.

  

### People of the Joined Hands

  

#### Garrin Whetforge, Blacksmith

Garrin Whetforge is an old, thick-browed blacksmith with more scars than patience. He distrusts superstition, ornamental titles, and tools designed by people who do not expect to hold them. His work is durable, his instruction demanding, and his criticism generally accurate enough to remain irritating.

Velka blessed his forge before a difficult delivery run, after which a persistent series of broken tongs and short tempers came to an end. Garrin describes the timing as coincidence. A driftwood charm nevertheless hangs above his anvil, and he rarely begins the day's first hammer stroke before Velka has finished her tea when she is visiting.

Garrin supervises the forge court and serves upon the Joined Bench when persuaded that the alternative would be allowing someone less sensible to do it.

  

#### Lissa Tarmel, Carpenter

Lissa Tarmel is one of Tatzlford's finest woodworkers and a principal organizer of the guild's apprenticeship program. She specializes in structural framing, bridges, water-resistant joinery, and repairs that must be completed while the damaged structure remains in use.

She trades shavings and offcuts to Velka for talisman work. Together they built the small riverside post known as the Bell at Tatzl Ford. Lissa designed it to survive seasonal floods without pretending that any structure beside a river can be made permanent.

Within the guild, Lissa is known for patient instruction and merciless inspection. She allows apprentices to make nearly any mistake once, provided they mark the failed piece, explain what happened, and do not install it in someone else's roof.

  

#### Fenno Pike, Leatherworker

Fenno Pike is a soft-spoken leatherworker whose anxious attention to detail has prevented more accidents than louder craftspeople care to admit. He produces harness, work gloves, protective aprons, boots, tool belts, hinges, seals, and the countless straps upon which frontier machinery quietly depends.

Fenno insists the guild operates more smoothly when Velka is present. She teases him, blesses his glue pots, and usually leaves before he discovers where she has hidden the newest driftwood charm. When she is away, he hums her river hymns while working through disputes that everyone else has made unnecessarily dramatic.

Fenno administers much of the mutual-aid fund and maintains confidential records concerning injured members and struggling households. His discretion is one reason workers seek help before a temporary difficulty becomes a catastrophe.

  

#### Nessa Vale, Clerk of Measures

Nessa Vale is a human clerk, surveyor, and former caravan quartermaster who maintains the guild's contracts, standard measures, equipment reservations, and material inventories. She is not a master craftsperson and regards that fact as useful: the guild requires at least one senior officer willing to ask what a technical term means before allowing it into a public contract.

Nessa also records apprenticeship agreements and the handprints in the membership ledger. During emergencies, she converts the ordinary records office into an information desk for missing people, available shelter, damaged roads, and urgent repairs.

  

### The Bell at Tatzl Ford

A short walk from the guildhall, a carved post and small brass bell stand beside the river. Lissa Tarmel and Velka built the first version together from flood-thrown timber. Apprentices leave small carvings, shavings, bent nails, worn tools, and other tokens in thanks for steady hands and work completed without injury.

The bell's tone changes with wind, temperature, water level, and the tension of its mounting cord. Velka maintains that Hanspur answers through those changes. Garrin maintains that wet cord contracts. Lissa sees no reason both explanations cannot remind someone to inspect the river.

The site is not a formal Hanspurite temple. It is a craft shrine, river gauge, memorial, and meeting point. Rescue rope and a throwing float are stored beneath its small roof. When a guild member departs upon a long journey, another member rings the bell once. It is rung again upon the traveler's return.

  

### Customs

  

#### The First Stroke

On the first working day of the year, members gather in the common workshop. A newly admitted apprentice performs the First Stroke: one measured hammer blow, saw cut, stitch, or other simple act chosen from their trade. The point is not spectacle. It is to begin the guild's year with work entrusted to someone who has only begun learning.

  

#### The Joined Repair

Each spring, the guild selects one structure belonging to a household or public institution unable to afford necessary repairs. Members contribute labor and the mutual-aid fund supplies materials. Apprentices participate under supervision. The recipient is not required to display a plaque or provide a public account of private hardship.

  

#### No Hand Builds Alone

At the completion of an apprenticeship, the new journeyworker presents one tool or useful object to the common workshop. It need not be expensive. The gift acknowledges the borrowed tools, corrected mistakes, shared meals, emergency help, and accumulated teaching that made independent work possible.

  

### Relations beyond Tatzlford

The Joined Hands exchanges apprentices, plans, and emergency support with craftspeople in other Republican settlements. Its closest institutional counterpart in the capital is the Hall of Common Works, though the two differ in character. Thumpington's guildhall represents a dense urban economy; the Joined Hands remains shaped by frontier repair, timber, livestock, and the expectation that specialists may be days away when something breaks.

Velka's travels have widened the guild's informal network. Through her introductions, Tatzlford craftspeople have exchanged materials and techniques with Mek-Mek the Copperhand in Sootscale Valley, the artisans of Longtail Island, Greengripe's experimental workshops, and river workers throughout the Republic.

The guild's members do not describe this network as diplomacy. They send the correct hinge, ask why the previous one failed, and remember who answered when the bridge was under water.

Within Tatzlford, the guildhall participates in the weekly rhythms described in the town's gazetteer. Notices for festival-hall dances and harvest markets share its boards with job postings. Visiting craftspeople commonly lodge at the Otter's Rest. Guild members join public celebrations in the green park beneath the riverstone monument to Loy and Latricia, whose inscription—*Faith builds what fear cannot*—expresses much the same conviction as the guild's plainer motto.

> Velka says every workshop is a chapel. I say a chapel with a cracked roof still needs a carpenter. She says that proves her point.
>
> — Lissa Tarmel
