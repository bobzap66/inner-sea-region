# Embeth Hall


### Embeth Hall

Once a small frontier village on the edge of the Greenbelt, **Embeth Hall** has grown into one of the Republic of Thumping Waters’ proudest towns. Built where the trade roads from Olegton and Fort Tuskwater meet, it serves as both marketplace and memorial—a living symbol of resilience born from hardship. Its people are industrious, practical, and fiercely loyal to their home, their town named for the great hall that still stands at its heart.

#### Overview

Embeth Hall rises on a low hill overlooking fertile fields and a winding river. Once surrounded by simple wooden palisades, it now boasts **wooden walls** reinforced with watch platforms and guarded by a **stone watchtower** that overlooks the northern approach. Inside, its **paved streets** form neat avenues radiating from the central plaza, where the rebuilt **town hall** and the original **festival hall** stand side by side as twin pillars of government and community life. The town’s **marketplace** is one of the busiest in northern Thumping Waters, its stalls piled high with produce, lumber, and crafts from across the republic.

To the east lies a small **pier** where barges carry goods downriver, while the town’s **stonemason’s yard** and workshops cluster nearby, shaping the gray-green rock of the Kamelands into everything from sturdy walls to fine hearths. The scent of sawdust and baking bread mixes with the tang of stone dust and smoke from forges. Clean, cobbled streets and a functioning **sewer system**—a rare luxury this far from the capital—keep the town healthy and orderly. Though the frontier still looms beyond its borders, Embeth Hall feels civilized, secure, and proud of it.

#### Leadership

**Governor Jamel Visser** rules Embeth Hall with steady confidence and pragmatic charm. A veteran of the republic’s early expansion, Visser earned his reputation by turning disorder into order and wilderness into livelihood. His leadership is marked by transparency and discipline: every citizen may attend the weekly council in the **town hall**, and every new project must be discussed in the open. He walks the streets daily, greeting workers, offering advice, and occasionally lending a hand where it’s needed. Under his direction, the town has grown from scattered cabins into a polished jewel of frontier governance.

Visser’s government is supported by a capable town council of merchants, priests, and craftsmen. Together they have cultivated a spirit of civic pride that runs deep; the people of Embeth Hall do not simply work for themselves—they work for the town. That philosophy has kept the settlement prosperous even through hard winters and lingering fears left by the Bloom.

#### Landmarks and Buildings

The original **great hall** still stands at the town’s center, rebuilt in stone and used for both public gatherings and ceremonies. The adjoining **festival hall** is its counterpart in spirit—its broad timber rafters hung with colorful banners from each district. Market days fill the plaza between them with chatter, laughter, and music. The **luxury tavern** nearby, a three-story inn called *The White Lantern*, caters to visiting merchants, offering fine wine, polished floors, and a balcony view of the town’s monument square.

The **monument to the victims of the Bloom** stands opposite the town hall: a simple column of pale stone surrounded by ever-burning lanterns. Its base bears the names of those lost when the supernatural corruption spread through the Greenbelt years ago. Pilgrims from across Thumping Waters visit the site to pay respects, leaving offerings of flowers, ribbons, or tokens of survival. The nearby **sacred grove** serves as both temple and sanctuary, tended by a circle of druids who blend reverence for nature with the pragmatic duties of civic caretakers.

Beyond the square, **houses** cluster in tidy rows, their stone chimneys and timber frames built by the town’s own **stonemason’s guild**. A **sewer system**—an innovation brought from Thumpington—keeps the streets clean, and a network of drains carries rainwater toward the river. Embeth Hall’s **inn** and taverns are lively but well-kept, their beer drawn from the same wells that feed the surrounding farms. The watch rotates patrols from the **stone watchtower**, maintaining peace and ensuring that Embeth Hall never again faces chaos unprepared.

#### Culture and Daily Life

Life in Embeth Hall moves with purpose. The marketplace hums from dawn to dusk, filled with the sounds of hammering, bargaining, and gossip. Every season brings a celebration: spring planting feasts in the festival hall, midsummer markets lit by lanterns, and the autumn remembrance held at the monument, when the bells toll once for every name carved upon its base. Evenings often end at *The White Lantern*, where music and laughter spill into the street until curfew.

The people of Embeth Hall are proud of their town’s order and craftsmanship. They take satisfaction in small perfections—a well-paved street, a polished stone lintel, a gate hinge that doesn’t creak. Outsiders sometimes mistake their seriousness for dourness, but to the locals, diligence is a kind of prayer. “Work done well,” Governor Visser likes to say, “is the best hymn we can offer the gods.”

#### Modern Role

Today, Embeth Hall stands as one of the key administrative and economic centers of northern Thumping Waters. Its roads and river docks make it a hub for trade between Olegton, Fort Tuskwater, and the southern frontier, while its workshops supply stone, tools, and finished goods throughout the republic. Plans are underway to expand the pier and dredge the river to allow larger vessels to dock, transforming the town into a true inland port.

Though it has grown beyond its humble beginnings, Embeth Hall remains a community bound by purpose and memory. Its walls stand as proof that peace can be built stone by stone, and its people live by a creed as enduring as the hall itself: that no tragedy—no Bloom, no war, no wilderness—is too great for hands joined in common cause.

## General Details

##### From the Journal of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

*Order, Stone, and Civic Pride*

I remember the first time I saw Embeth Hall—it wasn’t much to look at. A few muddy huts, a hall that leaned like it was tired of standing, and three chickens who considered themselves the town guard. The wind had opinions, the roads had ambitions, and the people had… potential. A great deal of potential, if one squinted hard enough.

The next time I visited, it was different. The mud had become **paved streets**, the leaning hall had turned to stone, and the chickens had been replaced by an actual **watchtower** and people who saluted when I passed. I nearly didn’t recognize the place. Jamel Visser met me at the gate with a ledger in one hand and a sandwich in the other, because that’s the sort of man he is—efficient, practical, and faintly terrifying in how much he gets done before breakfast.

The marketplace was alive with trade, the **luxury tavern** smelled faintly of cinnamon and triumph, and the people all walked like they had somewhere important to be. Even the river seemed to flow in neat, well-organized ripples. The **monument to the victims of the Bloom** caught my breath for a moment—simple stone, soft light, and a stillness that says more than a hundred speeches ever could. I set down a quill in their honor. It felt right.

There’s a joke that Embeth Hall runs so smoothly because Visser has secretly replaced the townsfolk with clockwork replicas. I don’t believe it, of course—but if it’s true, they’re the most polite automatons I’ve ever met. Everything here works. The **festival hall** rings with song, the **sacred grove** smells of pine and prayer, and even the **sewer system** (yes, they have one) hums with quiet dignity. It’s almost unnerving. Almost.

But as I stood beneath the lanterns strung across the plaza that night, watching the people laugh and dance, I realized something: Embeth Hall isn’t perfect because it’s planned. It’s perfect because its people care. They built this town from the same stubborn hope that founded the republic, one brick and one heart at a time. And if the gods themselves keep ledgers, I’m certain Jamel Visser’s name is written under “assets.”

*—Linzi*

Editor’s note: The “clockwork townsfolk” rumor remains unconfirmed. Governor Visser declined to comment.
