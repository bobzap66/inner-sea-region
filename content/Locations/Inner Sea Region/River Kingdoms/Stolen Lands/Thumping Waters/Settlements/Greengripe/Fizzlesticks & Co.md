# Fizzlesticks & Co

![[fizzlesticks-and-co-portrait.png]]

*Compiled by Rikka Tallowglass, Lecturer in Civic Alchemy, Nok-Nok College of Practical Arts and Civic Service, Free University of Thumping Waters, with the cooperation of Griggit Fizzlesticks, the Citizen Council of Greengripe, and the town's fire and sanitation crews*

> If it explodes, record why. If it does not explode, record why not.
>
> — Rule painted above the mixing floor

Fizzlesticks & Co. is an alchemical shop, laboratory, and civic testing house in Greengripe. Founded and directed by the goblin alchemist Griggit Fizzlesticks, it produces medicines, cleaning compounds, dyes, lamp oils, fire suppressants, smoke signals, construction mixtures, pest treatments, and a changing selection of preparations whose usefulness became apparent only after their intended purpose failed.

The establishment is best known for combining Greengripe's enthusiasm for experimentation with systems designed to ensure that experiments remain survivable, explainable, and preferably within the property line. Its low stone walls, copper vents, outward-opening shutters, and deliberately replaceable roof panels have become a recognizable part of the town's workshop district.

Fizzlesticks & Co. also serves as a field laboratory for Nok-Nok College of Practical Arts and Civic Service. Students come to Greengripe to learn production alchemy, emergency response, waste treatment, batch records, and the uncomfortable distinction between a dramatic reaction and a useful product.

  

### At a Glance

**Type:** Alchemical shop, laboratory, and testing house

**Location:** Workshop district, Greengripe

**Founder and Proprietor:** Griggit Fizzlesticks

**Primary Work:** Medicines, household compounds, civic alchemy, signals, and controlled experimental products

**Best-Known Product:** *Hanspur's Breath*

**Academic Partner:** Nok-Nok College of Practical Arts and Civic Service

**Glass Supplier:** Gleams and Shards

**Operating Maxim:** *Interesting is not the same as ready*

  

### From Market Stall to Testing House

Griggit Fizzlesticks began selling mixtures from a painted market cart whose drawers contained remedies, polishes, powders, snacks, and ingredients that could become any of the preceding categories if handled carelessly. He earned a reputation for inexpensive preparations that worked, unusual preparations that worked differently than expected, and demonstrations whose audiences learned to stand behind the cart rather than beside it.

Governor Nok-Nok encouraged Griggit and other local inventors to treat their work as an industry rather than a curiosity performed for visiting merchants. The new prosperity brought larger contracts, apprentices, glassware, imported reagents, and the realization that Greengripe could not conduct every promising reaction in a market crowded with food stalls and children.

The Citizen Council granted Griggit a workshop plot near the edge of the central district, within reach of the market but close to the prepared testing grounds outside the densest streets. The original building had one mixing room, a counter, and a chimney whose first design is now displayed upon the Wall of Useful Failures as an example of how not to persuade smoke to leave.

Later expansions added a separate Quiet Room, a bermed test yard, storage vaults, a public viewing gallery, a wash garden, and a second roof built according to lessons supplied by the departure of the first. The shop became a licensed producer for public works, healers, soldiers, breweries, and traders throughout the Republic.

Griggit has refused proposals to rename it the Greengripe Institute of Applied Alchemical Development. He maintains that institutions should have names short enough to shout while running away from them.

  

### The Building

Fizzlesticks & Co. is a low, broad structure of brick and stone around a central mixing floor. The walls nearest neighboring properties are the thickest. Roof sections above active work areas are built from light timber and fitted so pressure can lift them without bringing the main beams down. Replacement panels of several colors wait beneath a side awning.

Copper ventilation cowls turn above the roofline. Their different shapes serve particular rooms and make the building resemble, according to one visiting architect, a disciplined collection of hats. Gutters carry ordinary rainwater toward storage barrels while runoff from work surfaces passes through a separate treatment system.

The exterior bears repairs in brick, timber, plaster, ceramic tile, and one patch of fused green glass that proved easier to build around than remove. Greengripe residents regard the mismatched surfaces as a record of improvement. Griggit can identify the experiment associated with each repair and often does so before customers have decided whether they wanted to know.

  

#### The Front Counter

The public shop occupies the safest room in the building. Shelves hold sealed bottles, tins, ceramic jars, waxed packets, cleaning brushes, measuring spoons, lamp fittings, and printed instructions. Products are grouped by intended use rather than by color, since Griggit considers *the blue shelf* an inadequate safety system.

Common purchases include fever remedies, wound washes, stain removers, lamp oils, waterproofing wax, boot powder, insect repellent, dyes, smoke pellets, odor treatments, brewing cleaners, and fire-suppressing globes. Potent medicines, explosives, poisons, and preparations requiring professional use remain behind locked cabinets and are sold only under appropriate restrictions.

A broad section of counter is faced with thick glass. Customers may place damaged objects, unidentified residue, spoiled mixtures, or suspicious substances behind it for examination. They are requested not to open containers in order to demonstrate that the smell is unusual.

  

#### The Viewing Gallery

A narrow public gallery overlooks the principal mixing floor through heavy panes supplied by Kuppa and Snip of Gleams and Shards. The glassblowers developed the layered panels after concluding that ordinary windows allowed experiments to become conversations too quickly.

Visitors may watch scheduled demonstrations, apprentice examinations, and the preparation of large civic batches. Brass speaking tubes allow instructors to explain the work without opening the barrier. A weighted shutter can cover the gallery glass when a reaction ceases to deserve an audience.

Children from Greengripe's schools visit regularly. Their lessons emphasize labels, ventilation, fire response, and the fact that knowing an adult alchemist personally does not authorize touching anything in an alchemist's workshop.

  

#### The Mixing Floor

The central laboratory contains stone benches, adjustable burners, water taps, scales, presses, grinders, condensers, settling vessels, and racks fitted to secure glassware against vibration. Colored floor lines separate preparation, heating, cooling, and transit areas. Ingredients travel in one direction through the room so raw material, active mixtures, finished products, and waste do not cross unnecessarily.

Every active batch carries a slate identifying its maker, contents, starting time, expected behavior, known hazards, and the person authorized to declare it finished. An abandoned vessel without a slate is treated as dangerous until proved otherwise. A slate without a vessel is treated as evidence that someone has begun a very unpleasant conversation.

The maxim above the floor—*If it explodes, record why. If it does not explode, record why not*—is not entirely a joke. Greengripe's advances in civic alchemy depend upon preserving negative results, failed proportions, unexpected reactions, and near misses instead of allowing every apprentice to rediscover them personally.

  

#### The Quiet Room

The Quiet Room is a cool, windowless chamber separated from the mixing floor by two doors and a short empty passage. It houses preparations sensitive to heat, sunlight, vibration, open flame, magic, loud noises, sudden movement, or one another.

Tools are made from wood, ceramic, copper, bone, or other materials selected for the work. No fixed flame enters the room. Light comes from protected alchemical lamps mounted outside thick glass panels. Shelves stand within shallow containment trays, and every vessel is tied, padded, or nested according to its habits.

Conversation is kept low because several mixtures genuinely respond to vibration and because the rule prevents apprentices from competing to invent increasingly dramatic reasons for whispering.

  

#### The Test Yard

Behind the shop lies an earthen-banked yard used for fireworks, smoke devices, fire suppressants, construction compounds, demolition charges, and any prototype accompanied by the phrase *probably only a little*. Stone firing bays face away from the town. Observation shelters stand behind angled walls with narrow viewing slits.

Colored flags announce the day's authorized hazards. A blue flag indicates smoke, red indicates heat or flame, yellow indicates force or flying fragments, green indicates fumes requiring masks, and a white flag indicates a public demonstration considered safe from the marked line. More than one flag may fly. No flag means the yard is closed, not that conditions are ideal for an unscheduled experiment.

The town's famous prohibition against explosions before breakfast applies with particular force. The testing clock begins after the fire crew has eaten, inspected the yard, and confirmed that no civic festival, visiting delegation, funeral, nesting animal, or unexplained wagon occupies the impact area.

  

#### The Wash Garden

Water leaving the laboratory passes through settling tanks, charcoal beds, sand filters, and planted basins containing reeds and other hardy vegetation. Each stage can be isolated if contamination is discovered. Treated water is tested before entering the town's drainage system.

The wash garden began as a condition imposed by Greengripe's sewer crews. It has since become a working demonstration of how sanitation, herbal knowledge, and alchemy support one another. Plants unsuitable for medicine or consumption may still reveal changes in water quality through color, growth, or the development of qualities the gardeners record under *other*.

Velka of the Sootscale has assisted with the garden and insists that a workshop depending upon clean water incurs an obligation to return water capable of continuing downstream. Griggit summarizes the same principle as *do not poison the neighbors*. Both formulations appear in university teaching materials.

  

#### The Wall of Useful Failures

One long corridor displays cracked vessels, warped tools, failed valves, separated mixtures, scorched labels, collapsed packaging, and products that became useful for something other than their intended purpose. Each object bears a short account of its ingredients, result, failure, and later application if one was found.

The display includes a roofing sealant now sold as boot waterproofing, a medicine too unpleasant to swallow that became an effective pest treatment, an invisible dye visible only to certain animals, and the first chimney cap, whose principal contribution to alchemy was returning smoke with greater confidence.

The wall does not celebrate negligence. Objects produced by ignored instructions, concealed hazards, or reckless treatment of another person are kept in the restricted incident archive instead. A useful failure teaches because uncertainty was managed honestly. An avoidable injury teaches something else.

  

### Products of Fizzlesticks & Co.

  

#### Hanspur's Breath

*Hanspur's Breath* is a blue-green bubbling tonic developed by Griggit and Velka from restorative herbs, cleansing salts, mineral water, and several ingredients whose proportions remain proprietary. Properly diluted, it serves as a wound wash and treatment for common hangover symptoms. It is also advertised as making the drinker *more interesting*, a claim the university has classified as unverified and the customers have classified as obvious.

The preparation originated during one of Velka's visits to Greengripe. She and Griggit were attempting to produce a stable cleansing mixture suitable for river travelers. The first batch removed grime, dulled pain, produced a cloud of fragrant foam, and turned a wooden spoon purple for three days. Later batches retained the useful effects and most of the color while becoming less opinionated about utensils.

Velka blesses the water used for ceremonial batches when present. Ordinary production follows the same written formula without claiming that every bottle received individual divine attention.

  

#### Civic and Household Alchemy

Most of the shop's income comes from preparations less famous than Hanspur's Breath. Fire-suppressing globes break upon impact and spread a smothering foam across small fires. Luminous road paint marks bridge edges, steps, public pumps, and evacuation routes. Colored signal smoke allows scouts, road crews, and isolated settlements to communicate simple warnings across distance.

Breweries purchase vessel cleaners, yeast treatments, testing drops, and compounds that reveal contamination before a batch is served. Builders use waterproofing pastes, mortar additives, rust removers, wood treatments, and dyes that show whether a repaired surface has shifted. Households buy pest powders, soaps, stove cleaners, lamp oils, stain treatments, and sealed warming stones.

Fizzlesticks & Co. also fills military and emergency contracts. Such work includes smoke cover, signal devices, field disinfectants, water-testing preparations, portable light, cold-weather fire starters, and limited explosive charges. The shop no longer advertises weapons in its front window. Greengripe has learned that visiting children, foreign agents, and enthusiastic customers may be difficult to distinguish at a glance.

  

### People of Fizzlesticks & Co.

  

#### Griggit Fizzlesticks, Proprietor

Griggit Fizzlesticks is an energetic goblin alchemist whose coat pockets contain pencils, corks, string, chalk, measuring spoons, emergency biscuits, and at least one object he has forgotten. His laboratory smells of citrus, smoke, wet stone, and whatever problem occupied him most recently.

Griggit calls alchemy *the art of asking matter a question loudly enough to receive an answer*. He values curiosity, improvisation, and speed, but years of production have made him equally insistent upon records, clean apparatus, honest labels, and admitting when an answer is not yet understood.

He teaches through demonstration and interruption. Apprentices are encouraged to propose unusual methods, then required to identify containment, measurements, escape routes, and what evidence would prove the method unsuccessful. Griggit considers enthusiasm without preparation a valuable natural resource that must be refined before use.

  

#### Keeka Bottlesnap, Master Compounder

Keeka Bottlesnap directs ordinary production upon the mixing floor. A compact goblin with smoked-glass spectacles and an exceptional memory for ratios, she converts Griggit's successful experiments into formulas that other trained workers can reproduce safely.

Keeka has authority to stop any batch, including Griggit's. The condition was imposed by the Citizen Council after it became clear that a proprietor supervising his own favorite experiment might experience a temporary shortage of skepticism. She maintains production schedules, assigns inspections, and signs the final slate before a new preparation reaches the front counter.

  

#### Orrik Fume-Eater, Keeper of Vents

Orrik Fume-Eater maintains the chimneys, pressure shutters, water lines, drains, filters, fire stations, and test-yard barriers. He acquired his name after clearing a blocked vent during an emergency while wearing a filter mask large enough to cover most of his head. He denies the more colorful versions of the story.

Orrik is empowered to close rooms whose safety systems are not functioning. Researchers who describe ventilation as unrelated to their intellectual work receive a tour of the roof, gutters, filter beds, ash traps, replacement panels, and invoices produced by the last person who shared that opinion.

  

#### Vara Moss, Herbal Buyer

Vara Moss is a half-elf herbalist who purchases cultivated and gathered ingredients, verifies their identity, and maintains agreements with farmers, foresters, healers, and collectors throughout the western Greenbelt. She rejects rare plants harvested in ways that destroy the stand supplying them.

Vara also supervises the shop's small reference garden and drying loft. She frequently travels with university students conducting field surveys and has prevented several promising alchemists from poisoning themselves with plants they identified principally through confidence.

  

### Velka and Griggit

Velka visits Fizzlesticks & Co. whenever her travels bring her to Greengripe. She helps gather river and marsh plants, inspects the wash garden, blesses water used in particular preparations, and intervenes when an experiment catches fire or attempts to become a form of local wildlife.

Griggit describes magic as the science of convincing the world to behave differently. Velka describes it as learning which changes the world is already attempting. Their disagreement has produced useful medicines, prolonged arguments, and one cleaning compound that refuses to work unless its container is opened beside running water.

The shop keeps a low shelf of driftwood, blue glass, shells, wire, and failed metal fittings for Velka's talismans. In return, she supplies unusual ingredients and observations from settlements whose practical problems may suggest the next product worth developing.

  

### Warnings and Emergency Signals

The shop uses bells, whistles, flags, and lamps because no single warning method remains reliable in smoke, darkness, noise, or panic. One bell calls the floor supervisor. Two bells halt active heating. Three bells order evacuation of the affected room. A continuous bell empties the building and summons the town fire brigade.

A rising whistle indicates airborne fumes and requires masks or movement upwind. A descending whistle warns of liquid entering drains. Red lamps mark fire, green lamps mark fumes, and blue lamps mark contamination involving water. Workers learn the signals before they are allowed to prepare a batch.

Every alarm is investigated, including false alarms. The person who sounds one in good faith is not punished for interrupting work. Greengripe's safety officers regard embarrassment as cheaper than rebuilding.

  

### Fizzlesticks & Co. and the Free University

Nok-Nok College of Practical Arts and Civic Service recognizes Fizzlesticks & Co. as a field teaching laboratory. Students spend several weeks in Greengripe learning commercial production, fire response, sanitation, waste treatment, public demonstration, and the documentation of failure.

Griggit serves as a visiting practitioner rather than a conventional professor. His university lectures usually begin with a prepared topic and conclude with a demonstration the faculty believed had been described differently. Keeka teaches batch control and argues that her sessions should occur first, while students still possess notebooks with intact corners.

Research conducted at the shop remains subject to both university review and Greengripe law. Academic affiliation does not exempt an experiment from fire codes, drainage restrictions, controlled-substance rules, or the prohibition against explosions before breakfast. The Citizen Council required this sentence to appear in the agreement more than once.

The best-known paper produced through the partnership is *After the Flash: Converting Experimental Alchemy into Civic Supply*. It examines the steps between discovering that a mixture works once and producing it reliably for people who cannot afford to learn its limitations through personal experience.

  

### Customs of the Shop

  

#### Breakfast before Ignition

No burner, fuse, heated retort, or powered grinder begins before the morning crew has eaten. The custom began as compliance with Nok-Nok's famous law and survived because hungry alchemists make poor measurements. Cold preparation and cleaning may begin earlier, though Griggit insists cleaning is not sufficiently exciting to violate the governor's memory.

  

#### The Second Label

Every new preparation is labeled twice: once by its maker and once by someone who did not make it. If the second person cannot understand the first label, the product is not ready to leave the bench. Elaborate handwriting, private abbreviations, and confidence do not count as ingredients.

  

#### The Useful Failure

At the end of each month, workers nominate one failed experiment for the public wall. The maker explains what was attempted, what occurred, and whether any honest use was found. The selected object receives a brass tag and a place in the corridor. The ceremony includes applause but no claim that failure is automatically admirable.

  

#### The First Safe Sip

An apprentice who completes training prepares a simple restorative tonic from a standard formula. The apprentice tests the equipment, documents the batch, cleans the station, and serves the first safe sip to the person who most often stopped them from doing something memorable. Most choose Keeka.

  

### Place in Greengripe

Fizzlesticks & Co. embodies modern Greengripe's refusal to choose between goblin exuberance and public responsibility. Its patched walls, colored vents, experimental products, and unpredictable proprietor are not evidence that regulation failed. They exist beside batch ledgers, fire brigades, sewer authority, university review, and a master compounder empowered to tell the proprietor no.

The shop's most important products are not necessarily its most dramatic. A fire-suppressing globe in a kitchen, clean water leaving a workshop, a signal seen by a road crew, or an affordable wound wash may matter more than the firework visible above the walls. Greengripe understands spectacle. Fizzlesticks & Co. demonstrates that it also understands repetition, maintenance, and the quiet dignity of something working correctly for the hundredth time.

Visitors often pause outside to watch the copper cowls turn and guess which roof panel is newest. Residents listen instead for the ordinary sounds: grinders, bottles, whistles, laughter, and the absence of an alarm where one might reasonably have been expected.

> The difference between alchemy and a mess is whether the next person can learn from it.
>
> — Keeka Bottlesnap
