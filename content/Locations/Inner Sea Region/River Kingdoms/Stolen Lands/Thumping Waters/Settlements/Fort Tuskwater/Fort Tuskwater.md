# Fort Tuskwater


### Fort Tuskwater

Perched on the northern shore of Lake Tuskwater, **Fort Tuskwater** stands where civilization and wilderness meet—its timber walls reflected in the still waters below, its piers alive with the creak and salt of a dozen fishing boats. Once a place of violence and infamy, it has become a bustling lakeside town and a vital artery of commerce for the Republic of Thumping Waters. The wind carries both the scent of lakewater and the hum of daily life—a promise that the past will not be repeated, only reclaimed.

#### Overview

Fort Tuskwater rises where the ruins of the old bandit fortress once stood, rebuilt from blood and ash into something worthy of pride. The town’s **wooden walls** still bear the marks of old battles, though fresh carvings of the republic’s crest now line their gates. Within, narrow **streets** wind down toward a busy **waterfront** where fishermen mend their nets, traders haggle over hauls, and sailors sing coarse songs that carry across the lake. The **pier** teems with activity from dawn to dusk, its **fishing fleets** fanning out each morning and returning at sunset, their nets heavy with silver pike and lake trout.

The **inn**, *The Stag’s Respite*, stands as a landmark—a cheerful building raised over the bones of the Stag Lord’s old stronghold. Travelers speak of its roaring hearths, its infamous house ale (“Fortfire”), and its policy of throwing any drunk who mentions bandits directly into the lake. The **marketplace** nearby is the town’s heart, filled with traders from Olegton, Sootscale Valley, and beyond. Fishmongers call out prices beside cobblers, brewers, and the ever-smiling herbalist who claims to have a tincture for “sea legs and sore heads alike.”

#### History

Once the seat of the Stag Lord’s bloody reign, the fort’s hilltop keep was taken by the Tubthumpers in their earliest campaign. What remained was ruin—splintered gates, burned ramparts, and a place so soaked in blood that the crows refused to leave. But time, and purpose, turned it into something better. Builders from Thumpington and masons from Sootscale Valley worked side by side with fisherfolk to raise new palisades, dredge the old harbor, and carve a future out of a grave. Thus was born Fort Tuskwater—a place that remembers where it came from but no longer bows to it.

Today, a modest **museum** occupies the restored tower of the old keep, preserving relics from the republic’s founding days: the Stag Lord’s battered helm, the Tubthumpers’ campaign maps, and a massive antlered bow mounted over the entrance as a reminder of the price of tyranny. Visitors often leave flowers or coins beneath it—not out of reverence for the fallen, but in gratitude for those who rose after them.

#### Government and Economy

Fort Tuskwater is governed by a **harbormaster’s council** of merchants, fishermen, and guild representatives who manage the ebb and flow of trade across the lake. Their authority extends over the docks, fleets, and local militia, whose **barracks** stand watch at the town’s northern gate. A **watchtower** on the shoreline signals to passing vessels, while the **town hall**—once the Stag Lord’s banquet hall—now serves as the center of administration and law. A newly installed **sewer system** keeps the waterfront clean, a miracle many say rivals the Tubthumpers’ own victories.

The lake is the fort’s lifeblood. The **fishing fleets** are legendary throughout the Stolen Lands, their crews brave enough to ply even the deep southern waters where strange shadows sometimes stir beneath the surface. The daily catch sustains not only the town but much of the republic. The marketplace thrives on that bounty, supplemented by trade caravans and the occasional scholar drawn to study the lake’s unusual ecosystem—or the ruins that still lie sunken beneath its depths.

#### Culture and Daily Life

The people of Fort Tuskwater are pragmatic, proud, and loud enough to be heard across the lake. The day begins with the toll of the harbor bell, summoning the fishermen, and ends with song and laughter spilling from the **inn** and the piers. The **herbalist** is as much gossip hub as apothecary, and the **marketplace** hosts weekly festivals where fish stew and fiddle music flow in equal measure. Though the fort’s walls are wooden, its heart is iron—every citizen knows how to pick up a spear or throw a rope if called upon. Strangers are welcomed with wary curiosity, and troublemakers are dealt with by the same simple method as drunks: one good toss into the lake to cool their heads.

Despite its noise and salt, Fort Tuskwater is not without grace. The lake at twilight turns to glass, reflecting a hundred lanterns as the fleets return home. The museum’s bell chimes low across the water, and children chase fireflies along the dock. It is, at last, a place of peace built on the bones of violence—a city that forgave itself without forgetting.

#### Modern Role

Today, Fort Tuskwater serves as the republic’s southern trade hub, the gate between the Slough and the heartland. Its fleets ferry goods and messages across the lakes, its markets draw merchants from Brevoy to Pitax, and its people stand watch over the waters that once birthed monsters. The town’s motto, carved above the harbor gate, reads: *“From still waters, strength.”*

When the mists roll in and the lanterns of the fishing boats gleam like stars across the dark, one might almost forget that this place was ever cursed. Almost. But if you ask any local what keeps the shadows at bay, they’ll give the same answer every time: “The lake remembers, and so do we.”

## General Details

##### From the Chronicle of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

*The Night the Stag Fell*

I never saw the battle myself, but I’ve heard it retold a hundred ways—each voice different, each heartbeat the same. The night the Stag Lord fell was not the end of the war for the Stolen Lands. It was the moment the land remembered how to breathe again.

His fortress rose on the northern shore of the **Tuskwater**, a crooked crown of wood and stone surrounded by graves. His bandits ruled through fear, his undead through silence, and the fort itself stank of wine, blood, and arrogance. The Tubthumpers came to end it—with six against a fortress and the kind of courage that looks like madness until it works.

Their plan began with a bottle of poisoned liquor and a smile. Sean went alone through the front gates, claiming to be a survivor of the Thorn River camp. Inside, he found **Akiros Ismort**—a fallen paladin, half-drowned in shame—who saw in Sean a chance to climb back toward the light. While they plotted inside, the rest of the Tubthumpers circled the fort’s blind side, slogging through the graveyard under a black and moonless sky. That was where they found the truth: the dead didn’t rest at the Stag Lord’s command. They watched, waiting for the next to join them.

Tristian lit his torch and ran for the northern gate, a beacon in the dark. It was supposed to be a distraction. It nearly became a martyrdom. The bandits poured from the palisades, crossbows snapping, but their attention gave Mairi the moment she needed. She leapt the wall like an avalanche, sword gleaming, and hit the courtyard like a thunderclap. Behind her came Ally, bright-eyed and blazing with magic, and Ant, already loosing arrows faster than the bandits could count. Pai’s voice rose from behind them—not a battle cry, but a prayer—and her healing light wound through the chaos, closing wounds faster than blades could open them.

Inside, Sean’s ruse shattered as blood and fire met. He cut down **Dovan of Nisroch** before the man could draw breath for treachery, then turned to face the Stag Lord himself. They say the bandit king stood taller than any man, his face hidden behind a stag’s skull, his voice hollow as a tomb. He loosed three arrows before Sean reached him. The third pierced his chest. When Mairi saw him fall, she didn’t scream. She didn’t hesitate. She charged.

The duel that followed became legend. Mairi fought bareheaded, her long hair streaked with blood, her greatsword moving like a thing alive. The Stag Lord met her with cold precision, each strike meant to kill. She met him with fury that refused to die. Steel rang against steel, sparks scattered like stars, and for a moment even the dead in their graves might have turned to watch. When his bow shattered, he drew a blade. When that broke, he reached for her throat. She answered with the flat of her sword—and then the edge. One last breath, one final blow, and the Stag Lord fell. His mask cracked against the stones, and the undead around the fortress crumbled into dust.

By dawn, the fort was fire and ruin. The Tubthumpers buried the dead, tended their wounded, and watched the smoke rise across the lake. Tristaian treated Sean's wounds. Pai helped bury the dead. Ally cataloged the stolen goods. Ant interrogated the survivors. Mairi, bloodied and unbowed, stood on the rampart and said nothing at all. The sun broke over the Tuskwater, and for the first time, it did not rise on bandits. It rose on a beginning.

Now, when I walk the docks of **Fort Tuskwater** and hear the fishermen singing where soldiers once screamed, I think of that night. Of how six souls broke a kingdom’s curse with a sword, a lie, a prayer, and a promise. The fort they burned became the heart of a republic. And the woman who slew the Stag Lord became the one who taught a nation how to stand.

*—Linzi*

Editor’s note: While Linzi’s claim that Mairi “leapt the wall like an avalanche” has been disputed by surviving witnesses, Mairi herself refuses to comment, which only strengthens the legend.
