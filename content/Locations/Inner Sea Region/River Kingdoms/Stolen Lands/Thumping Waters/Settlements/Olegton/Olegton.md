# Olegton


### Olegton

Olegton stands at the northern edge of the Republic of Thumping Waters, where the grasslands of Rostland give way to the settled lands of the Greenbelt. Though modest in size, it holds immense historical weight—it was here, within the palisade of Oleg’s Trading Post, that the Tubthumpers struck their first victory against lawlessness and began the long work of founding a nation. Today, Olegton remains a thriving frontier town, proof that endurance and decency can build something lasting on even the roughest soil.

#### The Founding of the Trading Post

Before there was a republic, there was **Oleg’s Trading Post**. Oleg and Svetlana Leveton, seeking freedom from Brevoy’s intrigues, claimed the ruins of an abandoned border fort and rebuilt it as a trading station for trappers and travelers. A **ten-foot-high wooden palisade** surrounded the post, its four watchtowers crowned with rusting, useless catapults. The wide wooden gate creaked but held fast through storms and bandit raids alike. Life was hard, but Oleg was stubborn and Svetlana was kind—and together they managed to make a place that felt almost like home.

That home might not have survived if not for the Tubthumpers. When the Stag Lord’s bandits came to collect their “tolls,” the heroes of Thumping Waters were there to meet them. The skirmish was brief, fierce, and decisive. By the time the smoke cleared, the post was free, the bandits scattered, and the frontier had its first spark of hope. From that day forward, Oleg’s Trading Post became a symbol of the Greenbelt’s rebirth—and of what ordinary people could accomplish when backed by courage and friendship.

#### From Outpost to Town

Peace brought settlers. Caravans began to arrive from Brevoy, and artisans from across the frontier followed. A handful of homes and workshops grew up around the palisade, soon joined by a modest brewery whose rich ale still defines local hospitality. The outpost slowly evolved into a proper town, its wooden walls now ringed by cottages, warehouses, and fields. The old catapults were dismantled and replaced with bell towers and banners. By common acclaim, the settlement was renamed **Olegton**—in honor of the couple who built the first foothold of the republic.

#### Government and People

**Mayor Svetlana Leveton** now leads Olegton officially, guiding the community with the same empathy and iron will that once sustained it through famine and fear. Her administration focuses on self-sufficiency and fairness: new settlers receive aid until their first harvest, and disputes are settled as often with tea and conversation as with law. Her husband Oleg still manages the trade house and the brewery but happily leaves politics to his wife. The citizens call her “the heart of the north,” a title she pretends to dislike but secretly cherishes.

The town’s healer and spiritual guide is **Jhod Kavken**, a weathered traveler who serves as counselor, medic, and moral compass. Though not a formally ordained cleric, his faith in Erastil and his steady wisdom have kept the community grounded. Even **Bokken**, the eccentric hermit-alchemist who lives twenty miles away, visits twice a month with medicines and tonics—usually accompanied by long lectures about “the proper appreciation of chemistry” that no one dares interrupt.

#### Trade and Life

Olegton remains a small settlement—little more than the original trading post, a cluster of homes, the brewery, and a market square—but its influence reaches far. Merchants traveling between Brevoy and Thumping Waters stop here to rest, resupply, and exchange news. The brewery’s copper kettles have become a local landmark, and its amber ale, *Leveton’s Reserve*, finds its way into taverns as far south as Thumpington. Every week, the market within the old palisade fills with the clamor of barter, the smell of roasting meat, and the sound of laughter that seems to echo all the way to the border.

For all its growth, Olegton still feels like the frontier. The wind rolls through the grass, wagons creak along the old South Rostland Road, and the wooden walls of the trading post stand firm—less as defense, more as memory. The people here don’t talk much about founding a nation; they’re too busy living in the proof that it can be done.

## General Details

##### From the Journal of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

*The First Supper at Oleg’s*

You can always tell when a great story begins—not by the clash of swords, but by the smell of stew. That’s how it was the night the Tubthumpers freed Oleg’s Trading Post from the Stag Lord’s bandits. There wasn’t a parade, or fanfare, or even enough chairs to go around. Just smoke from the cookfire, rain in the air, and Svetlana Leveton ladling soup into mismatched bowls while Mairi stood guard at the gate and Antson tried to look modest about the arrow he’d put through a bandit’s hat. (He’s still telling that part wrong, by the way. The hat was already on the ground.)

Oleg was pacing the yard, muttering about how “heroes eat too much,” while Svetlana kept refilling our bowls every time his back was turned. The walls creaked, the torches hissed, and for the first time, the old fort didn’t feel like a trap. It felt like home. We didn’t know it yet, but that meal—bread torn by hand, ale shared straight from the jug—was the first council of what would become a republic.

Jhod arrived the next morning, all beard and good intentions, insisting that the victory was divine providence. Maybe he was right. Maybe it was just luck, or hunger, or stubbornness. But looking around that fire, I saw something taking root: trust. The kind that grows in hard soil and survives every storm. By the time the stew was gone, Svetlana had already started making lists of supplies, Mairi had plans for a watch rotation, and I was taking notes, of course—because someone had to make sure history remembered it properly.

Years later, when you walk through the gates of Olegton, you can still smell the same stew from the tavern and hear the same laughter spilling out of the old palisade. The catapults are gone, the walls are taller, and the Levetons have titles now—but it’s still Oleg’s place at heart. A fortress built on good soup, good company, and the impossible idea that ordinary people can change the world with both hands and a spoon.

*—Linzi*

Editor’s note: The “stew of destiny” is not on the current Olegton tavern menu, despite repeated public requests.
