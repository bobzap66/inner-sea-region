# Fort Drelev


### Fort Drelev

At the northwestern rim of the [Hooktongue Slough](../../../Hooktongue%20Slough/Hooktongue%20Slough.md) stands Fort Drelev—a fortress whose walls gleam with ambition but whose foundations are sunk deep in cowardice. Founded as [Brevoy](../../../../../Brevoy/Brevoy.md)’s bold claim on the western frontier, it has become a monument to vanity and decay, a reminder that tyranny requires no monsters to thrive.

  

#### Founding

When Brevoy’s Swordlords began their great project to civilize the [Stolen Lands](../../../Stolen%20Lands.md), Baron Hannis Drelev was among the first to petition for a charter. A younger son of a Brevic noble house, Drelev was determined to carve his own destiny, to prove he could rule as well as any Aldori or Lebeda. His wife, Pavetta Stroon-Drelev, secured financial and political backing from her wealthy Brevic family, and her brother, the wizard Imeckus Stroon, lent his arcane expertise and sharp ambition to the venture.

  

The chosen site—a hill overlooking the northern waters of the Hooktongue Slough—was defensible but isolated. Drelev proclaimed it a gateway to prosperity: a fortress between the wild [Narlmarches](../../../Greenbelt/Narlmarches.md) and the [River Kingdoms](../../../../River%20Kingdoms.md) beyond. Yet his first act upon arriving was not to build a port or farm, but to construct a keep. His second was to issue taxes to fund its decoration. Even before its walls rose, Fort Drelev was already rotting from the top down.

  

#### Rule and Reputation

Baron Drelev’s rule is marked by pomp and cruelty in equal measure. His court mimics Brevoy’s nobility—silk gowns in the mud, formal banquets in half-flooded halls. The outer town languishes while the baron’s inner circle drinks, gambles, and plots. Pavetta Stroon-Drelev wields quiet power through manipulation, her words honeyed and venomous in equal measure, while her brother Imeckus ensures obedience with arcane displays meant to cow dissenters.

  

The baron’s paranoia has become legend. He distrusts his own soldiers, surrounds himself with imported mercenaries, and forbids travel without writs bearing his seal. His fear of rebellion is justified; the people despise him, and the noble families he slighted in Brevoy whisper of his inevitable downfall. Yet Drelev’s grip endures, a mixture of fear, wealth, and the grim inertia of those too beaten to hope.

  

#### Life in the Fort

Fort Drelev is divided into two worlds. Within the keep’s inner walls lies opulence—ballrooms with marble floors, imported wine, and courtiers wearing the colors of a kingdom that never truly existed. Beyond those walls sprawls the Commons, a warren of sagging roofs and flooded streets. The outer gates are guarded by dour mercenaries who answer only to the baron’s chamberlain, and few citizens are permitted inside after dark.

  

The town’s most infamous landmark is Satinder’s Playhouse, an opulent inn and theater run by Satinder Morne, a half-orc courtesan whose establishment provides the only sanctioned relief from the baron’s dour rule. Her reputation as hostess, gossip, and broker of secrets gives her unusual freedom. Some say she knows more about the fort’s true politics than the baron himself, and that her stage performances hide coded critiques of his rule.

  

#### Politics and Paranoia

Drelev’s government is small but poisonous. His council consists of his wife Pavetta, the wizard Imeckus Stroon, and a handful of functionaries chosen for loyalty rather than skill. Taxes are collected ruthlessly, and the slightest criticism is treated as sedition. The dungeons beneath Drelev Keep are said to hold merchants accused of “treasonous accounting,” as well as soldiers who once dared to question an order. Despite this, the baron insists his people love him—an illusion carefully reinforced by staged public festivals and heavy-handed propaganda.

  

Trade caravans from Brevoy have grown rare. The swamps are treacherous, and few merchants wish to pay Drelev’s tolls. Yet he boasts of prosperity in letters to his patrons, each report polished to perfection and filled with lies. Pavetta ensures those lies reach the right ears, while Imeckus keeps unwanted truths from leaving the fort at all.

  

#### Relations with Thumping Waters

To the east lies Thumping Waters, a republic that has risen where Drelev expected only failure. The contrast cuts deep. [Mairi Blackwood](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/Characters/Mairi.md)’s government stands for merit and freedom, while Drelev’s court reeks of hereditary privilege and fear. The baron speaks of Thumping Waters with contempt, calling it a “bandit state,” but his sleepless nights tell another story. His spies report the Tubthumpers’ growing power, and the more he hears of their victories, the more he drowns his anxieties in wine and bluster.

  

Drelev’s envoys have been scarce and stiffly formal. He has yet to acknowledge Thumping Waters as a peer, but he fears its expansion. Rumors swirl that his advisors debate whether to ally with Brevoy once more, open trade with the new republic, or strike preemptively before the Tubthumpers turn their eyes west. Meanwhile, his people—hungry, restless, and desperate—pray that someone will come to liberate them from their lord before his pride destroys them all.

  

#### Notable Locations

**Drelev Keep:** The heart of the barony. Marble halls, gilt fixtures, and a constant damp that seeps up from the stone foundations. The dungeons below are rumored to connect to older tunnels leading into the Slough.**The Commons:** A wretched sprawl of hovels, fishmongers, and sunken streets. The poorest live on raised walkways to escape the mud, and the local militia extorts them for “maintenance fees.”**Satinder’s Playhouse:** Half theater, half tavern, and all intrigue. A sanctuary for merchants, mercenaries, and those who prefer to whisper their rebellion through art.**Temple of [Abadar](../../../../../../../Rules/Deities/Abadar.md) (Defunct):** Once the spiritual and financial heart of the fort, now shuttered after its high priest disappeared. Some say he fled east to Thumping Waters; others say he was buried beneath the keep for speaking of fairness.  

#### The Shape of Things to Come

Fort Drelev is a storm cloud on the horizon—its lightning delayed but inevitable. The baron’s excesses cannot last forever, and the people’s patience is brittle as glass. Whether the Tubthumpers move to liberate it, Brevoy intervenes, or Drelev’s own council turns upon him, the fort stands at the edge of its own reckoning. For now, its banners still fly, its guards still march, and its baron still smiles—but the foundations tremble, and the swamp is patient.
