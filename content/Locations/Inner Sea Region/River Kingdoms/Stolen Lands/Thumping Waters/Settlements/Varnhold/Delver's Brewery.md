# Delver's Brewery

![[delver-s-brewery-portrait.png]]

*Compiled by the College of Letters and Public Record, Free University of Thumping Waters, from Varnhold's municipal gazetteer, the Tubthumpers' expedition inventory, brewery ledgers, and interviews with Durnan Stoutbeard and Velka of the Sootscale*

> A stout should not be mysterious. It should be dark, honest, and waiting when you come back.
>
> — Durnan Stoutbeard

Delver's Brewery is Varnhold's historic riverbank brewery and the birthplace of Cheerful Delver Stout. Its sign—a barrel beside a smiling dwarf—survived the Varnhold Vanishing, as did sealed stock, a hidden operating reserve, and the brewery's original recipe. Under brewmaster Durnan Stoutbeard, the restored business now produces both its traditional stout and Varnling Pale, the crisp oak-aged ale exported throughout the Republic of Thumping Waters.

The brewery is as much a part of Varnhold's recovery as its beer is part of the town's economy. Its vats stood full while the city was silent. Today their working noise, yeast, river water, and malt scent are treated as evidence that Varnhold has found its rhythm again.

  

### At a Glance

**Type:** Brewery, loading house, tasting room, and cooperage yard

**Location:** Crooked River bank, Varnhold

**Owner and Brewmaster:** Durnan Stoutbeard

**Historic Beer:** Cheerful Delver Stout

**Modern Export:** Varnling Pale

**Known For:** Riverbank vats, oak aging, a smiling-dwarf sign, and surviving the Vanishing

**Associated Person:** Velka of the Sootscale

**House Toast:** *To those who returned, and those whose lights we carry*

  

### The Brewery above the River

Delver's Brewery stands partly over the Crooked River bank. Its riverward side rests upon heavy stone and timber supports, allowing water access beneath the brewing floor and direct loading at a small dock. A wagon court connects the dock and storehouse to Varnhold's paved streets and marketplace.

The arrangement made good commercial sense when Maegar Varn's colony was young. Grain arrived by road, clean water rose from the river, and finished barrels could leave by wagon or boat. It also placed part of the brewery in the path of flood, ice, and anything else the Crooked River chose to carry downstream.

The original sign still hangs above the front entrance: a painted barrel and a smiling dwarf. Weather and smoke have darkened it, but Durnan refuses replacement. The smile is widely assumed to represent him. He says it represents the customer after the second cup.

  

### Founding

Varnhold was founded on 9 Pharast 4710 AR by Lord Maegar Varn and the Varnling Host. Among its first permanent structures was a brewery serving the new colony's laborers, soldiers, traders, and settlers. Durnan Stoutbeard established the house and named its first beer Cheerful Delver Stout.

The name honored miners, builders, tomb-seekers, and other people who spend the day beneath earth or stone and prefer to finish it somewhere with light. Early Cheerful Delver was hearty and durable but inconsistent. Durnan calls this *traditional variation*. Surviving expedition notes describe the old stock as decent but lacking refinement.

As Varnhold's farms and trade improved, so did the brewery. Better grain, cooperage, temperature control, and written batch records turned the Delver from a frontier necessity into a recognizable local brand. Durnan retained the broad flavor of the original stout while correcting the qualities that polite investigators had called rustic and impolite investigators had called other things.

  

### The Vanishing

In the autumn of 4715 AR, the cyclops lich Vordakai stole the souls of Varnhold's people. Meals, workshops, homes, and streets were abandoned in the middle of ordinary life. Delver's Brewery fell silent with full vats and a wagon waiting at its loading dock.

When the Tubthumpers investigated, they found sealed barrels of Cheerful Delver still drinkable: a pair upon the wagon and roughly a dozen more within the brewery. Contemporary counts differ by one barrel, proving that even supernatural calamity cannot eliminate inventory disputes.

Beneath a loose floorboard near the brewing vats, the investigators discovered a locked box containing 115 silver pieces and the recipe for Cheerful Delver Stout. The recipe represented far greater long-term value than the surviving beer. Its concealment was not evidence of wrongdoing; Durnan had hidden the brewery's reserve and trade knowledge where an ordinary thief would be unlikely to find either quickly.

After the Tubthumpers destroyed Vordakai and freed the captured townsfolk, the recipe was returned to Durnan. He remembers darkness, then waking to learn that strangers had entered his empty brewery, sampled its abandoned stout, opened his lockbox, defeated an ancient lich, and brought his customers home. He considers the sequence difficult to criticize in good faith.

  

### Restoration

The restored brewery became an early symbol of Varnhold's decision to resume ordinary life. Its supports were inspected, water system cleaned, spoiled ingredients destroyed, and every usable surface scrubbed. The surviving sealed stout was examined carefully. Some was sold, some retained for study, and one barrel reserved for the first public remembrance meal after the town's return.

Durnan rebuilt the riverward supports and improved the loading dock rather than moving the business inland. He argued that retreating from the river would preserve the wrong lesson. Flood precautions, raised storage, drainage gates, and emergency procedures were added instead.

The brewery later developed Varnling Pale, a crisp ale aged in oak barrels. Easier to transport and suited to a wider range of meals than the heavy Delver, the pale became Varnhold's principal export. Cheerful Delver remains the house's identity and its preferred drink during winter, remembrance gatherings, and arguments about whether refinement was ever desirable.

  

### The Building

  

#### The Brewhouse

The main brewhouse contains the mash tun, boiling kettles, work platforms, pumps, cooling equipment, and the restored vats beneath which the recipe box was found. Steam rises through louvered roof vents, carrying malt into the streets and mixing with the clean-earth scent of the surrounding fields.

Floors slope toward controlled drains and settling traps. River water is filtered, boiled where appropriate, and tested by appearance, odor, and taste before brewing. Durnan has little patience for the claim that alcohol corrects every flaw in water. It corrects thirst and judgment in proportions that require management.

  

#### The River Intake

A protected intake draws water from a calmer portion of the Crooked River. Screens exclude debris and larger aquatic visitors. A marked gauge records water level, while a bell line reaches the brewhouse so workers at the dock can warn those inside when the river rises unexpectedly.

After the flood associated with Velka, the intake was rebuilt with faster shutoff gates and an elevated bypass tank. The changes ensure that divine favor is never the brewery's only flood plan.

  

#### The Barrel Hall

Cheerful Delver matures in a cool stone chamber beneath the inland half of the building. Varnling Pale ages in a raised oak-barrel room with better airflow. Each barrel is marked with batch, filling date, grain source, intended use, and the name of the worker responsible for the seal.

Historic bottles and small casks from important batches occupy a locked rack. The collection includes samples of the stout found after the Vanishing. They are no longer served. Durnan says history may be swallowed, but not when it has been sitting in a questionable bottle for years.

  

#### The Loading Dock

The river dock and wagon platform allow barrels to move without passing through the public room. Iron rings secure barges beneath the overhang, and removable rails protect workers when the water is low. Merchants bound for the Dunsward, Thumpington, and other republican settlements collect shipments here.

The brewery maintains records of each cask's buyer and destination. The practice supports trade, identifies recurring leaks or spoilage, and prevents three customers from claiming that the same barrel rolled into the river.

  

#### The Delver's Room

A modest tasting room occupies the streetward side beneath the smiling-dwarf sign. It is not Varnhold's principal inn; that role belongs to the Lost Cup. The room serves brewery samples, bread, cheese, pickles, and simple hot food to customers, workers, and visitors waiting upon orders.

One wall displays the history of the brewery through labels, tools, barrel marks, and copied expedition notes. The original recipe is not displayed. A framed sheet described as the recipe contains Durnan's instructions for boiling potatoes and a warning against industrial espionage.

  

### Durnan Stoutbeard

Durnan Stoutbeard is a barrel-shaped dwarf with a gravelly laugh, a brewer's pride, and the permanent expression of someone who has just heard a customer misuse the word *stout*. He founded the brewery, survived the Vanishing, and returned to a trade whose ingredients had continued changing while he could not.

His humor conceals a disciplined approach to brewing. Durnan records temperatures, grain sources, fermentation behavior, barrel condition, river level, and customer complaints. He distrusts fashionable additions intended to disguise weak beer and reveres any improvement that makes an honest beer more consistent.

Durnan participates in Varnhold's commercial life and supplies festivals, civic gatherings, and the Lost Cup. He contributes beer to the Festival of Lights but refuses to place alcohol in the floating lanterns, citing respect for the dead, river safety, and waste.

  

### The Cheerful Delver Stout

Cheerful Delver is a dark, malt-forward stout with toasted grain, restrained bitterness, and enough body to survive transport and cold weather. The modern version is smoother than the frontier batches discovered during the Vanishing, but Durnan deliberately preserves a dry, mineral finish associated with Varnhold's water.

The smiling dwarf and barrel appear on every cask. The commercial mark predates the Vanishing and has become a symbol of persistence: a cheerful face remaining above a door in an empty town, then welcoming its owners home.

The brewery releases a limited *Returned Batch* during the Festival of Lights. A portion of its proceeds supports the museum, library, and survivor services. Each cup is accompanied by the house toast to those who returned and those whose lights are carried.

  

### Varnling Pale

Varnling Pale is a crisp, oak-aged ale developed after Varnhold's restoration. Lighter than Cheerful Delver and better suited to long shipments, it became the brewery's principal republican export. Its label shows the Tors of Levenies beneath a rising lamp rather than the traditional smiling dwarf.

The beer's name honors Maegar Varn's settlers without treating the restored city as property of the past. Durnan describes the two house beers simply: the Delver remembers what Varnhold was building; the Pale celebrates that it kept building.

  

### Velka and the Flood

Velka of the Sootscale became Durnan's friend during one of her visits to Varnhold. She was drawn to the brewery's dependence upon grain, river water, yeast, time, and careful change—transformation through living processes rather than force alone.

Three years before this account was compiled, the Crooked River flooded and threatened the brewery's lower cellar and barrel stores. Workers raised what they could while Durnan prepared to lose a season's product. Velka waded into the water and prayed to Hanspur. Within the hour, the river receded enough for the remaining barrels to be secured.

Whether the change resulted from her prayer, the passing flood crest, opened debris downstream, or the River Lord's particular appreciation for stout remains unresolved. Durnan swears by Velka regardless. The brewery's subsequent floodgates and elevated storage demonstrate that gratitude has not replaced engineering.

  

### Blessing the Brew

When Velka is in Varnhold, she blesses the water before an important batch. Durnan then draws the first finished cup and offers a toast *to the god who keeps the taps full*. He drinks it himself, maintaining that delegating a sacred quality inspection would be disrespectful.

A braided charm of hops and river reeds hangs above the brewhouse door. Durnan says every batch produced since it appeared has tasted like luck. Velka points out that he also improved the cooling system that week. He regards this as supporting evidence.

The brewery is not a Hanspurite temple. Its blessing exists comfortably beside Varnhold's shrine to Sarenrae, Erastil, and Desna and the town's broader culture of mercy, family, safe journeys, and remembrance. Workers may participate, observe, or continue cleaning equipment.

  

### Customs of the Brewery

  

#### The First Returned Cup

At each Festival of Lights, the first cup of Returned Batch is poured and left untouched while lanterns are placed upon the river. After the observance, it is divided among brewery workers and survivors present in the room.

  

#### The Loose Board

A deliberately loose floorboard remains near the restored vats. It no longer conceals coin or trade secrets. Apprentices place written predictions beneath it before their first independent batch and read them after the beer is finished. Most predictions are less accurate than the original recipe.

  

#### Fifteen Barrels

During inventory night, workers toast the barrels found sealed after the Vanishing. One account records fourteen; another records fifteen. The brewery resolves the disagreement by pouring fifteen small cups and asking which person volunteers to surrender theirs for historical accuracy.

  

#### The River's Favorite Priestess

When Velka departs Varnhold, Durnan raises a mug toward the current and says, *To the river's favorite priestess*. Regulars repeat the toast. Visitors who ask whether the foam actually gleams brighter are served enough stout to conduct their own research.

  

### Place in Modern Varnhold

Delver's Brewery supports Varnhold's farms, cooperage, wagon traffic, river shipping, marketplace, festivals, and republican trade. Its two major beers carry different faces of the city outward: Cheerful Delver's dark survival and Varnling Pale's bright renewal.

More importantly, it fills the air with ordinary sound. Barrels roll. Pumps work. Laborers argue about grain. Customers laugh beneath a sign that once watched an empty street. In a city determined never to become silent again, the brewery's daily noise is a public service.

  
> We came back to find the beer waiting. Not perfect beer, mind you. But patient. You learn something about hope from a sealed barrel.
>
> — Durnan Stoutbeard
