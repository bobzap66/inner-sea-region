# Varnhold


### Varnhold

Tucked against the foothills of the [Tors of Levenies](../../../Nomen%20Heights/Tors%20of%20Levenies.md) where the [Dunsward](../../../Nomen%20Heights/Dunsward.md) meets the grasslands, **Varnhold** stands as both a memorial and a triumph. Once the proud colony of Lord [Maegar Varn](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/NPCs/Maegar%20Varn.md) and his Varnling Host, it fell silent for only a handful of weeks during the mysterious calamity known as the *Varnhold Vanishing*. Though brief, the event left scars that have never fully faded. Today, the town thrives again under the banner of the Republic of Thumping Waters—rebuilt not from ruin, but from remembrance.

#### Overview

Varnhold lies at the western edge of the Dunsward, its **paved streets** tracing the same careful grid first laid by Maegar Varn’s engineers. **Stone walls** guard a settlement of sturdy homes, bright workshops, and open courtyards where the wind carries the scent of rivergrass and grain. Within those walls stand the bustling **marketplace**, the domed **town hall**, and the restored **mansion** that once served as Varn’s residence and now functions as both museum and seat of government. A **park** of silverleaf trees fills the central square, its benches carved with the names of the vanished and the saved alike.

The town is orderly but not cold: children run beneath banners, traders call out from wagon-stalls, and the ring of hammers from the **smithy** mingles with the soft toll of the **shrine’s** bells. The air smells faintly of malt from the **brewery** and clean earth from the nearby fields. Even after everything it endured, Varnhold feels alive—like a heartbeat that skipped once, then found its rhythm again.

#### History

Varnhold was founded by **Lord Maegar Varn** and his mercenary company, the Varnling Host, as [Brevoy](../../../../../Brevoy/Brevoy.md)’s easternmost colony. For a time, it flourished, its people known for discipline, curiosity, and pride. But in the autumn of 4715 AR, all of that stopped. The town fell eerily silent—no signs of battle, no tracks, only open doors and abandoned meals. Within a month and a half, the Tubthumpers arrived to investigate. What they found beneath the Tors of Levenies would become legend: the cyclops lich **Vordakai**, awakened from his tomb, had drained the souls of the townsfolk to restore his unholy life.

The heroes destroyed the lich and freed the captured spirits, returning Varnhold’s people to the world of the living. Some remembered only darkness; others remembered nothing at all. But they were alive—and determined that their survival should mean something. The Tubthumpers helped them rebuild, strengthening the walls and sanctifying the streets. By the end of the year, Varnhold was once again a living town, its lights burning through the Dunsward night.

#### Government

**Governor Serana Lethro** now leads Varnhold—a scholar, diplomat, and one of Maegar Varn’s former scribes. She survived the Vanishing by sheer chance, having been west on a supply mission when it occurred. Returning to find the town lifeless, she took up the work of restoration and, after the lich’s defeat, was formally appointed governor by the republic. Serana rules with the patience of a historian and the resolve of a survivor. She listens first, acts second, and keeps detailed records of every decision—a habit born, she says, from living through a month when there were no voices left to record anything at all.

She works closely with **Xamanthe Silverfire**, emissary of the [Nomen Centaurs](../../../../../../../Groups/Nomen%20Centaurs.md), maintaining a fragile but enduring alliance between Varnhold and the tribes of the plains. Under their shared stewardship, the old borderlands between human and centaur have become meeting grounds instead of battlegrounds, their trade roads marked by both hoofprints and wagon tracks.

#### Economy and Life

Varnhold’s economy is built on craftsmanship, farming, and cautious trade. The **brewery** produces Varnling Pale, a crisp ale aged in oak barrels and shipped across the republic. The **smithy**, **tannery**, and **herbalist** supply everything a frontier settlement might need, while the **library**—once Maegar Varn’s private archive—now serves as school and record hall. The **inn**, *The Lost Cup*, still bears the soot marks from the fires of that terrible season but remains one of the most welcoming taverns in the east. Four **trade shops** line the main road, their wagons carrying tools and textiles to the centaur lands beyond.

Life here moves with gentle precision. Farmers rise with the dawn; artisans sing while they work; children light small candles at dusk to honor those who vanished and returned. The town observes a single great festival each year—the **Festival of Lights**—when every house sets lanterns on the river and the water glows like liquid stars. “For every light that was lost,” the governor says, “another still shines.”

#### Faith and Culture

The **shrine** at the heart of Varnhold honors [Sarenrae](../../../../../../../Rules/Deities/Sarenrae.md), [Erastil](../../../../../../../Rules/Deities/Erastil.md), and [Desna](../../../../../../../Rules/Deities/Desna.md)—gods of mercy, family, and safe journeys. Services are simple: song, prayer, and gratitude. Many of the priests are [Survivors](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Abomination%20Vaults/Vignettes%20-%20Abomination%20Vaults/Survivors.md) of the Vanishing, their faith tempered by what they endured. Art has become the town’s quiet obsession—murals of sunrise and open hands adorn its walls, and the **park’s** central fountain depicts a human and a centaur raising a shared torch. Varnhold’s people are not quick to laughter, but when they laugh, it sounds like something rediscovered rather than newly made.

#### Modern Role

Today, Varnhold serves as the republic’s eastern watchpost and cultural crossroads. Its **barracks** and **stable** house scouts who patrol the Dunsward, and its **marketplace** hums with trade between human settlers and Nomen traders. The **brewery** and **trade shops** sustain its economy, while the **library** and **museum wing** preserve its lessons. Varnhold’s people do not boast of their endurance—they live it quietly, day by day, under skies vast enough to remember everything that happened here.

When asked what drives her to remain, Governor Serana once said, “Because someone has to keep the lamps burning.” And each night, as those lamps flicker to life along the walls and the river reflects their light, Varnhold keeps its promise: it will never be silent again.

## General Details

##### From the Chronicle of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

*The Battle for the Blockhouse*

Dawn bled pale over **Varnhold**, and the air trembled with waiting. The town still wore its silence like a shroud—doors open, meals uneaten, lives interrupted. At its heart stood the blockhouse, a black scar of stone where the last noise in Varnhold had been screams. Within, the spriggans nested like carrion birds. The Tubthumpers came to drive them out.

They climbed the old watchtower as the first light touched the valley, hoping to slip in unseen. Matteo eased open the rooftop hatch—and the rusted hinges betrayed them with a shriek. The spriggans answered with howls. Then the morning broke apart in fire and fury.

The battle raged through the garrison like a storm let loose in a tomb. Spriggans swelled to monstrous size, smashing through beams and ceilings, their laughter twisting into roars. Mairi met them head-on, a whirlwind of steel and rage, her blade blazing brighter than the dawn outside. Fitzroy followed, his sword burning with divine light, each swing carving radiant scars across the dark. Ally’s magic turned the air molten, dazzling and terrible; Seamus thundered shot after shot into the melee while Matteo cut the shadows to ribbons. The blockhouse shook beneath the weight of it all—stone groaning, blood running in its seams.

By the time they reached the ground floor, the spriggan leader had broken. He fled through the smoke, but Matteo’s knives found his back before he could vanish into the mist. Silence fell again—final, absolute. The Tubthumpers stood amid ruin, breath ragged, armor dented, every heartbeat echoing in the emptiness that followed.

In the leader’s quarters they found the battle’s true prize: a bow of white yew, strung with silver hair and etched with symbols of wind and sky. **Skybolt**, the lost weapon of the centaurs. Even in the dim light, it gleamed like a promise. “Not ours,” Mairi said, her voice low. “A relic of the first guardians of these plains.” And the others knew she was right. Whatever curse had swallowed Varnhold, the key to its undoing was older than kingdoms.

When the Tubthumpers stepped from the blockhouse, the rising sun caught the smoke behind them and turned it to gold. Varnhold was still silent—but it was a different silence now, one left behind by victory. The heroes had broken the first grip of the darkness, and far to the east, something ancient stirred at the sound.

*—Linzi*

Editor’s note: Contemporary witnesses confirm that the rooftop hatch did, in fact, make the exact noise Linzi describes. Matteo continues to insist it was part of the plan.
