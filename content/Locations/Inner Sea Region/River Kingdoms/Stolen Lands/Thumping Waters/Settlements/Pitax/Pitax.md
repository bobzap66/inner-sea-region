# Pitax

![[shared-cover-110a4fd3e759.png]]

*A contemporary civic account compiled by Sella Copperclaw, Trade and Civic Affairs Correspondent, with archival assistance from Roald Celinnas*

Pitax is a city-state of the River Kingdoms situated along the Pitax River, west of the Republic of Thumping Waters. Once ruled by King Castruccio Irovetti, it is now governed by a provisional civic council formed from many of the artists, priests, merchants, scholars, and ordinary citizens who helped undermine his rule.

The city survived Irovetti. That fact has become the foundation of its new politics.

### At a Glance

**Government:** Provisional Civic Council

**First Speaker:** Annamede Belavara

**Former Ruler:** Castruccio Irovetti, deceased

**Current Status:** Independent provisional government negotiating an increasingly close relationship with Thumping Waters

**Principal Industries:** River trade, shipping, theater, music, education, crafts, and agriculture throughout the surrounding territory

**Notable Institutions:** The Red Crescent Theatre, the Academy of the Arts, the Crow's Feather, the Cathedral of Calistria, the Temple of Desna, and the Banded Houses

**Likely Future:** Accession to Thumping Waters has significant support but has not yet been formally approved

### The City and Its Reputation

Pitax has long considered itself the cultural heart of the River Kingdoms. Its theaters, academies, taverns, temples, and public squares attract performers and scholars from far beyond its borders. The city's river trade connects it to settlements throughout the Sellen basin, while its surrounding villages provide timber, wine, grain, and other goods that pass through its crowded docks.

Under Irovetti, culture became both ornament and weapon. The king spent lavishly upon monuments, private theaters, tournaments, and institutions intended to proclaim Pitax's greatness. He also demanded that artists flatter him, merchants serve his court, and public life reflect his personal taste. Talented performers learned to hide criticism inside comedy. Priests sheltered those discarded by royal prosperity. Merchants survived by appearing loyal while quietly calculating how long the arrangement could last.

The result was a city filled with institutions that remained stronger than the ruler attempting to possess them. When Irovetti led Pitax into war against Thumping Waters, those institutions became the foundation of the resistance that brought him down.

### The Fall of Irovetti

The overthrow did not begin with an army entering the streets. The Tubthumpers infiltrated Pitax in disguise and spent approximately ten days building support among people already prepared to resist. The Red Crescent Theatre provided refuge. Temples sheltered civilians. Merchants disrupted supplies. Harbor officials interfered with tariffs and shipping. Broadsheets and performances turned private resentment into public ridicule.

Annamede Belavara became one of the most visible voices of opposition. Her satire gave Pitaxians permission to laugh at a king who depended upon being feared and admired. Atlee Quinge and the Red Crescent Theatre helped organize the artistic community. Drey Yarness and Ghare Leotos brought the city's temples into the effort. Gasperre Liacenza, Jhofre Vascari, Joravin Pyathe, Roald Celinnas, Ingris, Saufie Cintost, and others supplied shelter, intelligence, trade pressure, or direct assistance.

The Tubthumpers entered the House of a Hundred Doors after the palace had been politically isolated. They killed General Avinash Jurrg and Villamor Koth, rescued Asmeranda Ilata, and pursued Irovetti into his hidden chambers. Irovetti surrendered during the final confrontation and was killed by Seamus while hostile magic still distorted what he perceived. His consort, Engelidis, escaped.

The king died beneath his palace. Pitax did not collapse with him.

### The Provisional Civic Council

In the days following Irovetti's death, the resistance coalition established a nine-member Provisional Civic Council. Its first priorities were food distribution, restoration of roads and bridges, payment or release of returning soldiers, care for the displaced, recovery of the missing, and prevention of retaliatory violence against citizens who had lived under Irovetti without meaningfully supporting his crimes.

Council decisions are made by majority vote. The First Speaker presides over meetings, represents Pitax in formal negotiations, and coordinates the work of the other speakers. The office does not possess the authority of the former crown, a limitation Annamede has described as its most important qualification.

#### Annamede Belavara, First Speaker

Annamede is the council's most prominent member and its principal public voice. A celebrated performer, formidable spellcaster, and longstanding critic of Irovetti, she helped turn satire into organized resistance and monitored the city's willingness to rise against the palace.

Following the liberation, she restored Ally Grainger after Irovetti's consort had transformed the Minister of Magic into stone. The agreement surrounding that service guaranteed Annamede a meaningful place in Pitax's new government. She has since made that place considerably larger through argument, competence, and an apparent refusal to end a meeting merely because everyone else believes it has concluded.

Annamede openly supports accession to Thumping Waters under a negotiated charter. She argues that Pitax requires dependable allies, representation within a larger republic, and institutions capable of restraining the next ambitious ruler before another palace grows a hundred doors.

> Pitax does not need another king. It needs neighbors who will notice if we begin building one.  
> —Annamede Belavara, First Speaker

#### Atlee Quinge, Speaker for Arts and Education

Atlee directs the Red Crescent Theatre, which served as a safe house and organizing center during the resistance. He represents theaters, performers, teachers, and cultural institutions. His work has focused upon ending royal control of artistic patronage, rebuilding the Red Crescent's company, and reforming institutions that received impressive buildings but inadequate teachers under Irovetti.

Atlee favors close cooperation with Thumping Waters but insists that any accession charter protect Pitax's artistic institutions from direct political control.

#### Drey Yarness, Speaker for Law and Redress

Drey Yarness is the sharp-tongued high priest of the Cathedral of Calistria and was among Irovetti's most persistent public critics. His knowledge of the city, its grievances, and the secrets of its powerful families made the cathedral a natural center of opposition.

Drey oversees the review of royal confiscations, political imprisonment, official corruption, and claims against former palace officers. He supports accession in principle but has demanded clear protections for local courts, religious freedom, and the right of Pitaxians to pursue lawful redress against officials of either government.

#### Ghare Leotos, Speaker for Relief and the Faiths

Ghare Leotos is the venerable priest of Desna whose temple quietly aided citizens oppressed or impoverished under Irovetti. During the uprising, the temple provided sanctuary and helped protect civilians from the violence surrounding the palace.

Ghare coordinates relief work, refugee returns, charitable distribution, and communication among Pitax's faiths. He has not opposed accession but argues that food, shelter, and the return of displaced families must remain more urgent than constitutional ceremony.

#### Gasperre Liacenza, Speaker for the Banded Houses

Gasperre Liacenza represents the old merchant families commonly called the Banded Houses. He supported the resistance from the Falling Star and helped persuade commercial interests that Irovetti's fall would not mean the destruction of trade.

His presence gives the council access to established credit, warehouses, and family networks, while also ensuring that the Banded Houses cannot claim exclusion from decisions they will be expected to obey. Gasperre supports a treaty with Thumping Waters but remains cautious about any arrangement that would subordinate Pitaxian merchants to ministries in Thumpington.

#### Jhofre Vascari, Speaker for Trade and River Commerce

Jhofre Vascari leads the Riversong Trade House and spent much of his life as a merchant captain along the Sellen. During the resistance, he helped obstruct Irovetti's supply network and brought his family's influence over river commerce to the coalition.

Jhofre oversees the reopening of trade routes, negotiation of tariffs, and restoration of confidence among visiting captains. He is among the strongest commercial supporters of accession, provided Pitax retains authority over the daily management of its harbor.

#### Joravin Pyathe, Speaker for Harbor and Public Works

Joravin Pyathe is the dwarven Shipmaster of the Yards. Long regarded as gruff but fair, he served as an arbiter among merchant families even when Irovetti's arbitrary codes and taxes made his position nearly impossible.

Joravin now supervises harbor repair, tariffs, bridges, roads, and the unglamorous physical work upon which Pitax's recovery depends. He has treated the accession debate as an engineering problem: determine what each government must carry, identify where the structure will fail, and refuse to approve it until the supports are visible.

#### Roald Celinnas, Speaker for Records and Learning

Roald Celinnas owns the Crow's Feather, an inn and informal library that supplied information and refuge to the resistance. His warning that members of Irovetti's inner circle might be magical shapeshifters proved especially valuable during the assault upon the palace.

Roald supervises the recovery of public records, the cataloguing of palace archives, and inquiries into property or legal claims obscured by Irovetti's bookkeeping. He denies having political ambitions and attends council meetings with the demeanor of a man unfairly required to prevent everyone else from losing the paperwork.

#### Ingris, Speaker for Markets and Households

Ingris is a Pitaxian cheesemaker whose son was beaten to death by Irovetti's wardens. She sheltered members of the resistance in her back room and used her deliveries to the palace as a quiet act of defiance.

Her appointment gave small traders, market workers, and ordinary households a council voice not dependent upon the Banded Houses. Ingris concerns herself with food prices, market safety, restitution for victims, and whether civic plans described as efficient remain efficient when attempted by someone carrying a basket through the street.

### Influential Figures Outside the Council

The council does not contain every person who aided the uprising. Saufie Cintost, who risked her life preparing to misdirect Irovetti's ships, now serves as Master of Beacons and advises Joravin on harbor security. Asmeranda Ilata returned to the Red Crescent Theatre after her rescue and has declined a permanent government office, although her public support remains influential. Headmistress Atalia Gitaren advises Atlee on reform of the Academy of the Arts.

Eliste Strocalle also assisted the final effort against Irovetti by misdirecting supplies and offering refuge. She was not granted a council seat. Her family's criminal ties and her earlier role in Irovetti's rise made inclusion politically unacceptable to much of the resistance. House Strocalle remains wealthy, organized, and fully capable of influencing events from outside the council chamber.

Engelidis remains at large. The possibility that she or another shapeshifter could return under an assumed identity has made verification, recordkeeping, and magical examination uncomfortable features of the new administration.

### The Accession Debate

Pitax remains formally independent, but negotiations with Thumping Waters have advanced beyond wartime cooperation. The two governments already coordinate road repairs, refugee returns, trade, security, and the search for surviving agents of Irovetti. Many Pitaxians now ask whether those practical arrangements should become permanent.

Supporters of accession argue that Pitax cannot safely stand alone while rebuilding. Membership in Thumping Waters would provide military protection, access to established ministries, broader trade, and representation within a republic whose leaders helped remove Irovetti without occupying the city as conquerors. Annamede has become the most forceful advocate of this position.

Opponents do not necessarily favor restoration of the crown. Some fear that rule from Thumpington would merely replace one distant set of priorities with another. The Banded Houses worry about tariffs and regulation. Artists remember too clearly what happened when government patronage became government control. Other citizens want Pitax to prove it can govern itself before surrendering any part of its independence.

The developing proposal would preserve the Civic Council as Pitax's local government while integrating defense, diplomacy, major roads, and national taxation with the Republic. Pitax would receive representation within the government of Thumping Waters and retain a local charter protecting its courts, temples, markets, and cultural institutions.

No final vote has been scheduled. Annamede has argued that accession should be approved both by the council and by a public vote rather than settled through private negotiation among former resistance leaders.

### A Possible Governor

If Pitax joins Thumping Waters, Annamede Belavara is widely considered the certain leading candidate to become its first governor. She possesses public recognition, magical power, support within the artistic community, ties to both governments, and the rare ability to command a Pitaxian crowd without first erecting a statue of herself.

Annamede has not formally declared her candidacy because no office yet exists. She has also made little effort to discourage the expectation. Her allies describe her as the person most capable of negotiating with Thumpington without allowing Pitax to disappear inside the agreement. Her critics describe her as exhausting, ambitious, and entirely too pleased by the sound of a difficult question.

Both descriptions may be accurate.

### Reconstruction

The work of recovery continues. Food stores redirected to Irovetti's army must be replenished. Roads and bridges require repair. Soldiers have returned to households that cannot always support them. Families seek the missing, victims seek restitution, and officials continue discovering neglected responsibilities beneath the ornamental remains of royal government.

Markets have reopened, ships have returned to the docks, and performances no longer require the approval of a king. Satire remains especially popular. Several statues of Irovetti have been dismantled; others have been retained because citizens discovered that a sufficiently pompous stone king makes an excellent public notice board.

Pitax's future remains unsettled. Its crown is gone, its council is provisional, and its relationship with Thumping Waters has not yet found a final form. For the first time in many years, those uncertainties belong to Pitax rather than to the man who ruled it.

### From the Correspondent's Desk

> Irovetti believed that every road, song, ship, and conversation in Pitax existed to lead eventually back to him. The council's greatest success may be that citizens can now disagree about their destination.  
> —Sella Copperclaw
