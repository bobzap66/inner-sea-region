# Fort Serenko


### Fort Serenko

Standing on the northern border of the Republic of Thumping Waters, **Fort Serenko** watches over the crossroads between the [Stolen Lands](../../../Stolen%20Lands.md) and [Brevoy](../../../../../Brevoy/Brevoy.md). Once a crumbling outpost of the old Taldan expansion, its stones cracked and its banners long since rotted away, the ruin was reclaimed and rebuilt by the Tubthumpers as a bulwark of trade, travel, and peace. Today, its palisades ring with the sound of saws and laughter, not swords, and its bridges carry more merchants than soldiers. But beneath the bustle lies the memory of watchfires once lit against a darker age.

#### Overview

Fort Serenko sits astride the old Rostland road, where the grasslands of Brevoy meet the tamed frontier of Thumping Waters. Its **wooden walls** follow the footprint of the ancient stone fort, enclosing a lively settlement whose rhythm hums to the clatter of wagon wheels and millstones. The **bridge** over the Shrike tributary marks the northern gate of the republic, its planks worn smooth by traders, diplomats, and the occasional would-be smuggler who misjudges the vigilance of the guards.

Within, the **marketplace** forms the heart of the town—half trade post, half fairground. Caravans from Brevoy meet frontier merchants beneath striped awnings while the scent of flour and resin fills the air from the nearby **mill** and **lumberyard**. The **inn**, *The Lantern Gate*, stands at the crossroads, its hearth burning day and night in welcome to travelers. Across from it, an **herbalist’s shop** overflows with wildflowers, salves, and gossip—especially about “that time the Prime Minister came through and bought cough syrup disguised as whiskey.”

#### History

The ruins of old Serenko once guarded Brevoy’s southern frontier, abandoned after the fall of Taldor’s Fifth Army of Exploration. For centuries, it was little more than a haunt for bandits and wind. When the Tubthumpers expanded northward, Mairi ordered the ruins rebuilt—not as a fortress of war, but as a fortress of welcome. Soldiers raised new walls atop ancient stone, engineers repaired the bridge, and settlers filled the empty courtyard with homes and workshops. The republic’s flag rose where Brevoy’s once fell, and the outpost took on a new life as a border town of unity rather than conquest.

Today, the name “Fort Serenko” honors both the ruin’s old title and its new purpose. The outer walls still bear carved sun-symbols from Taldan masons, while the town’s gate bears the motto added by its new keepers: *“Let none cross unwelcome, and none cross alone.”*

#### Government and Trade

Fort Serenko is governed by a **warden’s council** of merchants, guards, and guild leaders who report directly to the republic’s Minister of Trade. The garrison in the **barracks** is small but professional—trained soldiers and volunteer rangers who know the value of patience over aggression. The fort’s **stockyard** serves both Brevic caravans and Thumping Waters’ herders, its corrals alive with bleating goats, braying mules, and a chorus of negotiators arguing over whose livestock eats the better grass.

Timber and grain drive the economy. The **lumberyard** supplies boards for [Olegton](../Olegton/Olegton.md)’s traders and ships for [Fort Tuskwater](../Fort%20Tuskwater/Fort%20Tuskwater.md)’s fleets, while the **mill** grinds wheat from the nearby plains. In return, Brevoy’s merchants bring fine cloth, steel, and the occasional cask of Rostland brandy—though never enough to keep up with local demand. The **marketplace** thrums with the energy of cooperation: Brevic merchants haggling with kobold traders, centaur emissaries trading arrow fletchings for honey. Fort Serenko’s strength lies not in its walls, but in its bridges—literal and diplomatic alike.

#### Culture and Daily Life

Fort Serenko is a town that rises early and rests late. The millwheel begins turning with the dawn, and the lumberyard hammers echo long after sunset. The people are a mix of old soldiers, frontier craftsmen, and Brevic traders who decided they preferred freedom to formality. The **inn** hosts nightly music, usually of Brevic lutes and southern drums sharing the same rhythm, while the watchfires on the walls burn with lanterns enchanted to shimmer gold and blue—the colors of the republic. There’s a saying here: “A Serenko lantern never goes out, only home.”

Though small, the fort has a sense of purpose that feels larger than its size. Every wagon that crosses its bridge adds another note to its song, every deal another verse. It is not the heart of the republic, but its open hand—the first welcome and the last farewell for those who pass between kingdoms.

#### Modern Role

Today, Fort Serenko stands as the northern gateway of Thumping Waters and a living symbol of its diplomacy. It guards not just the border, but the peace beyond it. Caravans and envoys from Brevoy rest here before continuing south; soldiers and settlers pause here to remember that walls can protect more than they divide. When the lamps along the bridge ignite at dusk and their [Reflections](../../../../../../../Campaigns/Abomination%20Vaults/Vignettes%20-%20Abomination%20Vaults/Reflections.md) shimmer on the river below, it’s easy to believe the town’s old stones have finally found what they were built for—not war, but welcome.

## General Details

##### From the Journal of Linzi, Minister of Communication of Thumping Waters

*The Lanterns of Serenko*

Fort Serenko smells of sawdust, river mist, and ambition—a heady perfume for a frontier town. The first time I crossed its bridge, the guards were whistling one of my songs (badly, I might add), and a cart of lumber nearly ran me over because the driver was arguing about the lyrics. I felt at home immediately.

There’s something uncanny about Serenko’s energy. Most forts brood—Serenko bustles. Its walls creak with laughter, its streets hum with trade, and its people seem allergic to standing still. The innkeeper at *The Lantern Gate* told me they used to measure success by how many wagons left town each morning. Now they measure it by how many come back. “If the road brings them twice,” she said, “then the fort did something right.” I wrote that down. It’s going in a speech somewhere.

I toured the **old ruins** that the Tubthumpers rebuilt years ago. The lower stones are still Taldan—carved with suns and laurels, faded by centuries. Above them rise new timbers, carved with the crest of the republic and strung with paper lanterns that glow blue and gold. One of the guards called them “stars that don’t forget where the sky is.” I think that’s what this whole place is: a conversation between what was and what chose to stay.

Every evening, the bridge lamps are lit one by one. The mill quiets, the marketplace closes, and the water below mirrors a ribbon of light stretching north into Brevoy and south into the republic. Travelers stop to watch—farmers, merchants, a few weary guards leaning on spears—and for a moment the whole fort seems to hold its breath. Then someone starts singing, always a different tune, and the moment passes, warm as the lamplight on the river.

They say the first of those lanterns was hung by Mairi herself, when the Tubthumpers crossed into Brevoy for the first diplomatic summit. Whether that’s true or not hardly matters—it feels true, and that’s the kind of truth that keeps the night at bay. When I left Fort Serenko, I looked back from the hill and saw those lights winking through the mist. For a second, I couldn’t tell if they were lanterns or stars. Maybe they were both. Maybe that’s the point.

*—Linzi*

Editor’s note: Linzi’s claim that the guards of Fort Serenko “sing with heroic enthusiasm but absolutely no sense of pitch” has been verified by multiple witnesses.
