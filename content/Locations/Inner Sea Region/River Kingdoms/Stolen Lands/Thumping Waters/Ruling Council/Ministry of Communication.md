# Ministry of Communication

![](../../../../../../assets/images/shared-cover-48fb79abc6f1.png)

*An institutional profile maintained by the Office of Public Record*

The Ministry of Communication is the voice and memory of the Republic of Thumping Waters. It carries government notices across a wide and difficult territory, preserves the national record, maintains official correspondence, and helps citizens understand what their government has done, intends to do, and occasionally wishes it had explained sooner.

The ministry is led by [Linzi](../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/NPCs/Linzi.md), who has held the office since the Republic's founding. Its responsibilities have expanded from handwritten journals and courier routes into a national archive, a working government press, and the publication network supporting the *Lantern and Ledger*.

### Mandate

The Ministry of Communication records council proceedings, publishes laws and public notices, maintains correspondence between ministries and settlements, preserves historical documents, and distributes warnings during emergencies. It is also responsible for ensuring that official information reaches citizens who live far beyond the capital's roads and printing presses.

The ministry distinguishes among public news, official instruction, historical record, and restricted intelligence. Information may be withheld when publication would expose an operation, endanger a citizen, reveal a source, or materially assist an enemy. Withholding information does not remove the obligation to preserve it. Restricted reports remain part of the government record even when they cannot yet become part of the public one.

### Offices and Services

#### Office of Public Record

The Office of Public Record preserves laws, council minutes, treaties, maps, proclamations, reports, correspondence, and historical accounts. It maintains both working copies for government use and protected originals intended to survive fire, war, careless filing, and ministers borrowing documents without signing for them.

Linzi's journals form an important part of the early archive. Later research annotates those accounts when the identities of enemies, the sequence of events, or the testimony of participants becomes clearer.

#### Herald and Courier Service

Heralds, town criers, posting agents, and government couriers carry announcements between Thumpington and the Republic's settlements. The service distributes emergency warnings, council notices, election or appointment announcements, road closures, festival schedules, and bundles of printed material.

In distant communities, the same courier may carry military orders, tax forms, private letters, and three editions of the newspaper delayed by weather. The ministry coordinates routes with the Interior and Defense ministries but maintains its own chains of custody for sealed correspondence.

#### Government Printing Office

The Government Printing Office produces laws, forms, proclamations, instructional pamphlets, public broadsheets, and standardized copies of official documents. Its press components were recovered in Abadius 4711 AR after Linzi's unauthorized purchase led to the rescue of [Eobald](../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/NPCs/Eobald.md) and the machinery from the Jaggedbriar Coven.

It took nearly a year to repair and assemble the press. For its first years of operation, government work consumed most of its schedule. The *Lantern and Ledger* began regular publication on Sarenith 1, 4714 AR after Linzi established a permanent editorial staff and distribution system.

#### The University Press

The University Press uses the Republic's printing equipment and distribution network by agreement with the ministry. It publishes lectures, translations, maps, field reports, catalogues, scholarly papers, and practical manuals prepared through the Free University of Thumping Waters.

Access to public machinery does not make the University Press a government publisher. The College of Letters and Public Record maintains its own editorial review, and university affiliations do not convert scholarly conclusions into official policy. The ministry may preserve, print, and distribute a work without endorsing its interpretation. This distinction is especially important for research concerning Candlemere, the [Wild Hunt](../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/Vignettes%20-%20Kingmaker/Intelligence%20Reports/Wild%20Hunt.md), and other subjects upon which evidence remains incomplete.

#### Office of Cultural Record

The Office of Cultural Record collects songs, oral histories, local legends, memorial accounts, festival traditions, and testimony from the many peoples of the Republic. Its work is intended to prevent national history from becoming solely the story of ministers, battles, and capital projects.

Contributions are gathered from human settlements, kobold and goblin communities, iruxi, bog striders, centaurs, immigrants, refugees, and anyone else prepared to correct the capital's assumptions about life beyond Thumpington.

### The Minister

Linzi began recording the Tubthumpers before Thumping Waters existed. Her journal became the earliest sustained account of the Republic's founders, their expeditions, and the settlements that grew from them. When the nation formed, that work became the foundation of the Ministry of Communication.

As minister, Linzi prepares public statements, maintains sensitive council records, edits the *Lantern and Ledger*, and continues to write historical and memorial pieces. The combination gives her unusual influence over how events enter public memory. The ministry addresses that concentration of authority through named bylines, identified editorials, preserved source records, and the managing editor's authority over stories in which Linzi herself becomes the subject.

### The Lantern and Ledger

The *Lantern and Ledger* uses the government press and distribution network but maintains its own masthead, reporters, bylines, and corrections policy. It publishes several regular editions each month alongside special editions and urgent broadsheets.

The paper is not independent of government: its editor is a minister and its reporting draws heavily upon official access. It does, however, distinguish news from editorial opinion and has printed facts inconvenient to the Republic, including the circumstances of enemy surrenders and failures by its own leaders.

### Relations with Other Ministries

Every ministry depends upon Communication to publish rules, distribute notices, preserve decisions, and explain policy. Defense supplies battlefield reports and emergency instructions. Intelligence provides material cleared for public release and maintains separate secure channels for restricted information. Justice reviews official language whose ambiguity could alter legal meaning. Interior coordinates settlement and courier access. Magic verifies supernatural warnings that cannot safely be reduced to rumor. Treasury pays for paper and asks why every request marked “essential” contains illustrations.

The ministry also works closely with the Free University's College of Letters and Public Record. The two institutions cooperate on archival standards, oral histories, translation, printing instruction, and preservation while remaining separate in authority. Ministry records belong to the public administration; university scholarship may examine and criticize that administration under the terms of the university charter.

### Public Experience

Citizens most often encounter the ministry through posted notices, arriving couriers, printed forms, public warnings, and the newspaper. Its success is difficult to measure because useful communication usually prevents the confusion that would otherwise reveal its absence.

The ministry accepts corrections, local reports, letters, memorial notices, and announcements from throughout the Republic. It does not guarantee publication, return of submitted manuscripts, or agreement with the author regarding the historical importance of a particularly large fish.

> If we tell only the stories that make us look wise, future generations will conclude either that we were liars or that wisdom looked very different in our time.  
> —Linzi, Minister of Communication
