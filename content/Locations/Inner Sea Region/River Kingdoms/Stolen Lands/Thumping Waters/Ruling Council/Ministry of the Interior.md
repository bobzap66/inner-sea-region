# Ministry of the Interior

![](../../../../../../assets/images/shared-cover-48fb79abc6f1.png)

*An institutional profile maintained by the Office of Public Record*

The Ministry of the Interior manages the physical growth and civil administration of the Republic of Thumping Waters. It coordinates settlement development, public works, roads, surveys, governors, frontier administration, and the practical relationship between the capital and communities spread across the [Stolen Lands](../../Stolen%20Lands.md).

Antson “[Ant](../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/Characters/Ant.md)” Crazyfoot currently leads the ministry. [Seamus](../../../../../../Campaigns/Kingmaker/Characters/Seamus.md) held the office on the founding council before the two ministers exchanged responsibilities as the Republic's needs and their duties changed.

### Mandate

The ministry helps establish and expand settlements, plans national roads and bridges, records public land, coordinates governors and local councils, maintains surveys, and directs government response to civil infrastructure emergencies.

Interior does not govern every community identically. Many settlements entered the Republic with their own chiefs, mayors, councils, or customs. The ministry's task is to connect those governments to the Republic, ensure access to national services, and establish the minimum standards required by law without turning every village into a smaller imitation of Thumpington.

### Offices and Services

#### Office of Settlement Affairs

This office maintains contact with governors, mayors, chiefs, and local councils. It coordinates settlement charters, boundary changes, incorporation, emergency requests, censuses, and disputes over which government is responsible for a problem everyone agrees belongs to someone else.

When a community joins the Republic, Settlement Affairs records the terms of accession and identifies which local traditions remain in force. It also helps new settlements establish basic administration before enthusiasm outruns food stores, drainage, and defensible walls.

#### Office of Roads and Public Works

This office plans and maintains national roads, bridges, docks, public buildings, levees, and other infrastructure connecting the Republic. Local labor and contractors perform much of the work, while Interior establishes priorities and coordinates funding with the Treasury.

Projects requiring fortification or magical reinforcement are reviewed with Defense and Magic. Notices concerning closures, hazards, and construction schedules are distributed through Communication.

#### Office of Surveys and Maps

Surveyors, cartographers, scouts, and local guides chart rivers, forests, hills, roads, ruins, resources, and dangerous territory. Their work supports settlement, defense, trade, taxation, and exploration.

The office preserves both official maps and field corrections. A road drawn upon a map does not become passable merely because the cartographer used a confident line.

#### Office of Frontier Stewardship

Rangers and regional officers monitor dangerous wildlife, monster movement, hunting grounds, forest use, and conditions along unsettled borders. The office issues wilderness advisories and coordinates responses when a natural hazard becomes a public threat.

Stewardship is not solely a system for extracting resources. The Republic depends upon forests, rivers, grazing lands, and relationships with communities that inhabited the region before its charters were written. The office is expected to consider all of them before approving development.

### History of the Ministry

The ministry began when most of the Republic existed only as exploration notes and intended roads. Seamus served as the founding Minister of the Interior, coordinating early settlement and the practical work required to turn a wilderness charter into government.

Antson Crazyfoot later moved from Intelligence to Interior while Seamus assumed responsibility for Intelligence. The exchange placed Ant's extensive field experience, scouting, and knowledge of the Republic's terrain at the center of settlement and infrastructure planning.

The ministry's responsibilities expanded with every incorporated community. [Sootscale Valley](../Settlements/Sootscale%20Valley/Sootscale%20Valley.md), [Greengripe](../Settlements/Greengripe/Greengripe.md), [Longtail Island](../Settlements/Longtail%20Island/Longtail%20Island.md), [Varnhold](../Settlements/Varnhold/Varnhold.md), [Fort Drelev](../Settlements/Fort%20Drelev/Fort%20Drelev.md), [Tok-Nikrat](../Settlements/Tok-Nikrat/Tok-Nikrat.md), and the Nomen territories each required different arrangements. The resulting system is less uniform than a planner might prefer and considerably more durable than forcing every community into one model.

### The Minister

As minister, Ant approves major surveys, presents settlement and infrastructure priorities to the council, coordinates governors, and represents frontier needs in national planning. His personal experience exploring much of the territory gives the office direct familiarity with regions that older governments knew chiefly as blank spaces.

The ministry's permanent staff turns field observations into schedules, specifications, budgets, deeds, and maintenance plans. This is necessary because “there should be a bridge here” is a useful beginning and an inadequate construction order.

### Relations with Other Ministries

Interior works with Treasury on budgets and contracts, Defense on military roads and secure outposts, Magic on supernatural hazards and enchanted works, Justice on land and jurisdictional disputes, Communication on public notices and maps, and Intelligence on threats affecting frontier development. The Prime Minister sets national priorities when need exceeds labor and revenue, which it invariably does.

### Public Experience

Citizens encounter the ministry through roads, deeds, surveys, building projects, local officials, ranger warnings, and the long interval between approval of a bridge and the moment anyone can cross it.

Complaints about public works may be filed through a local government or directly with the ministry. Statements that a road has “always flooded there” are considered valuable evidence and an argument for repair, not an explanation for why repair is impossible.
