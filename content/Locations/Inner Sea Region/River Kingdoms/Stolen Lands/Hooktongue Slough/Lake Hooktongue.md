# Lake Hooktongue


### Lake Hooktongue

  

#### Geography

Nestled deep within the Hooktongue Slough, Lake Hooktongue is less a lake than a widening of the swamp itself — a sluggish, gray expanse where the reeds give way to deep, still water. Hemlock and willow choke its eastern banks, their roots curling into the black mire, while to the west the lake yawns open, dotted with small islands and half-sunken trees. The waters are cold, heavy, and never still for long.
  

#### Legends and Dangers

Every hunter and fisherman in the Slough knows the name Hooktongue Orm. Said to be a serpent as long as a galley and as black as midnight, the creature is whispered to dwell in the deepest trench of the lake. Outsiders dismiss the tales as rustic superstition, but those who live along the Slough know better. Whole barges have vanished beneath the surface with no trace but a ripple and the scent of brine.
  

Offerings of meat, silver, or blood are left along the stony shore before trapping or fishing expeditions — not as worship, but as insurance. The Orm’s appetite, they say, is endless, and its patience shorter than a summer storm.

#### Modern History

Since the founding of the Republic of Thumping Waters, Lake Hooktongue has remained largely unclaimed territory — too wild for settlement, too cursed for comfort. Expeditions from Thumpington have charted its borders but few dare its depths. Rumors persist that the Hooktongue Orm is not merely a beast, but something ancient and cunning, perhaps even a relic of the First World that never left when the rest of its kind withdrew.
